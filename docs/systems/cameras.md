# Cameras

`HBHT.Core.Cameras`.

## Why the original behaves as it does

The user timed the outdoor camera in 1.0. After running left or right and stopping, it takes 500–1000 ms to settle. Standing still, it turns slowly toward Alaron's back and decelerates as it gets there: 45 s from about 5° off head-on to 90°, then 70 s more to settle behind him. Facing it head-on, it stays put.

The shape of `Camera::Mode0` (`0x800a6e64`) explains that. The aim chases Alaron at 1/12 of the gap per 60 Hz tick. Standing still, `Actor::Move` calls `SetFeild70` every frame, so the goal behind him is approached at 1/144 a tick, and the camera at 1/16 of that. The turn is then proportional to the sine of the angle off his back, which stalls head-on. Both steps loop `delta` times against a goal fixed for the frame, so the original's turn rate goes with its frame rate: the user's timings fit a `delta` of about 2.6 ticks. The port steps it at the 30 fps cadence ([ADR 0017](../adr/0017-the-core-steps-every-tick.md)); the trek `delta` distribution is ADR 0006's follow-up.

## The follow camera (`Camera::Mode0`)

Ported as `HBHT.Core.Cameras.FollowCamera`, from `Camera::Mode0` and the R orbit (`0x800a72bc`, the decomp's `FUN_800b0fac`), which `Mode0` and `Mode1` both call.

- With R held, the orbit keeps the camera between 0 and 5 above the aim, its reach across the ground easing toward 5 by 1/32 of the gap a tick, so it tops out at 45° once eased out and is steeper while nearer, and never goes below level with Alaron. The user found the port's orbit matches 1.0's.
- **Port addition: moving while orbiting.** In the original the stick orbits with R held and he stands still. On a dual-stick pad the right stick, Q and E, and the mouse turn the camera while the left stick or WASD still walk him (`PlayerInput.Free`, in `HBHT.Core.Trek`); only R with the stick holds him, as on the N64. The orbit's goal is a fixed point it turns about, so a walking player would leave it behind and swing the view. While he walks, `FollowCamera.Orbit(…, moving: true)` carries that point by how far the aim moved each tick, so the view stays as it was turned. Letting go of the stick returns the camera behind him as it does after R. The user's choice.
- **Port addition: steering it.** What turns the camera is the player's to bind on the options' Controls page ([ui](pause-menu.md#controls), [ADR 0026](../adr/0026-controls-are-bound-by-situation.md)), and `Game.ReadInput` makes the `PlayerInput` from it:
  - **Camera's own sources** (by default the right stick) and Camera Left and Right (Q and E) turn it while he walks, as above.
  - **Camera Hold** (RB, or the right mouse button) is R. A source on both Move and Camera (by default the left stick) moves him, and while Camera Hold is held steers the camera instead, and he stands still (`PlayerInput.Free` false, as R with the stick). A source on Move alone still walks him under the hold, so WASD walks while the mouse turns the camera with the right button held. He stands still only while what would have moved him steers.
  - **The mouse** can't move him. Off the Camera row it steers while Camera Hold is held, a drag: about 0.3° of orbit and 0.02 of height a pixel, times Mouse Sensitivity. On the Camera row it's mouse look: it always steers, and the runtime captures it in trek and a battle's turn and lets it go elsewhere. The orbit is then held every tick (`PlayerInput.Orbit`), so the camera stays where the mouse leaves it and is carried with him as he walks, rather than returning behind him when the mouse stops, and the stick walks him the way the camera looks.
  - **Stick look:** the right stick on Camera, the default, turns it as Q and E do; pushed up it raises the camera, as R with the stick does. The mouse's up lowers it, as mouse look does in most games. There's no Invert row: the stick keeps the N64's sense, and the mouse the PC's.
- `Mode0` doesn't avoid walls at all. Only the R orbit pulls in. It moves an eighth of the way a frame toward a point 0.25 *past* the wall (the ROM has the −0.25), while still easing out toward 5. The two settle about 3.1 from the player, so with a wall any closer, the camera ends up behind it. The user found the port's matches 1.0's at walls.
- Interiors use `Mode1`, which does keep the camera off walls (below).
- Loading and dialogue set a quick swing (`unk80`), but the first tick after a load cancels it, because the anchor starts at the origin.

## Which mode

The camera's mode is chosen only when the player goes through a teleporter (`TeleportPlayer`), by the chunk he arrives in: `Mode1` where its terrain doesn't draw the sky (the original's sky type 4: terrains 6, 8, 9, 11 and 13–16, the importer's `TerrainRecord.SkyDrawn` false), `Mode0` elsewhere. Walking from chunk to chunk keeps it, so Gwernia castle's courtyard (terrain 25) keeps whatever mode the door into it gave. Loading a save from the game's menu teleports too, so it chooses the same way. Loading from the title screen doesn't: the zone engine starts in `Mode0` and keeps it until the next door, so the outdoor camera shows inside a house until a camera object takes over. That's a bug, and the port fixes it, as the user chose (ADR 0018): however the game starts, the chunk chooses. `FollowCamera.Mode` holds it, and `FollowCamera.ModeFor` chooses it.

A teleport places the camera (`FollowCamera.Arrive`): first it moves with the player and stops orbiting (`ApplyZoneVelocity`, `GiveCameraToPlayer`), then it goes to the teleporter's camera point if it names one. Otherwise, in an interior it's 4 *in front of* the player and 3 up, looking back at him: the original subtracts its facing, which points behind him. Outdoors it's behind him, as a map load puts it, and swings round quickly for 5 frames (`unk80`).

Camera objects work through a teleport's fade as well, as the original's zone engine runs on under it, so the room's camera has the camera as the screen comes up.

## The interiors' camera (`Camera::Mode1`)

Ported as `FollowCamera.Interior.cs`, with the arguments `ProcessGameCamera` passes: 2, 4, 0.5, 2.5, 1.5, 3, 16, 32, 0.25 and 0.1. The jump behind the player sweeps a sphere of 0.25.

- It hangs back on a leash rather than swinging behind. Its goal is 1.5 above the aim's goal, and only dragged along to stay 2 to 4 from it in X and Z. So walking away, it follows 4 behind; walking toward it, it backs away 2 ahead.
- It eases toward its goal at 1/32 of the way a tick, but no more than 0.1 (6 a second), and turns round the aim toward it by a degree a tick. It aims 1.05 above the player, chasing that at 1/16 a tick, at most 0.25.
- It's never nearer than 2 in X and Z, and it can't pass through a wall: the sphere swept from where it was stops 0.25 off it.
- When it has caught up with its goal and the aim is out of sight from there, its goal jumps to 3 behind the player, or to the wall in the way, and it doesn't do that again for 5 seconds.
- R orbits as outdoors, at a distance easing to 3, between 0.5 and 2.5 above the aim. Letting go zeroes the pitch, which only the orbit uses here.
- Every part loops over a frame's ticks or acts on a goal that only it moves, so stepping it each tick matches the original at any frame rate.
- It never wanders past a wall on its own, and running round a corner it snaps back behind Alaron. R's orbit can push it through a wall; letting go, it turns until it's back inside, with the same snap.

## The combat camera (`ProcessCombatCamera`)

Ported as `HBHT.Core.Combat.CombatCamera`, from `ProcessCombatCamera` (`0x8008D8BC`, camera mode 4). It's the interiors' camera with other numbers, which `FollowCamera.StepLeashed` takes (`LeashNumbers`; the interiors' are `FollowCamera.Interior`): the goal leashed 4 to 10 from the aim in X and Z (8 to 16 while aiming a flask or placing an area), 2 above it, the aim 1.05 above the body that owns the camera and chasing it at 1/16 a tick, at most 1, the eye at 1/32, at most 0.5. It's put 8 behind a party member away from the nearest enemy, 1.5 up, at once on the battle's first frame and as a goal when a member's turn takes the camera; through an enemy's turn the eye holds where its goal was, at least 4 from the aim; and it's kept a unit above the arena's floor. [Combat](combat.md#the-rest-by-entry-point) has the addresses.

## The enhanced battle camera (Camera: Enhanced)

A port enhancement, the options' **Camera: Original / Enhanced** on the Enhancements page ([pause menu](pause-menu.md#options)), for battles played turn by turn (Combat Turns: Original). Original is the default, and under it nothing changes. It's piece B of Start Early's plan in the [roadmap](../roadmap.md#planned); the user set what it must do, and its design is here rather than in an ADR, their choice. Under Start Early the camera is Start Early's, for now the newest action's ([combat](combat.md#overlapping-the-ais-turns)); piece C will make it this one's.

**It's in the core**, not the drawing. A turn's stick drives the body by the camera's look (`Battle.InputTurn`), and the free cursor and a placed area move by it (`MoveAimCursor`, `MoveArea`), so the camera the player sees has to be the one they're read against. `CombatCamera` steps the original's camera every tick, as it always has (`StepOriginal`), and then, under Enhanced, `EnhancedCamera` over it: `CombatCamera.Camera` is still the original's, which R's orbit and a turn's start move, and `CombatCamera.Shown`, which `Battle.Camera` is, is the enhanced camera's. The recording, the drawing, the keys' listener and the radar read `Battle.Camera`. Under Original, `Shown` is the original's camera itself.

**What it shows**, by the battle's state (`EnhancedCamera.Decide`). The user's first play (Nov 7, 2026) reshaped it: the first version targeted party members, framed from high and far, moved too fast and swung through enemies' turns. So the player's turn became over the shoulder at an enemy, an AI's turn one shot planned for all of it, and aiming kept the reticle in the middle. Their second play (Nov 8) asked for the slower walk, the narrower cone, no cuts while walking, the pack, the spell's targets and reach, no overshoot, and the player's own actions planned. Their third (Nov 8 again) asked for these:
- no one under the conversation box;
- the view quicker when the character isn't in it;
- the player's turn near the character and low, never from high above;
- the target held while the character runs round its pack;
- one vantage for the battle that sets every shot's side, in place of each party member's own;
- a spell's aim low at its targets, without the caster;
- an arrow or a spell at someone further off as two shots, the loosing and the hit.

Their fourth (Nov 8 again) found the projectiles and Dragon Flames' aim right, and asked for these:
- a new shot's angle arriving with its place: the camera had moved over to its new spot, and then the orbit settled while they waited;
- Fireball's ring bigger, down to the conversation box;
- a turn's opening shot not changed straight away for another;
- the view knowing what to do with no enemy ahead. Their idea was a target made up 10 ahead of the character.

Two corrections followed the same day. Kept still with no enemy ahead, so the stick wouldn't turn under the player, the view felt stiff and unfamiliar: players know a third person's camera moves all the time, trailing the character, the stick turning with it, and that's the contract to keep. And a new framing chosen every 15 ticks as the character ran round a pack was like bumping against a rotating gear: it should be one smooth movement, orbiting the pack.

Their fifth (Nov 8 again) still found it bumpy, and found these:
- Alaron walked at the left of a pack of four bandits, and the camera swung round in front of him;
- it jittered with the walk's stride, which needed smoothing before a camera could follow it;
- the cut to the shots from above for aiming left them not knowing where on the field they looked: it should be a fast pan;
- a buff on a party member should have the ranged cast's cut to its effect;
- aiming a blow went to a new angle where the shot already framed both;
- enemies weren't followed as they walked.

- **No one under the conversation box.** Below the middle of the screen a subject must sit within 0.55 of the half-height (`FrameFloor`, `Lens.Floor`), the box's top being two thirds of the way down; elsewhere within 0.85 of the edge (`FrameMargin`). Every shot is framed by that lens, so a subject's sphere is held above the box.
- **The player's turn** (state 1): low over the character's shoulder, near them, at the enemy they head for and its pack.
  - **The opening.** The turn opens on the enemy the cone picks, else the nearest within 30 (`TargetRange`), and holds it while the character stands as the turn began, turned no more than 2°. So the shot the player first sees doesn't change under them before they've done anything: through the rats battle, with a second's wait each turn, it's on an enemy in every turn that has one in range and changes on none of 5,464 standing ticks (`ATurnOpensOnOneShotHeldTillThePlayerMoves`). The nearest is whom the action before planned toward (`NextYaw`). It used to open on the cone's pick alone, and with none in it on the original's camera, still swinging over from the last turn's character, so the shot changed straight away.
  - **The target.** A cone 20° either side of the way the character points (the way it walks, as it walks) takes the living enemies within 30 across the ground (`ConeAngle`, `TargetRange`); party members are never targets. Each scores by how near the cone's middle it is, `0.5 × (1 − (angle/20°)²)` (`AngleWeight`), and how near it is, `e^(−d/6)`, so within the cone it's the nearest one the character is heading for, whatever the weapon. The pick holds while it stays within 45° and 38 (`ConeHold`, `TargetRangeHold`), and while it's within 10 of the character as they head round it, no more than 110° from the way to it (`HoldNear`, `HoldNearAngle`): running round a pack for a backstab keeps the camera on it, the user's case, and walking past it or away lets it go, the orbit stopping while it's being let go (`TargetCone.Losing`). Once it leaves them it's still held for 45 ticks (`LoseHold`), so a stick held across the cone's edge, or a walk along the range's, doesn't flick the camera between it and no one. Another takes its place only once it has scored 0.5 better for 45 ticks in a row (`TargetCone`), so the stick's wobble doesn't flick the camera between two bandits.
  - **The pack.** Enemies within 8 of the target across the ground are its pack, and the shot leads toward the pack's middle, so the user's rats are centred, though it's turned over the shoulder at the target itself (below). While the target is within 14 of the character (`PackRange`) the pack is framed with it, at half its weight (`ClusterRadius`, `ClusterWeight`). Further off, only the target is held, small beyond the character. The user's Dougal had been framed from 34 away and 45° above to hold four rats across the field, so walking away from them left him off the screen.
  - **The shot** (`ShotFinder.Best`). It's aimed near the character, 0.35 of the way toward the pack's middle and no more than 3 (`AimLead`, `AimLeadMost`), so the character is in the lower part of the screen with what's ahead above them. It stands at most 14 from that aim (`ShoulderDistance`). Turns from straight behind of 0° to ±45° (`ShoulderYaws`) and pitches of 4° to 30° are tried, and the best scoring is taken. Up to ±120° were tried until the fifth play, where one 80° round showed Alaron from the front. None is higher: the user found the shots from high above lost the third person's feel. The costs:
    - a subject hidden or out of frame, 2 times its weight;
    - each radian of pitch from 12°, 1.2;
    - each radian turned from straight behind, 0.8;
    - each radian turned from the view now, 0.4;
    - the pair framed against the vantage's side, up to 1.5 (below).

    A target far off may stay behind another body rather than be seen from high, and a near one while a new framing is eased onto (below): through the rats battle, none of 2,272 ticks with a target beyond 14 hide it, and 18 of 2,433 with one nearer.
  - **Held, and eased onto.** The framing is kept as a turn from straight behind and a pitch, so as the character walks it follows round with the line to the target, the way they came. It's found again only once the character or the target is hidden from it or leaves it (`Frame`'s `judged`), or for a new target, and a new one is eased onto at 40° a second, its turn and its pitch (`FramingTurn`), never stepped to. Found again every 15 ticks and stepped to, a rise to see past a rat or a turn of 15° to 45° at a time, it was the user's rotating gear. So a target near may be hidden for a moment while the shot eases off. A target found after the view has been trailing the character starts from the trailing view's angle, as near as ±45° allows, and is eased round from there.
  - **Orbiting the target.** As the character goes round the target, the line from them to it turns, and the shot is turned with it straight away (`CappedMotion.Turn`), as its aim is carried (below), so it orbits the pack with them. It turns up to 90° a second (`CarryTurn`), and less near the target, by the share of 3 the character is from it (`ShoulderNear`): there the line means little, and swung the shot round by as much as 166° as a character passed between two rats. Faster, it catches up after, at that rate, a new target's line among it. Walking round the rats, the shot never turns more than 2.2° in a tick, the orbit and the easing at full rate together (`GoingRoundAPackTheShotTurnsSmoothly`). It was the line to the pack's middle until the fifth play: walking straight at the left of the user's four bandits, that line turned 63°, and a new framing 80° round on top of it put the camera in front of Alaron.
  - **Never round to the front.** The orbit and a new framing never turn the shot further than 90° from straight behind the way a walking character heads (`MostAcross`), or than its last goal already was. Where the player turns the character toward the camera themselves it stays, as a third person's camera does, and turning them never turns it. Through four bandits, walked at and past either side, the shot is past the character's side on 12 of 3,845 ticks over the shoulder, each where the player turned them, and turns itself further on none (`OverTheShoulderTheShotNeverTurnsItselfToTheFrontOfAWalker`).
  - **With no enemy ahead** (`EnhancedCamera.Ahead`), it trails them on a leash, as a third person's camera does. From the shot as the target was lost, the eye stays where it is but for being drawn after the character at its distance: a step across the view turns it round after them, so walking on they come to be seen from behind, and a step toward it or away only draws it along. Through the rats battle, every step across turned it after the character and every step toward or away left its yaw be (`WithNoEnemyAheadTheViewTrailsTheCharacterOnALeashLeadingThem`). Its aim leads them the way they face: toward a point 10 ahead (`LookAhead`, the user's), 0.35 of the way and no more than 3 (`AimLead`, `AimLeadMost`), so there's room ahead of them on the screen. A body or a wall hiding them turns, raises or draws it back by the least that clears it (`ShotFinder.Clearing`), and it keeps that.
    - **The stick turns with it,** as in any third-person game: held sideways, the character runs a circle round the camera at its distance. The fourth version kept the view still to spare the player that, which the user found stiff (above).
    - **What it replaced.** It used to follow the original's camera, whose leash swings it round behind a walker too, but the shot trailed it by 10° to 50°, and changed style each time the target was lost and found.
    - **A trap for scripts.** A battle plan holding the stick down every turn walks the party away to the field's edge, where the rats rest out of reach, and the battle never ends.
  - **It moves with the character.** Each tick the shot is carried by the step of a point drawn after the character (`FollowPoint`, `CappedMotion.Carry`), as a third person's camera is, and only the change of framing is capped. So the view never trails one who runs far, and it stops as they stop, overshooting nothing. Chasing the character under the walk's caps instead had left the aim 6 to 15 behind a runner, often off the screen.
    - **The point** is a critically damped spring 0.15 seconds long (`FollowSmoothing`), so their stride doesn't jolt the view. Carried by the body's own step, it did, the user's jitter: the original's movement moves a body every other tick, its root stopping and pushing off each stride, and Smoother's mean of the root still runs from 0.03 to 0.10 a tick with the gait. Under the original's movement the point's step changes by 2% of itself a tick where the body's changes by 203% (`TheViewFollowsACharacterEvenlyThoughTheirStrideIsUneven`). It trails a walker by twice its time times their speed, about 1.3 at a walk, and settles on them as they stop, never past them. Further than 6 off (`FollowReach`), a body put somewhere, it's put on them. The shot is placed by it, its aim, its leash and the way to the target; what it holds is still the body.
  - **Slower, unless the character leaves it.** In the player's turn the aim's and the distance's caps are halved and their settle by its square root (`WalkPace` 0.5, `EnhancedCamera.Walking`), so the view's framing drifts round after them. The turn's caps aren't: with the shot carried along with the character, a halved turn trailed in after its place. The caps go up as the character goes out from the middle of the shot shown, from half way to its edge (`CatchUpFrom`). At the edge or beyond they reach twice the default (`LostPace`), and they come back down over 20 ticks (`CatchUpTicks`), since caps that fell under a quick move would let it overshoot. That's the user's rule: speed up if the character isn't visible. Through the rats battle the character is in the shot shown on 6,699 of 6,714 turn ticks.
  - **Never cut while walking,** but on the tick the turn begins or comes back from a menu or an aim. A cut turns the stick's way under the player, and with the target flicking it flipped the view back and forth. The turn's first tick cuts to its shot unless that's within 8 and 60° of the shot shown (`EnterCutDistance`, `EnterCutTurn`), and always when the shot shown doesn't hold the character. The turn begins with them in view; moved to from the last turn's shot, a long way, the eye swept past them.
- **An action** (every turn the AI plays, `Combatant.Controlled`, from its thinking to its result; and the player's once they've chosen, states 2 to 7): **one shot, planned once and carried along a walk.** The player's own attacks and casts were the original's shots kept clear, until the user found their angles peculiar (an archer framed in front of the rat they'd shot as it died).
  - While the AI thinks (state 8, before it first acts) the shot stays where it is.
  - **The plan.** Once it acts, the shot is planned to hold:
    - where the body stands, and where it walks to (the last corner it's queued);
    - whom it acts on: `EnemyAI.Target` for the AI, `Battle.Target` for the player, and a spell's targets selected (`TargetsB`);
    - whom the original's camera follows.

    Of yaws all the way round in 15° steps and pitches of 12° to 40° (20° liked best), it takes the one seeing them best that turns least from the shot now and from the next player's turn's likely shot (`ShotFinder.Planned`). That next shot is over that party member's shoulder at its nearest enemy, or, with no player's turn left in the round, the way from the party's middle to the enemies'. Turning from each costs 0.4 a radian, and the pair is framed the vantage's way.
  - **Following the walk.** While the body walks, the shot is carried with it by the point the camera follows them by, as the player's turn's is, so it follows them across the field, the user's ask: through the rats battle it moves with all 16 of the rats' walks of a unit or more (`AnAIsWalkIsFollowed`). It was held still, the walker crossing it, until the fifth play.
  - It's kept while each subject is anywhere on screen, and planned again only when one leaves it (the AI walking on, or changing its target), at most every 15 ticks. A plan made again keeps in view where the body stood and was going, so it widens rather than swings. In the rats battle, 45 AI turns take 48 plans.
- **An arrow, a throw or a spell at someone further off** (beyond 6, `ReleaseDistance`), **or a spell at an ally however near** (from its facing to its result, states 6, 2, 3 and 4, `EnhancedCamera.Volley`): **two shots, each cut to**, the user's idea to try, the hit a hard cut. A buff's or a heal's effect on its target is the point of it, so an ally's is shown landing whatever the distance, the user's ask (`ASpellAtAnAllyIsShownLandingOnThemHoweverNear`); one on the caster themselves is the action's one shot. The user found a ranged attack framed whole from an overhead distance unsatisfying.
  - **The loosing.** Low over the shoulder of the one who acts, near them as the turn's shot is, at whom they act on (at half weight), from the vantage's side.
  - **The hit** comes at whichever is first:
    - the arrow, throw or flask has flown half its way (`HitAt`, `Battle.Flight`);
    - 10 ticks have passed since a spell's visual started at its caster's strike (`ReleaseBeat`, `Battle.CastLoosed`);
    - the blow lands.

    The first two wait until the loosing has been shown 45 ticks (`LoosingTicks`): Dragon Flames' visual starts early in Alaron's cast, and cut to 10 ticks after it, the loosing was gone in a fifth of a second, half a second before the flames rose. The hit shows the target close, with a spell's other targets still to come at half weight. It looks along the way the missile came, turned 35° toward the vantage's side (`HitTurn`), so the target is seen three-quarters on and the missile comes in from the side the loosing was shot from.
  - A spell's next target is a volley of its own, as its caster casts again. In the skeleton archers' battle every arrow, 13 of them, is loosed and then cut to in flight.
- **The vantage: one side for every shot** (`Vantage`). This is the user's rule, film's line of action, in place of the second version's side per party member: with those, Keelin's camera swung left and then Alaron's right.
  - **Where it stands.** A point off the middle of the line between the party's middle and the enemies' (the living, on the field), by 0.6 of the line's length and at least 10 (`VantageReach`, `VantageNearest`). It's on the side of that line with more room on the field, where the shots' eyes have room to stand. It may itself stand beyond the field, since it's a side, not an eye: clamped inside the field it stood next to the party on F09's narrow field, nearly on the line, where it says nothing.
  - **What it sets.** Two who deal with each other, a party member and their enemy, are framed the way round it sees them (`Vantage.Side`, `ScreenSide`): every shot holding them costs up to 1.5 (`SideCost`) for framing them short of 0.3 of the half-width the right way round (`SideAcross`, `ShotFinder.Sides`). So every party member is on the same side of their enemies from shot to shot, whoever's turn it is. The shots needn't be taken from the vantage, only from its side. A pair it sees nearly in line, within a quarter of how far apart they are, may be framed either way.
  - **When it moves.** It's placed as both sides are first on the field. It's placed again when any of these happens:
    - a reserve comes onto the field;
    - the line has turned 60° from the one it was placed for (`VantageTurn`);
    - the line's middle or its length has moved by more than half the reach it was placed with.

    Placed again, it keeps its side of the way from the party to the enemies while that side has room for half its reach, so the camera doesn't cross the line as the fight moves about. Through the rats battle it's placed 11 times and never changes side, and 6,972 of 6,999 action ticks frame their pair its way.
- **Aiming** (state 18, a throw's free cursor; state 9 with a ranged weapon and a ranged action): the reticle (the cursor, or the body aimed at) in the middle of the screen, from 55° above, 20 away, at the yaw the view had as the aim began (`ShotFinder.Aimed`). It follows the reticle as it moves, its aim's cap six times as high (`AimingAcceleration`), so it stays near the middle, clear of the conversation box.
- **Aiming a blow** (state 9 otherwise, `EnhancedCamera.BlowAim`): the shot the turn was settling on, or else the one shown, kept while it holds the attacker and the body under the cursor and hides neither, the user's ask. Else, and for each body the cursor moves to that it doesn't hold, over the attacker's shoulder at that body, found once for it. It was the original's close shot, a new angle where the shot already framed both. Through the rats battle, all 7 blows aimed whose shot held both kept it (`AnAimAtABlowKeepsTheShotThatAlreadyHoldsBoth`).
- **A spell aimed at targets** (state 10, `EnhancedCamera.SpellAim`): low, from the caster's way, at every target selected and the cursor's, and some of the spell's reach. The reach is the point of its ring nearest the targets, beyond them from its centre (at half weight, `ReachWeight`), which a low shot sees just above them. The caster needn't be in it: the line in the box says who casts, and the user found a shot high enough to hold them only showed the ring barely. The ring is held only where it draws the shot back no more than 1.4 times (`ReachStretch`). The shot is found again as the cursor or the selection changes, and moved to with aiming's caps. The user's rule: of an ordinary spell (damage, buff, debuff), some of the ring, for its size, and all it will hit; of Fireball, all of the ring, since it hits the party too.
- **An area placed** (state 11, Fireball, Teleportation and the dispels): the reticle in the middle from 80° above, near enough straight down, centred on the area. It's as near as holds the ring drawn round it (its radius and 0.2, as the runtime scales its marker) with the ring's nearest side down to 0.64 of the half-height, the conversation box's top (`AreaFloor`, `ShotFinder.Placed`). So whatever its size the ring fills the screen above the box, the same size on it: the user found two casters' rings of different sizes the same, which they liked, but too small. They had been held as 1.6 times their radius round the centre, inside the frame's margins. The stick's up is the screen's up, since the look across the ground comes from the yaw (`Shot.LookXZ`), not the eye, which from straight above would be no way at all. 80° rather than 90° keeps it readable as ground. The user found this right.
- **The menus and the pause** (states 22, 23, 25, 14 and 27): the shot shown, held where it is while the player chooses. The original's close shot behind the character, which they had, framed the character half off the screen's edge.
- **Anything else**: the original's shot, moved to with the motion below. Every 15 ticks it looks whether the original's shot hides a subject it holds, behind the arena's collision or another body drawn across the sight line (`ShotFinder.Clearing`). If it does, the least turn, raise and draw-back that clears it (of 25° to 180° turns, 0° to 30° raises, 1 or 1.3 times as far) is put on the original's shot and kept, following it, until the original's has been clear three looks in a row.
- **The player's hand on the camera.** Turning the camera with its stick (R with the stick, the right stick, Q and E, mouse look moving) in the player's turn lets go of the director (`CombatCamera.TakeHand`, `EnhancedCamera.Released`). The director takes it back as the player aims (states 9, 10, 11 and 18) or the turn ends (`CombatCamera.Reset`). The original's camera is put where the shot shown is, so the orbit starts from what the player sees (the user's choice), and its leash swoops it in. It's moved to with the motion's caps ten times as high (`HandAcceleration`), so the view follows the hand closely.
- **The battle's first shot** is cut to, not moved to from the original's placing.

**Motion: acceleration capped, not speed** (`CappedMotion`). A shot is an aim point and a yaw, pitch and distance about it (`Shot`), so a swing round a subject keeps its distance and the view's turn is the yaw and pitch alone. Each of the four is moved toward the goal with its acceleration and its deceleration capped, its speed not: the aim 12 units a second squared (half that in the player's turn) and the turn 200° a second squared. Each tick a channel wants `min(√(2a|e|), s|e|)` toward the goal: the fastest from which the cap still stops it there, and near the goal a close at `s` of what's left a second, 2.5 for the aim (`Settle`) and 4 for the turn (`TurnSettle`). Its velocity changes toward that by at most the cap's share of a tick. So a 20-unit move takes about 2.6 seconds, peaking near 15 a second, and a short one is quick; nothing whips into a move or stops dead. The user found the first caps (30 and 240°) wildly fast.
- **The turn lands with the place.** With the turn at 100° and 2.5, as the aim's, the user found the camera moved over to its new spot and then sat while the orbit settled. A new shot 6 along and 40° round now has its turn within 5% after 52 ticks and its aim after 86 (`ANewShotsAngleArrivesWithItsPlace`). Through the rats battle, with the orbit carried and new framings eased onto, the yaw is more than 5° off on 11 of the 3,175 player's-turn ticks whose aim has landed. Before, it was 5% to 14% of them, by the shot's kind, most of it the walk's turn trailing the carried aim.

- **A moving goal is trailed, never overshot.** The director's shots follow none of a goal's own speed (`MostFollowed` 0): a goal moving at `v` is trailed by about `v/2.5`, and when it stops the camera is already at the speed it can stop on it from. Adding the goal's own speed, as the first two versions did, carried the camera past a goal that stopped by `v²/2a` (up to 40° of yaw), and it swung back: the overshoot the user found unpleasant, worst in aiming (`ADirectorsGoalThatMovesAndStopsIsTrailedNeverOvershot`). Only the player's own hand is followed at its speed, up to 6 a second and 90° a second (`FollowSpeed`, `FollowTurn`), so the view keeps up with the orbit. In the player's turn the shot is carried with the character instead (above), which neither trails nor overshoots.
- **Panned into an aim, not cut** (`PanTicks` 20, `PanTicksPerUnit` 0.5, `PanMostTicks` 40). Into an aim (states 9, 10, 11 and 18), a move about one that would have been a cut (the cursor to a far body), and back from one to the turn are a pan: from the shot shown to the goal, eased in and out, over 20 ticks and half a tick for each unit the aim travels, at most 40, its end following the goal as it moves (`EnhancedCamera.Blend`). The aim goes along a line, the turn the short way round and the distance by proportion, so a low shot rises into one from above as it travels. The user found the cut to the shots from above left them not knowing where on the field they looked; cuts between places the player knows, the loosing and the hit, stay. Through the rats battle, 41 pans, no cut while aiming, and each pan ends on its goal (`AnAimIsPannedToNotCutTo`).
- **A long way is cut** (`CutDistance` 30, `CutTurn` 120°, `CutPitch` 35°), but into, about and back from an aim (above). A goal is cut to rather than moved to when any of these holds: its aim is more than 30 from the camera's; it's turned more than 120° from the camera's yaw (where neither looks nearly straight down); it's pitched more than 35° from it. It's never cut while the player walks the character. That's the user's choice, once the first version's swoops (rising over the field and coming down) proved too much. The pitch's cut is for going into and out of an aim's or an area's view from above: moved under the caps, the tilt from Fireball's 80° down to the next turn's shoulder shot spent two seconds looking at the grass between. The swoop is still there for a mod (`TravelZoom`, 0 by default). The player's turn's start and the loosing and the hit cut too (above): through the rats battle with the party attacking each turn, 122 cuts.
- **The distance moves by proportion**, its logarithm capped at 1.5 a second squared and closing at 1.5 a second near its goal (`ZoomSettle`), so a zoom is even at every scale. In units, a zoom in from a wide shot turned round by the next turn's wide shot dived at the ground; by proportion, a time-optimal move turned round still gives up up to half of what's left, and the gentler close bounds that (`AZoomInReversedHalfWayDoesntDiveAtTheGround`).
- **The eye stays 3 from its aim** in the director's shots (`NearestDistance`); the player's own camera keeps the original's distance.
- **The ground.** The eye is kept a unit above the ground under it (a sphere swept down from 30 above), and a shot from inside a hill isn't chosen at all, since a sight line out through the back of a hill's triangles meets nothing.
- **The field.** A director's shot never puts its eye more than a unit beyond the battlefield's grid (`ShotFinder.Field`, `Outside`, `FieldMargin`): the arenas' walls and cliffs round it are scenery without collision, which the sight lines can't see. A planned cast's shot from behind the F09 field's stone wall looked at the wall for two seconds.

**Sight lines** (`SightLines`): a body is seen while its upper half is, the user's rule: the sight lines to its middle and to the top of its sphere must both be clear, and its legs may be hidden behind a rise or another's shoulder. A line is a sphere of 0.1 swept from the eye to the point. It's hidden where it meets the arena's collision facing it, see-through triangles passed over (`SpherePhysics.Blocked`'s `skipSeeThrough`), or where a body is drawn across it, taken as 1.2 times its radius for limbs and wings. Collision faces one way, so a wall seen from behind doesn't hide anything, as it isn't drawn either. Scenery without collision hides nothing to them, which is why the eye is kept on the field. The arenas are ordinary world chunks, and the indoor ones have no ceilings and a black sky.

- **A trap:** the sight lines are the costly part. An AI turn whose subjects no shot within 35 could hold once planned again every tick, with what it kept growing, which made a battle's camera take minutes; a plan is now made again only when more subjects leave it than it already missed, and at most every 15 ticks.

**Every number** is in `CameraTuning`, each set by name from the command line: `--battle-camera enhanced,AimAcceleration=20,ConeAngle=30` (lists joined with `;`, `Pitches=30;60`). F3's overlay has its line in a battle: what it shows, the target, whether the player has it, the shot's yaw, pitch and distance, the aim's speed, the cuts and the vantage. `--battle-plan "Name=step/step;*=step"` plays the party's turns by script (`BattlePlan`: `wait:n`, `stick:x:y:n`, `orbit:x:y:n`, `accept`, `back`, `skip`, `spell:Name`), for movies and tests comparing settings with the same inputs.

**Rules.** It reads the battle and draws no random numbers, and neither does `CombatCamera`. The player's stick read against a different camera is their own input, so a walk may go elsewhere. With the same presses the battle plays the same, tick for tick (`EnhancedCameraBattleTests`, on the rats and the skeleton archers). The same tests check, through the rats battle:
- every tick of a player's turn with a target holds both bodies and is 30° or lower and near the character, and one within 14 is seldom hidden;
- the character is in the shot shown on 98% of their turn's ticks;
- where the aim has landed the yaw is never more than 5° off for more than 45 ticks in a row;
- a turn opens on an enemy where one is in range, and its shot doesn't change while the character stands;
- going round a pack the shot never steps, and with no enemy ahead it trails the character on its leash, its aim leading them;
- the point it follows a character by moves evenly, where their stride doesn't;
- over the shoulder it never turns itself round to the front of a walker (through four bandits);
- an aim is panned to, never cut to, and an aim at a blow keeps a shot that already holds both;
- a spell at an ally is shown landing on them, and an AI's walk is followed;
- it never cuts while the player walks;
- only enemies are targets;
- an AI's turn is planned about once, and no planned shot's eye is beyond the field;
- every action's pair is framed the vantage's way, and the vantage is seldom placed again and keeps its side;
- aiming takes the camera back from the player's hand;
- between cuts the acceleration stays within its caps.

Through the skeleton archers' battle, every arrow is loosed and then cut to its target in flight. The cosmetic stream may differ: the bodies' sounds are heard from the camera shown, so which of a key's sounds plays, and whether one is heard at all, goes with where it looks, as it does when the player orbits under Original.

**What piece C can ask of it.** `ShotFinder` holds no shot of its own:
- `Best(subjects, from, behind, sides, aim, most, yaws)`: given the subjects (`ShotSubject`: a sphere and a weight) and the yaw the view has, the best shot over the shoulder (`behind` the yaw looking from the character at the target) holding them all and hiding none, each `ScreenSide` kept, with its score; aimed at `aim` and no further than `most` where given.
- `Planned(subjects, from, next, sides)`: the one shot for a stretch of play, turned least from the shots either side of it.
- `Sides` and `Across`: the screen's sides. `Vantage.Place` and `Vantage.Side` give the battle's vantage and the side it sets a pair.
- `Aimed`: an aim's shot. `Placed(centre, ring, yaw, pitch)`: an area's, its ring down to the box. `Framed(subjects, yaw, pitch, most, aim)`: the shot from a way.
- `Score` and `Seen`: how well a shot does. `HiddenWeight`: what it can't see.
- `ShotFraming.DistanceToHold` and `Holds`: the lens's arithmetic, its floor above the conversation box among it.

`SightLines.Bodies` is what may stand in the way, which a drawing fills with where its bodies are drawn. `CappedMotion.Step(goal)` moves under the caps toward any goal shot a tick at a time, `Cut` jumps, and `Carry` and `Turn` move and turn the shot with what it follows. `FollowPoint` is a point drawn smoothly after a body, to carry a shot by. So a director that knows its next shot early can start moving early and arrive on time.

## Camera objects (`processVoxelCamera`)

Ported as `CameraMove`. A camera object takes the camera while the player is in its reach ([map-objects](map-objects.md#camera-objects) has how he reaches it). While it moves the camera or its aim, the follow camera does nothing the next tick (`camera_mode` 2, then `RevertMode`), and when it lets go, the follow camera carries on from where the camera is, in its own mode.

- **Its first tick** starts the move from wherever the camera is now, toward its reference point (or, without one, the player's sphere's bottom plus 1.05, or 0.5 in a conversation). A cut that tracks aims straight at the player then. The original does that for every tracking move, which jolts the view when a pan or an ease takes over (below).
- **The moves,** each over its duration (the values' Z) in ticks: a cut (0x1) jumps there at once; a pan (0x2) goes at a steady speed; a sine (0x4) eases in and out, (1 − cos) / 2 over half a turn; 0x8 eases in, 1 − cos over a quarter turn; 0x10 eases out, sin over a quarter turn. Flag 0x4000 moves the aim and 0x8000 the camera; no object does both. Each writes the camera or its aim, not their goals.
- **Tracking** (0x2000) chases 1.05 above the player at 1/24 of the way a tick, at most 0.25. Running, at 0.06 a tick, that leaves the aim about 1.4 behind him, so he runs ahead of the middle of the screen and the camera catches up when he stops. In the original, a camera taking over snaps the aim onto him: a jump of 4° to 8° in one tick at 60 Hz, which jolts the view. The user chose to treat it as a bug, so the port snaps the aim only on a cut, where the view jumps anyway, and the runtime doesn't blend a cut across frames.
- **A finished move stays done** while the player stays in reach. The original marks it to start again, but the timestamp it writes at the end of the same call overwrites the mark. Out of reach for a tick, it starts afresh next time.
- **The timestamp** is taken from the original's frame counter (`GameFrame.Counter`, `gGlobals` + 8, `0x800d8090`), which `AppProc` counts once a frame whatever the game is doing (`0x8001ab0c`), and which camera objects and cut-scenes' cameras both read (`ProcessVoxelCamera`, `0x800160d4`: it starts the move again where the timestamp is below the counter, and writes the counter + 2). It isn't the time played a save keeps (`TerrainStruct.PlayTime`). The port counts it once a tick in every mode, beside the time played (`GameState.Ticker`, `CountTick`), and the move keeps the tick + 1.
- **Flags 0x100 and 0x200,** on the objects that don't move the camera, set how far behind the outdoor camera follows (the values' Y, at least 3) for the next 30 ticks, and its pitch (X), anchoring it at the player. The interiors' camera reads neither.
- Cut-scenes' cameras are the same moves (`AllocScriptCamera`): see [below](#cut-scenes-cameras).
- In a conversation the original counts the cameras holding it, and while any does, R doesn't orbit; the player has no input in a conversation anyway ([the conversation camera](#the-conversation-camera)).

## The conversation camera

From 1.0's machine code. Talking to a wanderer, both bodies are flagged `ACTOR_800`, and the player's camera focus is cleared (`ClearPlayerCamFocus`). Each frame, `ProcessPlayers` (`0x80012854`–`0x80012968`) gathers the flagged bodies and, in trek with no focus, frames them (`CreateFocusFromMultiple`, `0x800a77b4`):

- **The focus** is the midpoint of the two bodies farthest from their centroid: with two, the midpoint between them. It's anchored there (`SetFeild70`) and the camera aims at it as at the player.
- **The way it looks:** with h from the farthest body to the focus, the facing passed is (−|h.z|, |h.x|), normalised, in the original's reversed facing (`0x800a79cc`–`7a08`), so the camera sits that way from the focus: always toward −X and +Z, whoever stands where. That's across the line between the two when it runs from −X−Z to +X+Z, and along it, looking past one at the other, when it runs from −X+Z to +X−Z.
- **The distance** is (|h| + 4) / tan 33.5°, at least 5 (`0x800cdb14`): 33.5° is half of the 75° field of view (`0x800cdb10`) less 8. Two bodies 1.6 apart put it about 7.2 away.
- It's stepped as the follow camera with that distance (`unk5c`) for the frame, then the distance goes back to the default, and `unk80` is 1, a quick swing each frame.
- The port's framing matches the original's, the odd side included, the user's choice: it may be what keeps the camera out of walls somewhere. A better one, placed as cut-scenes' are, is for much later.
- **A camera object holding the camera** keeps it where it is: the conversation doesn't take over, which spares interiors' cameras. The port keeps camera objects and trigger triangles' cameras working through a conversation, and `FollowCamera.Frame` leaves a held camera be, as `Step` does.
- At the end the camera is given back to the player (`GiveCameraToPlayer`) with a quick swing of 5 frames (`unk80` 5), and its pitch back to the default.

## Cut-scenes' cameras

Framed as 1.0's: a cut-scene in the port looks wider than in the emulator only by 1.0's overscan bars, which the port doesn't draw ([ui](ui.md#the-screen)).

`ScriptCameras`, from 1.0's machine code. Each camera op of a script starts a move that holds the camera for its duration (`DialougCreateScriptCamera`, `AllocScriptCamera` at `0x800168f8`): a camera object's move (`CameraMove`) with the op's flags, aimed at a point the op chose as it ran.

- **The ops** (`DialoguePreCallback`, jump table `0x800d1d80`), each over the script's camera time (60 until set), or one tick for a cut:

  | Op | Moves | How | To |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | CutTo | camera | cut | a reference point |
  | CutToActor | camera | cut | the actor's eyes, and hides it |
  | On | aim | cut | a reference point |
  | OnActor | aim | cut | the actor's eyes |
  | AngleTo | aim | steady pan | a reference point |
  | AngleToActor | aim | ease (sine) | the actor's eyes |
  | PanTo | aim | ease (sine) | a reference point |
  | PanToActor | aim | steady pan | the actor's eyes |
  | SineTo | camera | ease (sine) | a reference point |
  | SineToActor | camera | ease (sine) | the actor's eyes |
  | TrackOn | aim | chase | where the speaker stood (no script uses it) |

  Angle-to and pan-to are crossed between the point and actor forms (AngleTo steady, AngleToActor eased, as the table has them).
- **An actor's eyes** are 0.15 in front of it, at its sphere's centre raised by its height × scale less 0.2 (`GetHeightMinPoint2`) and its scaled radius: Alaron's 1.62 above his feet. They're taken as the op runs, not followed. A point-of-view cut hides the actor (`ACTOR_2000`), but not its shadow or what it holds; a cut or sine to a point or an actor, or a spawn, shows every actor again (`0x80014dfc`). The flag is tested only where `Bodies.Render` (`0x80013dcc`) draws a body's own scene, and only while a cut-scene plays (`CutScene.Playing`, `0x800dfe14`, at `0x8001403C`–`0x80014058`); what it holds is drawn after that test (body `+0x730`), and the shadows (`Shadows.Render`, `0x80012358`, from `RenderZones` before the bodies) test only the body's slot, its distance from the aim and its own alpha (`+0x70C`), which a hide leaves alone. So the camera at an actor's eyes sees its shadow beneath it, as the port draws it (`CutSceneActorView`): the point-of-view shots of Pochanargat's (`aidyn:script/0x04FD`) and Phelan's (`0x04FE`) scenes have one, in footage of the original too.
- **A move with no reference point goes to the focus itself**: `processVoxelCamera` takes 0.5 off for the missing player and adds 0.5 back in a dialogue.
- **Several at once.** Up to seven (the eighth crashes), stepped in slot order, the last freed taken first, each writing the camera. A cut is done the step after it starts; an ease ends on its goal. The script's first nodes start their cuts together with any pan after them, which takes its first step in the same tick.
- **Nothing else moves the camera.** While a move holds it, the follow camera's mode is 2 and does nothing; once the last is freed, the camera's focus is cleared, so the follow camera doesn't run at all. The camera stays where the last move left it. Camera objects don't run in a cut-scene.
- **Waiting.** `counter1`, how many moves ran the step before, holds the player's presses and a `WaitForCamera` (`funcs1[1]`), so both carry on a step or two after the last move ends (`ScriptCameras.Busy`).
- **The planes** are 0.1 and 125 in the dialogue state (`RenderZones`, `0x8000d66c`). The field of view never changes. Retail crashes if a step leaves the camera within 0.05 of its aim.
