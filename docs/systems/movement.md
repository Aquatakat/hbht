# Movement and collision

`HBHT.Core.Physics` and `HBHT.Core.Movement`. The user's timings are in [playtests](../playtests.md).

Ported from `Actor::Move`, the player loop in `PlayerData.cpp`, `collide*.cpp` and `physics.cpp`, and checked against 1.0.

- Alaron is a sphere of radius 0.5 with gravity of 0.003 a tick², about 10.8 m/s². Each tick, the sphere steps by its velocity. Then it's pushed out of each triangle it's in, and out of the edges and corners the triangle flags as open. A ledge lower than the radius lifts it: the face below the sphere's centre misses, and the top pushes it up. Higher ledges stop it.
- His animations move him. Each tick adds the root's forward motion, except on the tick a state is entered and the two after, and only while the body is asked for something or entered its state under a request (`ProcessPlayers`, `0x80013318`–`0x80013328`: the body's request `+0x14`, or `+0x1A`, the request it had as its state was entered, `0x800132E8`). So a stop entered as the request goes back to idle plays in place: the Shadow's run stops carry its root about 5 units back to rest in their last two ticks, which never moves it, and Alaron's creep a little forward, which doesn't either (`Body.Animate`). That makes his walk about 1.26 units a second and his run about 3.6, which fit the user's timings of 24 s and 9 s over the same 30-odd units.
- The original also skips that move for a falling body (velocity at or under −0.048, `0x800132f4`) whose `+0x70B` is set, and zeroes its velocity across the ground. `+0x70B` is set as a body takes Alaron's actor, `0x25F9` (`0x800147e8`), so in trek it's the player. The port skips it for the player whenever he falls that fast (`PlayerMotion.Falling`), and never for other bodies, except where he's held falling without dropping ([below](#wedged-against-a-leaning-plain-wall)).
- The core works the root motion out headlessly (`ActorMotion`, from the actor's scene's asset: [actors](../formats/actors.md)), which a body takes as an `IRootMotion`. The runtime's `ActorAnimator` ticks a body's `ActorMotion` and poses the scene to match at once, so the pose is current when the particles step after trek.
- **Pace** (the options menu's Pace, F5, or `--pace`) plays him faster, steps and all. 1 is the original's, and Brisk is 1.3 (`SessionSettings.BriskPace`). His gravity and velocity run at his pace too, the user's choice (Oct 29, 2026): a frame moves him by 2.6 ticks of them at Brisk, the fractions carried from frame to frame (`PlayerMotion.Step`). With only his steps faster, his run's climb up a steep plain face beat the frame's fall back down it from a normal Y of about 0.46 rather than 0.55, and Brisk walked him up faces the original's pace can't, such as Q25's at (96.8, 6.1, 42.1) ([below](#movement-and-collision)). So at Brisk he falls faster too. In a battle it plays the body the player steers in a turn the same ([combat](combat.md#the-port)).
- **Movement** (the options menu's Movement, or `--movement original|smoother`) is how every body moves: the original's, all of this, by default, or **Modern**, the port's, which keeps to the ground and slides along walls ([below](#modern-movement)). The player sees it called Smoother, the user's choice.
- He turns toward the stick at **20° a frame**, which is 10° a tick (the constant is `0x3EB2B7FE`, 20° in radians).
- **Every body turns a fixed step per rendered frame,** not scaled by the frame's `delta`. `ProcessPlayers` (`0x80012784`) runs once a frame and calls `Body.StepTowardGoal` (`0x800159c8`, `MoveTo`) and `Body.ApplyInput` (`0x80014fec`, `Actor::Move`) once each, and `Facing.Turn` (`0x800a13f8`) gets an immediate: 10° where it stands (`0x3E32B7FE` at `0x80015b4c`), 20° moving or 10° sneaking (built at `0x80015290`). So the original's bodies turn slower as the console's frame rate drops: 300–400° a second moving at the 15–20 fps of Gwernia and the forest, against the port's 600 at its 30 fps `Cadence`. The port is 1.5–2 times faster outdoors and matches in 30 fps interiors; the user judged its faster-turning NPCs no worse.
- Most surfaces keep no velocity on contact. The plain collision surfaces 1 and 2 keep all of it and get an extra push down of 0.03 each frame (`0x80013030`, `0x800cdb48`, `Body.Press`). The keep is read from the surface table (`SurfaceTable`, `0x80362768`): 1.0 for surfaces 1 and 2, 0 for the rest.
- **He moves once a frame.** `ProcessPlayers` (`Bodies.Process`) runs once a frame, and in this order: the stick (`Body.ApplyInput`), the frame's ticks of gravity and velocity and then one collision (`SpherePhysics.Step`, `0x800a47cc`, called at `0x80012e84` with the frame's ticks), the ground rule, plain collision's push, the falling rule, and last the frame's root motion, along his facing and level (`0x80013330`), which the next frame's collision clips. Nothing in it or in the collision looks at how steep a triangle is. `PlayerMotion.Step` does the same on the tick that ends each 30 fps frame (`Cadence.FrameTicks`), reading the stick and playing his animation every tick and summing its root motion over the frame. The runtime draws him at the mean of his last two ticks' places (`Player.Sync`), so a frame's step is drawn evenly over its two ticks, half a tick late.
- **Steep plain faces.** The frame is what keeps him off them. Surface 5 and the rest keep no velocity, so running into a slope of them pushes him up and out along its normal, however steep: he climbs them, in 1.0 as in the port. On plain collision his fall over the frame's ticks, about 0.035 to 0.043 a tick after the push, carries him back down the face before the collision, and that balances his run's climb, 0.06 a tick times the face's normal Y, near a normal Y of 0.55. The port collided every tick until Oct 29, 2026, which damped that fall away each tick, and he walked up plain faces of normal Y 0.5 or so: the user ran up Q25's from (91.9, 1.4, 45.7) to (90.6, 8.6, 41.0) and out of the world, where 1.0 won't climb it, and at D14 some approaches carried him over and out (`GameSessionTests.Wedge.cs`).
- **The ground rule.** After the collision, a player on plain collision who ends lower than he began, with ground other than surfaces 1 and 2 within 0.05 below (a sweep of his sphere, `0x80012f40`–`0x80012fa4`, `0x800a4570`, `0x800cdb40`), is put back at the height he began at. His velocity is kept (`PlayerMotion.Step`).
- Falling faster than 0.048 a tick stops his steering.
- **Falling out of the world.** The original's `ConfirmPlayerWithinZone` (`0x8000c078`) stops him when he falls faster than 0.3 a tick or below −50, lifts him to 50 and sets him down from on high at his X and Z (mode 0). Where there's no ground there, that leaves him at 50 to fall again, for ever: the user has seen the original stuck so. The port finds the fall the same way (`PlayerMotion.FellOut`) and trek rescues him (`TrekLoop.Rescue`), as the user chose:
  1. **Set down at his X and Z**, the original's, where it finds ground above −50. A long drop off a cliff lands him at its foot, as in the original.
  2. **The newest breadcrumb he stood on that no rescue has tried** (`Breadcrumbs.Rescue`, [combat](combat.md#in-and-out) has the breadcrumbs), facing as he did there, with the camera's eye where it was, and a line on screen. This comes first instead when no breadcrumb has been kept since the last rescue (`Breadcrumbs.Kept`), since he may have fallen through where it put him. A breadcrumb keeps whether he stood on the ground (`Breadcrumb.Grounded`, the port's), so one kept mid-jump or mid-fall isn't used. A rescue marks every breadcrumb at its place tried, so all 15 go at once after trek is built; one kept since is untried, and building trek clears the marks.
  3. **The original's lift**, once every breadcrumb has been tried: set down from on high, or left at 50 to fall again.

  Fleeing a battle still takes the farthest breadcrumb, grounded or not, as the original.
- Quirks kept from the ROM:
  - The edge test places its closest point with the sweep's parameter instead of the edge's.
  - A bounce off an edge or corner only turns velocity already heading away from it.
  - A point exactly on a triangle's edge counts as inside for only half the triangles. A sphere balanced exactly on some shared edges can drop through.
- Other bodies, wanderers, cut-scenes' actors and combatants, move by the same rules, steered by a goal instead of a stick, and push each other and the player apart: see [wanderers](wanderers.md).
- Left out: the original only collides with the chunk the player is in, and keeps him 0.05 from its edges. The port collides with every loaded chunk. Where they overlap, a neighbour's triangles the original never meets from the player's chunk can differ from his chunk's own: O24 has a plain copy of the Jundar gate's toggles in P24, which open on the riddle, so a toggle's change carries to its copies ([map objects](map-objects.md#triggers)).

## Wedged against a leaning plain wall

The user found Alaron wedged at Overworld A22, feet at (46.7, −26.3, 40.1), unable to move any way. He stood in the crease where a rock face of plain collision (`aidyn:map/0x0ECB` triangle 476, surface 1, leaning back at 76°, normal Y 0.245) meets the grass below it (491). Both are A22's own triangles, and the ROM's grid lists both in the cells there (cell (8, 7) has 476 seventh and 491 thirteenth), so 1.0 collides with the same two. No neighbour's copy is involved.

- **The trap.** Each tick he fell into the face. Its bounce and the per-axis damping (`SpherePhysics.Dampen`) left him falling at 0.036. Because the face comes before the grass in the grid's order, that slower fall then missed the grass. The ground rule put him back at his height, and the push down on plain collision took his fall to 0.0505. That's past −0.048, so he couldn't steer and his animation didn't move him, and the next tick was the same. The fixed point is b = 0.664 (b + 0.003) + 0.015.
- **What 1.0 does there.** The original collides once a frame, after moving the frame's ticks (`ProcessCollisionSphere`, `0x800a47cc`), and pushes 0.03 once a frame. Modelled at 30 and 20 fps (2 and 3 ticks a frame), from 1,200 approaches to the crease, he falls far enough each frame to reach the grass, which keeps no velocity, and he walks on. The port's push of 0.015 every tick, `Cadence.PerTick`'s spreading, wedged him from 155 of the same approaches. So the port pushed 0.03 once a frame, on the tick that ends it, as ADR 0017 allows for per-frame code; and since Oct 29, 2026 it moves and collides him once a frame too ([above](#movement-and-collision)), as the model does.
- **1.0 can wedge too.** A scan walked his sphere into every plain wall of normal Y 0.05–0.7 in every map set, 23,615 walls with three points on each, and tried to leave 8 ways. The model of 1.0's frame wedges at 41 points at 30 fps (16 at 20 fps), among them Overworld B04, B15, D09, D14, I08, I12, P21, Q19 and Q25, RoogCave A01 and B01, the Misc interiors' I09, and Erromon C05, K03 and K07. In those spots a frame's fall isn't enough to reach the ground. The user tried three in 1.0 (Oct 29, 2026). At A22, where the port wedged him, he walks off, as the model says. At D14 the user couldn't wedge him, perhaps because the things on the ground before the tent there keep him from the crease. At Q25 he wedges easily and runs in place, and running away for a few seconds frees him in the end, which the model doesn't: the frame rate's changes, or a body passing, may tip him out.
- **The release, the port's, the user's choice** (Oct 24, 2026), as for falls: the port frees him rather than leave him stuck. Once he has been falling by his velocity at the end of each frame for `PlayerMotion.PinnedTicks` ticks (15, counted a frame of 2 at a time, about a quarter of a second), and no frame has ended more than 0.05 below where the count began (`PlayerMotion.Pinned`), he steers and his animation moves him again. A real fall drops at least 0.048 a tick, so it never counts past a frame, and nothing changes in normal movement. On D14's low rock, the V between the leaning Expansion Pak triangles 605 and 606 and the upright face behind them (627), running in from the south-west holds him about 1.4 up, and the release lets him away, most ways in about a third of a second.
- **What's left.** The same scan with the port's own `PlayerMotion` while it collided every tick, in which leaving means getting more than 1 away within 2 seconds, left him held at 45 points without the release and 38 with it; it hasn't been run since he moves once a frame. Collided once a frame, he walks out of the slot between two rock faces at Overworld C15 (58.4, −28.4, 38.8) without the release, which the per-tick port needed. The rest weren't the falling rule:
  - **Out of play's reach.** At Gwernia Castle E01 (12.1, 0.2, 11.3), behind triangle 44, and Overworld D09 (16.8, 12.7, 29.8), behind the see-through barrier 171, the scan's start put the sphere behind a one-sided wall.
  - **Crevices.** At Overworld B15 (76.5, −26.9, 30.0) and (97.1, −28.6, 10.8), H08 (71.4, −19.6, 55.2), H10 (114.0, −16.8, 90.2) and K18 (11.1, −11.4, 0.0), ChaosIsle C01 (63.2, 20.5, 60.4), the Misc interiors' I01 (36.0, 22.1, 19.5) and I07 (7.0, 0.5, 22.8) and (2.2, 1.7, 13.6), Erromon A03 (17.7, 1.3, 17.8) and the Barrows' A02 (21.5, 0.2, 7.0), his sphere drops into a V between two or more plain walls narrower than he is, with no ground under him, and the walls hold him whichever way he steers. The scan's C15 (−2.9, −28.5, 10.8) and I10 (14.0, −16.8, 90.2) are B15's second point and H10's, seen from across a chunk's border, and the Barrows' B02 is A02. 1.0's model sticks at B15 and I07 too. Neither the falling rule nor the fall rescue frees him there. Play can't reach the two the user tried in the port (Oct 29, 2026): B15's is the corner behind a tree, and H08's an area behind a building, with no way across to either.
- **Not ported.** 1.0's `ProcessPlayers` also puts Alaron back where he last stood touching the ground, at least 1 from the place before, when he falls faster than 0.3 a tick (`0x8001338c`, `0x800cdb68`). It keeps that place at `0x800e6810`, and the one before at `0x800e681c`, the one it uses. The port's fall rescue covers that case instead ([above](#movement-and-collision)).

## The Roog Cave's mushroom

A notorious spot. In the Roog Cave's D01, a mushroom's collision box stands in the neck of the passage, near (10.4, 22.8), and leaves less room beside it than Alaron's sphere needs. Walking or sneaking north to south, he wedges beside it from every start; northward, and running either way, he passes. The collision is the original's. Instead, the importer moves the mushroom and its box 0.71 out of the neck, the user's choice ([hardcoded content](../hardcoded-content.md#corrections-to-the-roms-maps)). It's the player's choice at import, on by default: the editor's import page offers **Fix the mushroom (recommended)** or **Don't fix the mushroom**, and `hbht import --keep-mushroom` leaves it where the ROM has it (`ImportOptions.FixMushroom`, [importer](importer.md#the-players-choices)).

## Modern movement

An enhancement, a setting ([ADR 0018](../adr/0018-the-port-fixes-bugs-and-sets-enhancements.md)): **Movement**, Original or Smoother, as the player sees Modern called (the user's choice, Oct 30, 2026), on the options menu's Enhancements page beside Pace, and `--movement original|smoother` on the runtime's command line (`modern` is taken too) (`Settings.Movement`, carried each tick by `SessionSettings.Movement` to `PlayerMotion.Movement`). Original is the default. It moves every body: the player in trek, wanderers, cut-scenes' actors, and combatants, the party member he steers in a battle among them, the user's choice (Oct 30, 2026), since one movement for some bodies and another for others would be a strange difference. A cut-scene's actors move at the original's pace whatever the Pace setting, as its script is timed by it (`CutScene.Tick`).

- **Why.** Once the port moved Alaron as 1.0 does (above), the user found the original's movement faithfully bad (Oct 29, 2026): spongy on terrain, bouncing off slopes, creeping up things slowly and awkwardly, and sluggish. Its parts are all above: the bounce and damping of every contact, the push out along a slope's normal that is his only way up it, the fall back down plain faces once a frame, a step once a frame drawn half a tick late, and a run whose root stops for three ticks every stride.
- **What the data says.** Nothing in 1.0 looks at how steep a triangle is, but its surfaces say what's ground. Over every map's collision, by the area of the triangles facing up:

  | | normal Y ≥ 0.9 | 0.8–0.9 | 0.7–0.8 | 0.6–0.7 | 0.5–0.6 | 0.3–0.5 | under 0.3 |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Other surfaces | 91.1% | 4.7% | 2.1% | 0.9% | 0.4% | 0.3% | 0.4% |
  | Plain, surfaces 1 and 2 | 0.7% | 0.4% | 0.8% | 1.5% | 2.7% | 11.6% | 82.4% |

  Plain collision is the barriers, and everything else is ground. The steep bits of the rest include ramps meant to be climbed, such as Q25's sand ramp (normal Y 0.48 and 0.79) and the Wizard's School's stairs (JundarInteriors A01, 0.71 to 0.82).
- **The rules** (`PlayerMotion.Modern.cs`, `ModernRules`). The same sphere of radius 0.5, so the same gaps fit, stepped every tick. A surface is **ground** if it faces up by a normal Y of 0.45 (63°) or more (`ModernRules.FloorUp`), except plain collision, which is ground only nearly level, from 0.97 (14°, `PlainLevelUp`); everything else is **wall**. The thresholds came from the places above and the [reachability check](#the-reachability-check): 0.45 takes in the sand ramp, where 0.55 stopped him on its steepest part, and leaves out less than 0.7% of the other surfaces' area, which the original climbs at any angle; at 0.4 he went up F08's grass mound of 0.43 at (11.5, 5.0, 27.4), which 1.0 can't, and with plain ground from 0.9 he walked along the tops of N27's plain faces at (97.4, 12.2, 83.1).
  - **Edges and corners** of a triangle of ground are ground where they're nearly under him, the contact's normal facing up by 0.7 or more, his sphere's middle no more than 0.36 out past them (`LipStand`), or where he moves onto the ground over them within 60° of straight at them, from 0.05 (`LipUp`), so he rolls up over a low lip. Further out he rolls off, unless there's more ground a step below, as going down stairs, where he stands on the edge till he's past it. With every edge ground, he hung over L20's drop at (41.7, −14.8, 38.4) round the end of the invisible wall the original stops at.
  - **One-sided, as the original's.** A triangle whose plane his sphere's middle is behind by more than his radius is left out, as `MovingPlane` leaves it (`0x800a4da4`); behind it by less, its edges and corners still stop him, so the end of a one-sided wall stops him coming round it from behind, as at L20.
  - **Put somewhere**, between one tick and the next, as a set-down, a pop to its goal, a teleport, the fall rescue or another body's push puts him, he first comes out of what he's in as 1.0's sphere does (`SphereCast.PushOut`'s `recover`, whenever his sphere isn't where his last tick left it): a face his sphere's middle is behind by less than his radius, and over, pushes him out to its front, as `MovingPlane` does every frame, and he stands on a floor whose face he was put a little under. Moving on his own, a face he's behind is one he's passing from behind, and is left alone, so the one-sidedness holds. The set-down from on high leaves Alaron's middle 0.07 behind A22's slope at (79.2, −31.0, 86.8) in 0x042C and 0x042F, and I13's actors 0.35 under a floor at (71.2, −21.9, 9.7) in 0x0527, and the pop to H08's point behind a plain wall puts entity 0x0226 0.53 into it in 0x0364: each now comes out where 1.0's does, within 0.01, where Modern dropped Alaron through the world and left the others in the floor and the wall ([the cut-scene comparison](#the-cut-scene-comparison)).
  - **Where chunks overlap,** a plain triangle lying on another's face makes it plain there (`SphereCast.PlainCopy`): P25's own collision has plain copies of the stone faces P24 reaches over their border with, and 1.0, colliding with the chunk he's in alone, slides off them there. Without it he walked up P24's stone of normal Y 0.53 at (40.7, 20.9, 99.9) onto the mountain top at P25 (53.5, 35, 5.5).
  - **On the ground** he has no velocity. His animation's root motion moves him along his heading, its distance across the ground kept and tilted to follow the ground's slope, so a slope or stair neither slows him nor bounces him, and the timings across a map stay the original's. A move up over a lip steeper than 68° is shortened along it.
  - **His body's steps.** How he sets off, stops and how steady his speed is are his body's, not the world's: the party leader's entity gives them (`EntityBody`'s `stopReach`, `stopPace`, `stride` and `setOffTurn`; `GameSession.KeepPlayerBody`, `ModernRules.For`), as it gives a throw's release, since each is tuned to an actor's run. The original has no such fields. The importer gives every entity the user's tuning to Alaron's (Oct 30, 2026), and a mod can tune its own leader's.
  - **Speed** is his animation's root motion's mean over the last 10 ticks (`stride`, 1 for none), about half his run's loop of 19, a gliding feel the user chose. Tick by tick, his footfalls jerk him: the run's root stops for the three ticks each of its states is entered on, and pushes off at more than twice its mean a little later. The mean counts from the first tick the root moves in the run's loop. The start of a run or walk, the first state his actor enters after standing (`Body.MovingStates`), moves him by its own root: its push-off isn't a loop's, and averaged, it left him 0.2 behind his feet as the loop began, which then slid for a stride as he caught up.
  - **Only under a stick.** The early stop and the set-off turn are the player's control, so they're only for a body a stick drives: the player in trek, and the party member he steers in a battle (`Body.Drive`). A body the game steers, a cut-scene's actor, the AI's or a wanderer, plays its whole step and turns as the original's, as its scripts and walks were staged by it (the user's choice, Oct 30, 2026; [the cut-scene comparison](#the-cut-scene-comparison)).
  - **Setting off** from standing, he turns 20° a tick toward the stick (`setOffTurn`, in degrees) until his root first moves him, on the fourth tick, and the original's 10° a tick after: pushing the stick back from standing, he has turned 80° as he moves and faces the stick 12 ticks in, 0.5 from where he stood, where the original takes 16 ticks and 0.79. At 60° a tick he'd face any way before he moves and set off straight.
  - **Stopping.** Let go of the stick, the original plays the rest of his step, up to 19 ticks and 1.2 units of his run, before his stop plays in place, so where he stops depends on where in the step he was let go. The rest of his step plays three times as fast (`stopPace`), carrying him as far a tick as it did, and no more than 0.3 in all (`stopReach`, before his scale), the most of it in a tenth of a second (`Body.Stopping`).
  - **Walls.** Collide and slide (`SphereCast`): the sphere is swept along the move to the first face, edge or corner it touches, kept 0.005 clear, and the rest of the move slides on, four times at most. Against a wall on the ground, the move is turned along the crease where the ground meets the wall, so what goes into the wall is taken away and he stays on the ground. A wall never lifts him, which is how the faithful port climbed Q25 before it collided once a frame. Of contacts at the same moment, where two triangles share an edge, ground wins.
  - **Steps.** A wall that stops him on the ground is tried as a ledge: up 0.5 (`StepHeight`, his radius, as 1.0's sphere is lifted over ledges lower than that), on, and down onto ground at least 0.02 higher than he was and no more than 0.5 above his feet (`StepUp`). Lower lips he rolls over as ground.
  - **Keeping to the ground.** After each move he's set down onto ground up to 0.5 below (`SnapDown`), so he follows slopes and stairs down rather than hopping off them. A wall he'd touch first doesn't stop that unless it holds him clear of the ground, so he stands in the crease where ground meets a wall (`Land`). With no ground below, or a wall such as a steep plain face, he's in the air.
  - **In the air** gravity is 0.003 a tick², and his velocity slides along what he touches: across the ground as along a wall, and his fall along the face, so nothing lifts him and nothing bounces him. He slides down a steep plain face he's on. The original's falling rule holds: falling at 0.048 a tick or faster, his animation doesn't move him. He lands on ground.
  - **Squeezing.** Held between two walls, on the ground or in the air, and making less than a quarter of his move, he's lifted 0.1 at a time to the least height where the move is clear, and moved there, off the ground (`Squeeze`). He's never lifted more than a step, 0.5, above where he last stood on the ground. 1.0's leaning walls lift its sphere through such places themselves. The Roog Cave's neck by the mushroom is narrower than his sphere at the floor and wider higher up, and without this Modern movement couldn't pass it, even with the mushroom moved.
  - **Slipping.** Held so on the ground, before he's lifted, his move is tried again with walls' edges and corners keeping him only 0.75 of his radius away (`ModernRules.WallEdge`, `Slip`), faces and the ground's edges still all of it, and taken if it gets him further on. A push out of what he overlaps leaves him as near walls' edges and corners as that (`PushOut`), so he goes on through. 1.0's sphere slips past walls' edges, through gaps between a wall's end and a post, or two posts, narrower than it ([narrow gaps](#narrow-gaps)).
  - **Steered to a point**, a body measures how near it is from where its move would have taken it had a wall not stopped it (`ModernMotion.Aim`, `Body.Steer`): 1.0 steers a body by where its frame's root motion left it, before the next frame's collision pushes it back out of what it ran into, so a body pressed against a wall is that much nearer its goal ([narrow gaps](#narrow-gaps)).
  - **What's kept.** `FellOut` is found the same way, so trek's fall rescue works. `Grounded` is whether he stood on ground after the tick; `Feet`, `Heading` and `Request` are the same. The trigger triangles he touches are those within 0.02 of his sphere after each tick (`SphereCast.Touched`), and the surface for his footsteps is the ground's. Turning is the original's, 10° a tick, but for setting off. The wedge release (`Pinned`), the ground rule and plain collision's push are the original's, and Modern doesn't need them. A body pushing him moves him as before, and a push into a wall is undone on the next tick (`SphereCast.PushOut`), which leaves him 0.002 clear of what it pushed him out of, so a move along the crease where it meets the ground doesn't touch it at once.
  - **Drawn** where he is each tick, not at the mean of his last two places (`Player.Sync`).
- **Where to feel it**, with the Movement setting on Modern:
  - Overworld Q25's sand ramp, from (88.3, −1.7, 48.0) up to about (98.5, 4.4, 44.3), straight up in three seconds; the plain faces beside it, from (91.9, 1.4, 45.7), stay walls he runs along.
  - The Wizard's School's steeper stairs, JundarInteriors A01 from (17.5, 0, 22.2) west: climbed at his whole pace, on the ground all the way, where the original climbs at half of it and leaves the ground at the top.
  - Q25's crease at (88.3, −1.8, 46.5), and the rock faces at A22 (46.7, −26.3, 40.1) and D14 (55.2, −16.1, 19.1), where the original wedges him or slides him about: he runs along them.
  - The Roog Cave's mushroom, D01 about (10.4, 22.8): he squeezes past it every way, walking and sneaking too.

## Narrow gaps

1.0's sphere gets through gaps narrower than itself, where a swept sphere of its size can't, and gets nearer a goal past a wall than one kept clear of the wall: the [cut-scene comparison](#the-cut-scene-comparison) found Modern giving up walks the original makes at G12, C05 of map set 6 and H08.

- **Faces.** `MovingPlane` (`0x800a4da4`) meets a face only where the sphere's middle, moved along its velocity by the share it takes to reach the plane, is inside the triangle seen along the axis its normal is nearest (`Inside`, `0x800a4ac0`): the middle itself, off the plane, not the point it would touch. So a face holds the sphere a whole radius off only while its middle is over the face, and a face whose normal is off its axis holds it over a slanting prism. At C05 (27.0, 3.7, 17.9) in 0x04EB, the invisible post's face 46, its normal (0.67, 0, 0.74) 42° off Z, let Alaron's sphere, of radius 0.7, 0.26 into it beside the post's corner.
- **Edges.** `SpherePhysics.Edge` (`0x800a5148`) meets an edge only where it passes within the segment from the sphere's middle a radius along its velocity, and then measures to the point on the edge at the segment's share rather than the edge's own. An edge beside the sphere or behind it, or one it stands against, holds nothing, and one ahead holds it erratically. At G12 (64.1, −17.5, 48.5) in 0x03FC entity 0x0237's sphere, of radius 1, went round the end of plain wall 397 0.46 into its end. Corners (`0x800a53b0`) hold it a whole radius off.
- **The flags aren't why.** 1.0 tests only the edges and corners its triangles flag, but each is flagged on the first triangle that has it and no other, so each is tested once, exposed or not ([maps](../formats/maps.md#collision-the-grid-and-placement-1c)). The wall's end at G12, the posts' corners at C05 and the diagonals across their faces are all flagged, and Modern's sweep, which meets every edge and corner, meets nothing 1.0's doesn't.
- **Pressed against a wall.** `ProcessPlayers` steers a body (`Body.StepTowardGoal`, `0x800159c8`) before the frame's collision and moves it by its root motion after, so the steering measures from where the last frame's root motion left it, a step into whatever it ran into. In 0x0365 the runner to H08's point 3 (97.3, 50.2), 0.5 behind a plain wall (625, 626), stands against the wall 1.01 from it, and 0.94 between frames, within the run's 1 of it.
- **Modern** keeps its sweep, which holds his sphere a whole radius from everything, as its feel wants, and gives three things back:
  - **Slipping** ([above](#modern-movement)): held between two walls, walls' edges and corners keep him 0.75 of his radius away (`ModernRules.WallEdge`), and faces all of it, as 1.0's.   - **Coming out of what he's put in** ([above](#modern-movement)).

## The reachability check

`hbht movement reach` (`Reach`, `ReachReport`): where the player can get to on foot under each movement, per map set and pace, and what only one of them reaches, across all the map sets, the Battle arenas included, which trek never plays in but combat's bodies walk across (below). Modern movement is held to it: what only Modern reaches is a way out of bounds until it's judged a fine new path, and what only the original reaches is a lost path. Run it again whenever either movement changes.

- **From where play puts him** (`Reach.Starts`): every teleporter's arrival into the map set (its reference point, or the teleporter's own position), the places it puts him entering by number (`MapSetRecord.Spawns`, the Overworld's portal 0 a new game's start) and its save points, each set down as trek sets him down.
- **The Battle arenas** (`Reach.ArenaStarts`, `ArenaPlacements`) are no one's arrival, so their starts are where combat makes a body: `Battle.Make` puts it a unit up at its spawn's cell, the point's X and Z truncated (`Battle.CellOf`, `BodyStart`), and `Body.Spawn` sets it down from on high. Every combatant of a battle, a party member, an enemy of the first four or a reserve, is made at one of an arena's 40 spawns, `good0a` to `bad4d` (`Battle.Spawn`, `ChooseSpawn`: the set 4 less the animation byte, shifted by an ambush's roll, and 0 for a reserve, falling back to the first free of every set), so those are the starts, taken from the arena's map (chunks sharing a map are one arena, so 15 are explored of the 28, by their first chunk). A spawn that falls on a triangle's edge can miss by rounding, as four of them do (A01 `bad4c`, K01 `bad2c`, G11 `good4a`, M13 `good3a`), and the original's set-down then tries again a little to one side (up to 0.25 each way, drawn from the generator), so the check does too (`Reach.SetDownAt`, from a generator of its own): without that, a body left at its starting height over nothing stood on no ground and was an isolated start. The flee points, `flee0` to `flee7`, are goals and not starts: a fleeing enemy walks to the nearest and leaves within 1.5 of it (`Battle.BeginFleeWalk`), so the check asks that something be walked to within 1.5 of each, and their cells aren't started from (the one on M11's rock below is one nothing reaches; starting from it reached 32 cells of the rock's neighbourhood that nothing else does). The arenas have no bounds of their own: each is the one chunk's collision, with an arena separated from any other by an empty chunk, so a run that leaves the floor falls out of the world. The combatants' field (`Body.Field`) is left out, since it only narrows what the player's stick reaches and an enemy's walk is not held to it, and the sphere is Alaron's, not each combatant's own radius and steps.
- **Walking.** From each place he comes to rest he runs 12 ways for 2 seconds with Alaron's actor, then lets go of the stick until he stands still: 10 ticks on something within 0.05 of one place, as the original's sphere on plain collision bobs by about 0.03. Where he comes to rest, in a cell of 2 a side and 2 high not set off from before, he sets off again, until nothing new is reached. A cell of 1 a side and 1 high is reached where his feet pass over it standing on anything within 0.1 below his sphere, judged the same way under either movement. Setting off only from where he rests means a slope he climbs a little and slides back down isn't a place to start from.
- **The game's own rules.** The real `PlayerMotion`, at the pace given, in the real `CollisionWorld`: each chunk's starts are run in parallel with the 3 × 3 chunks about it loaded by `ChunkStreamer`, a new game's flags gating them, and merged in order, so a check comes out the same every time. `TriangleGrid`'s scratch set is a thread's own so the runs can read one grid at once.
- **What it reports.** Clusters of touching cells only one movement reaches, a cell counting as reached by both where the other reached one beside it, across or up and down. Each has its chunk, its highest cell and how far that is above the nearest the other reached within 3 across, the highest first; with `--all`, every cluster and the runs that took him there. `--against original` checks the original against itself, `--plain-floor` takes plain collision for ground, and `--chunk` keeps to one chunk.

A run is 2 seconds, so a slope the original climbs only by pushing up it for longer, as M22's grass of 0.51 at (92.5, 6.0, 65.1), can show as only Modern's. Every map set at both paces takes 5½ to 10 minutes on 32 threads, the arenas about 20 seconds of it.

- **What it found** (Oct 29, 2026). Each found reach only Modern had was followed and settled by a rule above, or judged:

## Bodies and the player

The original has one body struct (`playerData`) and one loop over them (`ProcessPlayers`); the player is the first. The port has one `Body`, and `PlayerMotion` drives the player's.

- `Body` is what every body shares, with the step's parts in the original's order: the world's collision, the push down on plain collision (`Press`), the animation's root motion (`Animate`) and the move along the heading, kept in its chunk (`Advance`). As `ProcessPlayers` (`0x80012784`) does for every body, it collides and moves once a 30 fps frame, by the frame's ticks of gravity and velocity and of root motion, while its steering, stick and animation step every tick; collided every tick, as the port did till Oct 30, 2026, a body could creep up steep plain faces as Alaron climbed Q25's. The runtime draws a body at the mean of its last two ticks' places, as it draws the player (`BodyView.Sync`). Under Modern movement its step is `ModernMotion`'s, every tick, the player's among them, by its entity's steps (`Body.Spawn`, `ModernRules.For`). `Body.Step` is a body steered toward a goal or a heading (`MoveTo`), as wanderers, cut-scenes' actors and combat's enemies are; `Body.Drive` is trek's stick read on a body that isn't trek's player, as combat's party members are in their turn. A combatant's body is held in its field of influence after it moves (`Body.Field`, [combat](combat.md#a-turn)).
- `PlayerMotion` drives the player's `Body` from a `MoveInput` and the way the camera looks. What only he does is its own: the stick read along the camera's look, sneaking, a conversation's turn and request, keeping to the ground on plain collision, and finding he has fallen out of the world (`FellOut`), which trek rescues him from (`TrekLoop.Falls.cs`). It knows nothing of the trek camera.
- Trek decides what the controls do to its camera before he moves (`TrekLoop.StepPlayer`): R orbits it, otherwise it follows, and the player standing anchors it.
- Turning across the ground is `Facing` at the core's root, which the follow camera shares.

## Setting a body down

The original's `0x80011a64` (`Actor::CheckCollision`, `player.cpp`) takes `(body, y, mode, tries)`. The port's is `Body.SetDown(world, at, radius, random, near)`: a `near` height is mode 1, none mode 0.

- With mode 1, it sweeps the body's sphere at its X and Z from `y` + 1 to `y` − 1. Missing, or with mode 0, it sweeps from a height of 100 to −100, whatever the body's own. It skips see-through triangles (0x200) and takes the first that faces the sweep in its chunk's grid order (`Collision.SweepBlocked`, `0x800a42e8`), resting at the hit plus the normal times the radius, 0.01 higher.
- A sweep from on high that misses moves the body by up to 0.25 each way, (sin a + 1) / 2 · r in X and (cos a + 1) / 2 · r in Z, with a up to 2π and r up to 0.25 drawn from the game's generator, and tries again, until the fifth sweep from on high (counting from 1 after a mode-1 miss). The moves add up, and if every try misses the body stays where they took it, at its own height.
- Its callers: `TeleportPlayer` with mode 1 at the arrival's height less the radius, and again with mode 0 if that misses ([map objects](map-objects.md)); `AllocWanderer` with mode 1 at the party's height ([wanderers](wanderers.md)); and with mode 0 a cut-scene's `CreateActor` ([scripts](scripts.md)), a body giving up its goal (`Body.GiveUp`, `0x80015c28`), `InitGameTrek` and combat's placing (`0x8005c474`, `0x8005fe04`).
- **The first hit, not the nearest.** The port's grid order isn't the original's in general, but for every arrival and spawn the two first hits agree to a few hundredths. From on high the nearest is the topmost surface over the point, so where they differ the first is lower, and some wanderers' spawns rest well below the topmost surface, as the original's order has them.
- An arrival point usually lies exactly on its floor, so the mode-1 sweep starts with the sphere touching it and hits or misses by rounding. `doorfromP` (`aidyn:voxel/0x16BA/2`) hits; `gates\` (`aidyn:voxel/0x1193/3`) and `libraryEnt3` (`aidyn:voxel/0x18DC/0`) miss and are carried by the sweep from on high, whose first hit is the floor at the point. Taking the nearest would put Alaron on whatever is over the point.
- The port's sweep covers every loaded chunk; the original's covers the body's chunk alone. A body giving up its goal draws no tries to the side, having no stream to draw from. The teleport and the cut-scene draw from the world stream, a wanderer's spawn from the wandering one.

## Shadows

`BodyShadow` (`HBHT.Core.Movement`), from `Actor::CalculateShadow`, `renderPlayerShadows` and `PlaneObj_Render`. The runtime draws it: `ShadowDecal` for the player, and `BodyView` for the bodies that aren't his (wanderers and cut-scene actors), which also fades the body itself by the same distance (`BodyShadow.BodyAlpha`).

- Each tick, after he and the camera move, it sweeps a sphere of radius 0.5 from his centre down 3, skipping see-through triangles. It lies where that hits, directly below him, turned to the triangle's normal.
- **Unlike the original, it skips walls.** The original takes the first triangle its collision grid lists that the sweep touches. Against a leaning wall that's often the wall, and the shadow stands up on it. It's rare, and the port's grid order differs from the original's, so the spots differ. The port takes the nearest triangle no steeper than 60° and treats walls as no ground. Where nothing under 60° is in reach, it keeps its last placement.
- It's a square whose corners are twice his radius from its middle, of the shadow texture (`aidyn:texture/0x0003`, `BORG1_PlayerShadow`) in black, with the texture's intensity as alpha, times 80 of 255. It shrinks and fades as he rises. With no ground in reach, it keeps its last height and follows him in X and Z.
- Shadows are drawn within 20 of the camera's aim in trek and 128 in combat. Over the last 2 units, the fade starts from 255 rather than 128, so a shadow darkens before it goes. Other bodies' shadows show only within 10 of the aim, measured from the body's position in its own chunk's coordinates against the aim in the player's chunk's (`BodyShadow.ChunkAim`, `OwnDistance`), and come with the bodies.
- The original draws a flat square without a depth test, so it never sinks into the ground (`Shadows.Render`, `0x80012358`, and `Plane.SetDrawState`, `0x8004c350`, with flags 10). It sends no depth and compares the primitive's depth, which the game never sets (`G_ZS_PRIM`, no `G_SETPRIMDEPTH`), and writes none, blending normally (`G_RM_ZB_XLU_SURF`, `0x504A50`) with noise dither. It comes after the chunks' scenes, the neighbours' objects and the first pass's particles, and before the bodies and the centre chunk's objects ([rendering](rendering.md#the-n64-material)'s order), so it shows through what's drawn before it, and what's drawn after covers it. The port projects the same square as a decal instead, which bodies' visual layer (2) is left out of: it looks the same, but can't sink where the visible ground and the collision differ. The decal's box reaches 0.75 above and below the ground and fades toward its ends, so where the ground bends there's no hard edge. A wall or tree right beside him still takes a little, as the original's square would.
- It blends in on-screen values, as the N64's does (see [rendering](rendering.md#on-screen-values)).
