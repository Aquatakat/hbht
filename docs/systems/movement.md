# Movement and collision

`Aidyn.Core.Physics` and `Aidyn.Core.Movement`. The user's timings are in [playtests](../playtests.md).

Ported from `Actor::Move`, the player loop in `PlayerData.cpp`, `collide*.cpp` and `physics.cpp`, and checked against 1.0.

- Alaron is a sphere of radius 0.5 with gravity of 0.003 a tick², about 10.8 m/s². Each tick, the sphere steps by its velocity. Then it's pushed out of each triangle it's in, and out of the edges and corners the triangle flags as open. A ledge lower than the radius lifts it: the face below the sphere's centre misses, and the top pushes it up. Higher ledges stop it.
- His animations move him. Each tick adds the root's forward motion, except on the tick a state is entered and the two after, and only while the body is asked for something or entered its state under a request (`ProcessPlayers`, `0x80013318`–`0x80013328`: the body's request `+0x14`, or `+0x1A`, the request it had as its state was entered, `0x800132E8`). So a stop entered as the request goes back to idle plays in place: the Shadow's run stops carry its root about 5 units back to rest in their last two ticks, which never moves it, and Alaron's creep a little forward, which doesn't either (`Body.Animate`). The original also skips the move for a falling body (velocity under −0.048) whose `+0x70B` is set, which the port doesn't yet. That makes his walk about 1.26 units a second and his run about 3.6. Those fit the user's timings of 24 s and 9 s over the same 30-odd units. The core works the motion out headlessly (`ActorMotion`, from the actor's scene's asset: [actors](../formats/actors.md)), and a body takes it through `IRootMotion`, which `ActorMotion` is; a cut-scene also reads the state's length and footsteps its frame (`IActorMotion`, `Aidyn.Core.Actors`). The runtime hands the core each body's `ActorAnimator`, which ticks the body's `ActorMotion` and poses the scene to match at once, so the pose is current when the particles step after trek. Tests step a body by `ActorMotion` itself.
- **Pace** (F5, or `--pace`) plays him faster, steps and all. 1 is the original's, and the brisk profile is 1.3.
- He turns toward the stick at **20° a frame**, which is 10° a tick (the constant is `0x3EB2B7FE`, 20° in radians).
- Most surfaces keep no velocity on contact. The plain collision surfaces 1 and 2 keep all of it and get an extra push down each frame.
- Falling faster than 0.048 a tick stops his steering. Past 0.3 a tick, he's put back where he last stood.
- Quirks kept from the ROM:
  - The edge test places its closest point with the sweep's parameter instead of the edge's.
  - A bounce off an edge or corner only turns velocity already heading away from it.
  - A point exactly on a triangle's edge counts as inside for only half the triangles. A sphere balanced exactly on some shared edges can drop through.
- Other bodies, wanderers and cut-scenes' actors for now, move by the same rules, steered by a goal instead of a stick, and push each other and the player apart: see [wanderers](wanderers.md).
- Left out: the original only collides with the chunk the player is in, and keeps him 0.05 from its edges. The port collides with every loaded chunk.

## Bodies and the player

The original has one body struct (`playerData`) and one loop over them (`ProcessPlayers`); the player is the first. The port has one `Body`, and `PlayerMotion` drives the player's.

- `Body` is what every body shares: its sphere (gravity 0.003), heading, request, pace and scale, and the step's parts in the original's order: the world's collision, the push down on plain collision (`Press`), the animation's root motion (`Animate`) and the move along the heading, kept in its chunk (`Advance`). It holds `Actor::Move`'s stick read (`Go`: 0.05 deadzone, running from 0.8, 20° a frame) and `SetFacing`'s turn where it stands (`TurnToward`, 10° a frame), the request numbers and the step distance. `Body.Step` is a body steered toward a goal or a heading (`MoveTo`); combat's enemies will step that way too. A combatant's body is held in its field of influence after it moves (`Body.Field`, `KeepInField`: `Body.SetGoal` and `Body.ClampToGoal`, a sphere, [combat](combat.md#a-turn)), and in its turn the player drives it with `Body.Drive`, trek's stick read (`Body.ApplyInput`) on a body that isn't trek's player.
- `PlayerMotion` drives the player's `Body` from a `MoveInput` (the stick, sneaking, and whether something else has the stick) and the way the camera looks. What only he does is its own: the stick read along the camera's look, sneaking, a conversation's turn and request, keeping to the ground on plain collision, and the fall back to where he last stood. It knows nothing of the trek camera, so combat's `Actor::Move` can drive it.
- Trek decides what the controls do to its camera (`TrekLoop.StepPlayer`): R orbits it (`PlayerInput.Orbit`), otherwise it follows, and the player standing anchors it, before he moves.
- Turning across the ground is `Facing` at the core's root: `Turn` (`some_trig_func_2`), `Arrived` and `Rotate`, which the follow camera shares.
- Unlike the original, a fall faster than 0.3 a tick puts him back where he last stood. The original's `ConfirmPlayerWithinZone` (`0x8000c078`) instead stops him, lifts him to 50 and sets him down at his X and Z (mode 0), when he falls below −50 or faster than 0.3 a tick. Not ported yet.

## Setting a body down

`Body.SetDown`, the original's `0x80011a64` (`Actor::CheckCollision`, `player.cpp`): `(body, y, mode, tries)`.

- With mode 1, it sweeps the body's sphere at its X and Z from `y` + 1 to `y` − 1. Missing, or with mode 0, it sweeps from a height of 100 to −100, whatever the body's own. It skips see-through triangles (0x200) and takes the first that faces the sweep in its chunk's grid order (`Collision.SweepBlocked`, `0x800a42e8`), resting at the hit plus the normal times the radius, 0.01 higher.
- A sweep from on high that misses moves the body by up to 0.25 each way, (sin a + 1) / 2 · r in X and (cos a + 1) / 2 · r in Z, with a up to 2π and r up to 0.25 drawn from the game's generator, and tries again, until the fifth sweep from on high (counting from 1 after a mode-1 miss). The moves add up, and if every try misses the body stays where they took it, at its own height.
- Its callers: `TeleportPlayer` with mode 1 at the arrival's height less the radius, and again with mode 0 if that misses ([map objects](map-objects.md)); `AllocWanderer` with mode 1 at the party's height ([wanderers](wanderers.md)); and with mode 0 a cut-scene's `CreateActor` ([scripts](scripts.md)), a body giving up its goal (`Body.GiveUp`, `0x80015c28`), `InitGameTrek` and combat's placing (`0x8005c474`, `0x8005fe04`).
- The port's grid order isn't the original's in general, but here they agree: emulating the original's own grid from each Borg9's cells, its first hit matches the port's first in all but two of the 31 arrivals and spawns where first and nearest differ, and those two by a few hundredths. From on high the nearest is the topmost surface over the point, so where they differ the first is lower: two wanderers' spawns (in 0x10E1 and 0x1129) rest 15 and 21 below it, as the original's own order has it.
- An arrival point usually lies exactly on its floor, so the mode-1 sweep starts with the sphere touching it and hits or misses by rounding. `doorfromP` (`aidyn:voxel/0x16BA/2`) hits; `gates\` (`aidyn:voxel/0x1193/3`) and `libraryEnt3` (`aidyn:voxel/0x18DC/0`) miss and are carried by the sweep from on high, whose first hit is the floor at the point. Taking the nearest instead, as the port once did, put Alaron 6.3, 3.3 and 1.7 above the floor, on whatever is over the point. The user remembers nothing odd at any of the three in the original.
- The port's sweep covers every loaded chunk; the original's covers the body's chunk alone. A body giving up its goal draws no tries to the side, having no stream to draw from. The teleport and the cut-scene draw from the world stream, a wanderer's spawn from the wandering one.

## Shadows

`BodyShadow` (`Aidyn.Core.Movement`), from `Actor::CalculateShadow`, `renderPlayerShadows` and `PlaneObj_Render`. The runtime draws it: `ShadowDecal` for the player, and `BodyView` for the bodies that aren't his (wanderers and cut-scene actors), which also fades the body itself by the same distance (`BodyShadow.BodyAlpha`).

- Each tick, after he and the camera move, it sweeps a sphere of radius 0.5 from his centre down 3, skipping see-through triangles. It lies where that hits, directly below him, turned to the triangle's normal.
- **Unlike the original, it skips walls.** The original takes the first triangle its collision grid lists that the sweep touches. Against a leaning wall that's often the wall, and the shadow stands up on it. It's rare, and the port's grid order differs from the original's, so the spots differ. The port takes the nearest triangle no steeper than 60° and treats walls as no ground. Where nothing under 60° is in reach, it keeps its last placement.
- It's a square whose corners are twice his radius from its middle, of the shadow texture (`aidyn:texture/0x0003`, `BORG1_PlayerShadow`) in black, with the texture's intensity as alpha, times 80 of 255. It shrinks and fades as he rises. With no ground in reach, it keeps its last height and follows him in X and Z.
- Shadows are drawn within 20 of the camera's aim in trek and 128 in combat. Over the last 2 units, the fade starts from 255 rather than 128, so a shadow darkens before it goes. Other bodies' shadows show only within 10 of the aim, and come with the bodies.
- The original draws a flat square without a depth test, before the bodies and the centre chunk's objects, so it never sinks into the ground (`Shadows.Render`, `0x80012358`, and `Plane.SetDrawState`, `0x8004c350`, with flags 10). It sends no depth and compares the primitive's depth, which the game never sets (`G_ZS_PRIM`, no `G_SETPRIMDEPTH`), and writes none, blending normally (`G_RM_ZB_XLU_SURF`, `0x504A50`) with noise dither. It comes after the sky, every chunk's scene, the neighbour chunks' objects and the particles, and before the bodies in water, the centre chunk's objects and the other bodies. So it shows through the chunks' scenery and the neighbours' objects drawn before it, and those drawn after cover it, as they would anything. The port doesn't do that: the runtime projects the same square as a decal, which bodies' visual layer (2) is left out of. Otherwise it looks the same, but can't sink where the visible ground and the collision differ. The decal's box reaches 0.75 above and below the ground and fades toward its ends, so where the ground bends there's no hard edge. A wall or tree right beside him still takes a little, as the original's square would.
- It blends in on-screen values, as the N64's does (see [rendering](rendering.md#on-screen-values)).
