# Combat: battle mode (2.5, 2.6, M3)

The original's combat, read from 1.0's code and checked in the combat checkpoints and the user's logged battles, and its port. This is the hub: where combat's code is, how a battle starts and ends, the arenas, what it keeps in RAM, its states, a turn, the special battles, and the port's classes, cues, traps and differences. The rest is in:

- [combat-actions](combat-actions.md): an attack, spells, potions and skills in a turn, what spells look like, and what the bodies hold.
- [combat-ai](combat-ai.md): the enemy AI, its morale and fleeing.
- [combat-parity](combat-parity.md): roadmap 2.9, the replay of the user's logged battles against the port, what it has checked and what differs.
- [magic](magic.md): casting's rules, and where spells', potions' and flasks' effects land.

Addresses are 1.0's. Names are in the symbol map, prefixed `Combat.`, `Combatant.`, `TurnOrder.`, `ArenaGrid.`, `TargetSet.`, `EnemyAI.`, `AiScores.`, `CombatGui.`, `CombatMenu.`, `CombatMarkers.`, `CombatSpellFx.`, `CombatCamera.`, `CombatRadar.` and so on, all coined but `ProcessCombatCamera`. Fields marked **checked** were read in the combat checkpoints ([rig](../reference-rig.md#checkpoint-library)), one battle against ten rats; the rest are read from the code.

Combat is game mode 2 (`Game.Mode`, `0x800D80C8`). `ScreenFade.Step` (`0x8001F330`) calls `Combat.Frame` (`0x80022BA4`) each frame, and the mode it returns is the next: 2 to stay, 1 trek, 3 the pause menu, 6 the post-battle windows, `0xC` a cut-scene, `0xE` a cinematic, 0 leaves the game.

## In and out

**The setup block** (`0x800D9456`–`0x800D9485`, **checked**: empty in checkpoint 01, filled in 02) is what every source fills, and `BattleSetup` is the port's:

| Address | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| `0x800D9456` | 1 when filled; 0 makes the debug build's random test battle |
| `0x800D9458` | 12 entity IDs, 0 ending them (ten rats, `0x026E`): the first four start on the field, the rest are reserves |
| `0x800D9470` | bonus loot: an item, or a chest if its type is 8 |
| `0x800D9472` | the encounter flag (1321 for the rats; the Goblin Ambush's is 978) |
| `0x800D9474` | 1 if winning remembers the monster party's map object; then its index (`0x800D9476`), map set (`0x800D9478`), row and column (`0x800D947A`, `0x800D947C`) |
| `0x800D947E` | the collision byte, 0–2: how the party's placed. 2 is an ambush |
| `0x800D947F` | the arena, 0–27 |
| `0x800D9480` | the animation byte, 0–3: the enemies' spawn set is 4 less |
| `0x800D9481` | can flee |
| `0x800D9482`, `0x800D9484` | the monster party object's flag slot A (`+0x1C`) and its flags (`+0x14`), which `Combat.StateVictory` evaluates (`MapObjects.GateHolds`, `0x800116AC`) before it remembers the party |

- **Filled by** `BattleSetup.FromMonsterParty` (`0x800094B4`, the port's `BattleSetup.WithParty`: `Wanderer.Tick`, `IMapObjectHost.Talk` and `BattleSetup.Against`, below), `CampEncounters.Load` (`0x800096DC`: collision byte 2, animation 0, no fleeing, no loot or flag) and the script ops. `Wanderer.Tick` writes collision byte 2, and the animation byte by what the player was doing (3 running, 2 walking, 1 sneaking, else 0) on each frame its notice holds, so it's what he was doing when last noticed ([wanderers](wanderers.md)); `Talk` writes animation 0 and collision 0 with the player within 60° of the party's body's heading, else 1 (`0x80010AA8`–`0x80010B74`, [wanderers](wanderers.md#talking); the port's `TrekLoop.TalkByte`).
- **The enemy list** (`BattleSetup.FillEnemies`, `0x80009374`, given the monster party object's `+0x28` onward: eight kinds of a u16 entity, a u8 least and a u8 most, then the party's least and most as u16s, the object's `+0x50` and `+0x52`) is three passes over a party's eight kinds: each kind's least, in order, stopping at the party's most or 12 (`BattleSetup.SureKinds`, `0x80008EC8`); then, unless that reached the most, a `Range(0, most − least)` more of each kind whose most is above its least, in order, stopping the same way, and none at all if it comes to under the party's least (`BattleSetup.ChooseKinds`, `0x800090A4`); then, where the party's average level is below the enemies', each kind loses 0.3, 0.2 or 0.1 of its count by the party's level × 3 over the enemies' (`BattleSetup.TrimByLevel`, `0x800088E4`, the shares at `0x800CD470`), removed from the front, an entry shifted into a removed one's place being passed over. A list come to nothing is one Skeleton (`EntityList[0xBB]`, `0x0263`). So the field is the sure ones first, not a shuffle. The party's level is `Party.AverageLevel` (`0x80075134`): the members' modified Levels summed in a byte, over the count at the party's `+0x24`. Sure ones that reach the most come to the most, as `ChooseKinds` returns it rather than the count, so a most of 0 fights the Skeleton. Twelve sure ones under a most past 12 go on to the extras, and one written makes a thirteenth entry over the bonus loot (`0x800D9470`). The port stops at 12 and draws nothing there, and no party in the game has so many ([combat-parity](combat-parity.md#the-formula-vectors)). `BattleSetup.FromMonsterParty` calls `ChooseArena` before `FillEnemies`, so the arena's draws come first; `CampEncounters.Load` does too, with its two kinds laid out the same way on the stack. `Combat.ForbidFleeForBosses` (`0x8005CCCC`) clears the flee byte for the 12 entities at `0x800D2688` (up to `0xFF`) and sets it for the Shadow, whose place it compares with as an immediate (`addiu v0, zero, 0xAC` at `0x8005CCE8`), and `Combat.ApplyFleeRuleByFlag` (`0x8005D054`) has the last word by flag ([hardcoded-content](../hardcoded-content.md)). The pause menu offers Flee only while the byte is set, so not against a boss.
- **The arena** (`BattleSetup.ChooseArena`, `0x80008698`, for every battle but a cut-scene's, below) is by the terrain of the chunk the player's in (`World.GetTerrain`, the world's `+0x24`), through a jump table at `0x800CD400`. A range is a roll between them, inclusive (`Rng.Range`):

  | Terrain | Arena | Terrain | Arena |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 0, 7 | 6–7 | 11 | 23 |
  | 1, 24 | 2–3 | 12 | 22 |
  | 2 | 0–1 | 13, 25 | 27 |
  | 3, 27 | 14–15 | 14 | 24 |
  | 4 | 19 | 15 | 25 |
  | 5, 17 | 4–5 | 16 | 26 |
  | 6 | 18 | 18 | 20 |
  | 8 | 12–13 | 19, 23, 26 | 8–11 |
  | 9 | 17 | 20 | one in two 6–7, else 8–11 |
  | 10 | 16 | 21 | one in two 2–3, else 8–11 |
  | | | 22 | one in two 0–1, else 8–11 |

  A terrain of 28 or more writes nothing, so the last battle's arena stays. Nothing but the debug build's random battle reaches arena 21. Terrains 20–22 draw `RollD(1, 2)` first, 1 for the first range. The importer carries the table as a fact on each terrain record (`TerrainRecord.Arenas`, `HBHT.Rom.Engine.Arenas`).
- **A script's battle** (`BattleSetup.Scripted`, `0x80050804`, as its battle op ends it) fills the block from the script: its `AddEnemy`s, the collision byte (`+0x112` of the script's state), the arena (`+0x114`, above 27 made 0 at `0x80050888`; 0 when the script sets none) and the animation byte (`+0x113`), the block marked filled, and fleeing unless the script is a cut-scene (mode byte `+0xAA` ≠ 1). That's every cut-scene's battle, each of which sets its arena. **A conversation with a monster party** fills it again as the conversation ends: `Conversation.End` calls `Transition.AfterScript` (`0x80051AD4`), which by the ending kind (`+0x90`) sets `TrekLoop.State` to 5 or 7 for a shop or training, or for a battle (2) calls `BattleSetup.Against` (`0x80051A4C`). With a party talking (the script's `+0x78`, its body's map object), that sets `TrekLoop.State` 2, the collision and animation bytes 0, and calls `BattleSetup.FromMonsterParty` on the party's object with its flag C (`+0x20`): its enemies rolled, fleeing allowed, the party remembered on a win, and **the arena by the terrain** (`ChooseArena`), over what the script wrote. No such conversation sets an arena. So Kitarak's battle, in his room in the goblin camp (map set 1, C01, terrain 16), is in arena 26 (M19, the dark wooden floor), not arena 0's forest. The port's `Transition.AfterScript` applies `BattleSetup.Against` for a conversation's talker (`GameSessionTests`). With no party talking, `Against` does nothing and no battle starts, where the port starts the script's; no conversation in the game but a party's ends in a battle, so only a mod reaches it.
- **The switch.** `TrekLoop.QueueState(2)` (`0x8001FF04`) fades out; trek's state 2 marks trek to be built again on return (`0x800DB067`) and returns mode 2.
- **`Combat.Init`** (`0x80022D1C`, on the first frame, while `0x800DB0A4` is 1): takes down trek's HUD, allocates the combat struct (`Combat.Reset`) and loads the combat strings, sets map set 16 (`0x800D8414`) and the arena's chunk (`0x800D8416`), and starts the zone engine in mode 1 (`InitZoneEngine(1, 0)`, `Zones.Mode`): trek's chunk streamer, collision, lights, sky and weather, over the Battle arenas, with the zone engine's point at (20, 20) in the arena chunk, the shadows' distance 128, and the battle's song chosen in `Zones.SetMapSet` (one `Below(5)` of the map set's five, or `Below(4)` of four without the Expansion Pak). `Combat.Setup` (`0x8005D15C`) fills the struct: the counts, the arena grid, a combatant and a body for each living party member and enemy on the field, Sholeh moved to the party's side if she's in the first four, the leader, whether there are flee points, the turn order and the flee rules by flag. Then the GUI; a body for each combatant (`Combat.CreateBody`, `0x8005C474`, in slot order from `0x80022ED8`), set down from on high (`Body.SetDown(body, 0, 0, 0)`), so a body that finds no floor tries to the side with two draws each time, **after** the turn order's draws, as the port's are too; the first turn's line; the camera on the current combatant's body (`Camera.SetEye`, `GiveCameraToPlayer`, `CombatCamera.Reset`); the first turn (`Combat.StartFirstTurn`, `0x80022C9C`: its line, the radius, the ring and the field, but not the clock); the effects; and state 31 with the fade in, a sixtieth a tick (`0x800CE308`). The Goblin Ambush's flag (978) makes it non-lethal, sets the hold's count to 2 and heals the first member ([The special battles](#the-special-battles)). Its `0x800E6850` and `0x800E6860` are `Combat.StateMoving`'s walk positions, not cameras.
- **`Combat.Exit`** (`0x80023060`, after every frame, acting the frame a state asks, `0x800DB0A8`): marks the setup block used and clears `RememberParty` (`0x800D9474`); after the Goblin Ambush (978), however it ends, adds the party's first member's **base** Strength to its current and modified (`Stats.AddCurrent`: the ambush's second hit took it to 0, so a battle ended before that doubles it), sets flag 47, rewrites trek's kept place (`Zones.SavedPosition`) to Oriana's hut (map set 6, E03, (5.9, 0.5, 3.0), the eye at the position, facing (1, 0) in the kept field `0x800D9078`, which `InitGameTrek` copies back to the body's own reversed one, `+0x20`, so he faces (−1, 0), toward Oriana), as the map set's named place 0 (`Zones.SavedPlace`, `0x800d9092`), so trek's first frame walks into her first cut-scene and its end chains into the next ([scripts](scripts.md#the-world-it-plays-in)) and heals Alaron in full; takes every effect off every combatant (`Entity.ClearEffects`, `0x80071830`: the potions' but Stealth's, kind 0x10, and the 15 timed spells'; hit points stay); frees the GUI, the effects, the bodies, the struct and the zone engine; and last takes the dead out of the party (`Party.AfterBattle`, `0x80074EE8`, [Deaths](#deaths)). The bonus loot (`0x800D9470`) is never cleared, so a script's battle, which doesn't write it, pays out the last monster party's again. Where it goes is the mode the state returned:
  - **A win** (state 12, `Combat.StateVictory`, `0x80022450`), once faded out: `Combat.Exit` asked; for a monster party's battle alone (`RememberParty`, which only `BattleSetup.FromMonsterParty` sets), the encounter flag set, and if it still reads 0 (flag 0, a party with none, or a derived flag) while the party's own gate, its flag A (`MapObject.Enabled`), holds (`MapObjects.GateHolds`, `0x800116AC`, over `0x800D9482` and `0x800D9484`, which `BattleSetup.FromMonsterParty` copies from the object's `+0x1C` and `+0x14`), the party remembered (`MapObjectMemory.Remember`, kind 5, three map loads: [saves](saves.md#map-objects-memory)); `Combat.SetKillFlags` again; the snapshot, black; `PauseMenu.PrepareLoot` (`0x80048FF8`), which hands the loot window the loot and the gold and **adds the gold to the party at once**; the experience pool to `PauseMenu.ExperiencePool` (`0x800D94D0`); and mode 6, the post-battle windows: pause states 5 (experience and training) and 6 (loot) ([ui](pause-menu.md#after-a-battle)). The loot window closes to trek (`PauseMenu.CloseToTrek`, `0x80048F40`), mode 1, built again at the kept place exactly and fading in at 1/30, as after fleeing. State 16 never runs for a win: the `Combat.NextState` 16 that `StateVictory` writes is never read.
  - **A loss** (state 13, `Combat.StateDefeat`, `0x80022590`), once faded out: `Combat.StartGameOverScript` (`0x8001F860`) heals the party's first member in full, finds the first dialogue object in the Dreamscape's map (Borg9 `0xD5F`) whose script is `CodeTable.GameOverScenes`' first, `0x0512` (the other two are never read), starts it as using it would, and sets state 21 (`Combat.StateLeaveToScript`, `0x800227D4`), which returns mode `0xC`, the cut-scene. The script's ending kind 2 (`CodeTable.ScriptEndings`, `0x800DFE1C`, kept in `CutScene.EndingKind` `0x800DFE4C`) makes the cut-scene return mode 0, the title (`Title.Frame`, `0x8001B154`): nothing is saved or reloaded. A non-lethal battle (`Combat.NonLethal`, `0x800DA0DC`, which `Combat.Init` sets for encounter flag 978 alone) instead sets the encounter flag, remembers the monster party fought with no gate check, sets the clock to 6:00:00 the next day whatever the hour (`World.SetDate`, `0x8007B914`, the day byte increased), and returns mode 1. The port's game over goes back to the title, its intro cards first, as the original's restart does ([ui](ui.md#coming-back-to-it)).
  - **Fleeing**: the pause menu's Flee, offered only in state 14 and while `0x800D9481` is set, with no confirmation, roll or line in the log (the combat strings' "The Party flees" is never read): it sets `Combat.NextState` to 20 (`pauseMenuSelectCallBack`, `0x800403AC`), and once the menu has rolled up, `Combat.StateLeaveFled` sets `Zones.Fled` (`0x800D9376`) and returns mode 1. `Zones.Fled` is read only by `InitZoneEngine` and cleared by `Zones.ResetBreadcrumbs`: fleeing costs nothing else, but the party isn't remembered and `Combat.Exit` takes the dead out as after any battle.
- **Back in trek.** As trek faded out, `TrekLoop.Free` kept where the player stood, his heading, the camera's eye and the chunk (`Zones.SavedPosition`, `0x800D906C`, 0x2C bytes). Trek is built again there (`InitGameTrek`, `0x80020F40`), the body put at the kept place exactly, not set down, its heading the kept one (both the body's `+0x20` and `+0x28`), and the camera's eye at the kept eye, aimed at the body (`Camera.SetEye`, `Camera.SetTarget`). Having fled, it's the **breadcrumb** farthest from there instead, which `InitZoneEngine` (`0x8000DF84`) copies over the kept place while `Zones.Fled` is set, so the heading and the camera's eye are the breadcrumb's too: each is the same 0x2C record (`SaveZoneEngine`, `0x8000E3D4`: the position, the heading at +0xC, the eye at +0x14, the chunk and the map set), and the checkpoints show the kept eye 5 to 6.5 behind the body along the heading. He faces as he did when the breadcrumb was kept, seen from behind as he was then, not from where the battle began. The port keeps the eye with each breadcrumb and puts the camera there (`Breadcrumb.Eye`, `TrekLoop.Resume`); every other return to trek, a win's among them, finds the camera where trek left it, which is the kept eye. The Goblin Ambush's end is the exception: `Combat.Exit` puts the eye at Oriana's hut with the body, and `TrekLoop.Keep` doesn't move the camera, which is left so because her cut-scene starts on trek's first frame. The breadcrumbs: trek's zone engine keeps where the player stands every 120 ticks, unless the nearest kept in the map set is under 10 away (the places within their chunks compared, as though in one), in a ring of 15 (`Zones.Breadcrumbs`, `0x800D90E0`; `HandleZoneEngineFrame`, `0x8000E784`), and `Zones.FindBreadcrumb` (`0x80009A48`) takes the farthest in the same map set, a chunk counting 100 a row or column, the last of equals. Every time trek is built, all 15 are where the player stands (`Zones.ResetBreadcrumbs`, `0x8000DF00`), so they aren't saved. Checked in checkpoints 01 and 02. The monster party that started the battle is remembered only on a win; after fleeing it's back at its home node with every wanderer, and can hunt again.
  - **The Shadow merging**: state 17, whose first frame is `Combat.MergeShadow` (`0x800675C0`) and mode `0xE`, the Shadow's cinematic (record 1, `Cinematic.Load(1, 4, 1)`, [cinematics](cinematics.md)), played once combat is freed; then trek where the battle began. **Vanishing**: states 29 and 30, then trek where the battle began. Neither gives rewards or sets an encounter or kill flag ([The special battles](#the-special-battles)).

## The arenas

The Battle arenas are `aidyn:mapset/16` ("Battlefields", 150 to a cell): 28 chunks, the arena number being the chunk's place in the map set's list (`Zones.ArenaChunks`, `0x800DCA74`, which the importer reads), but only 15 maps. Chunks sharing a map are the same arena, terrain, scenes and music alike. `Zones.SetMapSet` puts the zone engine's point at (20, 20) in it.

- **What they place:** reference points and up to 12 scenes, all always there (flag 0 inverted), some only with the Expansion Pak. No lights, sounds or other objects. The 46 × 46 arenas are a flat disc of 24 triangles; the scenery reaches far past the collision, to ±300 in I11.
- **Arenas 24 and 27's floor streaks past its rim** (M15 and M21, scene `0x3671`, one model, Borg2 13936, under terrains 14 and 13, whose skies aren't drawn). It's one wrapping material (flags `0x305C`, both wrap bits) with a 32 × 32 texture (Borg1 4894): an octagon of radius 23 lit by its vertex colours (white at the middle, 51 at the rim), and a skirt from the rim down to four corners about 250 out, black at the corners. The disc's texture coordinates are a plane in X and Z, about 7 units a tile in S and 4 in T, each triangle shifted by whole tiles. The skirt's corners would need 35 to 60 tiles, past the ±16 a vertex's 16 bits hold at the lists' 0.5 texture scale, and the data holds them wrapped by whole 32-tile turns, so its triangles squeeze and shear the texture into long streaks fading into the dark (`GeometryTests`). It's the ROM's own vertices, and 1.0 wraps the tile as the port does (`Scene.SetTextureTiles`, [rendering](rendering.md#wrapping)), so the original streaks the same.
- **The points, by name** (`FindReferencePointName`, `0x8000B7C8`, with the combat strings' `%s%u%c`, `good`, `bad` and `flee%d`): every arena has the 40 spawns `good0a`–`good4d` and `bad0a`–`bad4d`, and `flee0` up to `flee7`. Arenas 17 and 20 have no flee points, so enemies there can't flee (`0x8005E76C` sets `+0x5440`). Arenas 12 and 13's one, `flee0` at (48.7, 11.7), is past the 46-wide grid, so every flight there leaves at once ([combat-ai](combat-ai.md#fleeing)).
- **Reachable on foot**: under the original's movement and Modern alike, each of an arena's 40 spawns reaches every other, and nothing is reached by one and not the other; every flee point is walked to within 1.5 but M11's `flee3`, on a rock 2 above the floor ([the reachability check](movement.md#the-reachability-check)).
- **Which spawn** (`Combat.FindSpawnPoint`, `0x8005C210`; `Combat.SpawnPosition`, `0x8005C294`): `%s%u%c`, the side's word, a set number and a letter. The party takes `good` and the enemies `bad`, swapped in an ambush (collision byte 2). Both sides start in set 4 − the animation byte, and reserves in set 0; in an ambush the party's set moves by the Ranger's roll (`Party.BattleAmbushRoll`, `0x80076C60`): the member, dead or not, with the strictly best modified Ranger who has the Stamina for it (5 less the Ranger, at least 1) rolls a d100 against Intelligence × 3 + Ranger × 10; failing, the set is 4 (3 with animation 3); under it, the margin's step (`Party.SkillCheckStep`) and the Stamina spent: step 1 moves it a set up (not with animation 0), 2 and 3 leave it, 4 or more a set down (not with animation 3); with no one to roll, no draw and no move. The party's letters are the living members' count so far, Sholeh's always `d`, and an enemy's its place in the list. A missing point stops the original (`FindReferencePointName` asserts). The spawn's x and z, truncated, are the combatant's cell (**checked**: Alaron's `bad2a` at (52.9, 52.3) is cell (52, 52)), and the body is made a unit up there and set down (mode 0). Set down on the floor, a body can move by float noise (32 to 31.999998), and its cell is read back from it.
- **Which way they face**: one draw each, as they're made: `Range(1, 3)` for the party, and for the enemies `Range(5, 7)` with collision byte 0, else `Range(1, 3)` too; the direction's vector is `Combat.DirectionVectors` (`0x800D27A8`, north (0, −1) round to north-west), and the body's heading is that way (the original's body field is its negation).
- **The ring's teardrops** are drawn by code, not placed (`CombatMarkers`, `combatmarkers.cpp`): a pool of 20 copies of `aidyn:scene/0x0794` (`CombatMarkers.Init`, `0x800854A8`, at `0x800E3138`, 0x1C each), a blended billboard about 0.41 × 1.0 with a scrolling texture (Borg1 1937); the code sets no scale, turn or scroll, and draws each with the zone's colours and fog (`Zones.SetSceneColors`). `CombatMarkers.Update` (`0x80085548`) works the radius out first, then lays the ring round where the combatant **began its turn** (`+0x08`), not where its body is: its radius R is the field's plus the body's scaled radius (`+0x34`), plus that again for one with an AI record; 6 teardrops up to R 3, 16 up to 7, else 20 (`CombatMarkers.CountFor`, `0x8008580C`, the floats at `0x800D43C8` and `0x800D43CC`); the first due +Z and the rest round toward −X by `(360 / N) × 0.017453`, the way round kept in two statics never reset (`0x800E3130`, `0x800E3134`, from (0, 1)), so each ring starts 0.006° further round than the last. Each is set on the ground as a body is (`CombatMarkers.RingPoint`, `0x80085240`): a sphere of 0.5 swept from 100 to −100 past see-through triangles (`Collision.SweepBlocked`) rests 0.01 above the first facing it. A miss tries again by calling itself with the try's number, up to 4 (`sltiu $s5, 4` at `0x80085380`): two draws on the one stream, an angle below 2π and a reach below 0.25, put the sweep at the ring's point plus (sin + 1) / 2 and (cos + 1) / 2 of the reach, so the retries don't add up and keep the first sweep's heights. One that misses all five sweeps keeps its point, and so takes eight draws; it then takes the body's height, and half way between its neighbours' once the ring is done. The ring is laid out only as a turn begins (and as the field follows a combatant near the Shadow), and drawn every frame (`CombatMarkers.Draw`, `0x80085848`, from `Combat.DrawFrame`) but from `Combatant.ExecuteAction` to the turn's end (`Combat.ActionActive`, `0x800DB0CC`) and from `Combat.StateVictory`; there's no fade in. **Checked** in 02–04, 09 and 10, every teardrop, and draw for draw in the parity replay's first rings ([combat-parity](combat-parity.md)).
- **The grid** (`ArenaGrid`, `0x800674A0`–`0x80068F7C`) is 100 × 100 cells of a byte a unit across in the arena chunk's own coordinates, index x × 100 + z, the height × 2 in the low bits and bit 7 for taken. `ArenaGrid.Init` (`0x80067960`) sizes it by the map's object grid (cell size × columns and rows, rounded half away from zero). `ArenaGrid.Fill` (`0x8005E900`) sweeps a sphere of 0.5 from 100 down to −100 through each cell's centre over the arena chunk's collision, see-through triangles included, the first facing it in the grid's order: the byte is twice the height it touches, truncated, and a miss is taken (0x80). Each combatant takes its footprint (`Occupy`, `0x8006820C`: from its cell on in +x and +z, none if any cell is outside), twice its scaled radius rounded half away from zero, at least 1 (the party 1, rats 2). **Checked** against checkpoint 02's. `WalkClear` (`0x80067E6C`) is a line over free cells with steps under 2; `HasSight` (`0x800684C4`) is the line in 3D for ranged attacks and spells, through the collision and the bodies between; `FindPath` (`0x80068CD4`) is a greedy walk of at most the grid's diagonal, which `StraightenPath` trims.

## A turn

1. **It ends** (`Combatant.EndTurn`, `0x80061300`): `CombatCamera.Reset` (`0x8008D210`, a glide stopped), the AI's target forgotten (`EnemyAI.ClearTarget`, `0x8005B998`), the cast chosen cancelled (the entity's `+0x3C` to `0xFF`; the selected spell stays), the acted flag cleared, a scroll or wand whose selected spell has no charges left taken off and gone (the entity's `+0x3D` of 5 with an item of type `0x11` or `0x0D`, `Entity.UnequipGear` `0x8006F5C4`) and no spell selected, state 1 and the ring shown again (`Combat.ActionActive` 0), the body asked to stand, the action put back (`Combatant.RevertAction`, `0x8006679C`) and with a flask in hand the weapon stowed (`Party.StowHeldItem`, `0x800763B0`), the last target forgotten if it's dead, then `TurnOrder.Advance`. Every end of a turn comes here: the skip, the action's result (state 4, 60 ticks on), the AI's rest, a weapon or flask taken in hand in the pause menu (19), Sense Aura's popup dismissed, and the AI's walk or turn with nothing to do (5, 6).
2. **The next** (`TurnOrder.Advance`, `0x8006AC4C`): nothing once the battle's won or lost (states 12, 13); the party's first member dead (`*GameState.Party`, not `Combat.AlaronSlot`) loses it (`Combat.Defeat`, `0x80023F98`: state 13 and the fade, or 28 in a non-lethal battle); the last combatant's body let go of its field (`TurnOrder.StopBody`, `0x8006A5C4`); then on through the round, its end building the next (step 7). Before each candidate the battle's end is checked (`TurnOrder.CheckEnd`, `0x8006A97C`, over `TurnOrder.CountActive`, `0x8006A880`, a reserve still to come counting as an enemy), and the candidate's radius, place and whether it can act are worked out (`Combatant.UpdateActive`, `0x8006615C`: not with no Intelligence, Willpower, Dexterity or Strength, nor immobilised), skipping it dead, unable or gone. Then the focus is on it, every combatant reads its body (`TurnOrder.ClearActions`, `0x8006AE64`, clearing the numbers to show), its turn begins, the clock moves on 600 (`World.AdvanceTurn`, `0x8007C01C`: `World.WrapYear` and `World.Update`, so the weather rolls if the part of the day changed; it ages no effects; **checked**, +600 a turn in 02–04), and a reserve that fled on arriving says "Enemy reinforcements flee" (`+0x543C`).
3. **It begins** (`TurnOrder.BeginTurn`, `0x8006A9C8`): the camera's given to its body (`GiveCameraToPlayer`, `0x80015E30`, which drops an R orbit) and anchored there; its line, "%s begins action", a newcomer's "joins the battle" (`0x7C`, clearing flag 10), or either overwritten by a performer's (`0xD0`); it reads its body (`Combatant.SyncFromBody`) and keeps where it starts (`SaveStartPosition`); the ring (which works the radius out again); its field (`Combatant.PlaceRingAndBody`, `0x8006627C`); then for a combatant with an AI record, its check to flee (`EnemyAI.CheckFlee`, `0x800572AC`, [combat-ai](combat-ai.md#morale)) and state 8, otherwise a flask or spell carried over looked up and state 1, as `State` and `NextState` both, after the target sets are built. The Shadow's aura is the round's, not the turn's, and nothing sets a body down: bodies are set down once, as they're made.
4. **The field's radius** (`+0x21`, `Combatant.UpdateRadius`, `0x8005F29C`) is the first place in `Combat.RadiusByDexterity` (`0x800E0C20`: 0, 0, 0, 3, 7, 12, 16, 21, 26, 28, 30, 33, 36, 38) whose byte reaches the **modified** Dexterity, 14 past it (3 for 1–3, 4 for 4–7, … 13 for 37–38). Then each timed effect in its slot's order: Haste (kind `0x1E`) adds its level, and Stellar Gravity (`0x38`) takes its level away, leaving 1 if that's all or more; an effect's kind is its spell ID's low byte's place in the code's spell list (`0x800E2B40`). Then **1 at most while engaged**: an opponent alive and able within the larger of each body's radii, scaled (`+0x34`) or not (`+0x38`), plus 0.75 (`0x800D2810`), across the ground, both bodies read again first (`Combatant.IsWithinReach`, `0x8006011C`, from `IsFreeOfOpponents`, `0x8005F1B8`); this sets or clears flag 7 and stops a performance. Not engaged, 1 at most while performing, or 1 near the Shadow (`Combat.NearShadow`, `0x80023F34`: within 10, strictly, which logs the vortex line on the current one's). Then 13 at most, and 0 for Pochanargat (entity list place `0xAB`, `0x800E28EB`). It's worked out as each body is made, before each candidate in `Advance`, with each ring, and in the Shadow's battle as the combatant walks, so flag 7 is as old as that: **checked** in 09, where Brenna is engaged with a rat whose own flag is clear until its turn (10).
5. **The player** moves the body with trek's stick handler (`Body.ApplyInput`, `0x80014FEC`, fed each poll): R orbits the camera as in trek, and otherwise up on the stick is the way the camera looks, walking, running from 0.8 or sneaking with Z held, a party member turning 10° a poll. The field holds it: `Body.SetGoal` (`0x80015BD4`) keeps a centre (the combatant's place and the body's height as its turn began, `+0x274`) and a radius (`+0x280`) with body flag 4, and `ProcessPlayers` (`Body.ClampToGoal`, `0x80014518`) puts the body's X and Z back on that **sphere** after it moves, before the bodies push each other, whenever it's at the radius or past it in 3D. So it slides round the edge, and on a slope reaches a little less far across the ground. **Checked**: 03's walked bodies are 10.0000 from their centres in 3D. Every body gets its field as it's made, about where it stands, and loses it as its turn ends. Nothing in the grid stops a body; the bodies' push does (`Crowd`). After each poll the combatant reads its body (`Combatant.UpdateFromBody`, `0x8005F784`): its footprint leaves the grid and takes it again where the body stands (`ArenaGrid.Vacate`, `0x800682E0`, and `Occupy`, the cell rounded half away from zero), its place is the body's X and Z exactly, the focus follows and its facing is the body's; a change counts a move (`+0x22`), the fourth sets flag 3 (**checked** in 02–04), and the target sets are built again. The radius isn't worked out again as it walks up to an enemy: `Combat.MovePlayerBody` (`0x80084CE0`) does that only near the Shadow, centring the field on the body.
6. **The action** ends the turn: an attack, melee or ranged by the weapon (`Combatant.ResolveAction`, `0x80065ACC`); a flask thrown or a potion drunk (action 3: `ThrowFlask` `0x80065600`, `DrinkPotion` `0x80064C84`); a spell (`CastCurrentSpell`, `0x80065C08`); Lay On Hands or Heal With Herbs (`ResolveHealer`, `0x8006591C`); the Troubadour (`StartTroubadour`, `0x800662F4`); a weapon changed in the pause menu; or a skip. `Combatant.ExecuteAction` (`0x80065F6C`) ends a performance, hides the ring, carries it out, applies the damage, checks for death (`CheckDeath`, `0x80065E98`) and starts state 2. Each is [combat-actions](combat-actions.md)'s.
7. **The round ends** (`TurnOrder.EndRound`, `0x8006A62C`) when the list runs out: the Shadow's aura first (`Combat.ShadowAura`, `0x80023AD0`, which can end the battle); each performer pays a Stamina point or stops with none; every combatant's moves and flag 3 are cleared and its effects aged a round (`Character.AgeEffects`, `0x80071444`: the potions', the timed spells', and what they do, [magic](magic.md#effects-on-a-character)); the reserves come in (below); the toggle flips and the next list is built. The leader's morale moves at each of the AI's turns, not here. Wins and losses are only ever found at a turn's change, not at a death.

**Turn order** (`TurnOrder.Build`, `0x8006A16C`). A combatant's turns a round are by its **base** Dexterity (`Combatant.TurnsPerRound`, `0x8005F4D0`): 0 none, 1–17 one, 18–24 one and two by turns (the toggle), 25–29 two, 30–34 two and three by turns, 35 and up three. The list is grouped: every third turn first, then every second, then every first, each group in random order (`Rng.Range`, drawing again on a repeat). While the toggle is set, a combatant with no turns gets one, in a group drawn before the thirds, so a base Dexterity of 0 acts first every other round. Only those able to act (flag 1) have turns, but the active counts (`+0x02`, `+0x04`, `+0x06`) count every combatant alive and not gone. The base is read capped at 30 (`Stats.Base`), so 35's three turns are never reached. Checked in 02: four party members and four rats gave `[1,3,0,2, 3,2,1,5,4,7,0,6]`. Combat parity's formula vectors (`TurnsVectorTests`) run the radius, the turns and the order against 1.0's code at every step ([combat-parity](combat-parity.md#the-formula-vectors)). They show too that the radius reads the modified Dexterity as signed, so 128 and above are 0, and that Haste's level wraps the radius's byte before the cap: a radius of 9 with a Haste of 250 is 3. Neither is reachable without a mod.

**Win and loss** (`TurnOrder.CheckEnd`, `0x8006A97C`): no party member active is a loss (`TurnOrder.Defeat`, `0x8006A780`: state 13 and `Combat.Defeat`, then with a current combatant the party's line, `0xD4`, and in a non-lethal battle Alaron's own over it for good, `0x74`, his portrait locked), and no enemy active with no reserve left a win (`TurnOrder.Victory`, `0x8006A804`: `Combat.SetKillFlags` `0x80067860`, `Combat.TallyRewards` `0x8005E2AC`, state 12, the fade out at the battle's 1/60, and with a current combatant the victory line but under encounter flag 978). Alaron's death is a loss alone (`TurnOrder.Advance`, straight to `Combat.Defeat`, with no line). `Combat.Defeat` (`0x80023F98`) is state 13 and the fade out (`Combat.EnterDefeat`, `0x80023FD0`), or in a non-lethal battle state 28 (`Combat.EnterDefeatWait`, `0x80024014`).

**The kill flags** (`Combat.SetKillFlags`) read the enemy list's first four entries, stopping at the first empty, and set the flag of each boss among them (a jump table at `0x800D2B60`, by entity list place from `0xA8`): Kitarak 154, Pochanargat 3204, Shamsuk 458, Ksathra 3738, Shatrevar 3737, Mehrdad 3736, Golnar 3735, Nasim 3734, Behrooz 3733, Assim 3739 and Lugash 533; the Lizard Man Boss, the Marquis, the Shadow, Sheridan and four more set none. Whether the boss fell or fled doesn't matter, and script battles count. It runs twice a win (`TurnOrder.Victory` and `StateVictory`) and never on a loss or a flight. Its writes don't bring the flag network up to date, and the only read after the first is `StateVictory`'s of the party's gate, so a boss's journal entry is told when something next reads a derived flag: as trek comes back where loading the chunks does, else later. Kitarak's waits in his room until its chest, the journal or leaving; Lugash's shows at once on the return to trek. The port's is told as trek fades in, the user's choice ([flags](flags.md#when-the-network-comes-up-to-date)).

**Rewards** (`Combat.TallyRewards`), as the battle is won, before the fade: the loot inventory (`+0x4FB4`, 64 stacks) and the pools are made; for **every** entry of the enemy list, killed, fled or never come alike, its experience (the entity's `+0x7C`, the DB's reward byte × 50: a rat 100) and its chest (the entity's `+0x7E`, the chest DB's ID `0x7FF` + the byte, through `Combat.AddChestLoot` `0x8005E0F0`); then the bonus loot (`Combat.AddBonusLoot`, `0x8005E238`: a chest if its type is 8, rolled with no entity, else one of the item, worth no experience); then each living member gains the whole experience pool (`Character.AddExp`, `0x80071528`, times 1.5: dead members gain nothing, and an ally in the enemy list gives nothing); and last the gold pool is **doubled** (`sll` at `0x8005E400`). A chest rolls, in this order, on the one stream: its gold (`Range` between its bounds, drawing nothing when they're equal); the enemy's own armor and shield, each by the chest's chance (`RollD(1, 100)`) if it has them, and its three weapons, each only of a class that drops (`Combat.DropWeaponClasses`, `0x800D26C8`: Hafted, Missile, Pole, Sword and Thrown) (`Combat.RollEquipmentDrops`, `0x8005DC78`); a reagent by its chance, then which (`Range(30, 32)`: spice, herb or gemstone) and how many; then the six drops (`Combat.RollChestItem`, `0x8005DF38`), the first two between their counts. **What's looted adds to the experience pool**: a weapon its `ExpModifier`, armor its `ExpBonus`, another item its `ExpMultiplier` (the reagents 5, the Sapphire Gem 500), a potion nothing; a reagent once whatever the count, a drop times its count. A drop's worth goes by its ID's type byte: 7 the weapon table's, 5 and 6 the armor table's, 1, 9–15 and 17–19 the item table's (`+0x18`), and any other (the potions' 0x10 among them) nothing. The enemy's armor and shield are rolled unless they're `0xFFFF`, and a weapon unless it's 0 or `0xFFFF`, its class checked before the roll, so one that doesn't drop draws nothing. The loot (`+0x4FB4`) keeps its stacks from `+0x04`, 0x18 each: the ID at `+0x00` and the count at `+0x14`, an item added again joining its stack. **Checked**: the original's `TallyRewards`, run on checkpoint 11's RAM, gives 12's pool of 1000 and +1500 to each member, and Lugash's win in the parity logs its loot and 8257 experience; combat parity's formula vectors (`RewardsVectorTests`) check the port's against it over every kind of chest ([combat-parity](combat-parity.md#the-formula-vectors)).

**Reserves** (`Combat.SpawnReserves`, `0x8005D914`, at a round's end after the effects age; running it on 08 gives 09's cells). It refills **every** enemy slot whose combatant is dead or gone, or that's empty, from the party's size to `+0x01`, while the list has a next (`+0x07`):

- A reserve that flees as it arrives (`EnemyAI.ReserveFleesOnArrival`, [combat-ai](combat-ai.md#reserves-that-flee-as-they-arrive)) is marked and skipped, its slot left empty, with the hold's line.
- The leader's slot taken sets `+0x0C`.
- The newcomer stands at `Combat.ChooseSpawn` (`0x8005D748`, through `SpawnPosition`: one draw for its direction, `Range(5, 7)` with collision byte 0, else `Range(1, 3)`; set 0, the enemies' side, the slot's letter, `a` + slot − party size), or if that cell is taken, or a body's centre is within its radius and the newcomer's, the first free and clear point of sets 0–4 in turn, its side's letters first (`0x8005D5B0`, `0x8005D474`).
- Its body, radius, field, portrait and action are made as `Combat.CreateBody` makes them, with flag `0x400`, and `+0x07` moves on. The counts aren't changed: `CountActive` finds the newcomers at the next turn's change. Checkpoint 09's newcomers walking is their own AI turn, not the spawn's.

### Deaths

- **`Combatant.Death`** (`0x80062080`), called by `CheckDeath` as the killing blow is resolved, before it lands ([combat-actions](combat-actions.md#an-attack)): Sholeh's 622 set first, and a member's died flags ([characters](characters.md#deaths)); the immobilising effects come off, its footprint leaves the grid, the active counts fall (`+0x02`, and `+0x04` or `+0x06`), a party member stops performing, and the first enemy's death sets `+0x08`. It doesn't set flag 6 or free the body: a dead combatant is known by its hit points. A death from the effects at a round's end (`Entity.ShowAgeResult`, `0x8007138C`) shows the number, then `Death` and `FreeBody` at once: no fall, no line, no wait, the body fading out standing as it was.
- **A freed body's fade** (`Body.FadeOut`, `0x80014AD4`, which `Combat.FreeBody` calls for every body it frees and `Combatant.Leave`, doing the same work inline, for a fled one): body flag `0x100` and 120 ticks at `+0x708`. `Combat.Bodies[slot]` is emptied, but the body stays in the zone's bodies where it lay or stood, and `ProcessPlayers` (`0x80012CA4`–`0x80012D40`) counts it down by the frame's ticks before the body moves: under 1, its alpha (`+0x70C`) and flags are cleared and it's freed (`FreePlayer`, `0x800146E0`); under 60 (`Body.FadeSpan`, `0x800CDB2C`), its alpha is `(u8)(left / 60 × 255)`. So it's drawn whole for the first second and fades to nothing over the next, linearly, by alpha alone; the body's alpha caps its distance's fade and its shadow's (`BodyShadow.DrawAlpha`, `0x800122C4`). While the flag is up its actor isn't stepped (`0x800130F0`), so a dead body keeps the last frame of its fall and a fled one the pose it fled in. It still pushes and is pushed as any body (the push skips only a body that's off or flagged `0x400`). A party member who dies is freed the same way, by the same `RemoveFallen` (it tests only `Character.IsDead`, `0x8006EA54`), and fades out as an enemy does; nothing keeps a dead member's body on the field. Alaron's keel-over in the Goblin Ambush isn't a death (he's left with some 45 hit points), so his body stays down at its fall's last frame. Checkpoint 06 holds rat 7 mid-fall in state 4 (`+0x18` 9, no fade, alpha 255); none catches a body fading.
- **The died flags and the dead leaving the party** (`Party.AfterBattle`, from `Combat.Exit`, after every battle) are [characters](characters.md#deaths)'. Alaron reaches it healed: the game over heals him first.

## The special battles

Read from 1.0's code: no checkpoint holds either fight. The user played both of the Shadow's ends in 1.0 and the port, from a save at its Overworld meeting (script `0x046E`, whose battle is the third answer, "Join me."), and they play as below; without the Horn, the arena's size means the Shadow mostly reaches Alaron and dissipates before its fourth round. The Goblin Ambush's hold, also read alone, the user has seen play as the port has it. The combat record carries both as rules ([hardcoded-content](../hardcoded-content.md#bespoke-behaviours), ADR 0008's layer 3): the Goblin Ambush's on its encounter rule, the Shadow's as `ShadowRule`.

**The Goblin Ambush** (encounter flag 978, script `0x0365`; the Shadow isn't in it, whatever the code's names say):

- **As it starts** (`Combat.Init`, `0x80022D1C`): non-lethal, `Combat.ShadowTurnsLeft` (`0x800D94CE`, a byte) 2, and the party's first member healed (`Character.Heal` of its deficit: Endurance, Stamina and the Level's points toward their bases).
- **The hold** (`Combatant.ShadowFightTrick`, `0x80065DCC`), called once by `Combatant.ExecuteAction` before `CheckDeath`, for whatever it resolves (an attack, a flask thrown, a spell's first target; not the Healer, a flask's blast or anything else): when the target's **entity** is Alaron (`0x023F`), the flag is 978 and the damage isn't 0, it queues "%s loses 1 hit point" (`LogHpLoss`, replacing the attack's own line in the one queued buffer), counts the byte down and returns 1, so 1 is taken and shown. When the count reaches 0 (the second hit) the target's Strength goes to 0 (`Stats.AddCurrent(-modified)`, current and modified) and `UpdateActive` finds him unable. The count wraps after, so it never reaches 0 again.
- **The keel-over**: `Combat.ApplyHit` (`0x80021930`) plays the death animation (`Combat.PlayDeathAnimation`, request 9) for a hit on **slot 0** while the count is 0: the second hit's own. At the next turn's change `CountActive` leaves the unable Alaron out, so `TurnOrder.Defeat` loses the battle with the locked line, though he has some 45 hit points; `Advance`'s first-member-dead test never fires. Then states 28 and 13 and the non-lethal ending.
- **The darts** are the four Goblin Poisoners' (entity `0x022A`); how they fly is [combat-actions](combat-actions.md#an-attack)'s.

**The Shadow** (entity `0x0226`, place `0xAC`). Its battles are scripts `0x046E` on the Overworld and `0x0497` at Ehud's tower, and the monster parties in H14 and B15 with four Zombies, all closed by flag 566. `Combat.CreateBody` sets `Combat.ShadowSlot` (`0x800D94CC`) for it and `Combat.AlaronSlot` (`0x800D94CD`) for Alaron, and `Combat.FreeBody` clears them; `Combat.Init` clears both but `Exit` doesn't, so they go stale after a battle (hence the mode tests below). The Horn of Kynon (`0x0D03`, `Items.HornOfKynon` `0x800E2A42`) is tested afresh at each use (`Combat.PartyHasHorn`, `0x80023AA4`, through `Party.HasItem`, `0x80079BCC`: the party's inventory or any member's things, the dead's too).

- **Made**: with the Horn, `Combatant.Init` (`0x8005EAE0`) raises its base Dexterity to 30 (`Stats.AddTrained`), for its turns. It can't be attacked (`CanAttack`) or cast by or at (`CanCast`), immobilising effects don't take (`Character.AddEffect`), and it lets the party flee ([In and out](#in-and-out)).
- **Its walk**: with the Horn its goal is combat slot 0's cell, whatever its style chose (`EnemyAI.RunStyle`, `0x80059628`). Every AI path in its battle is cut where it comes within 10 of it (`EnemyAI.ShadowSmoothPath`, `0x8005A734`, from `EnemyAI.PlanPath`): the first leg that crosses the circle ends at `ClipToCircle`'s point, rounded half away from zero, unless the walker's field is 1 (near the Shadow, or the Shadow itself). The AI's walk never works a radius out again.
- **Slowing** ([A turn](#a-turn), step 4): within 10 of it (`Combat.NearShadow`, `0x80023F34`, through `Combat.WithinRadius`, `0x80023D44`: each axis at most 10 and the distance strictly under 10, on the combatants' places) the field is 1, and the current combatant's line is "%s caught in the vortex of the Shadow" (`0x80`), shown and queued. The Shadow's own is 1 with anyone within 10 (`Combat.AnyoneWithin10`, `0x80023E9C`, the dead and gone too). As a player's combatant walks near it, its field and ring follow its body (`Combat.MovePlayerBody`, `0x80084CE0`).
- **The aura** (`Combat.ShadowAura`, `0x80023AD0`, first in `TurnOrder.EndRound`): without the Horn, the round count (`+0x52C8`, which `HoldForMessage` shares) vanishes it at 4 (`sltiu v1, 4`) and otherwise goes up, so the aura works at four round ends and the Shadow goes at the fifth, with no aura then. Then each other combatant in slot order, not gone, dead or of chaos (category 5), under 10 (`0x800CE3CC`) from it, by the places' distance as a float, takes 10 − d, squared without the Horn, truncated. A blow of at least its hit points ends the battle instead (`Combat.ShadowDissipates`), touching no one after. The damage goes through `Character.TakeDamage` (which ends a performance) and shows over the head.
- **Reaching Alaron** (`Combat.CheckShadowMerge`, `0x80023884`, the original's `alaron_shadow_merge_attempt`, from `Combat.AfterFrame` at every drawn frame's end but in states 17, 29 and 30): once the two bodies' centres are within their scaled radii and 0.5 (`0x800CE3C8`) in 3D (1.8 for 0.6 and 0.7), whoever moved and whatever the state, the battle ends: `Combat.StartShadowCinematic` (`0x80023984`) with the Horn, else `ShadowDissipates`.
- **`Combat.ShadowDissipates`** (`0x800239B4`): with the Horn, the merge (state 17, `Cinematic.Load(1, 4, 1)`); without, state 29, "The Shadow dissipates" (`0xF0`) shown, and every body back to idle.
- **Vanishing** (states 29 and 30): the field is drawn while `+0x52C8` counts on a frame at a time to 60 (56 frames after the fifth round end), then the screen fades out at 1/60 a tick, and state 30 leaves for trek (mode 1) once it's out. `Combat.Exit` does nothing more: no rewards, no flags, the monster party neither remembered nor fled, so trek comes back where the battle began.
- **The merge** (state 17, `Combat.StateShadowMerge`, `0x800226F8`): on its first frame `Combat.MergeShadow` (`0x800675C0`) raises the party's first member from the Shadow's sheet as it stands, and the battle leaves for mode `0xE`. `Combat.Exit` frees it the same frame, and the cinematic (record 1, Shadow Self Merge) plays after. Its end sets its done flag, 569 (`Cinematic.EndCase`), and goes back to trek where the battle began (`Cinematic.EndShadow`). No rewards, no encounter or kill flags.
- **The boost**: Strength +6, Intelligence, Willpower and Endurance +3, Stamina +10 (`Stats.AddTrained`: trained, base, current and modified, the trained byte up to its cap), protection +5 (a byte). Each skill and weapon skill the Shadow has (modified not 0) goes up by the difference of the two base levels (each read to 10 at most, `Skills.BaseLevel`), or 2 if that isn't positive (`Skills.AddSkillBase`, `AddWeaponBase`: the base up to 10, the modified by as much). The shield skill goes up the same way, but only when the Shadow's modified shield skill **is** 0, the test turned round (`Skills.AddShieldBase`). Each of the Shadow's spells goes up the same way to at most 10, or is learnt at the Shadow's level. Then flag 566. On the data the Shadow has Stealth and Wizard 10, no weapon skill, shield 8 (so the shield's branch does nothing) and five spells (`0x031C`–`0x0320`) at 10.
- **Healing barred**, only in combat (`Game.Mode` 2) with the Shadow's slot set: a potion's heal (`Entity.HealPercent`, `0x800715DC`) heals nothing and draws nothing, so the potion is used up and says "%s fails to apply potion". Lay On Hands (`Party.LayOnHands`, `0x80077778`) answers Common line 437, "The Shadow's presence prevents all healing.", before its cost, and Heal With Herbs (`Party.HealWithHerbs`, `0x8007798C`) the same **after** taking the herb and finding the Stamina, so the herb is lost. Either way `ResolveHealer` shows "%s fails to perform healing" over it the same frame, and the turn is spent. The pause menu's Party tab, in mode 3, isn't barred. **Teleportation** fails in `Combatant.ResistRoll` (`0x80064298`) with "%s fails to cast the spell" after its cost is paid.

**Lugash** (place `0xBA`) and **Pochanargat** (`0xAB`) have no code beyond `EnemyAI.HasBossStyle`, `Combat.SetKillFlags` (533 and 3204), Pochanargat's place in `Combat.ForbidFleeForBosses` and his radius of 0 (`Combatant.UpdateRadius`): a scan of every function for their places and IDs finds nothing else. Lugash's first script battle (`0x04D1`) can be fled by its encounter flag 5250, which is derived, so a win never sets it; his second (`0x04D6`) has no flag, and as a cut-scene's battle it can't be fled. He has no Cheat Death in a battle: his effect records are empty at every turn of the logged battles ([combat-parity](combat-parity.md)).

**Rain and snow** in combat are trek's: `Combat.DrawFrame` runs `HandleZoneEngineFrame` with the combat camera, and `ProcessWeather` has no combat test ([weather](weather.md)).

## The rest, by entry point

Targeting (`TargetSet`, `0x80068FE0`–`0x8006A0B0`), melee and ranged, spells, potions and skills in a turn, and the visuals are [combat-actions](combat-actions.md)'s; the AI is [combat-ai](combat-ai.md)'s. The rest:

- **Spells, effects, potions and flasks** are [magic](magic.md)'s: casting (`Combatant.CastSpell`, `0x800648CC`, through `CheckSpellAvailable`, `PrepareCast`, `CastChance`, `ResistRoll` and `ApplySpellEffect`), the 15 timed effects and 7 potion records on the entity and their ageing by round and by trek's clock, Cheat Death, the kind lists (`Combat.DispelKinds`, `LimitedKinds`, the charms that count toward the disable limit, `ImmobilisingKinds`), potions drunk, and the flasks' damage and blast. A spell's kind is its place in the spell table, not the combatant's `+0x27`, which holds a potion's.
- **The GUI** (`combatgui.cpp`, `CombatGui.Create` `0x80085B80`) and **the radar** (`CombatRadar.Draw`, `0x8008DF04`): the portraits in their frames down the top left and right with their HP bars, tinted by the target sets, the log box (a `TextBand`, the cinematics' captions' widget) and the radar, centred on the camera's owner and turned with the camera. [ui](ui.md#the-battle-screen) has them.
- **The camera** isn't trek's follow camera: `ProcessCombatCamera` (`0x8008D8BC`, from `ProcessPlayers` in mode 2) puts the camera in mode 4 and runs the interiors' `Camera::Mode1` (`0x800A69D8`) with combat's numbers on the body that owns it: near and far 4 and 10 (8 and 16 about the focus in states 11 and 18), the goal 2 above the aim, the aim 1.05 above the body, easing 1/16 a tick at most 1, the eye 1/32 at most 0.5. On its first frame it's put 8 behind the current party member (or the first combatant's body on an enemy's turn), away from the nearest enemy, 1.5 up (`CombatCamera.PlaceBehind`, `0x8008D78C`); a party member's turn puts only its goal there. Through an enemy's turn the eye goes back to its goal and stays, at least 4 from the aim. `CombatCamera.KeepInArena` (`0x8008D29C`) keeps the eye a unit above the floor. State 9's pull-back is [combat-actions](combat-actions.md#an-attack)'s. A spell's cast glides (`CombatCamera.StartGlide`, `0x8008D1F0`; `CombatCamera.CastGlide`, `0x8008D3C8`, checked in 08). No combat code sets the field of view or the clip planes. **Checked** on 02–04. The body it aims at is the camera's owner, which `GiveCameraToPlayer` makes the current combatant's at a turn's start and the cursor's target in states 9 and 10. States 1, 5, 8, 24 and 26 have no branch of their own: the hold on an enemy's turn is by whose turn it is. R orbits it in state 1 as in trek.
- **Random numbers** are the one stream (`Rng.Range` `0x80093214`, its state at `0x800D8088`): the turn order, d100s, damage dice, 2d6 for morale and casting, and loot. Besides the battle's own draws, `Weather.DriftWind` (`0x8007BAA0`, from `Combat.AfterFrame` through `World.SetTerrain` and `World.Update`) draws 2 every drawn frame, plus 3 with chance 0.05 and 1 more with 0.05, which accounts for every draw from checkpoint 04 to 05; the ring's retries, the particles, the sky and the spells' visuals draw on it too. The checkpoints hold the stream's state, so a battle's draws can be replayed from one ([combat-parity](combat-parity.md)).

## What it shares with trek

- **The zone engine**, in mode 1, framed each combat frame (`Combat.DrawFrame`) and freed after: the chunks, collision, scenes, lights, sky, rain and the terrain (`World.SetTerrain` every frame), with trek built again after; the port's arena is a stage of trek's `ZoneEngine`, as a cut-scene's is.
- **Bodies**: `AllocPlayer` and `FreePlayer`, `Body.SetDown`, `Body.Face`, the player handler with a goal for the AI's walks, shadows to 128, and the actors' requests (7 hit, 9 death).
- **The UI**: `TextBand`, the pause menu in modes 3, 5 and 6 (the flee option only in state 14), `MenuTween`.
- **Particles and lights**: actors' emitters, the party lit by the map's lights (`+0x70A`, [rendering](rendering.md)).
- **Scripts and flags**: the game over scripts, the encounter, boss and Sholeh's flags, the beaten monster party remembered, the Shadow's cinematic, the battle music with the map set.

**The combat strings** (String/Combat, 64 lines, [text](../formats/text.md)) are taken by byte offset: `0x00` "begins action", `0x04` the party flees, `0x08` victorious, `0x10`–`0x1C` healing, `0x20`–`0x38` avoid, attack, hit, critical, miss, `0x3C`–`0x4C` potions, `0x50` killed, `0x58`–`0x70` spells, `0x74` the Shadow, `0x78` flees, `0x7C` joins, `0x80` the vortex, `0x84` rests, `0x88` changes weapons, `0x8C`–`0xBC` a combatant's details (nothing in combat's files shows them), `0xC0`–`0xD0` performing, `0xD4` the party incapacitated, `0xD8` `flee%d`, `0xDC` `%s%u%c`, `0xE8` banished, `0xEC` "Enemy reinforcements flee", `0xF0` the Shadow dissipates, `0xF4` and `0xF8` `good` and `bad`, `0xFC` "cheats death".

| `Battle.End.cs`, `Battle.Rewards.cs` | the win (`StepVictory`), the loss (`StepDefeat`, `StepDefeatWait`), `EndAs`, which every ending goes through as a `BattleOutcome`, and `Leave`, `Combat.Exit`'s part on the party; `TallyRewards` into a `BattleSpoils` |
| `Battle.Shadow.cs` | [The special battles](#the-special-battles): the Goblin Ambush's hold and keel-over, and the Shadow |
| `CombatCamera` | `ProcessCombatCamera`, over `FollowCamera.StepLeashed` with combat's numbers (`LeashNumbers`, [cameras](cameras.md)) |
| `CombatLog`, `CombatGui` | the log box (`Battle.Lines`) and the portraits and radar ([ui](ui.md#the-battle-screen)) |
| `Combatant` | the 0x8C record: its `Sheet` is a member's own `Character`, or a fresh one from the entity's record for an enemy or Sholeh; `Controlled` means it has an AI record (the enemies and Sholeh) |
| `Arena`, `BattleSetup` | an arena's chunk and map with its points by name; the setup block: `WithParty` (a wanderer's or a used monster party's, the original's `BattleSetup.FromMonsterParty`), `Against` (a talker's), `Scripted`, `CampAmbush` ([camping](camping.md#the-ambushs-enemies)), `FillEnemies`, `ChooseArena` |

**Content** is the combat record, `aidyn:combat/0` (`CombatRecord`: Sholeh, the bystanders an enemy list drops, the Skeleton, the flee rules, `Untargetable`, `Rooted`, `Missiles`, `CloseRange`, `Bosses`, `BossStyles`, `DroppedClasses`, `Encounters`, `GameOver`, `Text`, `FlaskDanger`, `Shadow`, `UndrawnWeapons`, `OtherHand`), with the terrains' `Arenas` and `Ambushes` and the spells' and potions' `visuals`. [hardcoded-content](../hardcoded-content.md) says which layer each is in.

**Driving it**: `--battle <monster party>[,<arena>][,ambush]` starts a battle from the command line, and `--battle aidyn:dialogue/0x046E` a script's as its end would (the Shadow's, Lugash's, Pochanargat's); `--press` scripts presses for the movie writer (`wait:<seconds>`, `<action>:<seconds>` held); F3's overlay shows the state, the combatants, the ring and the arena's raindrops; F10 and F11 (`Battle.Rout`, `Collapse`) win or lose at the next turn's change, and B held two seconds on a player's turn wins (`Battle.ForceWin`, not in the Shadow's battles); `--spell-fx off` leaves out the visuals, the sky's dimming and the tints, as a baseline; `--spell-auras each` sets the lasting scenes' option.

**Pinned** against the checkpoints by Rom.Tests' `*CheckpointTests` (`BattleCheckpointTests`, `AttackCheckpointTests`, `AiCheckpointTests`, `CasterCheckpointTests` and the rest), most comparing with the original's own functions run on the checkpoints' RAM, and against the user's logged battles by `ParityReplayTests` ([combat-parity](combat-parity.md)); `GameSessionTests` play the special battles on NA 1.0's data, which no checkpoint holds.

### What a battle tells its host

Combat's presentation goes out as cue records through a host, and its ending comes back from its step (ADR 0019, `CombatCues.cs`).

- **The host** is `ICombatHost`, with one method, `Cue(CombatCue)`. The session takes it at `GameSession.Start` (`combatHost`, the runtime's `Game`) and gives it to `Battle.Begin`, so the first bodies are cued as they're made; a test sets `Battle.Host` on a battle it built.
- **The cues**, in the order the battle makes them. The views build and free their parts by them and read where things are from the battle's state each frame; nothing diffs the battle's lists.

  | Cue | When | The runtime |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | `CombatantJoined` | a body is made (`Add`): at the setup and as a reserve joins | makes its `CombatantView` |
  | `CombatantAnimated` | a one-off request through `Battle.Animate`: the swing 12–18, the throw 15, a cast or a drink 5; as the blow lands the hit 7, the dodge 8, the fall 9, an ally's help 10 | nothing: the core steps the actors |
  | `CombatantDied` | `Death`, as the killing blow is dealt, before it lands | nothing |
  | `CombatantGone` | a dead or fled body's fade is over (`Fled` says which) | frees the view |
  | `NumberShown`, `NumberGone` | a number shown; its two seconds over, the pool's oldest pushed out, or its body gone | `CombatNumbersView.Show`, `Remove` |
  | `MissileLaunched`, `MissileLanded` | `StartFlight`; `StepFlight` as the blow lands | `MissileView.Launch`, `Land` |
  | `SpellVisualStarted`, `SpellVisualEnded` | `SpellFx.Start` or `StartPotion`, once the cast knows both its bodies, so an actor's emitters attach to the right ones; `FreeIfFinished` | `SpellFxView.Started`, `Ended` |
  | `LogLine` | every change of what the log box says (below) | nothing: the box draws `CombatLog.Text` |
  | `BattlePaused` | the tick the pause menu's two frames have passed | nothing: the session acts on the step |
  | `BattleEnded` | `EndAs`: the win, the loss, the spared loss, fleeing by the pause or by Escape, the Shadow vanishing or merging | nothing |

  The battle's own `Free` at its end cues nothing: `BattleEnded` came first.
- **The step**: `Battle.Tick` returns `BattleStep.Ran`, `Paused(PartyTab, CanFlee)` while the pause menu is due (the Party tab and Flee from a turn's pause, states 1, 9, 10, 11, 18 and 25; the System tab alone from state 27's), or `Ended(Outcome)` once it has left (`Battle.Over`: states 16, 20 and 21). The session sends `Transition.PauseBattle` on the first and keeps the outcome of the second for `EndBattle`.
- **The log's one writer**: `CombatLog` notes in `History` (`Battle.Log`) and cues as `LogLine` every change of what the box says (`Text`): a line shown at once, a queued line or Banish's third flushed, the reinforcements' hold and the line it gives back, and the line it's locked on. A line put up over itself isn't one, as the box doesn't change. So the log holds what the player saw when they saw it: a blow's queued "loses %d hit points" as the blow lands, a spell's "cast on" at state 2's end.
- **The session**: trek cues a battle (`TrekLoop.CueBattle`, `QueueState(2)`'s fade at 1/30 a tick) rather than handing it to the host, and cuts the music as it goes (`TrekLoop.BeginBattle`). `GameSession.BeginBattle` stages the arena on trek's zone engine as a cut-scene's stage (`ZoneEngine.Stage`), its music held until the fade ends, begins the battle (mode `GameMode.Combat`, under the menu) and sends `Transition.StartedBattle`. `GameSession.EndBattle` cuts the music whatever the outcome ([audio](audio.md#music)), sends `Transition.EndBattle`, calls `Battle.Leave`, moves trek's kept place for an encounter's end (`TrekLoop.Keep`), then by the outcome: won, it holds the world as `PostBattle` and sends `Transition.PostBattle`, and trek comes back where the battle began once the windows close (`MenuClosed`, [ui](pause-menu.md#after-a-battle)); lost, the game over (`PlayGameOver`: the combat record's `GameOver` script from its dialogue object in the Dreamscape's map, as a cut-scene, whose end is `Transition.GameOver` by the base plugin's trigger on its `script.ended`, [events](events.md)); merged, the Shadow's cinematic (`PlayCinematic(cinematic, fromTheatre: false)`), whose end resumes trek where the battle began; otherwise `TrekLoop.Resume(fled)`, at the farthest breadcrumb having fled, with its heading and the camera at its eye ([In and out](#in-and-out)), and `Transition.ResumeTrek`.

### Traps

- **A tick's order** (`Battle.Tick`, as `Combat.Frame`): the reinforcements' hold; the controls; each body in turn (`AtStrike` reads the frame before the actor steps, then the step, the push and `AfterBodies`); the camera; the state's step; the Shadow's reach (`CheckShadowMerge`); the marks; the tints; the spells' visuals; the numbers; the fade. The marks set and clear tints before the tints step, so a cast's glow on a body the marks let go is cleared on the frame it starts, as checkpoint 08's tints (all 0) show.
- **A one-shot request is held a frame.** `Combat.AfterFrame` lets requests go once a frame, so a request combat makes is asked for through every tick of its frame (`Combatant.OneShot`, `Cadence.FrameTicks`), and an actor takes only a request its state has a branch for (`ActorStateMachine.Taken`), so the frame's second tick doesn't advance it. Without both the swing's, hit's, dodge's and death's keys don't sound ([audio](audio.md#bodies-sounds)).
- **The original's one focus is three aims**, each moved only by its own: `Battle.ThrownAim` (state 18's free cursor), `AreaAim` (state 11's centre, which set B is built about for the player's placed spell) and `AiAreaCentre` (the AI's `AreaCentre` and `AimAreaSpell`, which an AI's placed spell is cast about). `FocusOn(c)` puts all three on a combatant wherever the original puts its focus there, so each reads what the original's would; the camera looks at its state's.
- **A body keeps its cell while it hasn't moved** (`Combatant.SyncFromBody`): the port's bodies are in the world's coordinates and the original's in the arena chunk's, and taking the chunk's origin off loses a little, enough to move a cell after the AI's tries. `IsWithinReach` is false for a body gone from the field.
- **The fading.** `FreeBody` sets `Combatant.Fading` to `Battle.FadeTicks` (120) for the dead and the fled alike and keeps the combatant in the battle's fading list, which a reserve taking its slot doesn't empty. `Battle.Crowd` counts each down a tick, cues `CombatantGone` at 0, and keeps the fading among the bodies that push and are pushed, stepped with no animation, in slot order with a fading body before the reserve in its slot. `Combatant.Alpha` is the body's `+0x70C`: 255, then `(u8)(left / 60 × 255)` under `Battle.FadeShownTicks`. The next turn begins as the fade starts.
- **Content by its effects, not its kinds**: Darkness and Light are known by their `partOfDay` effect, the holds by `immobilise` (`Combatant.PoseHeld`), Fireball by its spell record's `area`, the Shadow by its `ShadowRule`. The original tests the Shadow's place (`0xAC`) in `ForbidFleeForBosses`, `CanAttack` and `CanCast` with no list; the record keeps two rules, `Flee` and `Untargetable`, so a mod's boss the party may flee from can still be hit.
- The AI's, the attack's and the visuals' own traps are in [combat-ai](combat-ai.md#traps) and [combat-actions](combat-actions.md#traps).

### Where the port differs

The user's choices. The AI's are in [combat-ai](combat-ai.md#where-the-port-differs), and an attack's, a spell's, a flask's and the visuals' in [combat-actions](combat-actions.md#where-the-port-differs).

- **The Goblin Ambush's end sets the leader's Strength to its base** rather than adding the base to it, which doubled it when the battle ended before the second hit.
- **A script's battle has no bonus loot**, where the original pays out the last monster party's again (`0x800D9470` is never cleared).
- **The game over's end goes back to the title**, its cards first, as the original's does, but the runtime starts its Godot scene again with a new game for it ([ui](ui.md#coming-back-to-it)).
- **The Pace setting quickens a turn's walk.** Brisk's 1.3 (`Battle.Pace`, which the session sets from each tick's `SessionSettings` and as the battle begins) plays the body the player steers in state 1 faster, steps and all, as trek's player's ([movement](movement.md)). It doesn't reach the field's radius, so a turn covers the same ground sooner, nor the AI's bodies, nor the attack, cast or turn's end after it, whose timings the strikes and the results wait on.
- **The ring's retries draw on the cosmetic stream** (ADR 0006): nothing but the drawing reads where a teardrop stands, so a ring's misses move no rules draw, where the original's move every one after them. Each ring starts due +Z rather than 0.006° further round than the last.
- **The loot is rolled on the loot stream**, not the battle's (ADR 0006), so a battle's draws after it don't shift.

Not ported:

- Combat's frame counter's limit on pausing (`+0x5448 < 4`).
- Half-alpha shadows: `EntityBody.ShadowAlpha` (`0x8006C798`) gives entity list places `0xAF`–`0xB5` and `0xC0` a shadow of alpha `0x7F`; the port draws every shadow whole.
- The idle bodies combat leaves still: `ProcessPlayers` doesn't step the animation of an idle body (its request, `+0x1A` and `+0x1C` all 0) 13.0 (`0x800CDB50`) or more from the camera's aim, unless its actor is in `Body.AlwaysAnimated` (`0x800CDA50`, nine actors, read onto the actor records as `AlwaysAnimated`, [hardcoded-content](../hardcoded-content.md)); the port steps every body's but the held and the fallen.
- The glide aims by the attacker's entity height less 0.2 and its radius; the original's `EyeHeight` (`0x8006C748`) isn't read.

By the tick:

- The camera acts every tick rather than once a frame; only its goal and holds are once-a-frame things, so it's the same at 30 fps.
- The Shadow's reach is tested every tick rather than at each drawn frame's end, and state 29 counts its frames by the cadence.

Others:

- A bystander in the first four leaves the original a null slot that it counts as an enemy on the field; the port leaves it out and says so (`Battle.Problems`). No monster party that fights has one.
- A win leaves through state 16 (`Battle.Over`), which the original never reaches for one, and the session opens the windows; the post-battle window opens on `PauseMenu.Member`, the member last shown or whose turn the last pause was on ([ui](pause-menu.md#opening-and-closing)).

## Not settled

- **The merge's shield test**, turned round in the original (the skill goes up only when the Shadow has none): whether to keep it or turn it the right way isn't chosen. The data never reaches it, as the Shadow has shield 8.
- **The frame's `delta` in combat at full speed**, taken as 2 ticks; checkpoint 10 suggests 3. It sets how long a one-shot request is held and rounds an attack's strike to a frame ([reference rig](../reference-rig.md)). The parity logs' deltas are the logger's slowed frames, so they don't settle it.
- Body flags `0x200` (set on enemies' bodies by `Combat.CreateBody`), `0x400` (skipped by the bodies' push) and `0x800` (`ProcessPlayers` calls `0x80015FEC`), which `TurnOrder.ClearActions` clears.
