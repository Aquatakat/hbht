# Combat: battle mode (2.5, 2.6, M3)

The original's combat, read from 1.0's code and checked in the combat checkpoints and the user's logged battles, and its port. This is the hub: where combat's code is, how a battle starts and ends, the arenas, what it keeps in RAM, its states, a turn, the special battles, and the port's classes, cues, traps and differences. The rest is in:

- [combat-actions](combat-actions.md): an attack, spells, potions and skills in a turn, what spells look like, and what the bodies hold.
- [combat-ai](combat-ai.md): the enemy AI, its morale and fleeing.
- [combat-parity](combat-parity.md): roadmap 2.9, the replay of the user's logged battles against the port, what it has checked and what differs.
- [magic](magic.md): casting's rules, and where spells', potions' and flasks' effects land.

Addresses are 1.0's. Names are in the symbol map, prefixed `Combat.`, `Combatant.`, `TurnOrder.`, `ArenaGrid.`, `TargetSet.`, `EnemyAI.`, `AiScores.`, `CombatGui.`, `CombatMenu.`, `CombatMarkers.`, `CombatSpellFx.`, `CombatCamera.`, `CombatRadar.` and so on, all coined but `ProcessCombatCamera`. Fields marked **checked** were read in the combat checkpoints ([rig](../reference-rig.md#checkpoint-library)), one battle against ten rats; the rest are read from the code.

Combat is game mode 2 (`Game.Mode`, `0x800D80C8`). `ScreenFade.Step` (`0x8001F330`) calls `Combat.Frame` (`0x80022BA4`) each frame, and the mode it returns is the next: 2 to stay, 1 trek, 3 the pause menu, 6 the post-battle windows, `0xC` a cut-scene, `0xE` a cinematic, 0 leaves the game.

## In and out

**The setup block** (`0x800D9456`–`0x800D9485`, **checked**: empty in checkpoint 01, filled in 02) is what every source fills, and `BattleSetup` is the port's:

| Address | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| `0x800D9456` | 1 when filled; 0 makes the debug build's random test battle |
| `0x800D9458` | 12 entity IDs, 0 ending them (ten rats, `0x026E`): the first four start on the field, the rest are reserves |
| `0x800D9470` | bonus loot: an item, or a chest if its type is 8 |
| `0x800D9472` | the encounter flag (1321 for the rats; the Goblin Ambush's is 978) |
| `0x800D9474` | 1 if winning remembers the monster party's map object; then its index (`0x800D9476`), map set (`0x800D9478`), row and column (`0x800D947A`, `0x800D947C`) |
| `0x800D947E` | the collision byte, 0–2: how the party's placed. 2 is an ambush |
| `0x800D947F` | the arena, 0–27 |
| `0x800D9480` | the animation byte, 0–3: the enemies' spawn set is 4 less |
| `0x800D9481` | can flee |
| `0x800D9482`, `0x800D9484` | the monster party object's flag slot A (`+0x1C`) and its flags (`+0x14`), which `Combat.StateVictory` evaluates (`MapObjects.GateHolds`, `0x800116AC`) before it remembers the party |

- **Filled by** `BattleSetup.FromMonsterParty` (`0x800094B4`, the port's `BattleSetup.WithParty`: `Wanderer.Tick`, `IMapObjectHost.Talk` and `BattleSetup.Against`, below), `CampEncounters.Load` (`0x800096DC`: collision byte 2, animation 0, no fleeing, no loot or flag) and the script ops. `Wanderer.Tick` writes collision byte 2, and the animation byte by what the player was doing (3 running, 2 walking, 1 sneaking, else 0) on each frame its notice holds, so it's what he was doing when last noticed ([wanderers](wanderers.md)); `Talk` writes animation 0 and collision 0 with the player within 60° of the party's body's heading, else 1 (`0x80010AA8`–`0x80010B74`, [wanderers](wanderers.md#talking); the port's `TrekLoop.TalkByte`).
- **The enemy list** (`BattleSetup.FillEnemies`, `0x80009374`, given the monster party object's `+0x28` onward: eight kinds of a u16 entity, a u8 least and a u8 most, then the party's least and most as u16s, the object's `+0x50` and `+0x52`) is three passes over a party's eight kinds: each kind's least, in order, stopping at the party's most or 12 (`BattleSetup.SureKinds`, `0x80008EC8`); then, unless that reached the most, a `Range(0, most − least)` more of each kind whose most is above its least, in order, stopping the same way, and none at all if it comes to under the party's least (`BattleSetup.ChooseKinds`, `0x800090A4`); then, where the party's average level is below the enemies', each kind loses 0.3, 0.2 or 0.1 of its count by the party's level × 3 over the enemies' (`BattleSetup.TrimByLevel`, `0x800088E4`, the shares at `0x800CD470`), removed from the front, an entry shifted into a removed one's place being passed over. A list come to nothing is one Skeleton (`EntityList[0xBB]`, `0x0263`). So the field is the sure ones first, not a shuffle. The party's level is `Party.AverageLevel` (`0x80075134`): the members' modified Levels summed in a byte, over the count at the party's `+0x24`. Sure ones that reach the most come to the most, as `ChooseKinds` returns it rather than the count, so a most of 0 fights the Skeleton. Twelve sure ones under a most past 12 go on to the extras, and one written makes a thirteenth entry over the bonus loot (`0x800D9470`). The port stops at 12 and draws nothing there, and no party in the game has so many ([combat-parity](combat-parity.md#the-formula-vectors)). `BattleSetup.FromMonsterParty` calls `ChooseArena` before `FillEnemies`, so the arena's draws come first; `CampEncounters.Load` does too, with its two kinds laid out the same way on the stack. `Combat.ForbidFleeForBosses` (`0x8005CCCC`) clears the flee byte for the 12 entities at `0x800D2688` (up to `0xFF`) and sets it for the Shadow, whose place it compares with as an immediate (`addiu v0, zero, 0xAC` at `0x8005CCE8`), and `Combat.ApplyFleeRuleByFlag` (`0x8005D054`) has the last word by flag ([hardcoded-content](../hardcoded-content.md)). The pause menu offers Flee only while the byte is set, so not against a boss.
- **The arena** (`BattleSetup.ChooseArena`, `0x80008698`, for every battle but a cut-scene's, below) is by the terrain of the chunk the player's in (`World.GetTerrain`, the world's `+0x24`), through a jump table at `0x800CD400`. A range is a roll between them, inclusive (`Rng.Range`). The port's terrain records name each range as a group of the arenas' chunks (ADR 0035), and roll over the group's places, which draws the same: `arenas` `[[16/G01, 16/G03], [16/I01, …, 16/I07]]` is terrain 20's two ranges below. A script's own battle that names no arena takes the combat record's `defaultArena`, arena 0's A01, and draws nothing:

  | Terrain | Arena | Terrain | Arena |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 0, 7 | 6–7 | 11 | 23 |
  | 1, 24 | 2–3 | 12 | 22 |
  | 2 | 0–1 | 13, 25 | 27 |
  | 3, 27 | 14–15 | 14 | 24 |
  | 4 | 19 | 15 | 25 |
  | 5, 17 | 4–5 | 16 | 26 |
  | 6 | 18 | 18 | 20 |
  | 8 | 12–13 | 19, 23, 26 | 8–11 |
  | 9 | 17 | 20 | one in two 6–7, else 8–11 |
  | 10 | 16 | 21 | one in two 2–3, else 8–11 |
  | | | 22 | one in two 0–1, else 8–11 |

  A terrain of 28 or more writes nothing, so the last battle's arena stays. Nothing but the debug build's random battle reaches arena 21. Terrains 20–22 draw `RollD(1, 2)` first, 1 for the first range. The importer carries the table as a fact on each terrain record (`TerrainRecord.Arenas`, `HBHT.Rom.Engine.Arenas`).
- **A script's battle** (`BattleSetup.Scripted`, `0x80050804`, as its battle op ends it) fills the block from the script: its `AddEnemy`s, the collision byte (`+0x112` of the script's state), the arena (`+0x114`, above 27 made 0 at `0x80050888`; 0 when the script sets none: the port's `battle.arena` names the arena's chunk, which the importer resolves, and `combat.defaultArena` is arena 0's) and the animation byte (`+0x113`), the block marked filled, and fleeing unless the script is a cut-scene (mode byte `+0xAA` ≠ 1). That's every cut-scene's battle, each of which sets its arena. **A conversation with an npc or encounter** fills it again as the conversation ends: `Conversation.End` calls `Transition.AfterScript` (`0x80051AD4`), which by the ending kind (`+0x90`) sets `TrekLoop.State` to 5 or 7 for a shop or training, or for a battle (2) calls `BattleSetup.Against` (`0x80051A4C`). With a party talking (the script's `+0x78`, its body's map object), that sets `TrekLoop.State` 2, the collision and animation bytes 0, and calls `BattleSetup.FromMonsterParty` on the party's object with its flag C (`+0x20`): its enemies rolled, fleeing allowed, the party remembered on a win, and **the arena by the terrain** (`ChooseArena`), over what the script wrote. No such conversation sets an arena. So Kitarak's battle, in his room in the goblin camp (map set 1, C01, terrain 16), is in arena 26 (M19, the dark wooden floor), not arena 0's forest. The port's `Transition.AfterScript` applies `BattleSetup.Against` for a conversation's talker (`GameSessionTests`). With no party talking, `Against` does nothing and no battle starts, where the port starts the script's; no conversation in the game but a party's ends in a battle, so only a mod reaches it.
- **The switch.** `TrekLoop.QueueState(2)` (`0x8001FF04`) fades out; trek's state 2 marks trek to be built again on return (`0x800DB067`) and returns mode 2.
- **`Combat.Init`** (`0x80022D1C`, on the first frame, while `0x800DB0A4` is 1): takes down trek's HUD, allocates the combat struct (`Combat.Reset`) and loads the combat strings, sets map set 16 (`0x800D8414`) and the arena's chunk (`0x800D8416`), and starts the zone engine in mode 1 (`InitZoneEngine(1, 0)`, `Zones.Mode`): trek's chunk streamer, collision, lights, sky and weather, over the Battle arenas, with the zone engine's point at (20, 20) in the arena chunk, the shadows' distance 128, and the battle's song chosen in `Zones.SetMapSet` (one `Below(5)` of the map set's five, or `Below(4)` of four without the Expansion Pak). `Combat.Setup` (`0x8005D15C`) fills the struct: the counts, the arena grid, a combatant and a body for each living party member and enemy on the field, Sholeh moved to the party's side if she's in the first four, the leader, whether there are flee points, the turn order and the flee rules by flag. Then the GUI; a body for each combatant (`Combat.CreateBody`, `0x8005C474`, in slot order from `0x80022ED8`), set down from on high (`Body.SetDown(body, 0, 0, 0)`), so a body that finds no floor tries to the side with two draws each time, **after** the turn order's draws, as the port's are too. If every try misses, 1.0 leaves the body where it was made, a unit above the arena's origin, which in G11 is eight under the floor, and the body falls through the world. That can happen where a spawn truncates into a band where the sweep misses at a crease ([movement](movement.md#setting-a-body-down)). G11's `good4a` (the party leader on set 4, which the Lizard King's fight uses) truncates to (29, 48), in a band about 0.09 wide for Alaron's radius of 0.7, where all five tries miss for about one seed in 14. The user found Alaron falling. The port, the user's choice, rests such a body on the floor under where its tries left it, found by a point's sweep (`Battle.OnFloor`, `Body.RestOnFloor`), as it does for a reserve coming on; the draws are the original's either way. Then the first turn's line; the camera on the current combatant's body (`Camera.SetEye`, `GiveCameraToPlayer`, `CombatCamera.Reset`); the first turn (`Combat.StartFirstTurn`, `0x80022C9C`: its line, the radius, the ring and the field, but not the clock); the effects; and state 31 with the fade in, a sixtieth a tick (`0x800CE308`). The Goblin Ambush's flag (978) makes it non-lethal, sets the hold's count to 2 and heals the first member ([The special battles](#the-special-battles)). Its `0x800E6850` and `0x800E6860` are `Combat.StateMoving`'s walk positions, not cameras.
- **`Combat.Exit`** (`0x80023060`, after every frame, acting the frame a state asks, `0x800DB0A8`): marks the setup block used and clears `RememberParty` (`0x800D9474`); after the Goblin Ambush (978), however it ends, adds the party's first member's **base** Strength to its current and modified (`Stats.AddCurrent`: the ambush's second hit took it to 0, so a battle ended before that doubles it), sets flag 47, rewrites trek's kept place (`Zones.SavedPosition`) to Oriana's hut (map set 6, E03, (5.9, 0.5, 3.0), the eye at the position, facing (1, 0) in the kept field `0x800D9078`, which `InitGameTrek` copies back to the body's own reversed one, `+0x20`, so he faces (−1, 0), toward Oriana), as the map set's named place 0 (`Zones.SavedPlace`, `0x800d9092`), so trek's first frame walks into her first cut-scene and its end chains into the next ([scripts](scripts.md#the-world-it-plays-in)) and heals Alaron in full; takes every effect off every combatant (`Entity.ClearEffects`, `0x80071830`: the potions' but Stealth's, kind 0x10, and the 15 timed spells'; hit points stay); frees the GUI, the effects, the bodies, the struct and the zone engine; and last takes the dead out of the party (`Party.AfterBattle`, `0x80074EE8`, [Deaths](#deaths)). The bonus loot (`0x800D9470`) is never cleared, so a script's battle, which doesn't write it, pays out the last npc or encounter's again. Where it goes is the mode the state returned:
  - **A win** (state 12, `Combat.StateVictory`, `0x80022450`), once faded out: `Combat.Exit` asked; for an npc's or encounter's battle alone (`RememberParty`, which only `BattleSetup.FromMonsterParty` sets), the encounter flag set, and if it still reads 0 (flag 0, a party with none, or a derived flag) while the party's own gate, its flag A (`MapObject.Enabled`), holds (`MapObjects.GateHolds`, `0x800116AC`, over `0x800D9482` and `0x800D9484`, which `BattleSetup.FromMonsterParty` copies from the object's `+0x1C` and `+0x14`), the party remembered (`MapObjectMemory.Remember`, kind 5, three map loads: [saves](saves.md#map-objects-memory)); `Combat.SetKillFlags` again; the snapshot, black; `PauseMenu.PrepareLoot` (`0x80048FF8`), which hands the loot window the loot and the gold and **adds the gold to the party at once**; the experience pool to `PauseMenu.ExperiencePool` (`0x800D94D0`); and mode 6, the post-battle windows: pause states 5 (experience and training) and 6 (loot) ([ui](pause-menu.md#after-a-battle)). The loot window closes to trek (`PauseMenu.CloseToTrek`, `0x80048F40`), mode 1, built again at the kept place exactly and fading in at 1/30, as after fleeing. State 16 never runs for a win: the `Combat.NextState` 16 that `StateVictory` writes is never read.
  - **A loss** (state 13, `Combat.StateDefeat`, `0x80022590`), once faded out: `Combat.StartGameOverScript` (`0x8001F860`) heals the party's first member in full, finds the first script object in the Dreamscape's map (Borg9 `0xD5F`) whose script is `CodeTable.GameOverScenes`' first, `0x0512` (the other two are never read), starts it as using it would, and sets state 21 (`Combat.StateLeaveToScript`, `0x800227D4`), which returns mode `0xC`, the cut-scene. The script's ending kind 2 (`CodeTable.ScriptEndings`, `0x800DFE1C`, kept in `CutScene.EndingKind` `0x800DFE4C`) makes the cut-scene return mode 0, the title (`Title.Frame`, `0x8001B154`): nothing is saved or reloaded. A non-lethal battle (`Combat.NonLethal`, `0x800DA0DC`, which `Combat.Init` sets for encounter flag 978 alone) instead sets the encounter flag, remembers the npc or encounter fought with no gate check, sets the clock to 6:00:00 the next day whatever the hour (`World.SetDate`, `0x8007B914`, the day byte increased), and returns mode 1. The port's game over goes back to the title, its intro cards first, as the original's restart does ([ui](ui.md#coming-back-to-it)).
  - **Fleeing**: the pause menu's Flee, offered only in state 14 and while `0x800D9481` is set, with no confirmation, roll or line in the log (the combat strings' "The Party flees" is never read): it sets `Combat.NextState` to 20 (`pauseMenuSelectCallBack`, `0x800403AC`), and once the menu has rolled up, `Combat.StateLeaveFled` sets `Zones.Fled` (`0x800D9376`) and returns mode 1. `Zones.Fled` is read only by `InitZoneEngine` and cleared by `Zones.ResetBreadcrumbs`: fleeing costs nothing else, but the party isn't remembered and `Combat.Exit` takes the dead out as after any battle.
- **Back in trek.** As trek faded out, `TrekLoop.Free` kept where the player stood, his heading, the camera's eye and the chunk (`Zones.SavedPosition`, `0x800D906C`, 0x2C bytes). Trek is built again there (`InitGameTrek`, `0x80020F40`), the body put at the kept place exactly, not set down, its heading the kept one (both the body's `+0x20` and `+0x28`), and the camera's eye at the kept eye, aimed at the body (`Camera.SetEye`, `Camera.SetTarget`). Having fled, it's the **breadcrumb** farthest from there instead, which `InitZoneEngine` (`0x8000DF84`) copies over the kept place while `Zones.Fled` is set, so the heading and the camera's eye are the breadcrumb's too: each is the same 0x2C record (`SaveZoneEngine`, `0x8000E3D4`: the position, the heading at +0xC, the eye at +0x14, the chunk and the map set), and the checkpoints show the kept eye 5 to 6.5 behind the body along the heading. He faces as he did when the breadcrumb was kept, seen from behind as he was then, not from where the battle began. The port keeps the eye with each breadcrumb and puts the camera there (`Breadcrumb.Eye`, `TrekLoop.Resume`); every other return to trek, a win's among them, finds the camera where trek left it, which is the kept eye. The Goblin Ambush's end is the exception: `Combat.Exit` puts the eye at Oriana's hut with the body, and `TrekLoop.Keep` doesn't move the camera, which is left so because her cut-scene starts on trek's first frame. The breadcrumbs: trek's zone engine keeps where the player stands every 120 ticks, unless the nearest kept in the map set is under 10 away (the places within their chunks compared, as though in one), in a ring of 15 (`Zones.Breadcrumbs`, `0x800D90E0`; `HandleZoneEngineFrame`, `0x8000E784`), and `Zones.FindBreadcrumb` (`0x80009A48`) takes the farthest in the same map set, a chunk counting 100 a row or column, the last of equals. Every time trek is built, all 15 are where the player stands (`Zones.ResetBreadcrumbs`, `0x8000DF00`), so they aren't saved. Checked in checkpoints 01 and 02. The npc or encounter that started the battle is remembered only on a win; after fleeing it's back at its home node with every wanderer, and can hunt again.
  - **The Shadow merging**: state 17, whose first frame is `Combat.MergeShadow` (`0x800675C0`) and mode `0xE`, the Shadow's cinematic (record 1, `Cinematic.Load(1, 4, 1)`, [cinematics](cinematics.md)), played once combat is freed; then trek where the battle began. **Vanishing**: states 29 and 30, then trek where the battle began. Neither gives rewards or sets an encounter or kill flag ([The special battles](#the-special-battles)).

## A battle's own rules

1.0 keys a few battles' rules by their encounter flag: `Combat.ApplyFleeRuleByFlag` (ROM `0xD3288`, `0xD32A0`) has the last word on fleeing for 978 (forbids), 5250 and 3749 (allow), and `Combat.NonLethal` (`0x800DA0DC`) and `Combat.Exit`'s branch (`0x80023060`) give 978 the Goblin Ambush's ending. The flag is the one the battle's source gives (`BattleSetup.FromMonsterParty`, `encounterDat_func`): an encounter's flag B, an npc's flag C (its `battleFlag`), or a script's battle flag when a script object starts it. So the port puts the rules on the source, as `battleRules` (`BattleRules`, ADR 0035), and the importer writes them on every source of each flag: 978's on script `0x0365` (its battle flag) and the three npcs whose `battleFlag` is 978; 5250's, `flee: true`, on Lugash's script `0x04D1` (when the npc in the Barrows G03 talks, its battle is under the npc's flag 1011, as 1.0 has it, with no rule); 3749's, `flee: true`, on the Marquis's npc in the Overworld E23 (object 2). `BattleSetup.Rules` carries them: `WithParty` and `Against` take the party's, `Scripted` the script's, and the flee rule and the non-lethal end read them there.

## The arenas

The Battle arenas are `aidyn:mapset/16` ("Battlefields", 150 to a cell): 28 chunks, the arena number being the chunk's place in the map set's list (`Zones.ArenaChunks`, `0x800DCA74`, which the importer reads), but only 15 Borg9s. Chunks copied from one Borg9 are the same arena, terrain, scenes and music alike. The port's records name an arena by its chunk (`aidyn:chunk/16/E11`), never by its number (ADR 0035): a terrain's `arenas`, a script's `battle.arena` and `combat.defaultArena`. `Zones.SetMapSet` puts the zone engine's point at (20, 20) in it.

- **What they place:** reference points and up to 12 scenes, all always there (flag 0 inverted), some only with the Expansion Pak. No lights, sounds or other objects. The 46 × 46 arenas are a flat disc of 24 triangles; the scenery reaches far past the collision, to ±300 in I11.
- **Arenas 24 and 27's floor streaks past its rim** (M15 and M21, scene `0x3671`, one model, Borg2 13936, under terrains 14 and 13, whose skies aren't drawn). It's one wrapping material (flags `0x305C`, both wrap bits) with a 32 × 32 texture (Borg1 4894): an octagon of radius 23 lit by its vertex colours (white at the middle, 51 at the rim), and a skirt from the rim down to four corners about 250 out, black at the corners. The disc's texture coordinates are a plane in X and Z, about 7 units a tile in S and 4 in T, each triangle shifted by whole tiles. The skirt's corners would need 35 to 60 tiles, past the ±16 a vertex's 16 bits hold at the lists' 0.5 texture scale, and the data holds them wrapped by whole 32-tile turns, so its triangles squeeze and shear the texture into long streaks fading into the dark (`GeometryTests`). It's the ROM's own vertices, and 1.0 wraps the tile as the port does (`Scene.SetTextureTiles`, [rendering](rendering.md#wrapping)), so the original streaks the same.
- **The points, by name** (`FindReferencePointName`, `0x8000B7C8`, with the combat strings' `%s%u%c`, `good`, `bad` and `flee%d`): every arena has the 40 spawns `good0a`–`good4d` and `bad0a`–`bad4d`, and `flee0` up to `flee7`. Arenas 12 and 13's one, `flee0` at (48.7, 11.7), is past the 46-wide grid, so every flight there leaves at once ([combat-ai](combat-ai.md#fleeing)).

- **Falling out of an arena.** Every arena's floor is ringed by walls (the 46 × 46 ones by 16 see-through walls 21 tall; arena 17's are surface 2), so no floor edge or ledge leads out of one, and the drops inside the hilly ones (A01, C01, I01, C11, M13) land on the arena's own floor. - **Which spawn** (`Combat.FindSpawnPoint`, `0x8005C210`; `Combat.SpawnPosition`, `0x8005C294`): `%s%u%c`, the side's word, a set number and a letter. The party takes `good` and the enemies `bad`, swapped in an ambush (collision byte 2). Both sides start in set 4 − the animation byte, and reserves in set 0; in an ambush the party's set moves by the Ranger's roll (`Party.BattleAmbushRoll`, `0x80076C60`): the member, dead or not, with the strictly best modified Ranger who has the Stamina for it (5 less the Ranger, at least 1) rolls a d100 against Intelligence × 3 + Ranger × 10; failing, the set is 4 (3 with animation 3); under it, the margin's step (`Party.SkillCheckStep`) and the Stamina spent: step 1 moves it a set up (not with animation 0), 2 and 3 leave it, 4 or more a set down (not with animation 3); with no one to roll, no draw and no move. The spawn's x and z, truncated, are the combatant's cell (**checked**: Alaron's `bad2a` at (52.9, 52.3) is cell (52, 52)), and the body is made a unit up there and set down (mode 0). Set down on the floor, a body can move by float noise (32 to 31.999998), and its cell is read back from it.
- **Which way they face**: one draw each, as they're made: `Range(1, 3)` for the party, and for the enemies `Range(5, 7)` with collision byte 0, else `Range(1, 3)` too; the direction's vector is `Combat.DirectionVectors` (`0x800D27A8`, north (0, −1) round to north-west), and the body's heading is that way (the original's body field is its negation).
- **The ring's teardrops** are drawn by code, not placed (`CombatMarkers`, `combatmarkers.cpp`): a pool of 20 copies of `aidyn:scene/0x0794` (`CombatMarkers.Init`, `0x800854A8`, at `0x800E3138`, 0x1C each), a blended billboard about 0.41 × 1.0 with a scrolling texture (Borg1 1937); the code sets no scale, turn or scroll, and draws each with the zone's colours and fog (`Zones.SetSceneColors`). `CombatMarkers.Update` (`0x80085548`) works the radius out first, then lays the ring round where the combatant **began its turn** (`+0x08`), not where its body is: its radius R is the field's plus the body's scaled radius (`+0x34`), plus that again for one with an AI record; 6 teardrops up to R 3, 16 up to 7, else 20 (`CombatMarkers.CountFor`, `0x8008580C`, the floats at `0x800D43C8` and `0x800D43CC`); the first due +Z and the rest round toward −X by `(360 / N) × 0.017453`, the way round kept in two statics never reset (`0x800E3130`, `0x800E3134`, from (0, 1)), so each ring starts 0.006° further round than the last. Each is set on the ground as a body is (`CombatMarkers.RingPoint`, `0x80085240`): a sphere of 0.5 swept from 100 to −100 past see-through triangles (`Collision.SweepBlocked`) rests 0.01 above the first facing it. A miss tries again by calling itself with the try's number, up to 4 (`sltiu $s5, 4` at `0x80085380`): two draws on the one stream, an angle below 2π and a reach below 0.25, put the sweep at the ring's point plus (sin + 1) / 2 and (cos + 1) / 2 of the reach, so the retries don't add up and keep the first sweep's heights. One that misses all five sweeps keeps its point, and so takes eight draws; it then takes the body's height, and half way between its neighbours' once the ring is done. The ring is laid out only as a turn begins (and as the field follows a combatant near the Shadow), and drawn every frame (`CombatMarkers.Draw`, `0x80085848`, from `Combat.DrawFrame`) but from `Combatant.ExecuteAction` to the turn's end (`Combat.ActionActive`, `0x800DB0CC`) and from `Combat.StateVictory`; there's no fade in. **Checked** in 02–04, 09 and 10, every teardrop, and draw for draw in the parity replay's first rings ([combat-parity](combat-parity.md)).
- **The grid** (`ArenaGrid`, `0x800674A0`–`0x80068F7C`) is 100 × 100 cells of a byte a unit across in the arena chunk's own coordinates, index x × 100 + z, the height × 2 in the low bits and bit 7 for taken. `ArenaGrid.Init` (`0x80067960`) sizes it by the map's object grid (cell size × columns and rows, rounded half away from zero). `ArenaGrid.Fill` (`0x8005E900`) sweeps a sphere of 0.5 from 100 down to −100 through each cell's centre over the arena chunk's collision, see-through triangles included, the first facing it in the grid's order: the byte is twice the height it touches, truncated, and a miss is taken (0x80). Each combatant takes its footprint (`Occupy`, `0x8006820C`: from its cell on in +x and +z, none if any cell is outside), twice its scaled radius rounded half away from zero, at least 1 (the party 1, rats 2). **Checked** against checkpoint 02's. `WalkClear` (`0x80067E6C`) is a line over free cells with steps under 2; `HasSight` (`0x800684C4`) is the line in 3D for ranged attacks and spells, through the collision and the bodies between; `FindPath` (`0x80068CD4`) is a greedy walk of at most the grid's diagonal, which `StraightenPath` trims.

## A turn

1. **It ends** (`Combatant.EndTurn`, `0x80061300`): `CombatCamera.Reset` (`0x8008D210`, a glide stopped), the AI's target forgotten (`EnemyAI.ClearTarget`, `0x8005B998`), the cast chosen cancelled (the entity's `+0x3C` to `0xFF`; the selected spell stays), the acted flag cleared, a scroll or wand whose selected spell has no charges left taken off and gone (the entity's `+0x3D` of 5 with an item of type `0x11` or `0x0D`, `Entity.UnequipGear` `0x8006F5C4`) and no spell selected, state 1 and the ring shown again (`Combat.ActionActive` 0), the body asked to stand, the action put back (`Combatant.RevertAction`, `0x8006679C`) and with a flask in hand the weapon stowed (`Party.StowHeldItem`, `0x800763B0`), the last target forgotten if it's dead, then `TurnOrder.Advance`. Every end of a turn comes here: the skip, the action's result (state 4, 60 ticks on), the AI's rest, a weapon or flask taken in hand in the pause menu (19), Sense Aura's popup dismissed, and the AI's walk or turn with nothing to do (5, 6).
2. **The next** (`TurnOrder.Advance`, `0x8006AC4C`): nothing once the battle's won or lost (states 12, 13); the party's first member dead (`*GameState.Party`, not `Combat.AlaronSlot`) loses it (`Combat.Defeat`, `0x80023F98`: state 13 and the fade, or 28 in a non-lethal battle); the last combatant's body let go of its field (`TurnOrder.StopBody`, `0x8006A5C4`); then on through the round, its end building the next (step 7). Before each candidate the battle's end is checked (`TurnOrder.CheckEnd`, `0x8006A97C`, over `TurnOrder.CountActive`, `0x8006A880`, a reserve still to come counting as an enemy), and the candidate's radius, place and whether it can act are worked out (`Combatant.UpdateActive`, `0x8006615C`: not with no Intelligence, Willpower, Dexterity or Strength, nor immobilised), skipping it dead, unable or gone. Then the focus is on it, every combatant reads its body (`TurnOrder.ClearActions`, `0x8006AE64`, clearing the numbers to show), its turn begins, the clock moves on 600 (`World.AdvanceTurn`, `0x8007C01C`: `World.WrapYear` and `World.Update`, so the weather rolls if the part of the day changed; it ages no effects; **checked**, +600 a turn in 02–04), and a reserve that fled on arriving says "Enemy reinforcements flee" (`+0x543C`).
3. **It begins** (`TurnOrder.BeginTurn`, `0x8006A9C8`): the camera's given to its body (`GiveCameraToPlayer`, `0x80015E30`, which drops an R orbit) and anchored there; its line, "%s begins action", a newcomer's "joins the battle" (`0x7C`, clearing flag 10), or either overwritten by a performer's (`0xD0`); it reads its body (`Combatant.SyncFromBody`) and keeps where it starts (`SaveStartPosition`); the ring (which works the radius out again); its field (`Combatant.PlaceRingAndBody`, `0x8006627C`); then for a combatant with an AI record, its check to flee (`EnemyAI.CheckFlee`, `0x800572AC`, [combat-ai](combat-ai.md#morale)) and state 8, otherwise a flask or spell carried over looked up and state 1, as `State` and `NextState` both, after the target sets are built. The Shadow's aura is the round's, not the turn's, and nothing sets a body down: bodies are set down once, as they're made.
4. **The field's radius** (`+0x21`, `Combatant.UpdateRadius`, `0x8005F29C`) is the first place in `Combat.RadiusByDexterity` (`0x800E0C20`: 0, 0, 0, 3, 7, 12, 16, 21, 26, 28, 30, 33, 36, 38) whose byte reaches the **modified** Dexterity, 14 past it (3 for 1–3, 4 for 4–7, … 13 for 37–38). Then each timed effect in its slot's order: Haste (kind `0x1E`) adds its level, and Stellar Gravity (`0x38`) takes its level away, leaving 1 if that's all or more; an effect's kind is its spell ID's low byte's place in the code's spell list (`0x800E2B40`). Then **1 at most while engaged**: an opponent alive and able within the larger of each body's radii, scaled (`+0x34`) or not (`+0x38`), plus 0.75 (`0x800D2810`), across the ground, both bodies read again first (`Combatant.IsWithinReach`, `0x8006011C`, from `IsFreeOfOpponents`, `0x8005F1B8`); this sets or clears flag 7 and stops a performance. Not engaged, 1 at most while performing, or 1 near the Shadow (`Combat.NearShadow`, `0x80023F34`: within 10, strictly, which logs the vortex line on the current one's). Then 13 at most, and 0 for Pochanargat (entity list place `0xAB`, `0x800E28EB`). It's worked out as each body is made, before each candidate in `Advance`, with each ring, and in the Shadow's battle as the combatant walks, so flag 7 is as old as that: **checked** in 09, where Brenna is engaged with a rat whose own flag is clear until its turn (10).
5. **The player** moves the body with trek's stick handler (`Body.ApplyInput`, `0x80014FEC`, fed each poll): R orbits the camera as in trek, and otherwise up on the stick is the way the camera looks, walking, running from 0.8 or sneaking with Z held, a party member turning 10° a poll. A stick held just under full can waver about 0.8, the body's request flicking between walk and run (2 and 3) and the walk slowing to about half for a moment: the user's stalls of Nov 10 and 11, a controller not pressed fully. The threshold is 1.0's, kept without a margin. The field holds it: `Body.SetGoal` (`0x80015BD4`) keeps a centre (the combatant's place and the body's height as its turn began, `+0x274`) and a radius (`+0x280`) with body flag 4, and `ProcessPlayers` (`Body.ClampToGoal`, `0x80014518`) puts the body's X and Z back on that **sphere** after it moves, before the bodies push each other, whenever it's at the radius or past it in 3D. So it slides round the edge, and on a slope reaches a little less far across the ground. **Checked**: 03's walked bodies are 10.0000 from their centres in 3D. Every body gets its field as it's made, about where it stands, and loses it as its turn ends. Nothing in the grid stops a body; the bodies' push does (`Crowd`). After each poll the combatant reads its body (`Combatant.UpdateFromBody`, `0x8005F784`): its footprint leaves the grid and takes it again where the body stands (`ArenaGrid.Vacate`, `0x800682E0`, and `Occupy`, the cell rounded half away from zero), its place is the body's X and Z exactly, the focus follows and its facing is the body's; a change counts a move (`+0x22`), the fourth sets flag 3 (**checked** in 02–04), and the target sets are built again. The radius isn't worked out again as it walks up to an enemy: `Combat.MovePlayerBody` (`0x80084CE0`) does that only near the Shadow, centring the field on the body.
6. **The action** ends the turn: an attack, melee or ranged by the weapon (`Combatant.ResolveAction`, `0x80065ACC`); a flask thrown or a potion drunk (action 3: `ThrowFlask` `0x80065600`, `DrinkPotion` `0x80064C84`); a spell (`CastCurrentSpell`, `0x80065C08`); Lay On Hands or Heal With Herbs (`ResolveHealer`, `0x8006591C`); the Troubadour (`StartTroubadour`, `0x800662F4`); a weapon changed in the pause menu; or a skip. `Combatant.ExecuteAction` (`0x80065F6C`) ends a performance, hides the ring, carries it out, applies the damage, checks for death (`CheckDeath`, `0x80065E98`) and starts state 2. Each is [combat-actions](combat-actions.md)'s.
7. **The round ends** (`TurnOrder.EndRound`, `0x8006A62C`) when the list runs out: the Shadow's aura first (`Combat.ShadowAura`, `0x80023AD0`, which can end the battle); each performer pays a Stamina point or stops with none; every combatant's moves and flag 3 are cleared and its effects aged a round (`Character.AgeEffects`, `0x80071444`: the potions', the timed spells', and what they do, [magic](magic.md#effects-on-a-character)); the reserves come in (below); the toggle flips and the next list is built. The leader's morale moves at each of the AI's turns, not here. Wins and losses are only ever found at a turn's change, not at a death.

**Turn order** (`TurnOrder.Build`, `0x8006A16C`). A combatant's turns a round are by its **base** Dexterity (`Combatant.TurnsPerRound`, `0x8005F4D0`): 0 none, 1–17 one, 18–24 one and two by turns (the toggle), 25–29 two, 30–34 two and three by turns, 35 and up three. The list is grouped: every third turn first, then every second, then every first, each group in random order (`Rng.Range`, drawing again on a repeat). While the toggle is set, a combatant with no turns gets one, in a group drawn before the thirds, so a base Dexterity of 0 acts first every other round. Only those able to act (flag 1) have turns, but the active counts (`+0x02`, `+0x04`, `+0x06`) count every combatant alive and not gone. The base is read capped at 30 (`Stats.Base`), so 35's three turns are never reached. Checked in 02: four party members and four rats gave `[1,3,0,2, 3,2,1,5,4,7,0,6]`. Combat parity's formula vectors (`TurnsVectorTests`) run the radius, the turns and the order against 1.0's code at every step ([combat-parity](combat-parity.md#the-formula-vectors)). They show too that the radius reads the modified Dexterity as signed, so 128 and above are 0, and that Haste's level wraps the radius's byte before the cap: a radius of 9 with a Haste of 250 is 3. Neither is reachable without a mod.

**Win and loss** (`TurnOrder.CheckEnd`, `0x8006A97C`): no party member active is a loss (`TurnOrder.Defeat`, `0x8006A780`: state 13 and `Combat.Defeat`, then with a current combatant the party's line, `0xD4`, and in a non-lethal battle Alaron's own over it for good, `0x74`, his portrait locked), and no enemy active with no reserve left a win (`TurnOrder.Victory`, `0x8006A804`: `Combat.SetKillFlags` `0x80067860`, `Combat.TallyRewards` `0x8005E2AC`, state 12, the fade out at the battle's 1/60, and with a current combatant the victory line but under encounter flag 978). Alaron's death is a loss alone (`TurnOrder.Advance`, straight to `Combat.Defeat`, with no line). `Combat.Defeat` (`0x80023F98`) is state 13 and the fade out (`Combat.EnterDefeat`, `0x80023FD0`), or in a non-lethal battle state 28 (`Combat.EnterDefeatWait`, `0x80024014`).

**The kill flags** (`Combat.SetKillFlags`) read the enemy list's first four entries, stopping at the first empty, and set the flag of each boss among them (a jump table at `0x800D2B60`, by entity list place from `0xA8`): Kitarak 154, Pochanargat 3204, Shamsuk 458, Ksathra 3738, Shatrevar 3737, Mehrdad 3736, Golnar 3735, Nasim 3734, Behrooz 3733, Assim 3739 and Lugash 533; the Lizard Man Boss, the Marquis, the Shadow, Sheridan and four more set none. Whether the boss fell or fled doesn't matter, and script battles count. It runs twice a win (`TurnOrder.Victory` and `StateVictory`) and never on a loss or a flight. Its writes don't bring the flag network up to date, and the only read after the first is `StateVictory`'s of the party's gate, so a boss's journal entry is told when something next reads a derived flag: as trek comes back where loading the chunks does, else later. Kitarak's waits in his room until its chest, the journal or leaving; Lugash's shows at once on the return to trek. The port's is told as trek fades in, the user's choice ([flags](flags.md#when-the-network-comes-up-to-date)).

**Rewards** (`Combat.TallyRewards`), as the battle is won, before the fade: the loot inventory (`+0x4FB4`, 64 stacks) and the pools are made; for **every** entry of the enemy list, killed, fled or never come alike, its experience (the entity's `+0x7C`, the DB's reward byte × 50: a rat 100) and its chest (the entity's `+0x7E`, the chest DB's ID `0x7FF` + the byte, through `Combat.AddChestLoot` `0x8005E0F0`); then the bonus loot (`Combat.AddBonusLoot`, `0x8005E238`: a chest if its type is 8, rolled with no entity, else one of the item, worth no experience); then each living member gains the whole experience pool (`Character.AddExp`, `0x80071528`, times 1.5: dead members gain nothing, and an ally in the enemy list gives nothing); and last the gold pool is **doubled** (`sll` at `0x8005E400`; the port multiplies it by the combat record's `lootGoldMultiplier`, 2, which a container's roll reads too, since doubling the bounds instead would roll another range). A chest rolls, in this order, on the one stream: its gold (`Range` between its bounds, drawing nothing when they're equal); the enemy's own armor and shield, each by the chest's chance (`RollD(1, 100)`) if it has them, and its three weapons, slot by slot (`joins`, `spare`, `fights`, each with the loot record's chance for the slot), each only of a weapon skill whose weapons drop (`Combat.DropWeaponClasses`, `0x800D26C8`: Hafted, Missile, Pole, Sword and Thrown; the weapon skill's `drops`) (`Combat.RollEquipmentDrops`, `0x8005DC78`); a reagent by its chance, then which (`Range(30, 32)`: spice, herb or gemstone) and how many; then the six drops (`Combat.RollChestItem`, `0x8005DF38`), the first two between their counts. The port's loot tables hold the drops that have an item, each with its count, which is 1 for 1.0's last four and draws nothing ([items](items.md#the-records)). **What's looted adds to the experience pool**: a weapon its `ExpModifier`, armor its `ExpBonus`, another item its `ExpMultiplier` (the reagents 5, the Sapphire Gem 500), a potion nothing; a reagent once whatever the count, a drop times its count. A drop's worth goes by its ID's type byte: 7 the weapon table's, 5 and 6 the armor table's, 1, 9–15 and 17–19 the item table's (`+0x18`), and any other (the potions' 0x10 among them) nothing. The enemy's armor and shield are rolled unless they're `0xFFFF`, and a weapon unless it's 0 or `0xFFFF`, its class checked before the roll, so one that doesn't drop draws nothing. The loot (`+0x4FB4`) keeps its stacks from `+0x04`, 0x18 each: the ID at `+0x00` and the count at `+0x14`, an item added again joining its stack. **Checked**: the original's `TallyRewards`, run on checkpoint 11's RAM, gives 12's pool of 1000 and +1500 to each member, and Lugash's win in the parity logs its loot and 8257 experience; combat parity's formula vectors (`RewardsVectorTests`) check the port's against it over every kind of chest ([combat-parity](combat-parity.md#the-formula-vectors)).

**Reserves** (`Combat.SpawnReserves`, `0x8005D914`, at a round's end after the effects age; running it on 08 gives 09's cells). It refills **every** enemy slot whose combatant is dead or gone, or that's empty, from the party's size to `+0x01`, while the list has a next (`+0x07`):

- A reserve that flees as it arrives (`EnemyAI.ReserveFleesOnArrival`, [combat-ai](combat-ai.md#reserves-that-flee-as-they-arrive)) is marked and skipped, its slot left empty, with the hold's line.
- The leader's slot taken sets `+0x0C`.
- The newcomer stands at `Combat.ChooseSpawn` (`0x8005D748`, through `SpawnPosition`: one draw for its direction, `Range(5, 7)` with collision byte 0, else `Range(1, 3)`; set 0, the enemies' side, the slot's letter, `a` + slot − party size), or if that cell is taken, or a body's centre is within its radius and the newcomer's, the first free and clear point of sets 0–4 in turn (`Combat.FindFreeSpawn`, `0x8005D5B0`; `Combat.SpotIsClear`, `0x8005D474`), each set's `bad` letters a–d first, then its `good` ones, ambush or not: `FindFreeSpawn` picks the side by its loop's count xor a flag that the reserves' one call (`0x8005DB4C`) passes as 0. So in an ambush, where the party stands on `bad` points, a reserve whose own point is taken comes in on the party's side; the port's comes in on its own ([Where the port differs](#where-the-port-differs)). A point is free if `ArenaGrid.IsOccupied` finds none of its footprint's cells taken (the slot's own point by its truncated cell, a fallback by its cell rounded), and clear if every combatant with a body is further than its scaled radius and the newcomer's from it, strictly (a fallback's raw point, the slot's own by its truncated cell); a dead combatant's body counts until it's freed. With no point free, it keeps its own.
- Its body, radius, field, portrait and action are made as `Combat.CreateBody` makes them, with flag `0x400`, and `+0x07` moves on. The counts aren't changed: `CountActive` finds the newcomers at the next turn's change. Checkpoint 09's newcomers walking is their own AI turn, not the spawn's.

### Deaths

- **`Combatant.Death`** (`0x80062080`), called by `CheckDeath` as the killing blow is resolved, before it lands ([combat-actions](combat-actions.md#an-attack)): Sholeh's 622 set first, and a member's died flags ([characters](characters.md#deaths)); the immobilising effects come off, its footprint leaves the grid, the active counts fall (`+0x02`, and `+0x04` or `+0x06`), a party member stops performing, and the first enemy's death sets `+0x08`. It doesn't set flag 6 or free the body: a dead combatant is known by its hit points. A death from the effects at a round's end (`Entity.ShowAgeResult`, `0x8007138C`) shows the number, then `Death` and `FreeBody` at once: no fall, no line, no wait, the body fading out standing as it was.
- **A freed body's fade** (`Body.FadeOut`, `0x80014AD4`, which `Combat.FreeBody` calls for every body it frees and `Combatant.Leave`, doing the same work inline, for a fled one): body flag `0x100` and 120 ticks at `+0x708`. `Combat.Bodies[slot]` is emptied, but the body stays in the zone's bodies where it lay or stood, and `ProcessPlayers` (`0x80012CA4`–`0x80012D40`) counts it down by the frame's ticks before the body moves: under 1, its alpha (`+0x70C`) and flags are cleared and it's freed (`FreePlayer`, `0x800146E0`); under 60 (`Body.FadeSpan`, `0x800CDB2C`), its alpha is `(u8)(left / 60 × 255)`. So it's drawn whole for the first second and fades to nothing over the next, linearly, by alpha alone; the body's alpha caps its distance's fade and its shadow's (`BodyShadow.DrawAlpha`, `0x800122C4`). While the flag is up its actor isn't stepped (`0x800130F0`), so a dead body keeps the last frame of its fall and a fled one the pose it fled in. It still pushes and is pushed as any body (the push skips only a body that's off or flagged `0x400`). A party member who dies is freed the same way, by the same `RemoveFallen` (it tests only `Character.IsDead`, `0x8006EA54`), and fades out as an enemy does; nothing keeps a dead member's body on the field. Alaron's keel-over in the Goblin Ambush isn't a death (he's left with some 45 hit points), so his body stays down at its fall's last frame. Checkpoint 06 holds rat 7 mid-fall in state 4 (`+0x18` 9, no fade, alpha 255); none catches a body fading.
- **The bodies' flags** that combat touches: `0x400` marks a reserve just come (`Combat.CreateBody`), which the push skips; `0x800` is trek's talker's (set by conversations at `0x800508D0` and `0x80051B64`), which has `ProcessPlayers` add the body to the follow camera's points (`Body.AddToFollowList`, `0x80015FEC`); `0x200` is set on every body not the party's by `Combat.CreateBody` and read by nothing. `TurnOrder.ClearActions` (`0x8006AE64`) clears `0xC00` on every combat body at each turn's change. The port has the first as `CombatantFlags.Newcomer` and needs neither of the others.
- **The died flags and the dead leaving the party** (`Party.AfterBattle`, from `Combat.Exit`, after every battle) are [characters](characters.md#deaths)'. Alaron reaches it healed: the game over heals him first.

## The special battles

Read from 1.0's code: no checkpoint holds either fight. **The Shadow** (entity `0x0226`, place `0xAC`). The body it aims at is the camera's owner, which `GiveCameraToPlayer` makes the current combatant's at a turn's start and the cursor's target in states 9 and 10. States 1, 5, 8, 24 and 26 have no branch of their own: the hold on an enemy's turn is by whose turn it is. R orbits it in state 1 as in trek.
- **Random numbers** are the one stream (`Rng.Range` `0x80093214`, its state at `0x800D8088`): the turn order, d100s, damage dice, 2d6 for morale and casting, and loot. Besides the battle's own draws, `Weather.DriftWind` (`0x8007BAA0`, from `Combat.AfterFrame` through `World.SetTerrain` and `World.Update`) draws 2 every drawn frame, plus 3 with chance 0.05 and 1 more with 0.05, which accounts for every draw from checkpoint 04 to 05; the ring's retries, the particles, the sky and the spells' visuals draw on it too. The checkpoints hold the stream's state, so a battle's draws can be replayed from one ([combat-parity](combat-parity.md)).

## What it shares with trek

- **The zone engine**, in mode 1, framed each combat frame (`Combat.DrawFrame`) and freed after: the chunks, collision, scenes, lights, sky, rain and the terrain (`World.SetTerrain` every frame), with trek built again after; the port's arena is a stage of trek's `ZoneEngine`, as a cut-scene's is.
- **Bodies**: `AllocPlayer` and `FreePlayer`, `Body.SetDown`, `Body.Face`, the player handler with a goal for the AI's walks, shadows to 128, and the actors' requests (7 hit, 9 death).
- **The UI**: `TextBand`, the pause menu in modes 3, 5 and 6 (the flee option only in state 14), `MenuTween`.
- **Particles and lights**: actors' emitters, the party lit by the map's lights (`+0x70A`, [rendering](rendering.md)).
- **Scripts and flags**: the game over scripts, the encounter, boss and Sholeh's flags, the beaten npc or encounter remembered, the Shadow's cinematic, the battle music with the map set.

**The combat strings** (String/Combat, 64 lines, [text](../formats/text.md)) are taken by byte offset: `0x00` "begins action", `0x04` the party flees, `0x08` victorious, `0x10`–`0x1C` healing, `0x20`–`0x38` avoid, attack, hit, critical, miss, `0x3C`–`0x4C` potions, `0x50` killed, `0x58`–`0x70` spells, `0x74` the Shadow, `0x78` flees, `0x7C` joins, `0x80` the vortex, `0x84` rests, `0x88` changes weapons, `0x8C`–`0xBC` a combatant's details (nothing in combat's files shows them), `0xC0`–`0xD0` performing, `0xD4` the party incapacitated, `0xD8` `flee%d`, `0xDC` `%s%u%c`, `0xE8` banished, `0xEC` "Enemy reinforcements flee", `0xF0` the Shadow dissipates, `0xF4` and `0xF8` `good` and `bad`, `0xFC` "cheats death".

## The port

`HBHT.Core.Combat` ports everything above and in the other combat docs. Its methods mostly carry the original's names, so a function cited is usually found by that name in the file below that ports its part. The runtime draws what the battle holds and carries out what it asks ([runtime](runtime.md), [ui](ui.md#the-battle-screen)): a `CombatantView` for each body, `CombatNumbersView`, `MissileView`, `SpellFxView`, `AimMarkerView`, `CombatRingView` and the battle screen's `CombatGuiView`.

**The battle asks the views nothing that changes its ticks** (for Start Early, [below](#drawn-from-a-recording)), so a battle stepped with no runtime, as the tests step it, plays tick for tick as the runtime's, and a later stage can run it ahead of the drawing. `Battle.Tick` takes the arena and the controls, and nothing else:
- **The combatants' actors are the core's.** `Battle.Make` gives each its motion as it joins (`Combatant.Actor`, `ActorMotion.AsDrawn`), for a scene with animations, moving or still, as the views give an animator to, rolling on the bodies' stream (`RandomStreams.Animation`) in the crowd's order as the views' did; one with none steps still (`Combatant.Motion`). `CombatantView` poses its scene where the recording has the motion on the tick drawn (below).
- **The hand's locator is the core's** (`Combatant.LocatorAt`): the actor's scene posed from its glTF where its motion stands (`ActorMotion.Locator`, `SceneSkeleton` in `HBHT.Plugins.Assets`, [actors](../formats/actors.md)), scaled, turned to the body's heading and stood at its feet. Logged in the runtime against `CombatantView.Locator` over a battle of four Skeleton Archers, every locator of every standing body agreed within 0.0004 units. So a missile's flight, and the ticks it takes, are the same with a view or without; headless it used to leave the body's middle.
- **A spell's visual's actor is the core's** (`SpellFxVisual.Actor`), made as its start is cued, on a stream of its own from 0 as `Models.InstantiateActor`'s was, so its strike (`SpellFxCast.Struck`) comes on the same tick headless; headless it used to strike at once. Its emitters run on a particle head of `SpellFx`'s own, with an anchor that poses the visual's and its two bodies' locators as the views do, and `SpellFx.Emitting` asks that head whether they're done. What `Emitting` gates is the visual's `Finished` alone, which frees it (`SpellVisualEnded`), its cast's entry in pool A and its bodies' entries in pool C. No rule waits for that: the battle waits for `Struck` only, a hold lets its pose go as its effect ends, and Darkness and Light hold the time until their effect ends whatever their visuals do (`SpellFxCast.HoldsTime`). The pool C order the lasting scenes take turns in is the one thing a visual's end moves, and that's drawing too. The core's head has its own 16 emitters and 384 particles, where the runtime's shares them with the weather and the bodies, and it frames from the battle's first tick, where the runtime's frames on its own phase, so a visual can be freed a tick apart from where the runtime's particles would have died, or later where the runtime's pool was full; only the drawing sees it.
- **The bodies' sounds go through `ISoundOutput`** (`AudioKeys.Output`), which a recording can take, and whether a map's or the arena's sound plays on is kept by the game's ticks, not the mixer ([audio](audio.md#what-the-game-hears-back)).
- **The sky's dimming** for a cast is drawn unless `--spell-fx off` (`GameSession.SpellFxDrawn`); the visuals play in the core either way, so the baseline no longer changes when a spell lands.

### Drawn from a recording

A battle is drawn from a recording of it, under both settings of Combat Turns: the views never read the core's live objects, so a battle is drawn one way.

Combat Turns' Start Early, an enhancement the user asked for after Ogre Battle 64, starts the next combatant while the last action is still being drawn. The core still steps the turns one after another, as Original does, and only the drawing overlaps. Stepping them at once would change the rules, because a turn's ticks feed them:
- every body's actor rolls on one shared stream at each state's end, and those rolls decide fidgets and refused runs;
- the stuck test counts over a body's root motion;
- a missile's flight time follows from where it leaves the hand;
- the AI chooses by where the other bodies stand.

So the battle runs ahead without drawing, and a wrong overlap only ever shows in the drawing.

- **`BattleRecorder`** is the battle's host (`Battle.Host`) and the bodies' sound output (`AudioKeys.Output`, set on the arena's keys for the battle). It keeps each tick's cues, its sound calls (`SoundCall`: start, set, duration, stop, one-shot, each under a handle of its own) and the emitters the combatants' and the spells' visuals' actors start (`EmitterStart`, heard through `ActorMotion.Starts`, which it sets as each joins or starts; a visual's first request's start is `Made` at once, as the view used to). After each tick `Sample` makes a `BattleFrame` of those and of everything the views show: each body on the field (`DrawnBody`: its centre, heading, movement, alpha, tint, fog, its actor's state and clock, what it holds), the missile (`DrawnMissile`), the visuals (`DrawnVisual`, with their actors' state and clock), the numbers (`DrawnNumber`, by how long each has shown), the ring and whether it shows, the aim, the fade, whether the music waits, the sky's dimming, the Wind's fog, the camera (`DrawnCamera`), the battle screen (`CombatGui.Snapshot`, a `CombatGuiFrame` laid out in the original's frame, which `Draw` places on a screen of any size), the held menu and Sense Aura's popup. Whether a sound it took plays on is answered from its own book in the battle's ticks; nothing a battle steps reads it back.
- **`BattleDrawing`** steps the battle and draws one frame a tick: it hands the frame's cues to the session's host, plays its sounds (each of the recorder's handles mapped to the audio's), and moves its own camera (`BattleDrawing.Camera`), which the arena (`ZoneEngine.Camera`) and the runtime's camera follow. Then the arena's music hold, map sounds and weather step at the drawing's pace by that camera ([audio](audio.md#bodies-sounds)): they're out of `Battle.Tick`. The session holds it as `GameSession.Drawing`, and the runtime draws `Drawing.Frame` alone: each `CombatantView` by its `DrawnBody` (`ActorAnimator.Follow(state, clock)`), first starting the emitters the frame says its actor started, which are the view's own (`ActorAnimator.Recorded`), never the core motion's; `SpellFxView`, the numbers, the missile, the ring, the aim, the battle screen, the fade, the lights and the sky's dimming the same way ([runtime](runtime.md#what-it-draws)).
- **Under Original** each tick's frame is drawn on the tick it's made. A movie of the battle at F09/3, 400 frames, is pixel for pixel the same as before the recording.
- **Under Start Early** the battle runs ahead while it doesn't wait on the player (`Battle.WaitsOnPlayer`: a turn the player plays, from its walk and aim to its held menus and the Troubadour's prompt; the pause; Sense Aura's popup; the battle's end), as many ticks a tick as `BattleDrawing.Budget` (3 ms) allows, up to 600 a tick and five minutes ahead, and the drawing plays its actions back, overlapping where they share nothing ([below](#overlapping-the-ais-turns)). A tick that finds the drawing caught up steps the battle once, so the drawing never waits. Once the battle waits on the player it's stepped no further till the drawing reaches it: a turn the player plays opens once its combatant is free in the drawing and is then stepped with the drawing on the present while earlier actions draw on behind it ([below](#opening-the-players-turns-early)); the battle's end (a victory, a defeat, the Shadow's, a flee) is returned to the session only once the drawing has caught up. The reinforcements' held line isn't a wait: it holds for a second by itself. The intro's fade runs ahead too.
- **Presses under Start Early** go to the battle once it takes them (`BattleDrawing.TakesPresses`): the drawing has caught up, or the player's turn is drawn up to the present (`BattleComposer.AtPresent`) and its aim isn't held ([below](#pacing-beats-and-the-players-wait)). Start, whenever the drawing hasn't caught up, fast-forwards every action drawing to the battle's present (`FastForward`: every frame's cues go to the host, so views come and go, but their sounds and emitters don't start, `BattleFrame.SkippedSounds` and `SkippedEmitters`; a sound's stop still goes through) and then goes to the battle, since the pause menu's Party tab reads the live sheets. Before the battle takes presses, A skips what holds the player, the user's choice: while the aim is held, what it could pick (`BattleDrawing.AimHeld`); while the player's turn waits to open, whatever holds it (`BattleComposer.SkipHold`); else, while any action drawing is an AI's walk (`BattleDrawing.Walking`), only the walks drawing, to their ends (`BattleComposer.SkipWalks`, [below](#opening-the-players-turns-early)). The rest wait. `Battle.SkipWalk` is Original's alone (`Battle.Turns`), so 1.0's skip, which frees a stuck body, never reaches a battle under Start Early.
- **The battle's ticks are Original's either way.** The battle isn't stepped in a turn the player plays till the turn is drawn up to it, so a player's press lands on the same tick of the battle under both settings, and A in a walk changes only the drawing; so the rules, their order and their random numbers are Original's. `StartEarlyTests` plays five battles both ways (checkpoint 01's rats with their reserves joining, setup 2's casters, setup 8's Skeleton Archers, setup 10's Trolls' defeat, and fleeing setup 4's bandits by the pause) and finds the same recorded frames tick for tick, the same log, health, cells, places, outcome and streams. The cosmetic stream is left out: the arena's sounds and weather draw on it at the drawing's pace, which Start Early runs the battle ahead of, so which of a key's sounds plays and where a teardrop of the ring settles can differ, which no rule reads.
- **What's still live**: the held menu is the battle's own (one is only up in a turn the player plays, drawn on the present); the pause menu's Party tab and the debug keys (B held, F10, F11) act on the live battle; the debug overlay prints the live battle, which runs ahead of the picture under Start Early.

#### Overlapping the AI's turns

Under Start Early the recording is drawn as actions that overlap where they share nothing. The core is unchanged: only how the frames are drawn. Under Original the drawing is the battle's own frame each tick, and a movie of the battle at F09/3 is pixel for pixel stage 2's.

- **Actions** (`BattleActions`, `DrawnAction`). The recording is cut into actions, one a turn, where `BattleFrame.Current` changes (`BeginTurn` sets it and writes the turn's line the same tick); the intro is an action with no combatant. A combatant that took two turns in a row would be one action, which only overlaps less.
- **Episodes** (`Episode`): which ticks of an action involve which body. A body is involved on a tick it moves (its centre more than `MoveEpsilon`, 0.004, from the tick before: the crowd's push nudges bodies by less), turns, plays outside its actor's idle loop (the states its first state reaches by request 0 and any request; a frozen pose doesn't count), changes its alpha, what it holds or its hit points, a cue names it (a request, a death, its joining or going, a number over it, a spell's visual on it or aimed at it), or a spell's actor on it hasn't played out or one aimed at it hasn't struck. An action's own combatant is involved on every tick of it. Involved ticks gather into episodes, gaps of up to 8 ticks bridged. A cue or the turn starts a new episode in the action whose frame it's on; involvement with no cue carries the body's last episode on past its action's end, for up to `TailCap` (180) ticks, so a body falling and fading as the next turn begins is drawn at the pace its fall began at. A Fireball's targets each have an episode from its visual's start to its strike and their hit or resist; a body it didn't touch has none.
- **Scheduling** (`BattleComposer.Search`). Actions start in the battle's order, never before the action before or the present drawn tick, and only once their ticks are settled: their turn over and 180 ticks recorded after it, or the battle waiting on the player. An action starts on the earliest drawn tick where both hold:
  - every body it involves has finished drawing its earlier episodes before its episode in this action begins: a body is released once its own part ends, not when the whole earlier action does;
  - nothing it draws crosses another's path. Its bodies (in or resting after one of its episodes), its missiles and its spells' actors (a capsule from the visual to its other body until it has played) are checked against everything else drawn then (the other actions' shapes and the idle bodies): two shapes drawn from different ticks may not come within their radii and 0.05 of each other nearer than both their ticks recorded them, less 0.02. Two walkers whose paths cross go one after the other, the second once the first has cleared the crossing;
  - none of its beats plays alongside an earlier action's unless one shot can hold them all ([below](#pacing-beats-and-the-players-wait)).

  The search precomputes the other shapes a drawn tick at a time and the action's own a tick at a time, leaves out pairs whose paths never come near or that both stand where both recordings have them, and tries delays in order; it spends at most 2 ms a drawn tick and goes on the next tick from the starts not yet passed, and past 1,200 ticks the action waits for every earlier one. A turn the player plays opens by its own rule ([below](#opening-the-players-turns-early)); any other action not settled (one the run-ahead's budget cut short, or the one the battle ends in) starts only once nothing else has ticks before it left to draw on an earlier drawn tick, everything put at its first tick first.
- **Composing a drawn tick** (`BattleComposer.Next`). An action's timeline runs from its start a tick a drawn tick, on past its last tick while what it began lives. A body is drawn from the tick of the action it's in an episode of; between episodes it idles on its own clock, a tick a drawn tick, never past its next episode's first tick or the newest action's tick, so its drawn tick never goes back. Where it meets its next episode from another tick, the view blends its pose over 8 ticks (`BattleFrame.Jumped`, `ActorAnimator.BlendFrom`); a node scaled to nothing takes its new pose at once. Numbers, missiles and spells' actors are drawn from the action that began them, a lasting scene over a head with its body; several missiles may fly at once (`BattleFrame.Missiles`). Once the newest action reaches the battle's present, every timeline is put there.
- **Each one-off plays once.** Every cue, sound call and emitter start is on a timeline: a body's (its cues, its keys' sounds by `AudioKeys.Keying`, its actor's emitters, the lasting scene over it) or an action's (what it began, the log's lines, the pause, the end, a sound no body's keys made, which keeps the timeline its start had). As each timeline passes a tick, what's on it there plays, in the recording's order.
- **The battle's own** come from the newest action drawing its own ticks: the camera, the fade, the sky's dimming, the music hold, the ring, the aim, the held menu and Sense Aura's popup. The camera drawn is Start Early's director's ([cameras](cameras.md#start-earlys-director)): Camera: Enhanced filming the drawing, beats first; the composer's own, the newest action's settled onto over 20 ticks, is drawn only with no director, as the composer's tests have it.
- **The battle screen** is composed (`BattleComposer.Gui`, `CombatGuiFrame`).
  - **Its frames, portraits and HP bars are one tick's**: the oldest any action drawing is at (`Oldest`), so they're laid out as one moment had them and show no death or reserve's joining the drawing hasn't come to. Over that, each combatant in play then has the skull, the frame's colour (fled) and the HP bar of the tick its own body is drawn from; one drawn past its leaving its slot (dead, its slot since taken) is under its skull with no bar. A slot is told by who has it on a tick (`Occupant`, the body on the field with it): one combatant's, and then, once it's dead, the reserve's who takes it. The frames an aim colours take the newest action's colours, the player's own aim's. A reserve keeps its small frame till the oldest action drawing is done, which the user found right (Nov 11): a reserve just come is rarely in the shot, and has its place by the time it has a turn.
  - **The radar** is the newest action's, its dots where the bodies are drawn, about where the camera's owner is drawn (`CombatRadar.Entries`, `Dots`).
  - **The log's line** is the last played, beside the portrait its own tick had (`_logPortrait`): the box shows whose turn a line was written in, and a bandit's line drawn in the player's turn stood by the player's portrait.
  - **What it replaced** (the user's, Nov 11: a reserve's portrait gone, a frame empty with its bar, portraits flickering through the enemies' turns). It was the newest action's screen with each slot's frame, portrait and bar swapped for those of the tick the slot's body was drawn from, places and all. The newest action is the furthest on, so its screen had deaths and joinings not drawn yet; a dead bandit and the reserve in its slot were told apart only by the slot; and a portrait swapped in came with the place it had on its own tick, mid-slide or in a reserve's small frame. `StartEarlyTests`' watch holds every drawn screen to what every recorded one keeps: an HP bar and a frame's slot each have a portrait, the layout never goes back within a second to one it left, and a line stands by its own portrait. Before, four of the six battles broke them.
  - **Left:** a reserve whose walk on is drawn while an older action still draws keeps its small frame until that action is done, since the layout is the oldest tick's.
- **Waits.** The battle's end is returned only once every action and every body has been drawn to the battle's present (`BattleComposer.CaughtUp`); a turn the player plays opens sooner ([below](#opening-the-players-turns-early)). The AI's `Rest` and a turn that only skips involve only their own combatant, so they overlap freely.
- **Checked** by `StartEarlyTests` on every drawn tick of its battles: no body drawn from an earlier tick than before, or other than the recording has it there; none in two actions' episodes at once; no two bodies drawn from different ticks touching nearer than either tick recorded them; a player's turn opening only once its combatant is free, and the end only once caught up; every cue, sound call and emitter start drawn once, played or skipped. `BattleComposerTests` checks made-up walks: two apart are drawn at once, one sharing a body waits only for that body's part, crossing ones go one after the other, and an idle body's drawn tick never goes back.
- **Traps.** A body idling from a far older tick can be drawn where it stood then: the path check takes that place, not the core's. An action not settled that isn't the player's turn starts only once the actions before it drew their last tick before it on an earlier drawn tick, or a body's tail and its next turn would be drawn on one drawn tick. The target marks pulse a body from dark to full once a second (`StepMarks`), so a target drawn in an overlap can look black on a frame; that's 1.0's. The idle states are found from the actor's branches, so a mod's actor whose idle loop takes another request would hold its body in every action.

#### Opening the player's turns early

Under Start Early a turn the player plays doesn't wait for the whole drawing to catch up. The core is unchanged, and the battle waits on the player at the turn's start as before; only the drawing (`BattleComposer`, `BattleDrawing`) changed.

- **When it opens** (`BattleComposer.Schedule`, `Opens`). The battle waits in a turn the player plays (`BattleFrame.PlayersTurn`: its walk and aim, the held menus, the Troubadour's prompt) and its action is the newest (`Pending` while it waits to open). It opens on the first drawn tick where every earlier action has started, every body it has reached so far (its combatant, as a turn begins) has drawn its episodes in earlier actions (`Free`), the beats before it have played, and no body still walking in the drawing is within its combatant's reach ([below](#pacing-beats-and-the-players-wait)); earlier actions that don't hold it draw on. It's marked `DrawnAction.Opened` and is the composer's `Live` while its turn goes on.
- **Drawn on the present.** It draws its first ticks, then, once it's at the battle's present (`AtPresent`), the battle is stepped with the drawing, a tick a drawn tick, with the player's controls (`BattleDrawing.Tick`), and takes the player's presses (`BattleDrawing.TakesPresses`). The camera, the ring, the aim, the held menu, Sense Aura's popup and the log's portrait are its own, as the newest action's. A body with nothing left to draw before it is drawn on the present too, its pose blending from where it idled (`Move`). Once the turn's action resolves the battle stops waiting and runs ahead again, and the player's action draws on at its own pace while the next turns are scheduled against it. Nothing snaps every timeline to the present while it draws: the earlier actions finish behind it.
- **What it touches is drawn where it is.** Its walk and its aim are held rather than what they touch fast-forwarded ([below](#pacing-beats-and-the-players-wait)), which piece A put in place of stage 4's guard and aim. What's left (`Bring`, each drawn tick): **anything it reaches**, a body it involves (a target hit, a body pushed) that still has an earlier part to draw, has the actions holding that part brought to their ends (`Release`). **The menus** touch nothing: they show the turn's own combatant, which is free. **Start** fast-forwards everything, as the Party tab reads the live sheets. Skipping the turn, or its action resolving, lets the battle run ahead as before.
- **Fast-forwarding one action** (`Forward`, `Finish`). Its timeline is put at the tick asked for now, and each body it has reached at that tick or its episode's end; what they pass is handed over with the next frame, its cues all, its sounds and emitters skipped but for a started sound's stop and duration. The whole of its timeline moves sooner, so before it moves, each earlier part of every body it involves, in an earlier action, is brought far enough to be drawn by the time this one reaches it (an action yet to start starts sooner): no body is in two actions at once or goes back, and a throw's thrower, missile, target and numbers stay together.
- **A in an AI's walk** (`SkipWalks`): each action drawing that's in its walk (state 5 on its own tick) goes to its walk's last tick, as Original's A skips one walk, and plays on from there; the actions not walking draw on. The core plays the walk as if A weren't pressed, sticking included.
- **The last word** (`Resolve`, every drawn tick): two bodies drawn from different ticks that touch nearer than either tick recorded them, as a walk skipped to its end where a body drawn behind still stands can leave them, have the one drawn behind brought on till none do.
- **Checked** by `StartEarlyTests` as above, on the five battles and on the rats three more ways: each party member walking forward 40 ticks of its turn before attacking; A pressed in every AI walk drawn, which still plays tick for tick as Original's; and the aim (below). `BattleComposerTests`: a turn opens while others draw once its combatant is free, and not before its combatant's part in an earlier action is drawn; releasing a body drawn behind fast-forwards only the action holding it; A in a walk brings only the walk to its end.
- **For the camera** (piece C): `BattleComposer.DrawingNow()` lists the actions drawing on a drawn tick, each with its episodes (`DrawnAction.Episodes`, `TickAt`), its beats (`Beats`) and its numbers, missiles and spells' actors (`Things`); `PlayingNow()` is the beats playing on the drawn tick, each as a shot would hold it; `DrawnTickOf(body)` is the tick a body is drawn from, and the frame drawn has each body where it's drawn (`BattleFrame.Bodies`), every missile in flight (`Missiles`) and every visual (`Visuals`); `Live` is the player's turn while it's drawn on the present, and `Pending` while it waits to open; `Coming(horizon)` is the beats of the actions scheduled that strike within that many drawn ticks, each as a shot would hold it as it strikes, which the director plans ahead from ([cameras](cameras.md#start-earlys-director)). Each frame keeps what the camera reads of its tick (`BattleFrame.Facts`, `DrawnBody.Standing`).
- **Traps.** A fast-forward can bring a walker to its end before a body drawn behind has moved off; `Resolve`, the last word, brings that body on rather than letting them overlap, and counts each time (`Resolved`): in the five battles it never fires, and with A pressed in every AI walk once.

#### Pacing: beats and the player's wait

The user found stage 4 faster than they'd expected, perhaps too fast, and asked for results to be held long enough to be seen, for a player's turn to wait for the results before it, and for the player's stick and aim to wait till no walking enemy can be reached (piece A, Nov 7, 2026). The core is unchanged: only when actions start drawing and when the player's turn takes the stick.

- **Beats** (`Beat`, `BeatKind`, `DrawnAction.Beats`, found as the frames come in by `BattleActions`). A result is a beat on the body it lands on, from its strike to the end of its reaction, by the ticks of the action whose frame it struck on:
  - **damage**: a hit asked of its actor (`CombatantAnimated` 7) or a number over it (`NumberShown`, healing being an effect);
  - **a miss**: a dodge (8, every miss of a blow), or a spell's actor in the resisted or refused role striking (`DrawnVisual.Struck` turning true);
  - **a defeat**: a fall (9), or `CombatantDied` while its beat plays;
  - **an effect added**: a spell's actor in the target or potion role striking its owner (a buff or debuff, Mirror, Weakness, Haste), or an ally's help (10). The caster's own actor isn't one.

  What lands on a body whose beat in the same action still plays joins it, so a killing hit is one beat, damage and a defeat; a newer action's result on a body still reacting ends the older beat there. A beat ends once its reaction has: the body's actor back where it stood before the strike (its idle loop, or the state it stood in, as a stance after a dodge), or still (a body lying where it fell), once it has left them, or the body fading; if its actor never leaves them, after `ReactionWait` (12) ticks. It lasts at least `BeatLeast` (30) ticks and at most `BeatMost` (180). In the casters' battle most last 30 to 100 ticks.
- **Beats playing at once** (`BattleComposer.Search`, `BeatsClear`). A beat may play alongside an earlier action's only where one shot can hold them all. As an action's start is searched for, at each delay tried, on the drawn tick each of its beats would first overlap one of theirs, every beat playing then, its own and theirs, is put to the framing (`IBeatFraming.CanHold`). Where it can't, that delay fails, so the action starts just late enough for its beat to strike the drawn tick after theirs ends; its walk, earlier in it, still starts as early as that allows, alongside the earlier action. An action is never paused midway: its whole start moves. An action's own beats play as its parts have them (a Fireball's targets all at once, below), and the search's window runs to the earlier actions' beats' ends as well as their tails.
- **The shot question** is piece C's to answer, the director's. `IBeatFraming.CanHold(drawn, beats, turn)` is asked with each beat as a shot would hold it (`FramedBeat`: the beat, the tick of its own it's drawn at, where its subject and its source are drawn) and, for a turn the player plays, where its combatant stands. - **A player's turn waits for the beats before it** (`BeatsBefore`). It opens once every beat of every earlier action has played. - **The player's stick is held** (`Walkers`). The turn opens only once its combatant, given the stick at drawn tick T as the turn reaches the battle's present, can't touch any body still walking in the drawing. For each drawn tick t from T till that body is drawn out to where the core has it, the combatant could be anywhere within its greatest travel a tick times t − T + 1 of where it stands, kept in its field (`BattleFrame.Field`: the field's centre and radius, `Body.Field`, and the greatest travel); that reach must stay farther from the body's drawn place than both radii and `Buffer` (1) of the body's own radius. A body drawn where the core has it can't be walked through, and one whose path never comes near the field blocks nothing. Until T the earlier actions draw on with their own camera; the turn opens, draws its few ticks and takes the stick at T.

### What a battle tells its host

Combat's presentation goes out as cue records through a host, and its ending comes back from its step (ADR 0019, `CombatCues.cs`).

- **The host** is `ICombatHost`, with one method, `Cue(CombatCue)`. In the session the battle's host is its recorder, given to `Battle.Begin` so the first bodies are cued as they're made, and the session's host (`combatHost` at `GameSession.Start`, the runtime's `Game`) hears each tick's cues as that tick is drawn ([drawn from a recording](#drawn-from-a-recording)); those `Begin` cued come with the frame drawn at once, before the first tick. A test sets `Battle.Host` on a battle it built.
- **The cues**, in the order the battle makes them. The views build and free their parts by them and read where things are from the frame drawn; nothing diffs the battle's lists.

  | Cue | When | The runtime |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | `CombatantJoined` | a body is made (`Add`): at the setup and as a reserve joins | makes its `CombatantView` |
  | `CombatantAnimated` | a one-off request through `Battle.Animate`: the swing 12–18, the throw 15, a cast or a drink 5; as the blow lands the hit 7, the dodge 8, the fall 9, an ally's help 10 | nothing: the core steps the actors |
  | `CombatantDied` | `Death`, as the killing blow is dealt, before it lands | nothing |
  | `CombatantGone` | a dead or fled body's fade is over (`Fled` says which) | frees the view |
  | `NumberShown`, `NumberGone` | a number shown; its two seconds over, the pool's oldest pushed out, or its body gone | `CombatNumbersView.Show`, `Remove` |
  | `MissileLaunched`, `MissileLanded` | `StartFlight`; `StepFlight` as the blow lands | `MissileView.Launch`, `Land` |
  | `SpellVisualStarted`, `SpellVisualEnded` | `SpellFx.Start` or `StartPotion`, once the cast knows both its bodies, so an actor's emitters attach to the right ones; `FreeIfFinished` | `SpellFxView.Started`, `Ended` |
  | `LogLine` | every change of what the log box says (below) | nothing: the box draws `CombatLog.Text` |
  | `BattlePaused` | the tick the pause menu's two frames have passed | nothing: the session acts on the step |
  | `BattleEnded` | `EndAs`: the win, the loss, the spared loss, fleeing by the pause or by Escape, the Shadow vanishing or merging | nothing |

  The battle's own `Free` at its end cues nothing: `BattleEnded` came first.
- **The step**: `Battle.Tick` returns `BattleStep.Ran`, `Paused(PartyTab, CanFlee)` while the pause menu is due (the Party tab and Flee from a turn's pause, states 1, 9, 10, 11, 18 and 25; the System tab alone from state 27's), or `Ended(Outcome)` once it has left (`Battle.Over`: states 16, 20 and 21). The session sends `Transition.PauseBattle` on the first and keeps the outcome of the second for `EndBattle`.
- **The log's one writer**: `CombatLog` notes in `History` (`Battle.Log`) and cues as `LogLine` every change of what the box says (`Text`): a line shown at once, a queued line or Banish's third flushed, the reinforcements' hold and the line it gives back, and the line it's locked on. A line put up over itself isn't one, as the box doesn't change. So the log holds what the player saw when they saw it: a blow's queued "loses %d hit points" as the blow lands, a spell's "cast on" at state 2's end.
- **The session**: trek cues a battle (`TrekLoop.CueBattle`, `QueueState(2)`'s fade at 1/30 a tick) rather than handing it to the host, and cuts the music as it goes (`TrekLoop.BeginBattle`). `GameSession.BeginBattle` stages the arena on trek's zone engine as a cut-scene's stage (`ZoneEngine.Stage`), its music held until the fade ends, begins the battle (mode `GameMode.Combat`, under the menu) and sends `Transition.StartedBattle`. `GameSession.EndBattle` cuts the music whatever the outcome ([audio](audio.md#music)), sends `Transition.EndBattle`, calls `Battle.Leave`, moves trek's kept place for an encounter's end (`TrekLoop.Keep`), then by the outcome: won, it holds the world as `PostBattle` and sends `Transition.PostBattle`, and trek comes back where the battle began once the windows close (`MenuClosed`, [ui](pause-menu.md#after-a-battle)); lost, the game over (`PlayGameOver`: the combat record's `GameOver` script from its script object in the Dreamscape's map, as a cut-scene, whose end is `Transition.GameOver` by the base plugin's trigger on its `script.ended`, [events](events.md)); merged, the Shadow's cinematic (`PlayCinematic(cinematic, fromTheatre: false)`), whose end resumes trek where the battle began; otherwise `TrekLoop.Resume(fled)`, at the farthest breadcrumb having fled, with its heading and the camera at its eye ([In and out](#in-and-out)), and `Transition.ResumeTrek`.

### Traps

- **A tick's order** (`Battle.Tick`, as `Combat.Frame`): the reinforcements' hold; the controls; the keys' listener from the camera; each body in turn (`AtStrike` reads the frame before the actor steps, then the step, the push and `AfterBodies`); the camera; the state's step; the Shadow's reach (`CheckShadowMerge`); the marks; the tints; the spells' visuals; the numbers; the fade. The marks set and clear tints before the tints step, so a cast's glow on a body the marks let go is cleared on the frame it starts, as checkpoint 08's tints (all 0) show.
- **A one-shot request is held a frame.** `Combat.AfterFrame` lets requests go once a frame, so a request combat makes is asked for through every tick of its frame (`Combatant.OneShot`, `Cadence.FrameTicks`), and an actor takes only a request its state has a branch for (`ActorStateMachine.Taken`), so the frame's second tick doesn't advance it. Without both the swing's, hit's, dodge's and death's keys don't sound ([audio](audio.md#bodies-sounds)).
- **The original's one focus is three aims**, each moved only by its own: `Battle.ThrownAim` (state 18's free cursor), `AreaAim` (state 11's centre, which set B is built about for the player's placed spell) and `AiAreaCentre` (the AI's `AreaCentre` and `AimAreaSpell`, which an AI's placed spell is cast about). `FocusOn(c)` puts all three on a combatant wherever the original puts its focus there, so each reads what the original's would; the camera looks at its state's.
- **A body keeps its cell while it hasn't moved** (`Combatant.SyncFromBody`): the port's bodies are in the world's coordinates and the original's in the arena chunk's, and taking the chunk's origin off loses a little, enough to move a cell after the AI's tries. `IsWithinReach` is false for a body gone from the field.
- **The fading.** `FreeBody` sets `Combatant.Fading` to `Battle.FadeTicks` (120) for the dead and the fled alike and keeps the combatant in the battle's fading list, which a reserve taking its slot doesn't empty. `Battle.Crowd` counts each down a tick, cues `CombatantGone` at 0, and keeps the fading among the bodies that push and are pushed, stepped with no animation, in slot order with a fading body before the reserve in its slot. `Combatant.Alpha` is the body's `+0x70C`: its entity's `EntityBody.Alpha`, then `(u8)(left / 60 × alpha)` under `Battle.FadeShownTicks` ([a body's alpha](#a-bodys-alpha)). The next turn begins as the fade starts.
- **Content by its effects, not its kinds**: Darkness and Light are known by their `partOfDay` effect, the holds by `immobilise` (`Combatant.PoseHeld`), Fireball by its spell record's `area`, the Shadow by its `ShadowRule`. The original tests the Shadow's place (`0xAC`) in `ForbidFleeForBosses`, `CanAttack` and `CanCast` with no list; the record keeps two rules, `Flee` and `Untargetable`, so a mod's boss the party may flee from can still be hit.
- The AI's, the attack's and the visuals' own traps are in [combat-ai](combat-ai.md#traps) and [combat-actions](combat-actions.md#traps).

### A body's alpha

`EntityBody.ShadowAlpha` (`0x8006C798`) gives `Combat.CreateBody` (`0x8005C474`, its only caller) the alpha it stores as the body's `+0x70C`: `0x7F` for entity list places `0xAF`–`0xB5` (Ksathra, Shatrevar, Mehrdad, Golnar, Nasim, Behrooz and Assim, all on actor `0x33DB`) and `0xC0` (the Spirit Wolf), `0xFF` for every other and for a dialogue entity. That alpha caps the body's own draw (`Bodies.Render`) and its shadow's (`BodyShadow.DrawAlpha`, `0x800122C4`), so these bodies are translucent, not just their shadows. The place is `GetIdIndex`'s (`0x8006CBF8`), the entity's position in the entity list ([code-tables](../formats/code-tables.md)). The importer carries the places as a fact (`EntityModels.AlphaOf`) onto the entity's `Body.Alpha` (`alpha`, 255 for most), `Combatant.Alpha` starts from it, and `CombatantView` draws the body and its shadow with it. The freed body's fade (above) takes it from there.

### Where the port differs

The user's choices. The AI's are in [combat-ai](combat-ai.md#where-the-port-differs), and an attack's, a spell's, a flask's and the visuals' in [combat-actions](combat-actions.md#where-the-port-differs).

- **The Goblin Ambush's end sets the leader's Strength to its base** rather than adding the base to it, which doubled it when the battle ended before the second hit.
- **A script's battle has no bonus loot**, where the original pays out the last npc or encounter's again (`0x800D9470` is never cleared).
- **The game over's end goes back to the title**, its cards first, as the original's does, but the runtime starts its Godot scene again with a new game for it ([ui](ui.md#coming-back-to-it)).
- **The Pace setting quickens a turn's walk.** Brisk's 1.3 (`Battle.Pace`, which the session sets from each tick's `SessionSettings` and as the battle begins) plays the body the player steers in state 1 faster, steps and all, as trek's player's ([movement](movement.md)). It doesn't reach the field's radius, so a turn covers the same ground sooner, nor the AI's bodies, nor the attack, cast or turn's end after it, whose timings the strikes and the results wait on.
- **The ring's retries draw on the cosmetic stream** (ADR 0006): nothing but the drawing reads where a teardrop stands, so a ring's misses move no rules draw, where the original's move every one after them. Each ring starts due +Z rather than 0.006° further round than the last.
- **The merge raises the shield skill as it does the others**, the user's choice: 1.0's test is turned round (`Skills.AddShieldBase`), raising Alaron's shield skill only when the Shadow has none, so with the Shadow's shield of 8 the merge never raises it. The port raises it as the code reaches for, by the difference of the two base levels or the least (`Battle.MergeShadow`). It matters little to play: a fighter is likely past 8 by then, and a caster has little use for it.
- **Every body animates in combat**, the user's choice, since at today's resolutions the far ones are plainly seen: 1.0's `ProcessPlayers` doesn't step the animation of an idle body (its request, `+0x1A` and `+0x1C` all 0) 13.0 (`Body.IdleAnimateDistance`, `0x800CDB50`) or more from the camera's aim, in combat only, unless its actor is in `Body.AlwaysAnimated` (`0x800CDA50`, nine actors, [code tables](../formats/code-tables.md)). The port steps every body's but the held and the fallen, so the list isn't imported.
- **A half-alpha body fades from its own alpha.** The original's fade is of 255 whatever the body's alpha was, so a translucent body jumps to nearly opaque as its fade starts; the port keeps `Combatant.Alpha` at the entity's through the fall and fades it from there to nothing over the same span, as every other body fades.
- **A reserve whose own point is taken falls back to its own side's points first**, the enemies' (`good` in an ambush), the user's choice: 1.0 tries each set's `bad` points first whatever the ambush ([above](#a-turn)), so in an ambush its reserves come in beside the party, which reads as an oversight. The draws are the same; only the point differs.
- **The loot is rolled on the loot stream**, not the battle's (ADR 0006), so a battle's draws after it don't shift.
- **Camera: Enhanced** directs the camera of a battle played turn by turn over the original's, which goes on underneath (`CombatCamera.Shown`); the stick and the aims are read against the camera shown, and no rule reads it otherwise. [Cameras](cameras.md#the-enhanced-battle-camera-camera-enhanced) has it.

By the tick:

- **Combat's frame is 2 ticks**, the user's choice, and 1.0's `delta` at full speed isn't measured (checkpoint 10 suggests 3; the parity logs' deltas are the logger's slowed frames). It sets how long a one-shot request is held and rounds an attack's strike to a frame, but measuring it would only line the parity logs up: the port's movement doesn't step by it.
- The camera acts every tick rather than once a frame; only its goal and holds are once-a-frame things, so it's the same at 30 fps.
- The Shadow's reach is tested every tick rather than at each drawn frame's end, and state 29 counts its frames by the cadence.

Others:

- A bystander in the first four leaves the original a null slot that it counts as an enemy on the field; the port leaves it out and says so (`Battle.Problems`). No npc or encounter that fights has one.
- A win leaves through state 16 (`Battle.Over`), which the original never reaches for one, and the session opens the windows; the post-battle window opens on `PauseMenu.Member`, the member last shown or whose turn the last pause was on ([ui](pause-menu.md#opening-and-closing)).
