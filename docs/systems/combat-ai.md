# Combat: the AI (2.5, 2.6)

The enemies' turns in a [battle](combat.md), and Sholeh's: what an enemy chooses, how it walks, why it rests, its morale and its flight. Read from 1.0's code (`0x80056E10`–`0x8005C0C0`, `aiInfo.cpp` and `aiscores.cpp`), checked by running the original's own functions on the checkpoints' RAM (`tools/aivectors`, [rig](../reference-rig.md#replaying-the-originals-ai-toolsaivectors)) and by the replay of the user's logged battles ([combat-parity](combat-parity.md)). Names are in the symbol map, prefixed `EnemyAI.`, `AiScores.` and `ArenaGrid.`. A spell's worth to the AI and what it may cast are [magic](magic.md#the-ais-values)'s.

## The record

0x1C bytes, a combatant's `+0x44` (`EnemyAI.Init`, `0x8005BA7C`; `EnemyAI.Free`, `0x8005BD90`). Checked in 02, 08, 09, 10 and 11, where the fleeing rat's flags went from 8 to 9.

| Offset | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| `+0x00` | the spare weapon: an item made from the entity's first weapon, only when its first and third are both set; what `EnemyAI.SwapWeapon` trades the held one for |
| `+0x04` | the type it fights as: 0 melee, 1 ranged, 2 caster, from the weapon and spells; `ChooseWeapon` makes it `+0x07` |
| `+0x05` | the target's slot, `0xFF` none |
| `+0x06` | morale, the entity record's (`+0x1B` in RAM): 5 for rats, 12 and up never flee; spells of kinds `0x19` and `0x2F` lower it until the effect ends, when `EffectRules.End` (`0x800707EC`) gives back what they took ([magic](magic.md#what-each-spell-does)) |
| `+0x07` | the type its style wants this turn; unlike `+0x04`, `Execute` swaps weapons first |
| `+0x08` | the attack style, 0–10 (`EnemyAI.StyleTable`, `0x800E0B80`, a function every 8 bytes) |
| `+0x09` | flags: 1 fleeing, 2 a morale check due, 4 two more morale (nothing sets it), 8 the AI plays its turns (set by `Init`, tested by `TurnOrder.BeginTurn`), `0x10` hit by a weakness (never cleared) |
| `+0x0A`–`+0x11` | eight action slots, spell kinds or `0xFF`: the entity's first five spells in its record's order (its spellbook's places 0–4), its armor's, its shield's and its weapon's own spell (the one it's cast from, not the one it strikes with); a slot chosen becomes the entity's selected spell by `EnemyAI.SlotIndex` and `SlotKind` (`0x800E0B70`, `0x800E0B78`: source 1 with the place for 0–4, then 2, 3 and 4). No enemy has an armor's or shield's spell; Kitarak, Shamsuk, Lugash and Golnar have a weapon's |
| `+0x12`, `+0x13` | the path's next corner and its length |
| `+0x14` | the combatant |
| `+0x18` | a flight's turns left: 4 as it starts |

**The style** (`Init`): no weapon, 5; a melee weapon, 0, or 1 with a base Intelligence of 15 or more, or with any spell 6 (9 for the bosses, `EnemyAI.HasBossStyle`, `0x8005BA40`, a jump table at `0x800D24B0`: entity list places `0x57`–`0x59`, `0x5E`, `0x5F`, `0x7A`, `0xA7`–`0xA9`, `0xAB`, `0xAD`, `0xBA`, `0xBC`, the combat record's `BossStyles`); a ranged one, 2, or 3 clever, 4 if it strikes in melee too (`Weapon.AllowsMelee`), or with a spell 7, 8 (10 for the bosses). Rats, wolves, trolls, skeletons and Goblin `0x0215` are style 0; Goblin `0x022E` and Goblin Scout `0x024E` 2; Goblin Scout `0x0208`, the Bandit Woodsmen and the Goblin Poisoners 3.

**An enemy's weapon** (the entity's build in its enemy mode, `0x8006E2E8` through `0x8006E434`): the third weapon slot's if it can wield it, else the first's; the second is never read. Every entity with a third weapon can, so the first is the fallback only for those without. The goblin archers hold a bow with a sword as the spare.

## A turn

`EnemyAI.Step` (`0x8005B314`), the first frame of state 8. An enemy decides once and walks once: one plan, at most one leg, then at most one action or a rest.

1. It reads its body (`Combatant.SyncFromBody`) and keeps where it starts.
2. Not fleeing, its style chooses (`EnemyAI.ChooseGoal`, `0x8005B144`; `EnemyAI.RunStyle`, `0x80059628`): the style fills `AiScores` and sorts it, and the first entry is the target (`+0x05`) and the goal cell. At the goal already, it acts (`Execute`). Fleeing, the goal is the nearest flee point ([Fleeing](#fleeing)).
3. **The path** (`EnemyAI.PlanPath`, `0x8005A974`; `ArenaGrid.FindPath`, `0x80068CD4`): both footprints are freed; from the start it steps to the free, unmarked, gentle neighbour (a rise under 3 half units, `ArenaGrid.IsGentle`) nearest the goal by the distance squared, the first of equals in the directions' order N to NW, marking each, until the goal or the grid's diagonal. It never searches: at a dead end it counts back without stepping back, so any pocket, a ring of bodies or a wall's corner between it and the goal, fails it. The grid is filled again with every living body that's still there (`ArenaGrid.RefillWithBodies`), and a path is cut to its corners (`TrimPath`, `0x80068784`, takes the loops out; `StraightenPath`, `0x8006894C`, keeps a run's last cell). With no path: walk straight at the target if it's within its bodies' radii and 2.25 (`EnemyAI.ApproachTarget`, `0x8005AB14`), else act from where it stands.
4. **The leg** (`EnemyAI.WalkPath`, `0x8005A0D8`): corner after corner, each queued for the body to run to (`SetPlayerMoveToQueue`, `0x800157C8`, a ring of 16) and the facing that way, until the path's end or a stop. A corner that's taken or too steep stops it at the first blocked cell of the straight line there (`EnemyAI.WalkIsClear`, `0x80059C94`, a Bresenham line), so a rat runs into its target's cell and the bodies' push holds it off. A corner past the field, measured from the turn's start, stops it where the line leaves the circle (`EnemyAI.ClipToCircle`, `0x80059A84`, whose quadratic has b where 2b belongs), rounded: an engaged enemy, of radius 1, moves a cell. The combatant stands on the leg's end at once, on the grid too, while its body runs; flag 3 is set for a leg of 3 or more. State 5, the stuck timer at 240. If no corner could be walked it only turns toward the first (`EnemyAI.FaceStep`) and approaches or acts.
5. **The walk** (state 5). **A skips it** (`Combat.InputMoving`, `0x80084F98`, on A's press): for the current combatant's body with a path (flag `0x10`), `Body.GiveUp` (`0x80015C28`) puts it at once at the last point queued (the ring's entry before `+0x254`, or with it spent, the goal it's heading for, `+0x260`), sets it down (`Body.SetDown(body, 0, 0, 0)`), faces it to `+0x28` if it was turning and clears the path, so the state's next step goes on as on arriving. Nothing between is tested, so a body that would have stuck on the way (another body's push, a wall's edge) is put at the leg's end all the same, where the combatant already stands on the grid: a rat skipped onto Sholeh's cell stood exactly at its leg's end, despite her body, and bit her from there. It asks only that the body walks a path, not whose side it's on: Sholeh's walks with an AI record skip too. No sound plays. Otherwise the body runs (stick 0.95, `Body.WalkStick`) to each point in turn, arriving within its scaled radius. A body that comes no nearer by 0.01 for 30 ticks, counted over the whole point, gives it up where it stands in combat (`Body.StepTowardGoal`, `0x800159C8`: with `Combat.Current` set it zeroes the stuck timer and clears the path, `0x80015AD0`–`0x80015AEC`, and only without one puts it at the goal through `Body.GiveUp`, so in a battle `GiveUp` is A's alone); and every tick it moves under 0.03 counts the stuck timer down. With no point left, state 6 turns it the way it walked (`Combatant.FaceAndSettle`, `0x8005FC98`, through `Body.Face`), and then, with `NextState` 8, `EnemyAI.FleeAdvance` (`0x8005B434`, which reads the body first, `SyncFromBody`): for an enemy not fleeing, `Execute`. The timer run out does the same from wherever it is.
6. **The action** (`EnemyAI.Execute`, `0x8005B594`): a weapon swap first if its style wants the other kind (`ChooseWeapon`, `0x8005A620`, which makes what it fights as, `+0x04`, what it wants, `+0x07`, and clears the selected spell's source, `ce+0x3D`; `SwapWeapon`, `0x8005A560`: "%s changes weapons", and the turn's spent resting); then (`EnemyAI.PrepareAction`, `0x8005B1D8`) Fireball's centre (`AimAreaSpell`, below) and both target sets from where it stands (`EnemyAI.PrepareTargetSets`, `0x8005B8F8`): for one that fights as a caster (`+0x04` 2) the spell's target first (`SelectSpellTarget`, `0x8005B878`: its target if set B has it selected, else B's cursor, set A emptied), then the weapon's; for any other the weapon's first (`SelectWeaponTarget`, `0x8005B7C8`: its target selected alone if set A has it, else whoever set A selected, set B emptied), then the spell's; and if it can act on that one (`EnemyAI.CanActOnTarget`, `0x8005B218`, which makes it the action's target, `+0x14`: a caster `CanCast` or `CanAttack`, the rest the other way round), it turns to it (`Combatant.FaceCombatant`, state 6) and attacks or casts through `Combatant.ExecuteAction` as the player does: state 6 attacks if set A has the target selected, else casts if set B has (nothing sets the AI's cast margin `+0x3C` first; `PrepareCast` rolls it). Otherwise it rests: state 24, 120 ticks (`Combat.EnterRest`), and the turn ends. So a caster whose weapon fallback has once won (styles 6 and 9 want melee, so `+0x04` becomes 0) is weapon-first from then on: with anyone in its weapon's reach it attacks, even when a spell scored best, as no fallback makes it a caster again.

### When the AI reads its body after a walk

1.0 runs each combat state once a frame, before that frame's bodies move (`Combat.Frame`, `0x80022BA4`: the input, the state's handler, then `Combat.DrawFrame`'s `Bodies.Process`). The arrival (`Body.StepTowardGoal` clears flag `0x10`) is found in the bodies' step of frame A. State 5 sees it in frame A+1 and calls `FaceAndSettle`. State 6 waits only on the body's turning flag (`0x20`, `Combat.StateFacing` at `0x80022234`), not on the body stopping. Facing the way it walked, the turn is done in A+1's bodies' step, so `FleeAdvance` reads the body in A+2. `Execute`'s `FaceCombatant` turns 10° a frame (`Facing.Turn`), and state 6's input snaps the rest (`InputFacing`), so the action comes two frames later. The body runs on through all of it: there's no velocity to stop. Its move is the run's root motion, which plays while the request (`+0x14`) or the one its state began under (`+0x1A`) is set, and `+0x14` goes to 0 only at A+1. So 1.0 decides from where the body is two frames after it arrives: about 4 ticks at 30 fps, and further on at longer frames.

## The styles

They score with these: `ListOpponents` (`0x8005783C`, one entry per opponent in slot order: alive, able and on the other side); `ScoreByDistance` (`0x80058B90`, the grid's distance from the AI's cell, `ArenaGrid.Distance`, a square root truncated); `AreaDanger` (`0x80058A48`, 5 for each ally within the party's area danger `Combat+0x12`, while the leader lives); `CollectAttackCells` (`0x80057908`, each free cell in its field from which set A has someone, with the nearest of those) and `CollectAttackCellsByReach` (`0x80057C30`, one cell per opponent, preferring cells no opponent's field reaches, then nearer); `ScoreAttackCells` (`0x80058C7C`, round each opponent the free cells from which set A has it: 25 its own cell, 20 the flank or back, `Combatant.IsFlanking`, 50 above it, up to 15 the more it's hurt, 10 if it holds a ranged weapon); `FarthestCell` (`0x800587BC`, the free cell in its field with the greatest summed distance from the opponents). The cell collectors try each cell by moving the combatant's place there and building set A, but `Combatant.IsWithinReach` reads both bodies again, so for melee reach only where its body stands counts: a clever melee fighter (style 1) far off finds no cell, and takes an opponent's own cell, the one the sort puts first.

| Style | Who | Chooses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | melee, Intelligence under 15 | the nearest opponent; its cell |
| 1, 4 | melee, clever; a weapon for near and far | each opponent's best cell (`ScoreAttackCells`), the best first |
| 2 | ranged, Intelligence under 15 | the nearest cell to attack from; with none, the nearest for the other kind of weapon, which it then wants; with none either, the nearest opponent |
| 3 | ranged, clever | with an opponent in reach, its best cell in melee, wanting melee; holding a melee weapon with none near, the farthest cell, wanting its bow; else the nearest cell to shoot from that no opponent's field reaches, or if all are reached the farthest; with none, style 0's |
| 5–10 | casters and bosses | their spells first (`EnemyAI.ChooseSpellOrAttack`, below), changing neither what they fight as nor what they want; with none that scores: 5 as 0, 6 its collected attack cells scored (or style 1), wanting melee, 7 as 3, 8 as 4, 9 as 6 over the opponents, 10 as 3 with the farthest cell as its fallback and the nearest order |

## The casters' spells

`EnemyAI.ChooseSpellOrAttack` (`0x800590B0`; `ScoreSpellCells`, `0x800581C8`). Every action slot with a spell, in slot order, is scored from every free cell within the field (the square about its cell, Z outer and X inner, within `ArenaGrid.Distance` of the radius, free for its footprint with its own taken off, so its own cell counts): the combatant's place across the ground is moved there, the slot's spell selected and set B built for it there, and if B selects anyone and the spell has a value (`EnemyAI.SpellValue`, `0x80057498`, [magic](magic.md#the-ais-values)), an entry of the cell, the slot + 1, B's cursor (the last it selected in slot order, or the combatant's last target or B's old cursor where `TargetSet.RestoreCursor` keeps it) and the score **(value + 10 × (selected − 1) + the spell's known level) & 0xFF** (the spellbook's level, or the item spell's, not the casting level). For Fireball (spell kind 8, and no other), the count is `EnemyAI.AreaCentre` (`0x80058008`): the area's centre (`Combat+0x18/+0x1C`) put at the mean place of the opponents, B built about it, and with anyone selected, +1 for each candidate of the other side and −1 for each of its own, itself included; a negative count wraps the byte, so a Fireball that would catch more of its own side than the other scores above everything. The list holds `(r² × 4 × 8) & 0xFFE0` entries, 32 for an engaged caster, and drops the rest. With a field of 0 (Pochanargat) only its own cell is scored (`ScoreSpellsInPlace`, `0x800585B0`). With any entry: sorted best first, the best's ties kept (`KeepBest`), each scored again by its distance (`ScoreByDistance` with the allies' area danger), and sorted nearest first: of the cells tied for the best spell, the nearest. `RunStyle` (`0x80059628`) then selects the entry's slot's spell. A slot the caster can't afford has no candidates (`AICanCast`, [magic](magic.md#the-ais-values)), so no entry; the flag `ScoreSpellCells` returns for it nothing reads.

**Why enemies buff first**: a spell for its own side is worth more than a debuff or a hold, and those more than a damage spell ([magic](magic.md#the-ais-values)), and the count adds 10 a target more, so a Starlight Shield on the caster and an ally (61 + 10 + 5 = 76) beats Web of Starlight (48) and Lightning on three (36). A buff isn't cast at one already under it (`Casting`'s test of the target), so once its side has it, it attacks or debuffs. Every cell that reaches its allies scores the same, so it casts from where it stands. Kitarak, with Tap Stamina 5 and Crushing Death 2, never casts the second: 55 + 5 wherever Tap Stamina reaches anyone, against 13 + 2. Nothing is drawn but the sort's ties.

**Fireball's aim** (`EnemyAI.AimAreaSpell`, `0x8005974C`, before the target sets, for kind 8 alone): of the cells of the arena within the spell's range byte × its casting level (3 × level) of the caster, Z outer and X inner, the first with the most candidates of the other side less its own, more than 0, becomes the centre; with none, the cell of `AreaCentre`'s mean. The blast's own reach about that centre is the casting level (`Combatant.CanCast`).

**The bosses** (`EnemyAI.HasBossStyle`, `0x8005BA40`, whose one caller is `EnemyAI.Init`) differ only in their styles' weapon fallbacks, 9 for 6 and 10 for 8; they score their spells as any caster. The 13: the six Bandit Bosses, the Chaos Major, Kitarak, the Lizard Man Boss, Pochanargat, Shamsuk, Lugash and the Wight.

**The sort** (`AiScores.Sort`, `0x8005BFC4`) is the game's own quicksort (`Quicksort.Sort`, `0x800B3978`, `quicksort.cpp`), not the C library's: three elements or two by comparisons, more by a pivot drawn at random and Hoare's partition. Its draws are on a stream of its own (`Quicksort.Seed`, `0x800E6B60`), 0 at boot, never seeded or saved, one draw for each partition of four or more, so a rat choosing among four draws once. Equal scores, common with distances truncated, fall in the order it draws. Nothing else in the AI's choice is random, and its turn draws nothing on the main stream but the action's own.

## Stuck, and resting for no visible reason

All the original's:

- The planner sees the grid alone: a cell can be free and gentle there and still blocked for the body (a wall's edge, a narrow gap), or held by the push of another body. The body then comes no nearer, gives up after 30 ticks where it stands, and the enemy acts or rests from there.
- A body pressed against another slides round it within a frame or two: the push leaves the two exactly touching, and the re-weigh, which a touch would send back where it began the frame, rounds either way ([wanderers](wanderers.md#bodies-pushing-each-other)). 1.0's frames vary, so a body sent back tries again from somewhere else, and which way its tie falls depends on its frames' lengths, which aren't part of the game's state: sometimes it sticks at a glancing angle and gives up. The port's frames don't vary ([Where the port differs](#where-the-port-differs)).
- **A caster with a 2-cell footprint can freeze.** `ScoreSpellCells` and `CollectAttackCells` lift the caster's footprint off the grid while they try cells, but `Execute`'s target sets (`PrepareTargetSets`) are built with it on. `CanCast` needs a clear walk over the grid from the caster's cell (`ArenaGrid.WalkClear`), or else a line of sight. Toward +X or +Z, the walk's first cell is under the caster's own footprint, so the walk fails, and the line of sight from 2 above its cell starts inside its own body. So a spell that scored from where it stands, the nearest of the cells tied for best, finds no target in `Execute`. The combatant doesn't walk, as its goal is its own cell, doesn't cast, and with no one in its weapon's reach rests (state 24). The next turn scores the same. The weapon fallback never comes, as a spell scores, and one that wants melee still scores its spells first (styles 6 and 9). Kitarak's Hobgoblins (footprint 2, Tap Stamina 5) rest every turn so with the party toward +X and +Z, while Kitarak, whose targets lie toward −X, where the walk isn't checked, casts. The port fixes it ([Where the port differs](#where-the-port-differs)).
- A body whose actor is mid-fidget doesn't walk. The idle state rolls its fidget (20%, on the animation stream) as each idle ends, and a fidget state has no branch for walk, run or sneak, so `ActorStateMachine.SetRequest` (`0x80096530`) leaves the request at 0 until the fidget ends. A walk begun then turns the body but never moves it, and the stuck timer gives it up after 30 ticks, where it stands. The port's state machine does the same. The body's best distance to the goal so far is at `+0x258` (f32) and its stuck timer at `+0x25C` (u16). The actor's state machine is at the actor's `+8`, with its clock the u16 at `+0x1F8`, and the actor's `+0x1C` leads to its current state's record {Borg6, flags, branch count, branches}.
- The combatant stands on its leg's end at once, so enemies planning later route round a place its body may never reach; every combatant reads its body again as the next turn begins.
- A rest, 120 ticks with no line: the greedy path fails (a ring of the party and its own allies round the target, anything concave), and the target isn't in reach where it stands; the first corner can't be walked; after the leg nothing is in reach (cut short by the field, the give-up or the timer, or an engaged enemy's single cell); a weapon swap; a flight's leg.

## Morale

`EnemyAI.CheckFlee` (`0x800572AC`), as an AI's turn begins (`TurnOrder.BeginTurn`): every AI combatant but the leader while it lives is asked; with morale under 12 and a check called for (`EnemyAI.MayCheckMorale`, `0x8005722C`: a check due, or no Stamina, or not already fleeing and no more than half the enemies that began able), the leader's morale is worked out first while it lives (`Combat+0x11`), then its own (`EnemyAI.Morale`, `0x80056FA8`, which clears the check due), against 2d6 on the main stream: rolled over, it flees (`EnemyAI.StartFleeing`, `0x8005B9E4`: four turns, unless it's engaged or the arena has no flee points), otherwise it stops fleeing. Its morale, from 2 to 11:

| Term | |
| --- | --- |
| its record's | `+0x06` |
| aspect against the time (Lunar by day, Solar at night) | −1 |
| a weakness has hit it (flag `0x10`, set with the check due by `ApplyResistance`) | −2 |
| no Stamina | −1 |
| strictly fewer than half the enemies that began able | −1 |
| a harmful effect on it | −1 |
| fewer than half the party able | +2 |
| its target has no Stamina, unless its selected spell, whether it casts this turn or not, is one for its own side (`Combatant.IsFriendlySpell`, `0x80061008`: target type Party) | +3 |
| flag 4 (nothing sets it) | +2 |
| its own check: the leader gone or unable | −4 |
| its own check: the leader's morale 8–10, 11 | +1, +2 |

The leader's morale (`Combat+0x11`) is worked out only while it lives and isn't cleared when it dies, so a dead leader's last still has its say: a Gorgon of morale 11 whose leader of 11 has died checks at about 8 (−1 Lunar by day, −4 the leader gone, +2 its morale), fleeing on a roll over it. An engaged enemy rolls and can't start a flight. The morale spells (Aura of Death, Solar Wrath) take their level off and make a check due ([magic](magic.md#what-each-spell-does)), which is how the party puts enemies to flight.

## Fleeing

`EnemyAI.BeginFleeWalk` (`0x8005B038`): each of its turns says "%s flees" and heads for the nearest flee point (`EnemyAI.NearestFleePoint`, `0x8005AE3C`, the last of equals), as any walk: one leg, bounded by its field. Within 1.5 of the point it leaves at once. After the leg (`EnemyAI.FleeAdvance`, `0x8005B434`) its turns count down, and it leaves on the last, within 1.5 of a flee point, or if it hasn't got a unit from where its turn began (`Combat+0x5318`); then it rests. With no path, it walks straight to a flee point within 2.25 (`EnemyAI.FleeStep`, `0x8005AA3C`). With no flee point at all it stops fleeing and chooses as any other. **In arenas 12 and 13 every flight is at once**: their one flee point, `flee0` at (48.7, 11.7), is past the 46-wide grid, so no path reaches it and none is within 2.25. The fleeing enemy's turn ends where it began, and it leaves.

**A flight never stays on the field and acts.** Every dead end of a flight's turn goes through `FleeAdvance`, and one that hasn't moved a unit leaves. With no path, `Act` (`0x8005AD9C`) runs `FleeStep` and `FleeAdvance` itself and the turn stops there. With a path whose first corner can't be walked, `Step` (`0x8005B3D0`–`0x8005B418`) goes on to `Execute` after `FleeAdvance`, for a combatant already gone. Nothing in `Execute` tests the actor's flag 6 (`CanTargetBySide` and `SpellCanTarget` test it only on the target), and the reach tests read its place, which `Leave` keeps, so with someone in reach of where it stood, `FaceCombatant` (`0x8005FD28`) puts state 6 over `FleeAdvance`'s rest. State 6 (`0x800221B0`) finds its body gone and ends the turn (`Combatant.EndTurn`), with no attack or cast: the turn ends the next frame instead of after 120 ticks. Otherwise `Execute` rests again, and a weapon swap logs "%s changes weapons" for the gone enemy. `WalkPath` (`0x8005A0D8`) walks no corner when the first step of `WalkIsClear`'s line is taken or steep, which `TrimPath` can bring about: it cuts a detour that comes back beside its start to a first step the planner never tested, such as up a ledge the detour went round. Or `ClipToCircle`'s quadratic finds no root, about 0.6% of corners outside the field. Neither has been seen in play.

**`Combatant.Leave`** (`0x80061480`): it leaves the grid, flag 6 (gone), the counts of those able fall, its effects and potions come off, its body fades out over 120 ticks (`Body.FadeOut`, `0x80014AD4`, [Deaths](combat.md#deaths)), `Combat.Bodies` is emptied at once, and its portrait is marked fled. It isn't dead: the kill flags aren't touched, and it still pays its experience and chest.

## Reserves that flee as they arrive

`EnemyAI.ReserveFleesOnArrival` (`0x80056E18`), inside `Combat.SpawnReserves` as a dead or gone combatant's slot is to be refilled ([combat](combat.md#a-turn)): only where there are flee points, with morale under 12, and while **more** than half the enemies that began are still able; its record's morale, less 1 for its aspect (any but Solar counting as Lunar), 4 with the leader gone or unable, and the leader's morale's say, from 2 to 11, against 2d6. Fleeing, it's passed over (`+0x07` moves on), its small portrait marked (`CombatGui.MarkReserveFled`), its slot left as it was until the next round, and `Combat+0x543C` set: the next turn's change shows "Enemy reinforcements flee" and the whole battle holds 60 ticks (`Combat.HoldForMessage`, `0x80022AEC`). `Combat.SkipFleeingReserves` has no caller.

**Sholeh** with an AI record plays her turns the same way; on the party's side she never counts as fleeing.

### Traps

- **The AI's tries are built in their own target sets** (`Battle._triesA`, `_triesB`), not the battle's, which hold the turn and the player's aim. An attack try moves the combatant's place to the cell and back (`TryAttack`, the action passed rather than set), as the original's does, and must: the reach's reads of the bodies (`IsWithinReach`, `FreeOfOpponents`) and an opponent's field worked out again (`UpdateRadius`, `NearShadow`) read the place and put it back where the body stands, which is why melee counts only the body's place. A spell try reads nothing of the bodies, so it asks `CanCast(c, source, t, from, centre)` and `SpellTargetCount(c, source)` of the slot's spell (`Combatant.SpellOf`) without selecting it or moving anyone. Each leaves the battle's sets as the original's tries would: the attack tries empty set A, the spell scores start from set B's cursor and empty it, and Fireball's aim leaves set B its last try's cursor, which `PrepareTargetSets`' rebuilding keeps (`TargetSet.TakeCursor`).

### Where the port differs

The user's choices:

- **A large caster's own footprint doesn't block its cast**: `CanCast`'s walk passes over the caster's own footprint (`ArenaGrid.WalkClear`'s `ownFootprint`), so what scored can be cast, where 1.0's 2-cell casters freeze ([above](#stuck-and-resting-for-no-visible-reason)).
- **A body pressed against another slides on.** The port's frames don't vary, so a body the re-weigh sends back would try again from the same place to the bit and give up; the push lets a touch within `Crowd.Rounding` (1e-4) go, so its bodies slide on as 1.0's mostly do. Where 1.0's tie fell the other way and its body stuck, the port's slides on; the user kept the slide over stepping combat's bodies at 1.0's frame lengths, which would carry a lot of machinery for movement checks without making the game play better.
- The original runs `Execute` after `FleeAdvance` even for a fleeing enemy that has just left, its body gone; the port stops there and rests. It differs only in time: where 1.0's finds someone in reach, its turn ends the next frame rather than after the rest's 120 ticks ([Fleeing](#fleeing)).
- A walk the AI gives up is forgotten at the turn's end; the original's body would run on into the next turn.

By the tick, the same as 1.0's at 30 fps but not at the parity logger's slower frames ([combat-parity](combat-parity.md#the-known-differences)):

- The AI's walk counts a tick's move toward the stuck timer, times a 30 fps frame's ticks, where the original counts a frame's; its body's 30 ticks of no progress are counted once a 30 fps frame by 0.01 a frame, two ticks at a time, as all steering toward a goal is ([wanderers](wanderers.md)). The stuck test leaves out the original's actor-speed term (an actor standing moves under 0.03 anyway).
- **An AI body turns 10° a tick as it walks,** where 1.0's turns 20° a frame whatever the frame's ticks ([movement](movement.md)). At frames of 3–6 ticks 1.0's bodies turn 3–7° a tick, so a body that sets off facing away walks a wider curve, and its stick keeps its last way while it comes no nearer ([wanderers](wanderers.md)), so it may give up where it stands.
- **The state is stepped every tick**, so the port decides 2–3 ticks after a walk arrives, as 1.0 does at 30 fps ([above](#when-the-ai-reads-its-body-after-a-walk)), its body running on through the rest as 1.0's does.

## Not settled

- The actor field `+0x34` in state 5's stuck test (about 0.037 for a running rat), taken as its speed.
