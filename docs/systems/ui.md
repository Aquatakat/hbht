# UI: text, the conversation box, the pause menu, the HUD and the battle screen (roadmap 1F, M2, M3)

How the port lays out and draws the original's UI. The fonts and string tables are in [text](../formats/text.md), the menus' sounds in [audio](audio.md#sound-effects), and the runtime's controls in [runtime](runtime.md). Positions are in the original's 320 × 240 frame, and addresses are 1.0's.

The menus' layouts come from the machine code and the user's captures from the emulator (`roms/menu-*.png`, `hud-*.png`, `pause-*.png`; see the [reference rig](../reference-rig.md#emulator-captures)), mostly from the "Before final encounter" checkpoint. Compare against those captures in the console's own pixels (`--ui-screenshot` in the editor), where the port's classic layouts match within the noise of the console's 16-bit dither, about 6 a channel.

## The screen

The original draws its UI on a 320 × 240 screen; the Expansion Pak's high-resolution mode is 512 wide, and `borg8DlistInit` scales images to it. Every UI image is a texture rectangle with bilinear filtering and a combiner of texel × primitive colour (`N64BorgImageDraw`), so text is its sheet's texels times the text's colour, alpha too. At 1:1 the filter samples each texel exactly, and only scaled images are filtered.

- **The soft look is the video output.** The emulator's captures are 640 × 240: each line's 320 pixels spread over 640 by linear interpolation, lines untouched. A real console's VI adds anti-aliasing and divot filters, and the TV more.
- **Overscan bars** (`Graphics::EndList`): in 1.0 and 1.1, black rectangles 16 pixels at the left and right and 12 at the top and bottom, drawn after everything; the EU version draws 18 and 12. The UI's own boxes run under them.

`UiScreen` (in `Aidyn.Game`, which the editor shares) draws the UI the same way: views draw in a `SubViewport`, in its pixels, with linear filtering, and the screen is scaled up to fill the window by one of three filters (`UiFilter`): **VideoOut** (the default, each line doubled in width with the averages between, then scaled up sharp), **Pixels** (sharp) and **Smooth** (bilinear). The screen is `Lines` pixels tall, 240 by default, and as wide as the window's shape, never narrower than 4:3. Views lay themselves out by its size; a taller screen draws the UI smaller, with room for more, and text is always drawn pixel for pixel and wraps at the same widths. The canvas blends in on-screen values, premultiplied, so a glyph's alpha and colour multiply as the N64's do.

**Scaled images sample as the RDP does:** its first pixel on the first texel and a step of the scale per pixel, where Godot's linear filter starts half a step in. `UiPainter` offsets the source rectangle to match.

## Laying text out

`TextLayout` (`Aidyn.Core.Ui`) is the font engine's layout, over a font record (`Font::PrintMain`, `GetHeightScaled`, `GetWidthScaled`):

- A character draws its glyph at the pen, as wide as its measured width and a whole cell tall, and moves the pen by that width. A character with no glyph is skipped and takes no room.
- **Text wraps only at spaces.** After a space, if the next word would end past the right edge, the line breaks and the space is dropped. A space at the start of a line takes no room, and a word longer than the line runs past the edge. `\n` breaks the line.
- Lines are the cell's height plus 2 apart: 11 pixels in both fonts.
- Text is drawn only inside its widget's bounds. A glyph across an edge is cut, and one outside is dropped. `UiPainter`'s text and images take a clip box, which crops without moving where text wraps.
- Characters from `0x7F` are the controller buttons, drawn in their own colour with the text's alpha.

## The conversation box

`DialogueBox` (`Aidyn.Core.Ui`) places what a conversation shows, as `DiaMenuSub::InitDefault` and `DialougeClass::Setup` do, and `ConversationBoxView` draws it in the main font. Backdrops and the help menu draw on a full-screen image instead ([below](#books-and-the-help-menu)). The box is laid out in the 320 × 240 frame, placed at the bottom centre of the UI screen. A on its page is `DialogueBox.Accept` ([scripts](scripts.md#how-it-walks)), which conversations, cut-scenes and the editor's script page all press through.

- **The original's band** is translucent black: image `0x1C4`, a single white texel of alpha 136, drawn black. It runs right across the screen from the box's top to 232 (180 plus the portrait border's height), so the overscan bars cut it at 16, 304 and 228. Above it a 10-pixel gradient fades upward, image `0x1C3` squeezed from 16 pixels.
- **The port floats it**, the user's choice, since a wide screen has no bars to cut it off: the band runs from 16 to 304 and down to 228, feathered on every side from the pieces the original's popups are made of (`WidgetChoiceDia::SetImages`, see `FeatheredPanel`): `0x1C3` above, `0x1C2` below, `0x1C0` left and `0x1C1` right, each fading outward from alpha 136, and the rounded corners `0x1BC`–`0x1BF`.
- **The portrait's border** (`0xDF`, 52 × 52) is at x 20, level with the box's top, and the portrait (40 × 40) sits 6 pixels inside it. A speaker with no portrait gets `0x0125` (`BORG8_PortraitNPCMale1`).
- **The line** is in (200, 200, 200), from x 78 to 294, and ends 2 pixels above what's under it: the band's bottom, or the choices. The box's top is 4 pixels above the line, but never below 180, so a short line sits at y 184, and the box grows upward from five lines.
- **Choices** are in tan (200, 180, 100), from x 88, 4 pixels apart, and end at 228. The cursor's choice pulses toward (50, 45, 25, 63) and back over 24 frames each way, whole steps per channel (`WidgetScrollMenu::Tick`).
- **A line before its own speaker's choices shows alone.** When a menu's first choice has the line's speaker, the line shows first, and A brings up the choices alone under Alaron's portrait. The box shrinks to the choices, but the portrait and its border stay where the line put them, since `Setup` doesn't move them on that path.

Not ported yet: the widgets' fade in and out (`fadeFloatMirror`) and scrolling a menu taller than its bounds.

## The pause menu

The System, Party and World tabs (`BigAssMenu.cpp`, `DollMenu*.cpp`), with the Party tab's barter and training states.

- **The tabs are one long scroll.** Each tab is a widget tree in its own 320 × 240 frame, 320 pixels apart, and changing tab slides them all. Over them is a 3D scroll, `aidyn:actor/0x0E6B` (scene `0x0E63`), seen from above, which slides with them: System shows its wooden roller at the left, World at the right, Party neither. It unrolls as the menu opens and rolls up as it closes ([opening and closing](#opening-and-closing)).
- **Each tab has a full-screen image** over the scroll (`pause_menu_borg8`): `0x00D8`, `0x00D9` and `0x00DA`, all 320 × 240.
- **The menus keep the original's layouts at every screen size** ([ADR 0014](../adr/0014-menus-keep-the-originals-layouts.md)): each tab's image is drawn whole at the screen's centre and filled in at the original's coordinates.

### The Party tab

`0x00D9` is one parchment with the silhouette and its slot frames, the portrait frames, the labels HitPoints, Level and Gold, the HP bar's frame, the knotwork divider down the middle and "Details" with the C-down button painted in; none of those labels are in the string tables. The widgets fill it in.

- **The header** (`DollMenuPortraits`, `WidgetHealthGold`): the member's portrait at (22, 26), 40 square, and the others' at (67, 39), (96, 39) and (125, 39), 24 square, in party order from the member shown. The name at (69, 28) in the slim font. Under the large portrait, 4 below it, the aspect's icon and 2 after it the school's. In the main font, hit points at (223, 41) and their maximum at (255, 41), the level at (223, 55) and gold at (223, 69). The bar, `0x00E0`, is drawn 80 × 10 at (220, 27) and cut to hit points ÷ maximum × 80, truncated.
- **The silhouette** (`DollEquipmentMenu`): 15 slots, 16 square, each the worn item's icon or the slot's own image of what it takes (`gItemSlotBorg8Indecies`). A gear slot shows the first, second or third piece of its kind in the gear's order. The helmet's slot shows helmets.
- **The list** (`DollMenuLists`) is one of four, in the order the stick steps through: inventory, spells, skills, stats (training drops stats; barter keeps only the inventory). It's at (160, 101), bounded to x 300 and y 205, in the slim font, rows in (130, 80, 80). Its title image is centred at x 230, 20 above it. The arrows that show hidden rows are 15 in from each side and 13 above, in (225, 225, 225). Changing member keeps each list's cursor: stats clamp it to the last row, skills and spells go back to the top if it's past the end.
  - **The cursor's row pulses** from (68, 42, 34) toward (151, 141, 191) and back over 20 steps (`WidgetFastScrollMenu.Step`, `0x80028b00`), but two steps a frame: each list's step steps its scroll menu once at the end of the lists' shared layout (`PartyMenu.ListBaseStep`, `0x80030888`, at `0x800309cc`) and again itself (`0x80035770` for the inventory). So it shows only even steps, peaking at 18, 19 frames a cycle, twice the other menus' pulses. The same doubling plays the cursor sound twice. Barter's and training's lists are the same widgets.
  - **Inventory**, 13 pixels a row: the wearer's portrait and the item's icon at 12 square, then the count at 26 and the name at 42, or 30 without a count. Worn items first, each member's weapon, armor, shield and gear, the member shown first and then round the party, then what's carried. Each part is sorted by kind and then by name in byte order. The kinds' order (`InventoryOrder`) is potions, weapons, wands, scrolls, amulets, rings, helmets, cloaks, gloves, boots, belts, armor, shields, key items, misc.
  - **Stats**, 11 a row: the six attributes' base values at 79, and modified less base, when it isn't 0, at 100, as "(+n)" in (0, 128, 0) or "(n)" in (128, 0, 0). Then 4 below the rows, XP Remaining (experience to spend), Total XP and Next Level (what the next level needs, or MAX), the values lined up 4 after the first label.
  - **Skills**, 13 a row: the skills above 0 with their icons, then the weapon skills and the shield, which have none; the level at 79 and the difference at 95.
  - **Spells**, 13 a row: the aspect's icon (a spell's special, Solar or Lunar), the school's at 12 and the spell's symbol at 24, the rank at 36 and the name at 50. Unschooled spells have no school icon.
- **C-down's details** (`WidgetItemDetail`), shown while it's held, on the 160 × 160 parchment `0x00E2` at (0, 58) with its shadow `0x00E4` at (5, 60), in the slim font from x 23 to 149 and from y 70:
  - **An item:** its icon and name level with the icon's foot, then label and value lines: a weapon's skill, aspect, Min. Strength, Base Hit and Damage; armor's aspect, Protection, a shield's Defense, Dexterity and Stealth. Then what it adds: a stat (Stamina as Spell Battery, what it holds now), a skill, Magic: and Spell: with the spell's name indented 16, Charges: on a wand, and Resists: with the element and the share it takes off. Armor ends with Total Protection and Total Defense, now and with it on, the second green or red, ending at y 213. Worn gear shows Min. Intelligence and, if it has one, Min. Strength; **the original prints each under the other's label, which the port puts right**. Potions, scrolls and other items show their description, and a scroll its spell's Min. Wizard Rank at y 187; key items and items without a description show nothing.
  - **A stat or skill:** its description. **A spell:** its symbol and name, its description, and at y 187 the Wizard rank it needs and the reagent it uses, with the reagent's icon at 9 square.
- **A red arrow** under the knotwork at (126, 207) points both ways in trek (`BORG8_SliderArrowLR`) and only left in combat, in (130, 80, 80).
- **Not ported yet:** the slot's and bar's crossfades when an item is equipped or a potion drunk (the same as a member change's), and what a mouse click lands on in the editor (hovering moves the cursor; the user left clicking for later).

### Changing member and list

The menu's widgets animate with tweens (`MenuTween.CreateShort` at `0x800cc880`, `CreateByte` `0x800ccb88`): a value moved from where it is to an end over so many frames, a step a frame, on a curve that eases in and out, 0.5 sin(πt − π/2) + 0.5 (`MenuTween.Ease`, `0x800e6590`), truncated to a whole number each step. A tween takes its frames and finishes on the next, so the widget is busy a frame longer. `MenuTween` in the core has the curves, and `MenuSlide<T>` (`MenuMotion.cs`) the frame count, which the Party tab's list slide and member change and the save screen's slide share. The widgets' fades are `WidgetFade` (`MenuMotion.cs`), 60 a frame in and out, which `PopupFade`, the journal, the backdrop page and the System tab's pages step, and the journal's arrows step by 30.

- **C-left shows the next member and C-right the one before** (`PartyMenu.CLeft` `0x8003e1c8`, `CRight` `0x8003e1f4`, slots 0xBC and 0xC4 of the menu's handlers, which the C-buttons' bits 0x2 and 0x1 call).
- **The portraits move** (`PartyMenu.PortraitsChange`, `0x8003b3cc`): each member's portrait moves and resizes from its old place to its new one over 15 frames, x, y, width and height each tweened. With C-left, the next member's grows into the large frame, the one shown shrinks into the last small one, and the others move one frame left. The white frames are painted into `0x00D9` and stay. One widget per member, drawn in party order.
- **The rest crossfades over the same 15 frames:** the name; the aspect's and school's icons, where they change; the hit points, their maximum and the level, always (`PartyMenu.HealthGoldChange`, `0x8003be5c`); and the silhouette's slots. The bar's width tweens to the new member's. The gold and the list on the right change at once.
- **The silhouette** (`PartyMenu.EquipmentChange`, `0x8003e374`): the original crossfades each slot whose item differs. The port crossfades a slot only where one member wears something in it and the other doesn't, and changes one worn item to another at once, the user's choice.
- **The stick's left shows the next list and its right the one before** (`PartyMenu.ListsLeft` `0x800319f0`, `ListsRight` `0x80031a10`), wrapping from stats to the inventory. The list, its title and its arrows slide together, the old and the new 140 pixels over 20 frames (`PartyMenu.ChangeList`, `0x80031620`): left brings the next list from x 20 to 160 and pushes the old from 160 to 300; right the other way. The rows are cut to the list's bounds, x 160 to 300 and y 101 to 205, and the title to x 170 to 300 (`PartyMenu.ListsCreate`, `0x80030c50`); the port cuts the title at 160 with the rows.
- **A change waits for the last** (`PartyMenu.Changing`, `0x800304b4`): no member change while the header, the silhouette or the lists are moving, and no list change while the lists are. Training's member change animates the same way; its lists don't slide yet.

### The model and the layouts

`PartyMenu` (`Aidyn.Core.Ui`) is the tab's state: the member shown, the list showing, each list's rows (`PartyMenuRows`) and cursor (`MenuList`), the silhouette's slots, and C-down's details (`MenuDetails`). `MenuImages` and `InventoryOrder` hold the chrome's image IDs and the kinds' order, which the tests check against the ROM's tables. `PartyTabView` (`Aidyn.Game`) draws it with the widget kit, `UiPainter`: text in either font, images whole, scaled, cut out or feathered, the parchment popup, and lists with a cursor and arrows.

**The Party tab's windows** are `IPartyWindow` (`Aidyn.Core.Ui`): the tab itself (`PartyMenu`), the loot window and shops (`BarterMenu`) and the training window (`TrainingMenu`), each a `WidgetDollMenu` in one of the original's pause states. The view holds one as `Window` and the pause menu sends it every press, so neither knows which is up: `Menu` is the header and lists under it (the window itself for the tab), and the window takes the cursor (`MoveCursor`), the stick's left and right (`Sideways`: the tab's next or previous list, the loot window's pile or party list, training's skills or spells), C-left and C-right (`ChangeMember`, which `ChangesMember` says the loot window ignores), A, B, C-down (`PressDetails`, `ReleaseDetails`, `CloseMessage`), C-up (`Appraise`, which only the loot window and shops take) and `Step`, and says when it closed itself (`Closed`) and what popups are up (`HasPopup`, `Overlay`, `DetailsPane`). `PartyTabView` draws the header and lists for every window and a window's own parts, with a case in its `_Draw` and `Hover` for each; combat's post-battle window (pause state 5) and its pause menu will be further windows, each an `IPartyWindow` and a case in the view.

**Lists are laid out in the core.** `ListLayout` is a list's bounds in the 320 × 240 frame and its rows' pitch, which ADR 0014 fixes: `PartyMenu.Layout` (the tab's lists, 8 rows of 13, the stats 9 of 11), `BarterMenu.PileLayout` and `TrainingMenu.Layout` (6 of 13) and `CraftingMenu.Layout` (11 or 10 rows of a slim-font line, or 4 of a member's 28). The menus build their lists with the layout's count (`ListLayout.List`), so a list scrolls with no view drawn, and the view draws a list and tests the mouse against the same layout (`UiPainter.List`, `Arrows` and `ListLayout.RowAt`) instead of repeating the pitch and bounds. The one a view moves is the Party tab's list as it slides (`Shifted`). A wide layout, growing the original's sheet to fill the screen, was tried and dropped: bespoke art leaves seams when grown, and new layouts want new art, which is for mods after 1.0.

### Opening and closing

`PauseScroll` in the core has the rules, and `PauseMenu` and `PauseScrollView` in the runtime draw them (`PauseMenu.DarkenStep` at `0x8004847c`, `DrawBackdrop` `0x800484cc`, the menu's `OpenStep` `0x8002f1d0`, `CloseStep` `0x8002f4d4`, `SlideOut` `0x8002e9d0` and `Update` `0x8002ee38`).

- **Everything in the menu steps once a frame.** The menu's frame (`PauseMenu.Frame`) counts its delta, but the widgets, the scroll and the fades take one step a frame whatever it is (`WidgetManager.Update`, `0x800ad10c`). So their times are in frames. The port runs the menu at 60 frames a second (`Cadence.MenuFrameTicks`), which matches the original's timing in the emulator, tweens and pulses alike (`MenuPulse`).
- **The world freezes and darkens first.** Any way into the menu (Start, a shop or trainer's script, a container) copies the frame on screen into a 320 × 240 image (`GetSnapShot`, `0x800208b4`), drawn behind the menu multiplied by a grey (`PauseMenu.BackdropTint`, `0x800d9564`): 255, then 10 less a frame to 128 (the last step 7), 13 frames. The menu is built on the 14th. The copy isn't blurred. Once the menu is open, the original draws black behind it and only the copy's top and bottom 40 lines, which the scroll leaves showing.
- **In the port the world isn't copied but drawn on, still**, the user's choice: the menu stops trek, with the scenery's actors and the free camera, and the world and the HUD go on being drawn as they stand under a black layer at one less the tint. So resizing the window redraws the world at its new shape, and the HUD is laid out again as the Menu size option changes. Keep the HUD drawn while the menu or a page shows: hiding it at the start of each tick left it missing for a tick after the menu closed and under pages opened as a conversation ended. The port still copies the frame as the menu opens, for a save's screenshot ([below](#saving-and-loading)).
- **The scroll.** The actor's states, by request: idle (0, 11 frames, the rollers at ±35.3, off the frame), opening on System or World (22, 37 frames, the rollers parting from ±2.6), opening on Party (10, 53 frames: the scroll comes up from below rolled, then parts from the middle to about ±13.5, then idles), closing from Party (19, 53) and from System or World (11, 39); 20 and 21 aren't the menu's. The opening is the tab's: barter, training and the loot window open on Party. The original's actors take a request for one step and then go back to 0, so each plays once. The menu sets the scene's x by the tab, 27 for System, 0 for Party and −26 for World (`0x800de9c8`), and slides it with the tabs, 1/32 of the way a frame. Its camera is 27 above the scene, looking straight down with −Z up the screen, 45° over the 240 lines, near 1 and far 256. It's drawn over the tabs. The scene's two lights light it as directional lights, warm from the left and dim blue from the right, with no ambient.
- **The tabs show only where the scroll has unrolled.** Each tab's image and widgets are cut to columns of the frame (`PauseMenu.ClipLeft` and `ClipRight`, `0x800d9568`), in x only, that follow the scroll's frame f in a straight line:

  | Tab | Opening | Closing |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | System | from the right: the left edge 320 until frame 8, then 0 by 36 | from the left: the left edge 0 at frame 1, 320 by 31 |
  | Party | from the middle: 160 to 160 until frame 36, then 0 to 320 by 55 | to the middle: 0 to 320 at frame 1, 160 to 160 by 14 |
  | World | from the left: the right edge −5 until frame 6, then 320 by 35 | from the right: the right edge 320 at frame 2, 0 by 32 |

  Each opens when its animation ends: Party at frame 53, its clip 17 to 303, its tab showing from frame 37; System and World at 37. Only then does the menu take input. The original's clips stop short of the frame's edges, behind the overscan. The port shows those columns, so a side within 17 columns of the frame's edge counts as at it (`PauseScroll.Overscan`), and it also cuts the tabs at the rollers, along their middles (`PauseScrollView.Arrange`), so the parchment never shows past one; a side at the frame's edge reaches the roller on that side. The user chose both.
- **On screens wider and taller than the frame**, the port changes what the original's scroll does beyond it, the user's choice (`PauseScroll`, `PauseScrollView`):
  - **The animations run on from one another.** The Party opening's last 9 frames are the System and World opening's first 9, and the System and World closing's frames 31 to 39 are the Party closing's 1 to 9. So the Party tab's scroll runs on into the System and World opening at its frame 10 (`ActorStateMachine.Join`), its rollers going out to the ends of the System and World tabs rather than jumping there once off the frame. The menu still takes input from frame 53. Closing from Party, the System and World closing brings the rollers back in to the frame's edges, then the Party closing runs from its first frame.
  - **Fades.** The Party scroll fades in over its opening's first 12 frames, and out over its closing's last 12, as it comes up from below the frame. The System and World tabs and their scroll fade out together from their closing's frame 29, gone by its end.
  - **Each roller has its own camera** past the frame, following it, its picture moving out with the tabs a tab's 320 pixels a tab. One camera over the scene's middle showed a roller further out wider and reaching in over its tab, and let the World roller drift 13 pixels in at Party, as the scene's x steps aren't quite 320 pixels at the rollers' depth.
  - **Barter and training** can't change tab, so their scroll runs on only to frame 11 of the System and World opening and holds there, the rollers just past what the Party tab draws.
  - **The scroll's viewports don't interpolate** between physics ticks, or its first frame would show where it was reset to.
- **Closing,** the scroll rolls up at once (`SlideOut` steps it), and when its animation ends the port hides it, and the world brightens 10 a frame back to 255, 13 frames, before trek goes on. The loot window and shops (Game.Mode 7) snap back to full brightness instead. Camping waits for the menu to close; loading a save closes it at once.
- **How it ends** is its exit code (`PauseMenu.Exit`, `0x80048e64`, a jump table at `0x800d1240`), which the trek pause menu leaves as it rolls up and which sets trek's next state as the slide ends (`PauseMenu.NextTrekState`, `0x800df031`, copied at `0x8004898c`): 0 for B or Start (trek runs on, state 3), 1 for Camp (state 0x11, the camp), 2 for the Theatre's cinematic (states 0x16 and 0x17), 3 for Help (state 0x12, script `0x054B`), 4 once a save has loaded (state 4, trek built again from it) and 5 for the credits (state 0x19). The loot window, shops and training close straight to trek (`PauseMenu.CloseToTrek`, `0x80048f40`). In the port the pages say which (`PauseExit`: `SystemMenu.Accept` and `WorldMenu.Accept` return it), the menu keeps it as it rolls up, and `PauseMenu.Step`, which the game calls every tick after its own, returns it once the menu has closed; the game carries it out ([runtime](runtime.md)).
- **The tabs** are the menu's `IPauseTab`s: each view is made the first time the menu opens and given each opening's new model, is veiled beside the tab showing, says whether the menu can slide away from it, and steps only while it shows.
- **The post-battle window** (pause state 5) opens and closes without the scroll, its widgets fading in and out 60 a frame (`OpenFade`, `CloseFade`). That comes with combat.
- **Each opening starts the tab afresh:** the first member, the inventory, the top row. The original remembers only which tab was open ([changing tab](#changing-tab)), so the presses to reach something are the same every time.
- **Input is translated once** (`MenuStick.Translate`): an event becomes a `MenuAction` (up, down, left, right, A, B, Start, C-down going down and up, C-up, C-left, C-right and the two tab buttons), the movement keys and the D-pad counting alike, and every handler (the pause menu's tabs, trek's popups, conversations, backdrops, cut-scenes, cinematics and credits) switches on it.
- **The stick counts once as it crosses a threshold** (`MenuStick`, shared with trek's popups, conversations and cut-scenes). The original's menus count a direction from 56 of the N64 stick's 80, 0.7 (`Controller::ReadInput`, `0x800915d8`); ares scales a full push to about 100, so the port counts from 0.58 and lets go below 0.5. **Held,** the stick or the D-pad repeats after 0.5 s and then 12 a second: the original's widgets count 30 of the controller's 60 samples a second, then 5 (`BaseWidget::Control`, `0x800ad708`), for the stick, the D-pad, C-up and C-down.

### The Party tab's actions

What A does (`DollMenuLists::AFunc`), in `PartyMenu.Actions`, with the popups as `MenuMessage` and `CraftingMenu` and `PartyTabView` drawing them.

- **The inventory** (`WidgetInvShop::AFunc`). A on a worn item takes it off into the inventory, silently staying on if the inventory has no room. The Archmage Staff stays, and says it "contains powerful magic that only" its wielder "can control" (`XPowerfulMagic`). A on a carried item: a potion is drunk by the member shown ([items](items.md#potions)); a scroll is read, which says how it went whatever happens (`Party::UseScroll`); anything else is equipped on the member shown, putting back what it replaces ([items](items.md#equipment)). A refusal says why, from `equip_error_labels` (common strings 95–101): the aspect, Strength or Intelligence it needs, or else "You cannot equip that item!", which is also what too many rings, no free gear slot and no room for what it replaces say (`GetEquipError3` and the item kinds' functions return 1). The port's own refusal, replacing the staff, uses the staff's message. After equipping, the cursor stays on what's left of the stack, or on its row, which the next item moves up into, so it never jumps up to the worn items; after taking off, it stays on its row. **Open:** equipping the last item of the list, when no item moves up into its row; the port leaves the cursor clamped to the list's last row ([user checks](../user-checks.md)).
- **The skills** (`WidgetSkillInfo::AFunc`, trek only): A on the Alchemist, Mechanic or Healer opens their popup; on any other skill, a spell or a stat it does nothing. The Mechanic with no hide for a recipe their base level reaches says "You do not have the components to create any armor." instead (`makeArmorMenu`).
- **A popup takes every press** while it's open, as the original's widget handler gives them to the top widget: the stick moves its cursor, A chooses, B closes it, and the member and list don't change. `MenuOverlay` (`Aidyn.Core.Ui`) is that rule once, for the popups the Party tab, the loot and shop window, the training window, the World tab and the save screens keep (each holds one as `Overlay`): a message, the help, a question over choices (`MenuChoice`, with `MenuChoice.Question` for the white question over tan answers the loot and training windows ask) and C-down's details (`DetailsPane`). `Top` is the newest, in the order message, help, choice, details; the menu closes that one (`Dismiss` closes a message, else the help) and the one under it takes the next press. `Step` steps the choice's pulse and the details' fade, and `Highlight` is the cursor's colour. The menus keep their own popups beside it, and count them in their own `HasPopup`: the Party tab's crafting popup and the drop question (`BarterConfirm`). ### The credits

The credits widget (game mode 0x13, `Credits.Frame` at `0x8001f648`, `Credits.Start` at `0x8002c0b0`, `Credits.Step` at `0x8002c6d8`), which the System tab's Credits row plays, and which the ending will (2.10). `CreditsRoll` in the core, drawn by `CreditsLayer`, from the credits record (`aidyn:credits/0`), which carries the cards, the number shown one at a time and "The End".

- **The way in:** the row (id 9999 in `pauseMenuSelectCallBack`, `0x800403ac`) sets `Credits.Source` to 1 and the menu exits with code 5 (`PauseMenu.Exit`, `0x80048e64`): it rolls up as closing does, then trek's state 0x19 returns mode 0x13. There's no fade: the screen cuts to black, filled every frame (`Screen.FillRect`), and the trek is held as it stood, not freed.
- **The cards.** `Credits.Start` cuts the credits' string table (table 10) into 33 cards by a table of their first lines in the code (`Credits.CardStarts`, `0x800CF060`, ROM `0xCFC60`); a card's first line is its heading, in yellow (225, 225, 0), and the rest its names, in white (225, 225, 225). There's no marker in the text: THQ Credits and Studio X are cards of a heading alone. Each line is a text widget in the main font (`WidgetClipText`), 11 pixels a line, centred across at (320 − width) / 2; three names start with a newline, which leaves a blank line above them. The table's last line, "The End", is the ending's alone.
- **The first 12 cards** (H2O Credits to Lead Tester) show one at a time (`Credits.StepCards`, `0x8002c7b8`): each is moved up from the screen's foot by half its height and the screen's, which centres it, fades in by 4 × delta a frame, holds for 120 / delta frames and fades out by 4 × delta, and the next is set up on the frame after. They stop when the card ending at line 29 (a literal `0x1D`), Designers, comes up: it's the first to scroll.
- **The rest scroll** (`Credits.StepScroll`, `0x8002ca64`), every one starting with its top at the screen's foot. Every other frame, the first card still on screen moves up a pixel, and each after it too once the one before has its foot 32 pixels above the screen's, so the cards come 32 apart. A card that's gone off the top is passed. This counts frames, not ticks; the original runs it at 60 frames a second, and so does the port. The scroll ends when Special Thanks has gone off the top, on a black screen.
- **The end:** from the System tab, the credits end there (`Credits.StepEnd`, `0x8002cb50`). From the ending, "The End" shows in white at y 114, centred, fading in by 2 × delta a frame, holding 240 frames and fading out, and then the game goes back to the title.
- **A, B and Start** end them at once, with no fade (`Credits.Skip`, `0x8002cd90`); the ending's can't be skipped.
- **The music:** `Credits.Start` plays 0x05D9 (the sounds record's `Credits`), looped, at the music volume as it is then, and sets the sound and music volumes to 0, so trek's music and the map's sounds go on silently under it (`ProcessAudioBubbles` keeps running; `GameAudio.Muted` in the port). As they end (`Credits.Destroy`, `0x8002c5a0`) 0x05D9 stops at once and the volumes are set back. The calendar and the weather stand still; the play time counts on.
- **The way out:** the System tab's credits return mode 9: trek fades in from black over 30 ticks (`0x800ce214`, 1/30), with input ignored, and then the menu opens on the System tab's list with the cursor on Load Game, rolling in as it always does. The original skips the fade-in the first time after the console starts (its flag `0x800db06c` is 0 only then); the Theatre's way back shares this. The port always fades in.
- **In the port** they're drawn on a UI screen of their own, as tall as the menu's lines, with the cards centred across and down it.
- **Not ported yet:** the ending's credits, which follow the last cinematic ([cinematics](cinematics.md#what-starts-them)), and the title it goes back to.

## The trek HUD

Trek's HUD (`draw_hud_elements`, `0x80020bd4`) is the sundial at the bottom left, the party's portraits at the bottom centre, and at the bottom right the minimap where the player's chunk has a piece, or else the compass under an outdoor sky (sky type 3), or nothing. `TrekHud` (`Aidyn.Core.Ui`) is the state, with `Sundial`, `Compass`, `PartyPortraits` and `Minimap`; `TrekLoop` owns it and `HudView` draws it.

- **Drawn only in trek frames**, and not while a conversation or sign shows (`diaClass->menu`), the pause menu, a shop, training or a cut-scene. Popups draw over it. Every colour and alpha is the scene's brightness times its own, so it fades with the screen.
- **The images** are texture rectangles at quarter-pixel positions, texel × colour, blended (`borg8DlistInit` with flag 6, `Borg8_DrawSimple`). The rings and the frames have their feathering painted in.

### The sundial

`Sundial::Draw` (`0x800248a4`), centred on (49, 199):

- **The ring** `0x001B` (60 square) at (19, 169), white at alpha 80.
- **The moon** at (39, 189), in (160, 160, 220) at alpha 160, by its phase ([rendering](rendering.md#the-skys-objects)): new `0x01B8`, crescent `0x01B3` or `0x01B4` and quarter `0x01B6` or `0x01B7` in the first and second halves of the moon's four weeks (`(week & 3) >> 1`), full `0x01B5`. It shows at every hour.
- **The rays** `0x01BA` (50 square) at (24, 174), at alpha 80, in the hour's colour.
- **The sun** goes round the ring **twice a day**, 30° an hour clockwise from the top, so noon and midnight are both at the top: its centre is (49 + 25 sin a, 199 − 25 cos a), a in whole degrees, (int)(time of day × 720) mod 720. By day it's the glow `0x01BB`, by night the small dot `0x01B9`, each 20 square.
- **The hour's colour**, for the rays and the sun: from 7 to 21 peach (255, 190, 150), the glow at alpha 160 and no dot; from 23 to 5 blue (120, 100, 190), the dot at 160 and no glow. At dawn (5 to 7) and dusk (21 to 23) the colour blends linearly between them, and the glow and dot cross-fade at 80 an hour. The original dims day's and night's colour by the scene's brightness but not the blend's.
- **The moon and sun hide in caves** and interiors: under sky type 4 (terrains 6, 8, 9, 11 and 13–16, the port's terrains that don't draw the sky). The original decides only on crossing from a chunk under one sky to the other (`TeleportPlayer` at `0x8000bfd0`, through `Sundial::ToggleSun` and `ToggleMoon`, whose names are swapped), and trek starting shows them wherever it is (`Sundial::Init`), so after loading in a cave they show until the player leaves and comes back. **The port fixes it**, deciding by the sky when trek starts too, the user's choice. The Detect Moon Phase and Detect Sun Phase spells show them too (`0x800703c8`), which wait for 2.5. The ring and rays always show.

### The compass

`Compass::Draw` (`0x800240b8`), centred on (271, 199):

- **The ring** is the sundial's, at (241, 169), white at alpha 80.
- **The letters** are 16-square images, N `0x0018`, S `0x0019`, W `0x001A` and E `0x0017`, drawn in that order in (160, 160, 220), each centred 25 out from the centre. With (a, b) the camera's look in X and Z, normalised (`rotationXZ`): N at (−25a, 25b) and alpha 160 − 95b, S at (25a, −25b) and 160 + 95b, W at (25b, 25a) and 160 − 95a, E at (−25b, −25a) and 160 + 95a. So the way the camera looks is at the top, at alpha 255, and behind it at the bottom, at 65. North is −Z and east +X.
- **It shows** only under an outdoor sky with no minimap showing.

### The minimap

The minimap (`MiniMap`, `minimap.cpp`) is pieces of a drawn plan, one per chunk or part of one, in eight map sets (`LoadData` `0x80049a08`, `Update` `0x8004a5a8`, `Render` `0x8004ad14`, `UpdateSection` `0x8004af0c`, `LoadSections` `0x8004b404`, `toggle_show_minimap` `0x8004b6f0`, `ShowingSpecialSection` `0x8004b96c` and `BarrowsFlags` `0x8004bfac`). The pieces and the rules are the minimap records ([hardcoded content](../hardcoded-content.md)).

- **Which map sets:** Gwernia castle (4), Ugarit 2 (14), Erromon (7), the Ehud underground (12), the Jundar interiors' mage school (11), the misc interiors' Roog entrance (6, only K03), the Barrows (9) and the Roog cave (10), loaded with the map set (`BorgMaps::LoadMap`).
- **The player's piece** is the first piece for the chunk he's in, but where a rule says otherwise. The rules: Gwernia's courtyard (I07) by floor, its ground by which side (y under 4: z above 26 or not; under 8; above); the castle walls (A01) by quarter (x under 35, z under 25); the tower (O09) and the mage school's stairs (A01) by floor; the Ehud underground's C11 by quarter (48) and I09 by x under 16; the Ehud maze room (O05) and the Roog entrance (K03) by the first of their room flags set (4222–4232, 4233–4237), or none; and in the Barrows, nine room flags (4286–4294) that show a room's other state, some another chunk's piece, and E01's strip under x 1.3 showing D01's. Positions are the player's in the chunk. The courtyard's piece `0x29` is never chosen, though it shows dim, seen from the start. A chunk with no piece shows no minimap, and the compass or nothing takes its place.
- **The box** is `0x00F5`, a square with a feathered rim, in black, stretched around the plan with a border of 8 times the scale. **Small**, with the portraits down: the plan from (237, 163) to (297, 223), the box 76 square at (229, 155), the player's piece centred, (267 − x − w/2, 193 − y − h/2) for a piece at (x, y) of width w and height h, halved by shifts. **Large**, with them up: from (177, 52) to (297, 172), the box 152 square at (161, 36), the whole plan from the corner. Over the pieces, the vignette `0x00F3` in black, stretched to the plan. The pieces are cut to the plan's area.
- **Growing and shrinking** go with the portraits (`toggle_show_minimap`, which does nothing if it's that size already): the box's edges, the scale and the plan's place each move a 14th of the way they started from a frame, so 14 frames, linearly.
- **The group:** the pieces that show are the player's group's seen ones: a floor or a building. Another group's piece takes over at once; the old group fades out, 10 an alpha a frame, and the plan snaps to the new piece. A piece of the same group slides into place over 14 frames.
- **The colours:** the player's piece starts at grey 155 and alpha 0, rises 10 an alpha a frame to 255, its grey shimmering between 165 and 175 until its alpha is past 145, then 155. The others settle at grey 96 and alpha 128, 10 a frame. The original draws the group's pieces newest first, so the first piece of the table is on top.
- **Seeing** a piece is when it's first chosen. The courtyard's five and the tower's upper floor in Gwernia castle, and the mage school's stairs' upper floors, are seen from the start. The original saves one bit a piece, 188 in all, in the table's order (`MiniMap_Save`, `0x80049760`); the port saves each minimap's seen pieces ([saves](saves.md)).
- **No sounds.**

### In the port

- **On other screens,** each part keeps its place from the bottom and from its side: the sundial from the left, the compass or minimap from the right, and the portraits centred.
- **Frames:** the portraits' slide and the minimap's choice and animation step once every two ticks, the original's frame at an idealized 30 fps ([runtime](runtime.md)).
- **The fade:** the runtime's fade is a black overlay on trek's whole UI screen, so the HUD is drawn at full brightness under it, where the original dims each colour and alpha.
- **Not ported yet:** the Detect Moon Phase and Detect Sun Phase spells, and the editor showing the HUD.

## The battle screen

What a battle draws over its arena (`combatgui.cpp`, `0x80085A80`–`0x80087C60`, and the radar, `0x8008DD30`–`0x8008E3C0`), matched to the user's capture `roms/combat.png` (Brenna's turn against four rats with six in reserve) and checkpoint 02's RAM. `CombatGui` (`Aidyn.Core.Ui`) is the model, which the battle holds (`Battle.Gui`) and steps each tick; `CombatRadar` and `CombatLogBox` are its radar and log box; `CombatGuiView` (`Aidyn.Game`) draws it on trek's UI screen. Every part is drawn in every state of a battle, the original's in this order (`CombatGui.Draw`, `0x80085F64`; `DrawAll`, `0x800863E4`): the log box, the radar, the frames, the portraits, the HP bars.

### The portraits

`CombatGui.Create` (`0x80085B80`) makes an entry for each party member, two placeholders and each of the enemy list, reserves too, and a frame (a "bar", `CombatGui.Bars`) for each member, two spares under them and each of the list (`CreateBars`, `0x800864F4`). Their order is fixed.

- **The frames** are opaque images drawn under the portraits and tinted, so only their rims show: large `0x01C6` (28 × 26) with a 23-square portrait, small `0x01C7` (14 × 13) with an 11 × 10 one, the portraits scaled from 40. The party's run down x 270 and the enemies' x 21, from y 18, 28 apart; the party's portrait is 4 in (its HP bar's strip on the left), the enemies' 2 (the strip on the right). The first four of the list are large; the rest are small, 12 apart from y 130 (overlapping a pixel), four at x 21 and then at 35. The two spares under the party (y 18 + 28 × size) are hidden, for whoever's in neither list (the Shadow, a summons). Empty frames draw nothing.
- **Who goes where** (`AssignPortrait`, `0x80085FC8`, as `Combat.CreateBody` makes each body): the first waiting entry of the combatant's entity takes its slot and the first free frame of its side, so the party in slot order top to bottom and the enemies on the field in the large frames. The first tick gives the reserves the small frames in the list's order (`0x80086C84`). A reserve that joins (session 5, `Combat.SpawnReserves`) swaps frames with the first dead or fled enemy, which goes out of the fight into the small frame. Anyone in neither list takes a placeholder and the first spare.
- **They slide** (`PlaceInBar`, `0x800869F4`): every portrait starts at (160, 120), 40 square, and goes to its frame over 60 ticks of the frame's delta (`0x800D43E4`), so a second whatever the frame rate: x in a straight line, y easing out (`MenuTween.EaseOut`), the width and height easing in and out (`MenuTween.Ease`). At the start that's under the fade in.
- **The dead and fled** (`MarkDead` `0x80086208` as the body is freed, `MarkFled` `0x80086230` as a flight ends, `MarkReserveFled` `0x80086338`): the skull `0x016D` fades in over the portrait, 0 to 255 over 60 ticks, sized to it, and the portrait stays in its frame. A fled one's frame is orange (200, 164, 0, 255). They lose their HP bar and tints.
- **The HP bars** (`DrawHpBars`, `0x80087168`), over everything, at the frame at once and from the live hit points: 3 wide and the portrait's height, 4 left of the party's portrait (x 270) and 24 right of an enemy's (47), black, then the fill from the bottom, (int)(height × hp ÷ max) and never under a pixel. Its colour has no steps (`HpColour`, `0x80085A80`): u = min(255, hp × 255 ÷ max), (255 − u, u, 0).
- **The tints** are the frames' colours (`CombatGui.TintAll`, `0x8008713C`), worked out every frame, each rule over the one before: black at alpha 200; orange for a fled one; then for each in play, orange while the AI has it fleeing (`EnemyAI.IsFleeing`), dim green (0, 200, 0, 150) as set A's candidate (what the current combatant can attack), **dim red (200, 0, 0, 150) as set B's candidate**, bright green (0, 255, 0, 255) as set A's selected while an attack or a flask is aimed (states 9 and 18), and bright red (255, 0, 0, 255) as set B's selected while a spell or its area is (10 and 11) (`TintByState`, `0x80086EB4`). They don't pulse, and side doesn't matter.
- **Why red shows when nothing's being cast:** set B holds what the current combatant's *selected* spell could be cast at, and a caster keeps a selected spell (its entity's `+0x3D` and `+0x3E`) from turn to turn. So on Alaron's, Rheda's or Sholeh's turn, the dim red is on everyone their spell reaches, the party too for a healing spell, from the turn's first frame and in every state. Brenna and the rats have no spell, so their turns, the capture's among them, show none. All 11 checkpoints' frames match this.
- **C-left does nothing.** It toggles a flag (`CombatGui.Shown`, `0x800E3140`, in `Combat.ControlsByState` at `0x80085114`) that nothing reads, in 1.0 or the debug build, so nothing hides the screen. The port has no flag.

### The log box

A `TextBand` (`CombatGui.LogBoxCreate`, `0x80087A78`), the cinematics' caption box's widget ([cinematics](cinematics.md#the-caption-box)), not the conversation box's, that never drops away. `CombatLogBox` lays it out.

- **The original's** rectangle is (0, 180) to (246, 240), with no growing: the band from 180 down and the gradient 11 tall above it, both right across the frame; the line in the main font in (225, 225, 225, 200) at (76, 190), wrapping at 241 and cut at 230, which keeps it off the radar; the conversation box's border `0x00DF` at (20, 174) and the current combatant's portrait at (26, 180), level with the band's top. An enemy's turn shows the enemy's portrait. Before the first turn's line it shows only the band.
- **The port floats it**, the user's choice, as the caption and conversation boxes do: laid out in the original's frame at the bottom centre of the UI screen, the band from 16 to 304 down to the screen's foot, feathered beside it and above.
- **The lines** are the battle's (`CombatLog`, `Battle.Lines`, the struct's `+0x4FC4`–`+0x52C4`): shown, queued and a third. `Show` replaces the line at once (`Combat.ShowLog`, `0x8005E604`); `Queue` only keeps the next (`QueueLog`, `0x8005E658`), and `Flush` shows it at a state's change (`FlushQueuedLog`, `0x8005E6F4`: an attack's result as its animation ends, a cast's end, a potion), or the third in its place if Banish queued one (`QueueLog2`). So "Brenna attacks Giant Rat" shows at once, and "Giant Rat killed" queued behind it comes up as the blow lands. **There's no timer**: a line holds until the next.
- **The hold** (`+0x543C`, `Combat.HoldForMessage`, `0x80022AEC`): only a reserve fleeing as it arrives sets it. For 60 ticks the box shows "Enemy reinforcements flee" and the battle's states and input wait; then the shown line comes back. `CombatLog.HoldOn` and `StepHold` have the rule; session 5's reserves set it.
- **Locked** as the party falls (`TurnOrder.Defeat`, `0x8006A780`): its line, and in a non-lethal battle Alaron's portrait, and nothing changes the box after (`CombatLog.Lock`).

### The radar

`CombatRadar.Draw` (`0x8008DF04`):

- **The ring** is the sundial's, `0x001B`, at (245, 170), white at alpha 164 (the HUD's are 80), under the dots.
- **The dots** are 2 × 2, alpha 164, one for each body in use, by slot: the camera's owner yellow (150, 150, 0) at (274, 199), the party green (0, 150, 0) and the rest red (150, 0, 0), under the frames.
- **It's centred on the camera's owner** (`0x800D8448`): the current combatant, or while an attack is aimed the target cursor's body (session 5). **It turns with the camera**: with (a, b) the camera's look in X and Z (`rotationXZ`), a body (dx, dz) from the owner goes (dx × −b + dz × a) × 0.5 right and (dx × a + dz × b) × 0.5 up, truncated, so the way the camera looks is up. Half a pixel a unit. A body more than 20 pixels out is held at 20, its colour halved.
- **While a spell, its area or a flask is aimed** (states 10, 11 and 18), the dots of set B's selected throb, each channel that isn't 0 plus the pulse, and the rest are halved. The pulse (`CombatRadar.Step`, `0x8008DE44`) goes from 0 to −150 and back, 4 a frame, 76 frames a cycle. A flask aims with set A, which the radar doesn't read, so in state 18 every dot is halved.
- **Checked** against checkpoint 02 (Rheda's turn, the camera's look read from RAM): every dot where the original's arithmetic puts it (`BattleCheckpointTests`).

### On other screens

The user chose where the screen sits on a screen of another shape (`CombatGui.Draw`), and it follows the Menu size setting, as the HUD does:

- **The enemies' column** keeps its place from the screen's top left corner, and **the party's** from the top right.
- **The log box** floats at the bottom centre.
- **The radar** sits in the log box at the original's place, or in the screen's bottom right corner (`RadarAnchor`), its place in the frame kept from that corner, as the compass's is. On a 4:3 screen the two are the same. The runtime starts in the box; F9 swaps them and `--radar corner` starts in the corner.

### In the port

- **Ticks:** the portraits' tweens step a tick at a time (the original's delta), and the radar's pulse once a frame at 30 fps.
- **The fade** darkens the whole UI screen, as for the HUD; the original fades each widget's colour but not the HP bars' black.
- **The target sets** aren't ported (session 5): `Battle.Marks` holds each slot's `TargetMarks` and `Combatant.Fleeing` the AI's flag, which the tints and the radar read. F4 shows the tints in turn, an attack aimed and a spell aimed, and `--tints 1` or `2` starts with one.
- **Measured** against the capture in the UI screen's own pixels (`--ui-screenshot`): the portraits, the reserves' icons and the HP bars differ by about 4 a channel on average, within the console's dither; the text starts at (76, 190), and the ring's rim falls on the capture's rows and columns.
- **Not ported yet:** the numbers over the targets in states 10, 11 and 18 (`Combat.DrawTargetCounts`, `0x80021448`), with session 5.

## A faithful mode, later

The floating panel and the resizable screen are enhancements, which the user chose over the original's look, leaving a faithful mode for a setting later (ADR 0018, [roadmap](../roadmap.md) Phase 6). What it would take:

- **The screen.** `UiScreen` fixed at 320 × 240 whatever the window's shape, shown pillarboxed at 4:3, with the VideoOut filter. The 3D view would need the same 4:3 frame.
- **The overscan bars** over the whole frame, 3D and UI, drawn last, as `Graphics::EndList` does ([the screen](#the-screen)).
- **The conversation box.** A style for `DialogueBox`: a band right across the frame, from 0 to 320 and down to 232, with only the gradient above it: a `FeatheredPanel` whose bottom and side feathering are 0, which `UiImages.DrawPanel` already skips.
- **The battle screen's log box** as the original's band right across the frame from 180, with the radar in it ([the battle screen](#the-battle-screen)).
- **Other UI** is already at its original place on a 320 × 240 screen.
- **High-resolution mode**, if a setting offers the Expansion Pak's option: a 512 × 240 screen, where the original lays UI out in 320 × 240 and `borg8DlistInit` stretches every image 1.6 times across, bilinear. `UiScreen` would draw its 320-wide canvas into a 512-wide one with linear filtering before the VideoOut filter. Its overscan bars are 20 pixels at the sides.
- **The VI and the TV**: the VI's anti-aliasing and divot filters and a composite or CRT look would be further `UiFilter`s.

## In the editor

The editor shows the UI over a plain backdrop on the pages that use it: a script's player the conversation box, a loot table's roll and a container on the map the loot window, a shop's page its window, a trainer's character page the training window, and the inventory record's page the Party tab, whose buttons act on the editor's game. The UI screen passes the mouse to its views in its own pixels (`ForwardsMouse`), and hovering a choice or row moves the cursor. `UiScreenOptions` holds the zoom, aspect, lines and filter; the command-line flags for screenshots are in [inspector](inspector.md).
