# UI: text, the conversation box and the pause menu (roadmap 1F, M2)

How the port lays out and draws the original's UI. The fonts and string tables themselves are in [text](../formats/text.md).

## The screen

The original draws its UI on a 320 × 240 screen (`SCREEN_WIDTH`, `SCREEN_HEIGHT`); the Expansion Pak's high-resolution mode is 512 wide, and `borg8DlistInit` scales images to it. Every UI image is a texture rectangle with bilinear filtering and a combiner of texel × primitive colour (`N64BorgImageDraw`), so text is its sheet's texels times the text's colour, alpha too. At 1:1 the filter samples each texel exactly: text and portraits land on the framebuffer pixel for pixel, and only scaled images, such as a box's gradient or the party's portraits at 0.75, are filtered.

**The soft look is the video output.** The emulator's captures of the original are 640 × 240, and their columns alternate between the framebuffer's pixels and the average of the two either side (the compass's letters read 153, 109, 65 across). Each line's 320 pixels are spread over 640 by linear interpolation, and the lines aren't touched. On a real console the VI does that resampling and adds its own anti-aliasing and divot filters, and then a composite or S-Video signal and a CRT soften it further; the captures show only the first step.

**The original covers the screen's edges** with black bars for overscan (`Graphics::EndList`): in 1.0 and 1.1, 16 pixels at the left and right and 12 at the top and bottom, filled rectangles drawn after everything else. The EU version draws 18 and 12 with its image code. So the UI's own boxes run under the bars and are cut off by them.

`UiScreen` (in `Aidyn.Game`, which the editor shares) draws the UI the same way: views draw in a `SubViewport`, in its pixels, with linear filtering as the console has it, and the screen is scaled up afterwards to fill the window, by one of three filters (`UiFilter`). The screen is `Lines` pixels tall, 240 by default as the original's, and as wide as the window's shape, but never narrower than 4:3. Views lay themselves out by its size rather than assuming 320 × 240. A taller screen draws the UI smaller, with room for more: at 480 lines, text is half the size. Text itself is always drawn as authored, pixel for pixel, and wraps at the same widths. The filters:

- **VideoOut**, the default: as the captures show it, each line doubled in width with the averages between, then scaled up sharp.
- **Pixels:** sharp.
- **Smooth:** bilinear both ways.

The canvas blends in on-screen values, as Godot's 2D does without HDR, so a glyph's alpha and colour multiply as the N64's do. It's premultiplied, since alpha blending into a transparent target leaves colour times alpha, and the upscale filters average it that way. A later pass could add the VI's other filters or a CRT's.

## Laying text out

`TextLayout` (`Aidyn.Core.Ui`) is the font engine's layout, over a font record (`Font::PrintMain`, `GetHeightScaled`, `GetWidthScaled`):

- A character draws its glyph at the pen, as wide as its measured width and a whole cell tall, and moves the pen by that width. A character with no glyph is skipped and takes no room.
- **Text wraps only at spaces.** After a space, if the next word would end past the right edge, the line breaks and the space is dropped. A space at the start of a line takes no room, and a word longer than the line runs past the edge. `\n` breaks the line.
- Lines are the cell's height plus 2 apart: 11 pixels in both fonts.
- Text is drawn only inside its widget's bounds. A glyph across an edge is cut, and one outside is dropped.
- Characters from `0x7F` are the controller buttons, drawn in their own colour with the text's alpha.

## The conversation box

`DialogueBox` (`Aidyn.Core.Ui`) places what a conversation shows, as `DiaMenuSub::InitDefault` and `DialougeClass::Setup` do, and `ConversationBoxView` draws it in the main font. It's for conversations and the other modes without a backdrop; backdrops and the help menu draw on a full-screen image (`BigBGDialoug`), which isn't ported yet.

The box is laid out in the original's 320 × 240 screen, placed at the bottom centre of the UI screen, so on a 4:3 screen of 240 lines it's where the original's is, and elsewhere it keeps its size. The positions below are in that frame.

- **The original's band** is translucent black: its image, `0x1C4`, is a single white texel of alpha 136, drawn black. It runs right across the screen from the box's top to 180 plus the portrait border's height, 232, so the overscan bars cut it at 16, 304 and 228. Above it a 10-pixel gradient fades upward, image `0x1C3` squeezed from 16 pixels.
- **The port floats it** instead, since a wide screen has no bars to cut it off. The band runs from 16 to 304 and down to 228, where the bars would stop it, and it's feathered on every side where the bars were, from the pieces the original's own popups are made of (`WidgetChoiceDia::SetImages`, see `FeatheredPanel`): the 10-pixel gradient above, `0x1C2` below, `0x1C0` and `0x1C1` beside it, each fading outward from alpha 136, and the rounded corners `0x1BC`–`0x1BF`. On a 4:3 screen it looks as the original's does inside its bars. The decomp names three of the four gradients wrongly; the machine code puts `0x1C3` above a popup, `0x1C2` below, `0x1C0` left and `0x1C1` right, and the dialogue box's gradient is `0x1C3`.
- **The portrait's border** (`0xDF`, 52 × 52) is at x 20, level with the box's top, and the portrait (40 × 40) sits 6 pixels inside it. A speaker with no portrait gets `0x0125` (`BORG8_PortraitNPCMale1`).
- **The line** is in (200, 200, 200), from x 78 (the border's width + 26) to 294, and ends 2 pixels above what's under it: the band's bottom, or the choices. The box's top is 4 pixels above the line, but never below 180, so the box is at least the border's height: a short line sits at y 184, and the box grows upward from five lines.
- **Choices** are in tan (200, 180, 100), from x 88, 4 pixels apart, and end at 228. The cursor's choice pulses toward (50, 45, 25, 63) and back over 24 frames each way, whole steps per channel (`WidgetScrollMenu::Tick`).
- **A line before its own speaker's choices shows alone.** When a menu's first choice has the line's speaker, the line shows first, and A brings up the choices alone under Alaron's portrait. The box shrinks to the choices, but the portrait and its border stay where the line put them, since `Setup` doesn't move them on that path.

Not ported yet: the widgets' fade in and out (`fadeFloatMirror`), scrolling a menu taller than its bounds, and the full-screen backdrops' text with its shadow (`WidgetShadowText`).

## The pause menu (M2)

The Party tab is ported (M2's first session, Oct 1, 2026), with what A does in it (the third, the same day); the rest isn't yet. What the decomp, the machine code, the imported images and the user's captures (Oct 1, 2026, from the "Before final encounter" checkpoint; see the [reference rig](../reference-rig.md#emulator-captures)) show (`BigAssMenu.cpp`, `DollMenu*.cpp`). Positions are in the 320 × 240 frame.

- **The tabs are one long scroll.** System, Party and World are each a widget tree in its own 320 × 240 frame, 320 pixels apart, and changing tab slides them all 10 pixels a frame. Under them is a 3D scroll, `aidyn:actor/0x0E6B` (scene `0x0E63`), seen from above, which slides with them: System shows its wooden roller at the left, World at the right, and Party, in the middle, neither. Its states unroll and roll it on opening and closing; the user reads some as the full menu's and some, shorter, as the smaller windows' (barter, training, containers). Past the torn top and bottom edges the world shows through.
- **Each tab has a full-screen image** over the scroll (`pause_menu_borg8`): `0x00D8`, `0x00D9` and `0x00DA`, all 320 × 240. `0x00D9` is opaque inside its torn edges.
- **The Party tab's layout is painted into its image.** `0x00D9` is one parchment with the silhouette and its slot frames, the portrait frames, the labels HitPoints, Level and Gold, the HP bar's frame, the knotwork divider down the middle and "Details" with the C-down button. - **The header** (`DollMenuPortraits`, `WidgetHealthGold`): the member's portrait at (22, 26), 40 square, and the others' at (67, 39), (96, 39) and (125, 39), 24 square, in party order from the member shown. In the main font, hit points at (223, 41) and their maximum at (255, 41), the level at (223, 55) and gold at (223, 69). - **The silhouette** (`DollEquipmentMenu`): 15 slots, 16 square, each the worn item's icon or the slot's own image of what it takes (`gItemSlotBorg8Indecies`). A gear slot shows the first, second or third piece of its kind in the gear's order. The decomp reads cloaks for the helmet's slot; the machine code reads helmets.
- **The list** (`DollMenuLists`) is one of four, in the order the stick steps through: inventory, spells, skills, stats (training drops stats; barter keeps only the inventory). It's at (160, 101), bounded from x 160 to 300 and y 101 to 205, in the slim font, rows in (130, 80, 80). Its title image is centred at x 230, 20 above it; the decomp's `- 70` is `+ 70`. The arrows that show hidden rows are 15 in from each side and 13 above, in (225, 225, 225). The cursor's row pulses from (68, 42, 34) toward (151, 141, 191) and back over 20 frames, as the conversation box's choices do. Changing member keeps each list's cursor: stats clamp it to the last row, skills and spells go back to the top if it's past the end.
  - **Inventory**, 13 pixels a row: the wearer's portrait and the item's icon at 12 square, then the count at 26 and the name at 42, or 30 without a count; the decomp's 40 is 42 in the machine code. Worn items first, each member's weapon, armor, shield and gear, the member shown first and then round the party, then what's carried. Each part is sorted by kind and then by name in byte order. The kinds' order is potions, weapons, wands, scrolls, amulets, rings, helmets, cloaks, gloves, boots, belts, armor, shields, key items, misc; the decomp's copy leaves scrolls out.
  - **Stats**, 11 a row: the six attributes' base values at 79, and modified less base, when it isn't 0, at 100, as "(+n)" in (0, 128, 0) or "(n)" in (128, 0, 0). Then 4 below the rows, XP Remaining (experience to spend), Total XP and Next Level (what the next level needs, or MAX), the values lined up 4 after the first label.
  - **Skills**, 13 a row: the skills above 0 with their icons, then the weapon skills and the shield, which have none; the level at 79 and the difference at 95.
  - **Spells**, 13 a row: the aspect's icon (a spell's special, Solar or Lunar), the school's at 12 and the spell's symbol at 24, the rank at 36 and the name at 50. Unschooled spells have no school icon.
- **C-down's details** (`WidgetItemDetail`), shown while it's held, on the 160 × 160 parchment `0x00E2` at (0, 58) with its shadow `0x00E4` at (5, 60), in the slim font from x 23 to 149 and from y 70:
  - **An item:** its icon and name level with the icon's foot, then label and value lines: a weapon's skill, aspect, Min. Strength, Base Hit and Damage; armor's aspect, Protection, a shield's Defense, Dexterity and Stealth (the decomp labels Stealth with the armor's name). Then what it adds: a stat (Stamina as Spell Battery, what it holds now), a skill, Magic: and Spell: with the spell's name indented 16, Charges: on a wand, and Resists: with the element and the share it takes off. Armor ends with Total Protection and Total Defense, now and with it on, the second green or red, ending at y 213. Worn gear shows Min. Intelligence and, if it has one, Min. Strength; the original prints the Strength it needs under Intelligence and the other way round, which the port puts right. Potions, scrolls and other items show their description, and a scroll its spell's Min. Wizard Rank at y 187; key items and items without a description, such as the maps, show nothing. Descriptions are on the item records.
  - **A stat or skill:** its description. **A spell:** its symbol and name, its description, and at y 187 the Wizard rank it needs and the reagent it uses, with the reagent's icon at 9 square.
  - The user's capture of Mechanic's "Create Armor" listing the hides is A's crafting popup on the same parchment: see [the actions](#the-party-tabs-actions).
- **Scaled images sample as the RDP does:** its first pixel on the first texel and a step of the scale per pixel, where Godot's linear filter starts half a step in. `UiPainter` offsets the source rectangle to match, which brought the 12- and 24-square portraits to within the captures' noise.
- **A red arrow** under the knotwork at (126, 207) points both ways in trek (`BORG8_SliderArrowLR`) and only left in combat, in (130, 80, 80).
- **Later:** clicking. Hovering a row moves the cursor, but what a click lands on in the editor isn't clear yet, and the user has left it for later (Oct 1, 2026).
- **Not ported yet:** the slides and fades (the list sliding in over 20 frames, the portraits moving and resizing over 15, the details fading in and out 60 a frame), the sounds, and the 3D scroll under the tabs.
- **The other tabs:** System is a list (Load Game, Save Game, Options, Theater, Credits, Help) under its title image. World is the world map at the left with its compass, the calendar (the season's name, the month's days with the moon's phases, today boxed) and Camp and Journal. The journal is a book filling the screen, its entries in fixed order, scrolling, with the newest in purple and an arrow at the right.

Measured against the captures in the console's own pixels (every eighth column and fourth line of ares' 2560 × 960), the classic layout's text, icons and portraits match to within the noise the console's 16-bit framebuffer and dithering leave, about 6 per pixel.

### The model and the layouts

`PartyMenu` (`Aidyn.Core.Ui`) is the tab's state: the member shown, the list showing, each list's rows (`PartyMenuRows`) and cursor (`MenuList`), the silhouette's slots, and C-down's details for the cursor's row (`MenuDetails`). `MenuImages` and `InventoryOrder` hold the chrome's image IDs and the kinds' order, which the tests check against the ROM's tables. `PartyTabView` (`Aidyn.Game`) draws it with the widget kit, `UiPainter`: text in either font, images whole, scaled, cut out or feathered, the parchment popup, and lists with a cursor and arrows. It picks the layout by the screen's size in its own pixels:

- **Classic**, below 560 × 320: `0x00D9` whole at the screen's centre, filled in at the original's coordinates.
- **Wide**, from 560 × 320, as 16:9 at 360 lines gives: the original's sheet, grown. `0x00D9` is drawn at its own size where the original has it, through the portraits, hit points, level and gold and the rules, and the silhouette's column below, so all of that is the original's to the pixel and lines up with its own art; only the list's side and the foot are new. Where the sheet grows past the image it's plain parchment at the image's own size: rows of the plain swatch under the original's list (`0x00D9` from (162, 102), 154 × 102), every other copy along a row mirrored so the joins meet themselves, each row half a copy along from the last and faded into it over 24 pixels. The torn top and bottom edges are repeated across the same way, mirrored. A first try stretched a 60 × 96 swatch over the whole sheet, over 10 times across, which smeared the grain to streaks and lost about four fifths of its fine detail beside the pieces drawn at their own size. The list starts where the original's does, at (160, 101), takes about half the room right of it in the main font with as many rows as fit and the numbers at the right; its rules' middle repeats to its width and the knotwork's braid, which repeats every 20 pixels, runs its height. A deep margin under it holds the arrow, centred on the screen: it says the other tabs are either side, and isn't part of the knotwork (the original's just happens to sit under its foot). C-down's details, always open in the main font with the footer at their foot, sit on `0x00E2` from the list to the screen's right edge, mirrored. `0x00E2` has a straight left edge, which the original puts against the screen's edge (under the overscan bar, so its text starts 23 in), and torn top, right and bottom edges; its shadow, `0x00E4`, is a separate soft square at up to 204 alpha, 2 larger, drawn 5 right and 2 down. Mirrored, the straight edge meets the screen's right edge and the shadow falls 5 to the left, on the list's side; the text keeps 11 of 160 from the torn edge, as the original's does from its right one, and 8 from the straight one. "Details" and its button aren't drawn, since there's nothing to press. The user chose this over an earlier version with the member's column set in from the edges (Oct 1, 2026): the margins were lost so the art lines up, and the space under the silhouette is free for something later, such as experience.

Still as proposed: the tabs a screen apart, and the reuses' columns.

**In the runtime** (`PauseMenu`), Start opens the tab over the world, on a `UiScreen` 240 lines tall by default, and the runtime's F7 changes it. C-left and C-right are Q and E or the shoulders, the stick is the movement keys, arrows, left stick or D-pad, C-down is Tab or the top face button, held, A is Space or the bottom face button, and Start or B closes a popup, or the menu if none is open. The user found the shoulders and the top face button feel right (Oct 1, 2026). Z/L and R, which change the tab, wait for the other tabs.

- **Each opening starts the tab afresh:** the first member, the inventory, the top row. The user knows the original's to remember only which tab was open (System, Party or World), not the member or where the cursor was, and that this makes the presses to reach something the same every time, which a player learns by heart. Remembering the tab waits for the other tabs.
- **The stick counts once as it crosses the deadzone.** Godot sends an event whenever the stick moves, each past the deadzone reading as pressed, so at first a held stick changed the list once a frame. Held up or down, it repeats after 0.4 s every 0.1 s, as a held key does; that rate is the port's, not measured on the console.

### The Party tab's actions

What A does (`DollMenuLists::AFunc`), in `PartyMenu.Actions`, with the popups as `MenuMessage` and `CraftingMenu` and `PartyTabView` drawing them. Ported in M2's third session (Oct 1, 2026).

- **The inventory** (`WidgetInvShop::AFunc`). A on a worn item takes it off into the inventory, silently staying on if the inventory has no room. The Archmage Staff stays, and says it "contains powerful magic that only" its wielder "can control" (`XPowerfulMagic`). A on a carried item: a potion is drunk by the member shown ([items](items.md#potions)); a scroll is read, which says how it went whatever happens (`Party::UseScroll`); anything else is equipped on the member shown, putting back what it replaces ([items](items.md#equipment)). A refusal says why, from `equip_error_labels` (common strings 95–101): the aspect, Strength or Intelligence it needs, or else the general "You cannot equip that item!", which is also what too many rings, no free gear slot and no room for what it replaces say, as `GetEquipError3` and the item kinds' functions return 1. The port's own refusal, replacing the staff, uses the staff's message. After equipping, the cursor stays on what's left of the stack it came from, or on its row, which the next item moves up into, so it never jumps up to the worn items; after taking off, it stays on its row. The user confirmed the equipping half on the console (Oct 1, 2026). **Still open:** equipping the last item of the list, when no item moves up into its row. The port leaves the cursor on the row past the end, clamped to the list's last. It's hard to test on the console, since the game soon gives the party an item that can't be equipped and sorts to the bottom; a save with one equippable item at the bottom and nothing after would settle it.
- **The skills** (`WidgetSkillInfo::AFunc`, trek only): A on the Alchemist, Mechanic or Healer opens their popup; on any other skill, a spell or a stat it does nothing. The Mechanic with no hide for a recipe their base level reaches says "You do not have the components to create any armor." instead (`makeArmorMenu`).
- **A popup takes every press** while it's open, as the original's widget handler gives them to the top widget: the stick moves its cursor, A chooses, B closes it, and the member and list don't change.
- **A crafting popup** (`WidgetCrafting`, `WidgetMenuPopup`) is on the details' parchment at (0, 58), in the slim font: its title in the rows' colour centred on x 93, 70 down, and its list from x 23 to 163 under it, 122 tall for the Alchemist and 117 for the rest, with the list's cursor pulse. The title is centred by its width in the main font, as the original builds the popup while the main font is set and draws it in the slim one, so "Create Armor" sits 7 left of centre. Against the user's capture of Brenna's, in the console's pixels, the title, the row and the parchment are within 4.5 a channel on average. The wide layout puts it at the same place, over the silhouette, and C-down's details at the right show its product.
  - **The Alchemist's** lists the recipes their modified level reaches by the potion's name, and **the Mechanic's** the armor whose hide the party has. C-down shows the product's details. A brews or makes it ([items](items.md#crafting)) and says so: made, failed, or for the Alchemist not enough reagents, with Flask in place of Potion for the flasks.
  - **The Healer's** lists Lay On Hands, Heal With Herbs and Restore. Each lists the living members, but the Healer for laying on hands, every 25 pixels: the portrait at 25 square, the name 2 after it, and "Health (n / max)" 16 below the name, which the next row's name overlaps by 2, as the original's group is as tall as its portrait. Restore then lists Intelligence, Willpower, Dexterity, Endurance, Strength and Stamina for the member chosen. The results say who healed whom by how much, who's already at full health, whose attribute was restored by how much or is at its maximum, or that the Healer failed, which also covers having no herb or too little Stamina.
- **A message** (`TextBox_Centered`) is off-white (225) on a feathered black box at the screen's centre, the gradients at their own sizes (`IntroMenu_ShadowBG`), in the main font: as wide as its text up to 150, wrapping there, and as tall as that plus 5, with the text 4 in. A crafting popup's result (`WidgetCrafting::TextPopup`) is 16 wider and 5 taller, and closing it closes the popup too, so each opening does one task. A, B or C-down closes a message.
- **Dropping** isn't the Party tab's: the original drops only in [the loot window](#the-loot-window).
- **Not ported yet:** the popups' fades (a message's 90 a frame) and sounds, and the combat pause menu's rules: only the member whose turn it is may equip, and potions are used from C-up.

### The loot window

The pause menu's barter state with no shopkeeper (`WidgetDollMenu` with `WidgetBarter`, `Shopkeep` −1), which a container or a herb, spice or gem source opens ([map-objects](map-objects.md#containers)). Ported in M2's session 4b (Oct 1, 2026), and matched to the user's captures from the original (`roms/menu-loot-*.png`, at its own 320 × 240): the pile, the help, the drop's question, the leave-items choice and C-down's details are within 5.2 to 5.9 a channel on average inside the torn edges, the noise of the console's dithering, with the popups' panels' edges within a pixel. `BarterMenu` in the core, over the rules in `LootWindow` ([items](items.md#containers)), drawn by `PartyTabView` in its barter state (`PartyTabView.Barter.cs`). A shop is the same widget with prices, a title of Buy, the party's list titled Sell and C-up's appraisal, which session 7 adds to `BarterMenu`.

- **It's the whole pause menu**, so trek stops under it, unlike its popups. Its gold is the party's before it opens (`build_loot_menu`), and the pile is lost when it closes.
- **The layout** (classic): the header and the party's inventory list where the Party tab has them, but no silhouette (`DollEquipmentMenu` isn't made) and no arrow under the knotwork. Over the silhouette, the details' parchment at (0, 58) with its shadow, and on it the pile: its title, `0x00F0` (`BORG8_TitleTreasure`, 84 × 17), centred on x 90 with its foot at y 85; its list from (20, 88), bounded to x 157 and y 166 (six rows), in the slim font, each row as the inventory's without a portrait; and under it in red at (30, 169), "Found: n gold.", which shows even when it's 0, as harvesting gives. - **The two lists.** The pile's starts active if it holds anything. C-left and C-right do nothing here (`WidgetDollMenu`'s C-buttons skip `CHorizontal` while barter is up), and the member shown is the first. C-down shows the active row's details over the pile, as the Party tab's do over the silhouette.
- **A on the pile** takes the stack, or as many as the party's stack has room for up to 99, leaving the rest: "Nothing there." with nothing to take, "You cannot hold any more of that item." at 99, "The party's inventory is full." with no general slot free. ## A faithful mode, later

The floating panel and the resizable screen are enhancements. The user chose them over the original's look for now (Sep 30, 2026), and left a faithful mode for the fidelity profile at the very end ([roadmap](../roadmap.md), Phase 6's enhancements). What it would take:

- **The screen.** `UiScreen` fixed at 320 × 240 whatever the window's shape (`Lines` 240, width 320), shown pillarboxed at 4:3, with the VideoOut filter. The 3D view would need the same 4:3 frame, since the bars below cover it too.
- **The overscan bars.** Black rectangles over everything, drawn last, as `Graphics::EndList` does: 16 pixels at the left and right and 12 at the top and bottom in 1.0 and 1.1, 18 and 12 in the EU version. They'd belong over the whole frame, 3D and UI, not in a UI view, and they'd cut the conversation box off at 16, 304 and 228 as the original's is.
- **The conversation box.** A style for `DialogueBox`: a band right across the frame, from 0 to 320 and down to 180 plus the border's height (232), with only the 10-pixel gradient above it. That's a `FeatheredPanel` whose bottom and side feathering are 0, which `UiImages.DrawPanel` already skips, and the frame at (0, 0).
- **Other UI.** Everything laid out by the screen's size would be at its original place once the screen is 320 × 240, as the conversation box is. Nothing else changes.
- **High-resolution mode**, if the fidelity profile offers the Expansion Pak's option: a 512 × 240 screen, where the original lays UI out in 320 × 240 and `borg8DlistInit` stretches every image 1.6 times across, bilinear, so text is filtered horizontally. `UiScreen` would draw its 320-wide canvas into a 512-wide one with linear filtering before the VideoOut filter. Its overscan bars are 20 pixels at the sides.
- **The VI and the TV**, which apply to either mode: the VI's anti-aliasing and divot filters and a composite or CRT look are further `UiFilter`s.

## In the editor

A script's player shows the conversation box above the page's controls, a loot table's roll and a container on the map the loot window, and the inventory record's page the Party tab, whose A and B buttons act on the editor's game, each over a plain backdrop, at 1×, 2× or 3×, on a 4:3 or 16:9 window, with a UI screen of 240, 360 or 480 lines and with any of the three filters (`UiScreenOptions`). When a line shows alone before its choices, Continue brings them up, as A does. Hovering a choice puts the cursor on it, as hovering a row of the Party tab's list does; the UI screen passes the mouse to its views in its own pixels (`ForwardsMouse`). `--zoom <1-3>`, `--aspect 4:3|16:9`, `--ui-lines <n>` and `--ui-filter pixels|videoout|smooth` set these for screenshots, `--press a,down,a` presses the Party tab's buttons, and `--ui-screenshot <png>` saves the UI screen in its own pixels, to compare with a capture.
