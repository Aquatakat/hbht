# UI: text, the conversation box, the pause menu and the HUD (roadmap 1F, M2)

How the port lays out and draws the original's UI. The fonts and string tables themselves are in [text](../formats/text.md).

## The screen

The original draws its UI on a 320 × 240 screen (`SCREEN_WIDTH`, `SCREEN_HEIGHT`); the Expansion Pak's high-resolution mode is 512 wide, and `borg8DlistInit` scales images to it. Every UI image is a texture rectangle with bilinear filtering and a combiner of texel × primitive colour (`N64BorgImageDraw`), so text is its sheet's texels times the text's colour, alpha too. At 1:1 the filter samples each texel exactly: text and portraits land on the framebuffer pixel for pixel, and only scaled images, such as a box's gradient or the party's portraits at 0.75, are filtered.

**The soft look is the video output.** The emulator's captures of the original are 640 × 240, and their columns alternate between the framebuffer's pixels and the average of the two either side (the compass's letters read 153, 109, 65 across). Each line's 320 pixels are spread over 640 by linear interpolation, and the lines aren't touched. On a real console the VI does that resampling and adds its own anti-aliasing and divot filters, and then a composite or S-Video signal and a CRT soften it further; the captures show only the first step.

**The original covers the screen's edges** with black bars for overscan (`Graphics::EndList`): in 1.0 and 1.1, 16 pixels at the left and right and 12 at the top and bottom, filled rectangles drawn after everything else. The EU version draws 18 and 12 with its image code. So the UI's own boxes run under the bars and are cut off by them.

`UiScreen` (in `Aidyn.Game`, which the editor shares) draws the UI the same way: views draw in a `SubViewport`, in its pixels, with linear filtering as the console has it, and the screen is scaled up afterwards to fill the window, by one of three filters (`UiFilter`). The screen is `Lines` pixels tall, 240 by default as the original's, and as wide as the window's shape, but never narrower than 4:3. Views lay themselves out by its size rather than assuming 320 × 240. A taller screen draws the UI smaller, with room for more: at 480 lines, text is half the size. Text itself is always drawn as authored, pixel for pixel, and wraps at the same widths. The filters:

- **VideoOut**, the default: as the captures show it, each line doubled in width with the averages between, then scaled up sharp.
- **Pixels:** sharp.
- **Smooth:** bilinear both ways.

The canvas blends in on-screen values, as Godot's 2D does without HDR, so a glyph's alpha and colour multiply as the N64's do. It's premultiplied, since alpha blending into a transparent target leaves colour times alpha, and the upscale filters average it that way. A later pass could add the VI's other filters or a CRT's.

## Laying text out

`TextLayout` (`Aidyn.Core.Ui`) is the font engine's layout, over a font record (`Font::PrintMain`, `GetHeightScaled`, `GetWidthScaled`):

- A character draws its glyph at the pen, as wide as its measured width and a whole cell tall, and moves the pen by that width. A character with no glyph is skipped and takes no room.
- **Text wraps only at spaces.** After a space, if the next word would end past the right edge, the line breaks and the space is dropped. A space at the start of a line takes no room, and a word longer than the line runs past the edge. `\n` breaks the line.
- Lines are the cell's height plus 2 apart: 11 pixels in both fonts.
- Text is drawn only inside its widget's bounds. A glyph across an edge is cut, and one outside is dropped.
- Characters from `0x7F` are the controller buttons, drawn in their own colour with the text's alpha.

## The conversation box

`DialogueBox` (`Aidyn.Core.Ui`) places what a conversation shows, as `DiaMenuSub::InitDefault` and `DialougeClass::Setup` do, and `ConversationBoxView` draws it in the main font. It's for conversations and the other modes without a backdrop; backdrops and the help menu draw on a full-screen image (`BigBGDialoug`), which isn't ported yet.

The box is laid out in the original's 320 × 240 screen, placed at the bottom centre of the UI screen, so on a 4:3 screen of 240 lines it's where the original's is, and elsewhere it keeps its size. The positions below are in that frame.

- **The original's band** is translucent black: its image, `0x1C4`, is a single white texel of alpha 136, drawn black. It runs right across the screen from the box's top to 180 plus the portrait border's height, 232, so the overscan bars cut it at 16, 304 and 228. Above it a 10-pixel gradient fades upward, image `0x1C3` squeezed from 16 pixels.
- **The port floats it** instead, since a wide screen has no bars to cut it off. The band runs from 16 to 304 and down to 228, where the bars would stop it, and it's feathered on every side where the bars were, from the pieces the original's own popups are made of (`WidgetChoiceDia::SetImages`, see `FeatheredPanel`): the 10-pixel gradient above, `0x1C2` below, `0x1C0` and `0x1C1` beside it, each fading outward from alpha 136, and the rounded corners `0x1BC`–`0x1BF`. On a 4:3 screen it looks as the original's does inside its bars. The decomp names three of the four gradients wrongly; the machine code puts `0x1C3` above a popup, `0x1C2` below, `0x1C0` left and `0x1C1` right, and the dialogue box's gradient is `0x1C3`.
- **The portrait's border** (`0xDF`, 52 × 52) is at x 20, level with the box's top, and the portrait (40 × 40) sits 6 pixels inside it. A speaker with no portrait gets `0x0125` (`BORG8_PortraitNPCMale1`).
- **The line** is in (200, 200, 200), from x 78 (the border's width + 26) to 294, and ends 2 pixels above what's under it: the band's bottom, or the choices. The box's top is 4 pixels above the line, but never below 180, so the box is at least the border's height: a short line sits at y 184, and the box grows upward from five lines.
- **Choices** are in tan (200, 180, 100), from x 88, 4 pixels apart, and end at 228. The cursor's choice pulses toward (50, 45, 25, 63) and back over 24 frames each way, whole steps per channel (`WidgetScrollMenu::Tick`).
- **A line before its own speaker's choices shows alone.** When a menu's first choice has the line's speaker, the line shows first, and A brings up the choices alone under Alaron's portrait. The box shrinks to the choices, but the portrait and its border stay where the line put them, since `Setup` doesn't move them on that path.

Not ported yet: the widgets' fade in and out (`fadeFloatMirror`), scrolling a menu taller than its bounds, and the full-screen backdrops' text with its shadow (`WidgetShadowText`).

## The pause menu

The Party tab is ported, with what A does in it, and the [System](#the-system-tab) and [World](#the-world-tab) tabs. What the decomp, the machine code, the imported images and the user's captures (Oct 1, 2026, from the "Before final encounter" checkpoint; see the [reference rig](../reference-rig.md#emulator-captures)) show (`BigAssMenu.cpp`, `DollMenu*.cpp`). Positions are in the 320 × 240 frame.

- **The tabs are one long scroll.** System, Party and World are each a widget tree in its own 320 × 240 frame, 320 pixels apart, and changing tab slides them all 10 pixels a frame. Under them is a 3D scroll, `aidyn:actor/0x0E6B` (scene `0x0E63`), seen from above, which slides with them: System shows its wooden roller at the left, World at the right, and Party, in the middle, neither. Its states unroll and roll it on opening and closing; the user reads some as the full menu's and some, shorter, as the smaller windows' (barter, training, containers). Past the torn top and bottom edges the world shows through.
- **Each tab has a full-screen image** over the scroll (`pause_menu_borg8`): `0x00D8`, `0x00D9` and `0x00DA`, all 320 × 240. `0x00D9` is opaque inside its torn edges.
- **The Party tab's layout is painted into its image.** `0x00D9` is one parchment with the silhouette and its slot frames, the portrait frames, the labels HitPoints, Level and Gold, the HP bar's frame, the knotwork divider down the middle and "Details" with the C-down button. None of those labels are in the string tables. The widgets only fill it in.
- **The header** (`DollMenuPortraits`, `WidgetHealthGold`): the member's portrait at (22, 26), 40 square, and the others' at (67, 39), (96, 39) and (125, 39), 24 square, in party order from the member shown. The name at (69, 28) in the slim font. Under the large portrait, 4 below it, the aspect's icon and 2 after it the school's. In the main font, hit points at (223, 41) and their maximum at (255, 41), the level at (223, 55) and gold at (223, 69). The bar, `0x00E0`, is drawn 80 × 10 at (220, 27) and cut to hit points ÷ maximum × 80, truncated (a float division in the machine code).
- **The silhouette** (`DollEquipmentMenu`): 15 slots, 16 square, each the worn item's icon or the slot's own image of what it takes (`gItemSlotBorg8Indecies`). A gear slot shows the first, second or third piece of its kind in the gear's order. The decomp reads cloaks for the helmet's slot; the machine code reads helmets.
- **The list** (`DollMenuLists`) is one of four, in the order the stick steps through: inventory, spells, skills, stats (training drops stats; barter keeps only the inventory). It's at (160, 101), bounded from x 160 to 300 and y 101 to 205, in the slim font, rows in (130, 80, 80). Its title image is centred at x 230, 20 above it; the decomp's `- 70` is `+ 70`. The arrows that show hidden rows are 15 in from each side and 13 above, in (225, 225, 225). The cursor's row pulses from (68, 42, 34) toward (151, 141, 191) and back over 20 frames, as the conversation box's choices do. Changing member keeps each list's cursor: stats clamp it to the last row, skills and spells go back to the top if it's past the end.
  - **Inventory**, 13 pixels a row: the wearer's portrait and the item's icon at 12 square, then the count at 26 and the name at 42, or 30 without a count; the decomp's 40 is 42 in the machine code. Worn items first, each member's weapon, armor, shield and gear, the member shown first and then round the party, then what's carried. Each part is sorted by kind and then by name in byte order. The kinds' order is potions, weapons, wands, scrolls, amulets, rings, helmets, cloaks, gloves, boots, belts, armor, shields, key items, misc; the decomp's copy leaves scrolls out.
  - **Stats**, 11 a row: the six attributes' base values at 79, and modified less base, when it isn't 0, at 100, as "(+n)" in (0, 128, 0) or "(n)" in (128, 0, 0). Then 4 below the rows, XP Remaining (experience to spend), Total XP and Next Level (what the next level needs, or MAX), the values lined up 4 after the first label.
  - **Skills**, 13 a row: the skills above 0 with their icons, then the weapon skills and the shield, which have none; the level at 79 and the difference at 95.
  - **Spells**, 13 a row: the aspect's icon (a spell's special, Solar or Lunar), the school's at 12 and the spell's symbol at 24, the rank at 36 and the name at 50. Unschooled spells have no school icon.
- **C-down's details** (`WidgetItemDetail`), shown while it's held, on the 160 × 160 parchment `0x00E2` at (0, 58) with its shadow `0x00E4` at (5, 60), in the slim font from x 23 to 149 and from y 70:
  - **An item:** its icon and name level with the icon's foot, then label and value lines: a weapon's skill, aspect, Min. Strength, Base Hit and Damage; armor's aspect, Protection, a shield's Defense, Dexterity and Stealth (the decomp labels Stealth with the armor's name). Then what it adds: a stat (Stamina as Spell Battery, what it holds now), a skill, Magic: and Spell: with the spell's name indented 16, Charges: on a wand, and Resists: with the element and the share it takes off. Armor ends with Total Protection and Total Defense, now and with it on, the second green or red, ending at y 213. Worn gear shows Min. Intelligence and, if it has one, Min. Strength; the original prints the Strength it needs under Intelligence and the other way round, which the port puts right. Potions, scrolls and other items show their description, and a scroll its spell's Min. Wizard Rank at y 187; key items and items without a description, such as the maps, show nothing. Descriptions are on the item records.
  - **A stat or skill:** its description. **A spell:** its symbol and name, its description, and at y 187 the Wizard rank it needs and the reagent it uses, with the reagent's icon at 9 square.
  - The user's capture of Mechanic's "Create Armor" listing the hides is A's crafting popup on the same parchment: see [the actions](#the-party-tabs-actions).
- **Scaled images sample as the RDP does:** its first pixel on the first texel and a step of the scale per pixel, where Godot's linear filter starts half a step in. `UiPainter` offsets the source rectangle to match, which brought the 12- and 24-square portraits to within the captures' noise.
- **A red arrow** under the knotwork at (126, 207) points both ways in trek (`BORG8_SliderArrowLR`) and only left in combat, in (130, 80, 80).
- **Later:** clicking. Hovering a row moves the cursor, but what a click lands on in the editor isn't clear yet, and the user has left it for later (Oct 1, 2026).
- **Not ported yet:** the slides and fades (the list sliding in over 20 frames, the portraits moving and resizing over 15, the details fading in and out 60 a frame), the sounds, and the 3D scroll under the tabs.
- **The World tab:** [below](#the-world-tab).

Measured against the captures in the console's own pixels (every eighth column and fourth line of ares' 2560 × 960), the classic layout's text, icons and portraits match to within the noise the console's 16-bit framebuffer and dithering leave, about 6 per pixel.

### The model and the layouts

`PartyMenu` (`Aidyn.Core.Ui`) is the tab's state: the member shown, the list showing, each list's rows (`PartyMenuRows`) and cursor (`MenuList`), the silhouette's slots, and C-down's details for the cursor's row (`MenuDetails`). `MenuImages` and `InventoryOrder` hold the chrome's image IDs and the kinds' order, which the tests check against the ROM's tables. `PartyTabView` (`Aidyn.Game`) draws it with the widget kit, `UiPainter`: text in either font, images whole, scaled, cut out or feathered, the parchment popup, and lists with a cursor and arrows. It draws `0x00D9` whole at the screen's centre, filled in at the original's coordinates, at every screen size ([ADR 0014](../adr/0014-menus-keep-the-originals-layouts.md)). A wide layout, the original's sheet grown to fill the screen, was tried (Oct 1–3, 2026) and dropped: growing bespoke art leaves seams all over, and new layouts want new art, which is for mods after 1.0.

**In the runtime** (`PauseMenu`), Start opens the tab over the world, on a `UiScreen` 240 lines tall by default, and the runtime's F7 changes it. C-left and C-right are Q and E or the shoulders, the stick is the movement keys, arrows, left stick or D-pad, C-down is Tab or the top face button, held, A is Space or the bottom face button, and Start or B closes a popup, or the menu if none is open. The user found the shoulders and the top face button feel right (Oct 1, 2026). Z or L, and R, change the tab: Z and X or the triggers ([below](#changing-tab)).

- **Each opening starts the tab afresh:** the first member, the inventory, the top row. The user knows the original's to remember only which tab was open (System, Party or World), not the member or where the cursor was, and that this makes the presses to reach something the same every time, which a player learns by heart.
- **The stick counts once as it crosses the original's threshold** (`MenuStick`, shared with trek's popups, conversations and cut-scenes). Godot sends an event whenever the stick moves, each past the deadzone reading as pressed, so at first a held stick changed the list once a frame, and at trek's 0.1 a push up easily counted as sideways too. The original's menus count a direction from 56 of the N64 stick's 80, 0.7 (`Controller::ReadInput`, `0x800915d8` in 1.0); the user read 0.58 on a PC stick tester as where the emulator's menu starts moving (Oct 3, 2026), which fits ares scaling a stick pushed all the way to about 100. The port counts from 0.58 and lets go below 0.5. **Held,** the stick or the D-pad repeats after 0.5 s and then 12 a second: the original's widgets count 30 of the controller's 60 samples a second, then 5 (`BaseWidget::Control`, `0x800ad708`), for the stick, the D-pad, C-up and C-down. The user timed about 0.7 s and 12 a second, and the 88-item inventory end to end in 7 to 7.5 s.

### The Party tab's actions

What A does (`DollMenuLists::AFunc`), in `PartyMenu.Actions`, with the popups as `MenuMessage` and `CraftingMenu` and `PartyTabView` drawing them.

- **The inventory** (`WidgetInvShop::AFunc`). A on a worn item takes it off into the inventory, silently staying on if the inventory has no room. The Archmage Staff stays, and says it "contains powerful magic that only" its wielder "can control" (`XPowerfulMagic`). A on a carried item: a potion is drunk by the member shown ([items](items.md#potions)); a scroll is read, which says how it went whatever happens (`Party::UseScroll`); anything else is equipped on the member shown, putting back what it replaces ([items](items.md#equipment)). A refusal says why, from `equip_error_labels` (common strings 95–101): the aspect, Strength or Intelligence it needs, or else the general "You cannot equip that item!", which is also what too many rings, no free gear slot and no room for what it replaces say, as `GetEquipError3` and the item kinds' functions return 1. The port's own refusal, replacing the staff, uses the staff's message. After equipping, the cursor stays on what's left of the stack it came from, or on its row, which the next item moves up into, so it never jumps up to the worn items; after taking off, it stays on its row. The user confirmed the equipping half on the console (Oct 1, 2026). **Still open:** equipping the last item of the list, when no item moves up into its row. The port leaves the cursor on the row past the end, clamped to the list's last. It's hard to test on the console, since the game soon gives the party an item that can't be equipped and sorts to the bottom; a save with one equippable item at the bottom and nothing after would settle it.
- **The skills** (`WidgetSkillInfo::AFunc`, trek only): A on the Alchemist, Mechanic or Healer opens their popup; on any other skill, a spell or a stat it does nothing. The Mechanic with no hide for a recipe their base level reaches says "You do not have the components to create any armor." instead (`makeArmorMenu`).
- **A popup takes every press** while it's open, as the original's widget handler gives them to the top widget: the stick moves its cursor, A chooses, B closes it, and the member and list don't change.
- **A crafting popup** (`WidgetCrafting`, `WidgetMenuPopup`) is on the details' parchment at (0, 58), in the slim font: its title in the rows' colour centred on x 93, 70 down, and its list from x 23 to 163 under it, 122 tall for the Alchemist and 117 for the rest, with the list's cursor pulse. The title is centred by its width in the main font, as the original builds the popup while the main font is set and draws it in the slim one, so "Create Armor" sits 7 left of centre. Against the user's capture of Brenna's, in the console's pixels, the title, the row and the parchment are within 4.5 a channel on average.
  - **The Alchemist's** lists the recipes their modified level reaches by the potion's name, and **the Mechanic's** the armor whose hide the party has. C-down shows the product's details. A brews or makes it ([items](items.md#crafting)) and says so: made, failed, or for the Alchemist not enough reagents, with Flask in place of Potion for the flasks.
  - **The Healer's** lists Lay On Hands, Heal With Herbs and Restore. Each lists the living members, but the Healer for laying on hands, every 25 pixels: the portrait at 25 square, the name 2 after it, and "Health (n / max)" 16 below the name, which the next row's name overlaps by 2, as the original's group is as tall as its portrait. Restore then lists Intelligence, Willpower, Dexterity, Endurance, Strength and Stamina for the member chosen. The results say who healed whom by how much, who's already at full health, whose attribute was restored by how much or is at its maximum, or that the Healer failed, which also covers having no herb or too little Stamina.
- **A message** (`TextBox_Centered`) is off-white (225) on a feathered black box at the screen's centre, the gradients at their own sizes (`IntroMenu_ShadowBG`), in the main font: as wide as its text up to 150, wrapping there, and as tall as that plus 5, with the text 4 in. A crafting popup's result (`WidgetCrafting::TextPopup`) is 16 wider and 5 taller, and closing it closes the popup too, so each opening does one task. A, B or C-down closes a message.
- **Dropping** isn't the Party tab's: the original drops only in [the loot window](#the-loot-window).
- **Not ported yet:** the popups' fades (a message's 90 a frame) and sounds, and the combat pause menu's rules: only the member whose turn it is may equip, and potions are used from C-up.

### The loot window

The pause menu's barter state with no shopkeeper (`WidgetDollMenu` with `WidgetBarter`, `Shopkeep` −1), which a container or a herb, spice or gem source opens ([map-objects](map-objects.md#containers)). Matched to the user's captures from the original (`roms/menu-loot-*.png`, at its own 320 × 240): the pile, the help, the drop's question, the leave-items choice and C-down's details are within 5.2 to 5.9 a channel on average inside the torn edges, the noise of the console's dithering, with the popups' panels' edges within a pixel. `BarterMenu` in the core, over the rules in `LootWindow` ([items](items.md#containers)), drawn by `PartyTabView` in its barter state (`PartyTabView.Barter.cs`). A shop is the same widget with a shopkeeper: [below](#the-shop).

- **It's the whole pause menu**, so trek stops under it, unlike its popups. Its gold is the party's before it opens (`build_loot_menu`), and the pile is lost when it closes.
- **The layout** (classic): the header and the party's inventory list where the Party tab has them, but no silhouette (`DollEquipmentMenu` isn't made) and no arrow under the knotwork. Over the silhouette, the details' parchment at (0, 58) with its shadow, and on it the pile: its title, `0x00F0` (`BORG8_TitleTreasure`, 84 × 17), centred on x 90 with its foot at y 85; its list from (20, 88), bounded to x 157 and y 166 (six rows), in the slim font, each row as the inventory's without a portrait; and under it in red at (30, 169), "Found: n gold.", which shows even when it's 0, as harvesting gives. The list's arrows sit as the party's do, 15 in and 13 above.
- **The two lists.** The pile's starts active if it holds anything. The stick's left makes the pile's active, if it isn't empty, and right the party's (`WidgetBarter::LeftFunc`, `RightFunc`); the active list's cursor pulses brown to purple and the other's is plain red. The decomp's `RightFunc` makes the pile's active again, which would leave the party's list unreachable; the machine code makes the party's active, as the port does. C-left and C-right do nothing here (`WidgetDollMenu`'s C-buttons skip `CHorizontal` while barter is up), and the member shown is the first. C-down shows the active row's details over the pile, as the Party tab's do over the silhouette.
- **A on the pile** takes the stack, or as many as the party's stack has room for up to 99, leaving the rest: "Nothing there." with nothing to take, "You cannot hold any more of that item." at 99, "The party's inventory is full." with no general slot free. The pile's cursor stays on its row, which the next moves up into, and the party's goes to what it took. When the pile is empty, the party's list becomes active. The first potion the party gains, with flag 3612 clear, sets it and shows the help (`WidgetMenuPrompt`): "You've acquired a potion! To select a potion in combat, press and hold the [C-up] button. For more info, see the help menu.", in red in the main font on the parchment at (0, 58), from (23, 70) wrapping at 149, which A, B or Start closes.
- **A on the party's list** drops. The price comes first: an item worth 1 gold or less at half its price (3 or less) says "You cannot drop that item.", worn or not; a worn one worth more says "You must unequip that to drop it." Otherwise the drop's question (`DollBarterConfirm`): "Are you sure you wish to drop %s?" in white over its options in tan, "Oops!  No way." first, then "Drop it." for one, or "Drop %d of them." for 1, 5, 10 and 20 below what's held and "Drop all of them.". The question wraps across 160 and the options start 8 under it; together they're centred on the screen on a feathered panel 4 beyond them above and below, from x 80 to 240. The decomp centres them on the screen's width, both ways; the machine code uses 240, the height, for the top and 240 for the panel's right. Dropped items are lost: the pile doesn't take them. The party's cursor stays on the row. **B does nothing** in the question, as the original's has no B, which the user confirmed: "Oops!  No way." is where the cursor starts.
- **B, or Start** (`WidgetBarter::BFunc`, which the machine code shows returns the widget, closing the menu, when the pile is empty): with the pile empty, the window closes. With items left and none that must be taken, the choice (`WidgetChoiceDia`, 150 wide, no gap): "There are still items that you have not picked up!  Are you sure you wish to exit?" in white, over "Yes, leave them." and "No, I changed my mind." in tan, the cursor on Yes and pulsing grey to white. A chooses; B does nothing, as the original builds it without a B, which the user confirmed. With a must-take item left: "You have a feeling that there's something you really need in here." in white in a 155 × 40 box (`TextPopup_New`).
- **Messages** are the Party tab's (`ErrPopup`, `TextBox_Centered`), closed by A, B, C-down or C-up.
- **In the runtime**, `PauseMenu.OpenLoot` opens it; B or Start closes it as above, or choosing to leave the items, and the map is gated again as the menu closes, as it is after the Party tab.
- **Not ported yet:** the coin jingle as it opens with gold, the parchment's slide in, and the popups' fades and sounds.

### The shop

The barter state with a shopkeeper (`WidgetBarter` with `Shopkeep` set, pause state 6), which a script's Shop command opens ([items](items.md#shops)). From 1.0's code (`WidgetBarter` at `0x8003cb68`, `AFunc` `0x8003d7cc`, `BFunc` `0x8003d614`, C-up `0x8003de10`) and the user's captures (Oct 3, 2026, `roms/menu-buy-*.png`, from the "Before final encounter" checkpoint at STAI01's shop, `aidyn:shop/30`), which the port matches within 3 to 4 a channel on average in every part. `BarterMenu.ForShop`, over the rules in `Shop`. It's the loot window but for these:

- **The titles:** Buy (`0x00E7`) over the stock, where the loot's Treasure goes, and Sell (`0x00EB`) over the party's list in place of Inventory.
- **The stock has no counts**, as it never runs out: each row is the icon and the name at 30, as `WidgetInvShop::InitMenu` builds them with none. It's sorted as the inventory is.
- **The line under it** is "Value: n" (common line 531) in place of the loot's gold: the active list's cursor row's price, what one costs to buy from the stock or brings when sold from the party's list, worn or not; blank with no row. It's there for every move and trade. The header's gold changes as the party trades.
- **A on the stock buys one at once**, with no question. In order: "Nothing there.", "You cannot afford this item." (523), "You cannot hold any more of that item." at 99, and "The party's inventory is full.". The party's cursor goes to what it bought, and the stock's stays, with the stock still active. The first potion bought shows the loot's help.
- **A on the party's list sells.** "Nothing there.", then "I do not want that item." (526) for what would sell for 1 gold or less, then "You must unequip that to sell it." (528) for what's worn; otherwise the drop's question, as "I'll give you n gold for that item." (471) over "No, thanks.", then "Sure, here it is." for one, or "I will sell n of them." for 1, 5, 10 and 20 below what's held and "I will sell all of them.". Choosing pays the price for each, and the cursor stays on the row.
- **C-up on the stock** appraises the row's price (`Party::ApraisePrice`, [items](items.md#shops)), in a message: one of common lines 384–390. It does nothing on the party's list, and nothing in loot. It also closes a message, as C-down does.
- **B or Start closes the shop** at once, whatever's left: the stock is the shop's. The decomp's `BFunc` returns nothing for a shop, which would leave B doing nothing; the machine code returns the widget, closing it.
- **The shadow is drawn twice** under C-down's details, as the details' parchment and its shadow go over the pile's. That's the original's too.
- **Under the menu**, the original still shows the conversation box's last page at the foot of the screen, past the parchment's torn edge. The port's box has gone by then.

## The trek HUD

Trek's HUD (`draw_hud_elements`, `0x80020bd4` in 1.0) is the sundial at the bottom left, the party's portraits at the bottom centre, and at the bottom right the minimap where the player's chunk has a piece, or else the compass under an outdoor sky (sky type 3), or nothing. The compass and the sundial are separate widgets. From 1.0's machine code and the user's captures (Oct 4, 2026, `roms/hud-*.png`: outside Gwernia castle with the portraits up and down, and inside it with the minimap large and small), which the port's positions match to the pixel. `TrekHud` (`Aidyn.Core.Ui`) is the state, with `Sundial`, `Compass`, `PartyPortraits` and `Minimap`; `TrekLoop` owns it and `HudView` draws it.

- **Drawn only in trek frames**, and not while a conversation or sign shows (`diaClass->menu`): not under the pause menu, a shop, training or a cut-scene. Popups draw over it. Every colour and alpha is the scene's brightness times its own, so it fades with the screen.
- **The images** are texture rectangles at quarter-pixel positions, texel × colour, blended (`borg8DlistInit` with flag 6, `Borg8_DrawSimple`). None uses the feathered popups' pieces: the rings and the frames have their feathering painted in.

### The sundial

`Sundial::Draw` (`0x800248a4`), centred on (49, 199):

- **The ring** `0x001B` (60 square) at (19, 169), white at alpha 80: a dark disc with a thin white rim.
- **The moon** at (39, 189), in (160, 160, 220) at alpha 160, by its phase ([rendering](rendering.md#the-skys-objects)): new `0x01B8`, crescent `0x01B3` or `0x01B4` and quarter `0x01B6` or `0x01B7` in the first and second halves of the moon's four weeks (`(week & 3) >> 1`), full `0x01B5`. It shows at every hour.
- **The rays** `0x01BA` (50 square) at (24, 174), at alpha 80, in the hour's colour.
- **The sun** goes round the ring **twice a day**, 30° an hour clockwise from the top, so noon and midnight are both at the top: its centre is (49 + 25 sin a, 199 − 25 cos a), a in whole degrees, (int)(time of day × 720) mod 720. By day it's the glow `0x01BB`, by night the small dot `0x01B9`, each 20 square. The capture's glow at about (72.5, 208.8) is a quarter to four in the afternoon, where one turn a day would put it at the lower left.
- **The hour's colour**, for the rays and the sun: from 7 to 21 peach (255, 190, 150), the glow at alpha 160 and no dot; from 23 to 5 blue (120, 100, 190), the dot at 160 and no glow. At dawn (5 to 7) and dusk (21 to 23) the colour blends linearly between them, 67.5, 45 and 20 an hour a channel, and the glow and dot cross-fade at 80 an hour. The original dims day's and night's colour by the scene's brightness but not the blend's.
- **The moon and sun hide in caves** and interiors such as Gwernia castle's: under a cave's sky (sky type 4: terrains 6, 8, 9, 11 and 13–16, the port's terrains that don't draw the sky). The original decides only on crossing from a chunk under one sky to the other (`TeleportPlayer` at `0x8000bfd0`, through `Sundial::ToggleSun` and `ToggleMoon`, whose names are swapped), and trek starting shows them wherever it is (`Sundial::Init`): a new game, loading, or after a cut-scene or battle. So after loading in a cave they show until the player leaves and comes back, as in the user's capture inside Gwernia castle; the user confirmed this in the original and that walking in hides them (Oct 4, 2026). **The port fixes it**, deciding by the sky when trek starts too, which the user chose. The Detect Moon Phase and Detect Sun Phase spells show them too (`0x800703c8`), which wait for 2.5. The ring and rays always show.

### The compass

`Compass::Draw` (`0x800240b8`), centred on (271, 199):

- **The ring** is the sundial's, at (241, 169), white at alpha 80.
- **The letters** are 16-square images, N `0x0018`, S `0x0019`, W `0x001A` and E `0x0017` (the decomp names them wrongly), drawn in that order in (160, 160, 220), each centred 25 out from the centre. With (a, b) the camera's look in X and Z, normalised (`rotationXZ`): N at (−25a, 25b) and alpha 160 − 95b, S at (25a, −25b) and 160 + 95b, W at (25b, 25a) and 160 − 95a, E at (−25b, −25a) and 160 + 95a. So the way the camera looks is at the top, at alpha 255, and behind it is at the bottom, at 65. North is −Z and east +X. The capture, the camera looking along about (−1, 0.11), has W at the top, its letter in (158, 158, 218), and E under the bottom overscan bar.
- **It shows** only under an outdoor sky with no minimap showing.

### The party's portraits

`Portraits::Draw` (`0x8002569c`):

- **The frames**, `0x00DF` (the conversation box's) at 0.8, so 41.6 square and overlapping by 3.6, at x 84, 122, 160 and 198, all four even for an empty slot, in white at alpha 128: they glow grey-white. **The portraits** at 0.75, 30 square, 6 inside, at x 90, 128, 166 and 204, at alpha 128 too, so they're half seen, in party order. A dead member looks the same; nothing shows hit points or who's chosen. The original never sets the portraits' blue, which carries the frames' over and is dimmed by the brightness again; at full brightness that's the same.
- **They slide** 4 pixels a frame, once a rendered frame whatever its ticks: up to 184 while they're up, down to 240, off the screen, while they're down, 14 frames either way. Their top up is 184, the portraits' 190, which the capture shows.
- **B raises or lowers them** (`Actor::Move` at `0x80015110`), but not through a fade. **Pushing the stick past 0.05 lowers them** (`0x8001536c`), unless R holds the camera. **Standing still doesn't raise them**: nothing in the code does but B and trek starting (`initGameTrek`), which raises them at a new game, on loading, and after a cut-scene or battle, but not after the pause menu. The user confirmed it in the original (Oct 4, 2026).

### The minimap

The minimap (`MiniMap`, `minimap.cpp`) is pieces of a drawn plan, one per chunk or part of one, in eight map sets. From the machine code (`LoadData` `0x80049a08`, `Update` `0x8004a5a8`, `Render` `0x8004ad14`, `UpdateSection` `0x8004af0c`, `LoadSections` `0x8004b404`, `toggle_show_minimap` `0x8004b6f0`, `ShowingSpecialSection` `0x8004b96c` and `BarrowsFlags` `0x8004bfac`). The pieces and the rules are the minimap records ([hardcoded content](../hardcoded-content.md)).

- **Which map sets:** Gwernia castle (4), Ugarit 2 (14), Erromon (7), the Ehud underground (12), the Jundar interiors' mage school (11), the misc interiors' Roog entrance (6, only K03), the Barrows (9) and the Roog cave (10), loaded with the map set (`BorgMaps::LoadMap`). Anywhere else there's none.
- **The player's piece** is the first piece for the chunk he's in, but where a rule says otherwise. The rules: Gwernia's courtyard (I07) by floor, its ground by which side (y under 4: z above 26 or not; under 8; above); the castle walls (A01) by quarter (x under 35, z under 25); the tower (O09) and the mage school's stairs (A01) by floor; the Ehud underground's C11 by quarter (48) and I09 by x under 16; the Ehud maze room (O05) and the Roog entrance (K03) by the first of their room flags set (4222–4232, 4233–4237), or none; and in the Barrows, nine room flags (4286–4294) that show a room's other state, some another chunk's piece, and E01's strip under x 1.3 showing D01's. Positions are the player's in the chunk. The courtyard's piece `0x29` is never chosen, though it shows dim, seen from the start. A chunk with no piece shows no minimap, and the compass or nothing takes its place.
- **The box** is `0x00F5`, a square with a feathered rim, in black, so a translucent dark box, stretched around the plan with a border of 8 times the scale. **Small**, with the portraits down: the plan from (237, 163) to (297, 223), the box 76 square at (229, 155), the player's piece centred, (267 − x − w/2, 193 − y − h/2) for a piece at (x, y) of width w and height h, halved by shifts. **Large**, with them up: from (177, 52) to (297, 172), the box 152 square at (161, 36), the whole plan from the corner. Over the pieces, the vignette `0x00F3` in black, stretched to the plan. The pieces are cut to the plan's area. The capture's corridor (`0x97`, Gwernia castle M05) is at (247, 187) small and (187, 101) large, and the round room beside it, seen earlier, dim.
- **Growing and shrinking** go with the portraits (`toggle_show_minimap`, which does nothing if it's that size already): the box's edges, the scale and the plan's place each move a 14th of the way they started from a frame, so 14 frames, linearly.
- **The group:** the pieces that show are the player's group's seen ones: a floor or a building. Another group's piece takes over at once; the old group stays where it was and fades out, 10 an alpha a frame, and the plan snaps to the new piece. A piece of the same group slides into place over 14 frames.
- **The colours:** the player's piece starts at grey 155 and alpha 0, rises 10 an alpha a frame to 255 (26 frames), its grey shimmering between 165 and 175 until its alpha is past 145, then 155. The others settle at grey 96 and alpha 128, 10 a frame. The original draws the group's pieces in a list whose newest is drawn first, the first piece of the table on top.
- **Seeing** a piece is when it's first chosen. The courtyard's five and the tower's upper floor in Gwernia castle, and the mage school's stairs' upper floors, are seen from the start. The original saves one bit a piece, 188 in all, in the table's order (`MiniMap_Save`, `0x80049760`); the port saves each minimap's seen pieces ([saves](saves.md)).
- **No sounds.**

### In the port

- **On other screens,** each part keeps its place from the bottom and from its side: the sundial from the left, the compass or minimap from the right, and the portraits stay centred. On a 4:3 screen of 240 lines that's where the original has them. The user agreed (Oct 4, 2026).
- **Frames:** the portraits' slide and the minimap's choice and animation step once every two ticks, the original's frame at an idealized 30 fps ([runtime](runtime.md)).
- **The fade:** the runtime's fade is a black overlay on trek's whole UI screen, so the HUD is drawn at full brightness under it, where the original dims each colour and alpha.
- **In the runtime** B is Backspace or the right face button. The pause menu, shops, training and cut-scenes hide the HUD.
- **Not ported yet:** the Detect Moon Phase and Detect Sun Phase spells, and the editor showing the HUD.

## A faithful mode, later

The floating panel and the resizable screen are enhancements. The user chose them over the original's look for now (Sep 30, 2026), and left a faithful mode for the fidelity profile at the very end ([roadmap](../roadmap.md), Phase 6's enhancements). What it would take:

- **The screen.** `UiScreen` fixed at 320 × 240 whatever the window's shape (`Lines` 240, width 320), shown pillarboxed at 4:3, with the VideoOut filter. The 3D view would need the same 4:3 frame, since the bars below cover it too.
- **The overscan bars.** Black rectangles over everything, drawn last, as `Graphics::EndList` does: 16 pixels at the left and right and 12 at the top and bottom in 1.0 and 1.1, 18 and 12 in the EU version. They'd belong over the whole frame, 3D and UI, not in a UI view, and they'd cut the conversation box off at 16, 304 and 228 as the original's is.
- **The conversation box.** A style for `DialogueBox`: a band right across the frame, from 0 to 320 and down to 180 plus the border's height (232), with only the 10-pixel gradient above it. That's a `FeatheredPanel` whose bottom and side feathering are 0, which `UiImages.DrawPanel` already skips, and the frame at (0, 0).
- **Other UI.** Everything laid out by the screen's size would be at its original place once the screen is 320 × 240, as the conversation box is. Nothing else changes.
- **High-resolution mode**, if the fidelity profile offers the Expansion Pak's option: a 512 × 240 screen, where the original lays UI out in 320 × 240 and `borg8DlistInit` stretches every image 1.6 times across, bilinear, so text is filtered horizontally. `UiScreen` would draw its 320-wide canvas into a 512-wide one with linear filtering before the VideoOut filter. Its overscan bars are 20 pixels at the sides.
- **The VI and the TV**, which apply to either mode: the VI's anti-aliasing and divot filters and a composite or CRT look are further `UiFilter`s.

## In the editor

A script's player shows the conversation box above the page's controls, a loot table's roll and a container on the map the loot window, a shop's page its window, a trainer's character page the training window, and the inventory record's page the Party tab, whose A and B buttons act on the editor's game, each over a plain backdrop, at 1×, 2× or 3×, on a 4:3 or 16:9 window, with a UI screen of 240, 360 or 480 lines and with any of the three filters (`UiScreenOptions`). When a line shows alone before its choices, Continue brings them up, as A does. Hovering a choice puts the cursor on it, as hovering a row of the Party tab's list does; the UI screen passes the mouse to its views in its own pixels (`ForwardsMouse`). `--zoom <1-3>`, `--aspect 4:3|16:9`, `--ui-lines <n>` and `--ui-filter pixels|videoout|smooth` set these for screenshots, `--press a,down,a` presses the Party tab's buttons, and `--ui-screenshot <png>` saves the UI screen in its own pixels, to compare with a capture.
