# UI: the screen, text, the conversation box, the title screen, the credits, the HUD and the battle screen (roadmap 1F, M2, M3)

How the port lays out and draws the original's UI. The pause menu and its windows, the Party, System and World tabs, the loot window, shops, training and the post-battle windows, are in [pause menu](pause-menu.md). The fonts and string tables are in [text](../formats/text.md) and the menus' sounds in [audio](audio.md#sound-effects). Positions are in the original's 320 × 240 frame, and addresses are 1.0's.

The menus' layouts come from the machine code and the user's captures from the emulator (`roms/menu-*.png`, `hud-*.png`, `pause-*.png`; see the [reference rig](../reference-rig.md#emulator-captures)), mostly from the "Before final encounter" checkpoint. Compare against those captures in the console's own pixels (`--ui-screenshot`), where the port's classic layouts match within the noise of the console's 16-bit dither, about 6 a channel.

## The screen

The original draws its UI on a 320 × 240 screen; the Expansion Pak's high-resolution mode is 512 wide, and `borg8DlistInit` scales images to it. Every UI image is a texture rectangle with bilinear filtering and a combiner of texel × primitive colour (`N64BorgImageDraw`), so text is its sheet's texels times the text's colour, alpha too. At 1:1 the filter samples each texel exactly, and only scaled images are filtered.

- **The soft look is the video output.** The emulator's captures are 640 × 240: each line's 320 pixels spread over 640 by linear interpolation, lines untouched. A real console's VI adds anti-aliasing and divot filters, and the TV more.
- **Overscan bars** (`Graphics::EndList`): in 1.0 and 1.1, black rectangles 16 pixels at the left and right and 12 at the top and bottom, drawn after everything; the EU version draws 18 and 12. The UI's own boxes run under them.

`UiScreen` (in `HBHT.Views`, which the runtime and the editor share) draws the UI the same way: views draw in a `SubViewport`, in its pixels, with linear filtering, and the screen is scaled up to fill the window by one of three filters (`UiFilter`): **VideoOut** (the default, each line doubled in width with the averages between, then scaled up sharp), **Pixels** (sharp) and **Smooth** (bilinear). The screen is `Lines` pixels tall, 240 by default, and as wide as the window's shape, never narrower than 4:3. Views lay themselves out by its size; a taller screen draws the UI smaller, with room for more, and text is always drawn pixel for pixel and wraps at the same widths. The canvas blends in on-screen values, premultiplied, so a glyph's alpha and colour multiply as the N64's do. A screen that `ForwardsMouse` passes the mouse to its views in its own pixels, where hovering a choice or row moves the cursor (the pause menu, the title and the editor's panels). The editor's `UiScreenOptions` holds a preview's zoom, aspect, lines and filter ([editor](editor.md#the-command-line)).

**Scaled images sample as the RDP does:** its first pixel on the first texel and a step of the scale per pixel, where Godot's linear filter starts half a step in. `UiPainter` offsets the source rectangle to match.

## Laying text out

`TextLayout` (`HBHT.Core.Ui`) is the font engine's layout, over a font record (`Font::PrintMain`, `GetHeightScaled`, `GetWidthScaled`):

- A character draws its glyph at the pen, as wide as its measured width and a whole cell tall, and moves the pen by that width. A character with no glyph is skipped and takes no room.
- **Text wraps only at spaces.** After a space, if the next word would end past the right edge, the line breaks and the space is dropped. A space at the start of a line takes no room, and a word longer than the line runs past the edge. `\n` breaks the line.
- Lines are the cell's height plus 2 apart: 11 pixels in both fonts.
- Text is drawn only inside its widget's bounds. A glyph across an edge is cut, and one outside is dropped. `UiPainter`'s text and images take a clip box, which crops without moving where text wraps.
- Characters from `0x7F` are the controller buttons, drawn in their own colour with the text's alpha.
- **A text widget has no font of its own.** It draws, and measures itself, in whatever font the printer has set at the time (`Font.Select`, `0x800AA5D4`; `WidgetClipText.Draw`, `Height`). The main font is set but while a container draws or steps its children: the Party tab's lists (`PartyMenu.ListsDraw` `0x8003140C`, `ListsStep` `0x800314CC`), training's trainer (`TrainingMenu.Draw` `0x80032288`), barter's pile (`BarterMenu.Draw` `0x8003D0D8`), the portraits (`PartyMenu.PortraitsDraw` `0x8003B9A0`) and the details' and parchment popups (`MenuPopup.Draw` `0x80034AF0`) set the slim font and the main one back after. So everything those hold draws in the slim font, whatever it was made under, and a text measured as it's built is measured in the main font unless its builder set the slim one first.

## Input

Every menu, popup and page takes a press as a `MenuAction`, which the runtime translates once from Godot's event (`MenuStick.Translate`) by the controls of the situation the game is in ([controls](pause-menu.md#controls)). What a press does is the core's: `GameSession.Press` gives it to the mode that runs and says what it came to (`PressOutcome`). Trek's (`TrekLoop.Press`) goes to a backdrop's page, a map object's popup or a conversation first, and otherwise Start opens the menu, A uses the map object reached and B raises the portraits, none of them while trek fades into a cut-scene or goes through a teleporter.

- **The stick counts once as it crosses a threshold** (`MenuStick`). The original's menus count a direction from 56 of the N64 stick's 80, 0.7 (`Controller::ReadInput`, `0x800915d8`); ares scales a full push to about 100, so the port counts from 0.58 and lets go below 0.5.
- **Held,** the stick or the D-pad repeats after 30 ticks and then every 5: the original's widgets count 30 of the controller's 60 samples a second, then 5 (`BaseWidget::Control`, `0x800ad708`), for the stick, the D-pad, C-up and C-down. The session's `StickRepeat` counts the ticks, so a hold repeats on the same ticks whatever the frame rate: in the pause menu a repeat moves the cursor (not while it opens, closes or slides); in trek's popups, conversations and backdrops, a cut-scene and combat's held menus it's a press; and where nothing has a cursor the hold is forgotten, so the next doesn't start by repeating. A keyboard key repeats as the system repeats it.

## The conversation box

`DialogueBox` (`HBHT.Core.Ui`) places what a conversation shows, as `DiaMenuSub::InitDefault` and `DialougeClass::Setup` do, and `ConversationBoxView` draws it in the main font. Backdrops and the help menu draw on a full-screen image instead ([below](#books-and-the-help-menu)). The box is laid out in the 320 × 240 frame, placed at the bottom centre of the UI screen. A on its page is `DialogueBox.Accept` ([scripts](scripts.md#how-it-walks)), which conversations, cut-scenes and the editor's script page all press through.

- **The original's band** is translucent black: image `0x1C4`, a single white texel of alpha 136, drawn black. It runs right across the screen from the box's top to 232 (180 plus the portrait border's height), so the overscan bars cut it at 16, 304 and 228. Above it a 10-pixel gradient fades upward, image `0x1C3` squeezed from 16 pixels.
- **The port floats it**, the user's choice, since a wide screen has no bars to cut it off: the band runs from 16 to 304 and down to 228, feathered on every side from the pieces the original's popups are made of (`WidgetChoiceDia::SetImages`, see `FeatheredPanel`): `0x1C3` above, `0x1C2` below, `0x1C0` left and `0x1C1` right, each fading outward from alpha 136, and the rounded corners `0x1BC`–`0x1BF`.
- **The portrait's border** (`0xDF`, 52 × 52) is at x 20, level with the box's top, and the portrait (40 × 40) sits 6 pixels inside it. A speaker with no portrait gets `0x0125` (`BORG8_PortraitNPCMale1`).
- **The line** is in (200, 200, 200), from x 78 to 294, and ends 2 pixels above what's under it: the band's bottom, or the choices. The box's top is 4 pixels above the line, but never below 180, so a short line sits at y 184, and the box grows upward from five lines.
- **Choices** are in tan (200, 180, 100), from x 88, 4 pixels apart, and end at 228. The cursor's choice pulses toward (50, 45, 25, 63) and back over 24 frames each way, whole steps per channel (`WidgetScrollMenu::Tick`).
- **A line before its own speaker's choices shows alone.** When a menu's first choice has the line's speaker, the line shows first, and A brings up the choices alone under Alaron's portrait. The box shrinks to the choices but never below 180, its four lines (Brenna's recruiting shows it); the portrait and its border stay where the line put them, since `Setup` doesn't move them on that path.

Not ported yet: the widgets' fade in and out (`fadeFloatMirror`) and scrolling a menu taller than its bounds.

## Books and the help menu

A backdrop script (78 of them: books, scrolls, plaques and stones in the world, all started by A from a dialogue object) and the help menu (script `0x054B`) show their text over a full-screen picture rather than in the conversation box. The script runner runs them, offering taken choices again and setting their done flag ([scripts](scripts.md)); they use only flags and skill checks. `BackdropPage` (`HBHT.Core.Ui`) lays the page out and `BackdropStyle` dresses it by picture; trek makes the page as the conversation shows it (`TrekLoop.ShowBackdrop`), and `BackdropLayer` and `BackdropView` draw it.

- **Starting.** The script table (`Dialogue.ModeTable`, `0x8005102c`) has five functions a phase, by `start_func`; backdrops and help share theirs (`Backdrop.Start` `0x80051904`, `Backdrop.End` `0x80051950`). Help from the System tab (`pauseMenuSelectCallBack`, `0x800403ac`) sets the menu's exit code 3 (`PauseMenu.Exit`, `0x80048e64`), so the menu rolls up and the world brightens as when it closes, and a frame or two later trek starts the script.
- **No fades.** The original's page appears and goes at once; its picture is opaque and covers the 320 × 240 frame.
- **The page** (`Backdrop.Widgets` `0x80046db8`, `Backdrop.Page` `0x80047930`, `Backdrop.Node` `0x80047e14`): the picture at (0, 0), full white; one text in the main font at (64, 30), wrapping and cut between x 64 and 256 and y 30 and 205, each node's text replacing the last with no paging; and the choices, at most 8, in a scroll menu (`WidgetScrollMenu`) from x 75 to 256 and y 55 to 205, the first 4 below the text, each wrapped between 75 and 256 and stacked 4 apart. No arrows, bar or cursor image.
  - **The list slides** to keep the cursor's choice inside the menu, at once, by as much as its foot is below 205 or its top above 55 (`WidgetScrollMenu.Step`, `0x800b01c0`). That keeps the choices from rising above 55 under a short text, and lifts them over a text too tall for them. Choices past the menu's edges are cut. **The slide is never reset from node to node** (`WidgetScrollMenu.Clear`, `0x800b24e4`, clears only the choices and the cursor), so after a one-line text has pushed the list down 10, a taller text's choices are 14 below it, not 4. The port keeps this.
  - **The cursor** starts on the first choice of each node, and up and down wrap (`WidgetScrollMenu.Up` `0x800b0480`, `Down` `0x800b0548`). Left and right do nothing. A takes the choice, or with none moves on (`Dialogue.OnA`, `0x800482f0`). In the help menu, B takes the last choice, Exit Help or Back, or with none moves on as A does (`Dialogue.OnBHelp`, `0x80048354`); in a book it does nothing. The difference is the script's: `Dialogue.ShowNode` gives the page a B only when its start mode is 4, help (`0x80047714`), which only `aidyn:dialogue/0x054B` has, and B on a widget with none returns 0 (`Widget.CallB`, `0x800ade68`). **No sounds.**
- **The pictures,** by the script's picture number (`libraryType`), all opaque 320 × 240. ## The title screen

The intro cards and the title (`Title.Frame` `0x8001b154`, then game mode 0xF, `Title.ModeFrame` `0x800551c0`), with its menu (`IntroMenu`, built by `IntroMenu.Build` `0x800429b8`). `TitleScreen` and `IntroCards` in the core, which the session shows over a new game's session (`GameSession.ShowTitle`) and steps as its own mode, `GameMode.Title`; `TitleLayer` and `Game.Title.cs` in the runtime draw it. The user's captures are `roms/intro-*.png` and `roms/menu-main2*.png`.

### Power-on

`App.Init` (`0x8001ac7c`) sets `App.State` (`0x800d808c`) to 5, and `HandleAppFrame` switches on it: 5 checks the region (a PAL console gets Borg8 `0x37C3`, "This game is not designed for use on this system", for ever) and the controller (none: "WARNING NO CONTROLLER", `0xFB`, state 4, for ever); 3 is the Controller Pak's check (`PakCheck.Frame`, `0x80082280`: Start held at boot opens its manager, and a pak with no note and fewer than 28 free pages gets the Controller strings' screens); then 0, the cards, and 1, the game's modes, whose first call is `Game.Restart` (`0x8001f284`): mode 0xF, the title, fading in. The port has none of the checks: its saves are files.

### The intro cards

`Title.CardsFrame` (`0x8001b614`) clears the screen to black and steps a state machine (`0x800d80a8`) through five cards, each its own state and alpha. The title's music starts with them (`Title.StartMusic`, `0x8001b0b0`): 0x0632 (0x05D9 without the Pak), looped at the music volume, which plays on under the title; nothing else sounds on the cards.

| Card | State | What it draws | Hold |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Expansion Pak | 5, `0x8001b86c` | Borg8 `0x3758`, "Expansion Pak Enhanced / Expansion Pak Detected" (`0x3759`, "No Expansion Pak Detected", without it) | 30 |
| The legal text | 4, `0x8001b9bc` | The Copyright string table's 12 lines in the slim font, each centred (`Title.LegalLine`, `0x8001b928`), from y 59, 9 a line and 5 more after lines 1, 3 and 7 | 120 |
| The N64 logo | 1, `0x8001bba8` | Scene `0x25A8`, not animated, seen from a camera going round it 40 out and 15 up, looking at its origin, 2° a tick from 0 (`Title.LogoAngle`, `0x800daebc`), with its own 45° and planes of 1 and 256, tinted grey by the fade; and "Licensed by Nintendo", `0x00FA`, centred at y 190 | 120 |
| THQ | 2, `0x8001be3c` | `0x0101` | 120 |
| H2O | 3, `0x8001bf10` | `0x00DE` | 120 |

- **The images** are centred, white at the card's alpha (`Title.DrawCardImage`, `0x8001b778`). The legal text and the caption have every byte of their colour the alpha, so they fade as its square.
- **The fade** (`Title.CardFade`, `0x8001b250`): 15 more a tick until it's whole (17 ticks), the hold, 15 less a tick to 0 (17 ticks), and black until 36 ticks from when it began to fade out; the next card starts on the tick after. 53 ticks and the hold a card, 775 ticks in all, about 13 seconds.
- **Ticks:** the cards step once a pad event, which the controller's thread pushes once a 60 Hz retrace (`Input.Thread`, `0x80091344`), so a tick each whatever the frame rate. The menu's fades and blink step the same way.
- **No button skips them** in the original: the pad's events are only counted. **The port lets A, B or Start skip all five** to the title when there's a saved game to load, the user's choice (`IntroCards.Skippable`); with none they play through. The title's music swells as the cards end, so a skip moves it on by the ticks the cards had left (`IntroCards.Skipped`, `GameAudio.AdvanceMusic`, which plays the sequence on unheard, `AudioEngine.Advance`), and it swells as the fly-through starts, as when they play through; the user's choice too.
- **In the port** the cards are drawn in the 320 × 240 frame at the centre of the title's own UI screen, which follows Menu Size, so they shrink with the menus; the N64 logo's scene is drawn in a view of the frame's size on that screen.

### The title

`Title.Build` (`0x80054f04`) makes the menu and the scenery; then each frame `Title.Input` (`0x80055560`) hands the pad's events to the menu, the sky is drawn with the weather restored to clear (`Weather.Restore` with nothing), then the scenery and the menu, and the music's volume follows the Audio page.

- **The fly-throughs** (`Title.StartScenery` `0x80054d70`, `Title.SceneryFrame` `0x80054f70`, the title record's `flycams`): a scene with a camera, moved by its Borg6 at a tick a tick, over the Overworld's chunks round the fly-through's row and column (`InitZoneEngine(2, 0)`). Its coordinates are from the corner of the fly-through's origin chunk (`originRow`, `originColumn`, the two u16s stored at `0x800D90DC` and `0x800D90DE`): from that corner, and no other, every fly-through's camera runs a few units over the ground. The clock stands at a new game's noon (`Game.Init`, which `Game.Restart` runs; `GameClock.Step` is trek's alone).
- **Their fades:** the first fades in from black over 15 ticks. Once its animation is within 101 ticks of its end, it fades out over 100 (0.01 a tick), its scenery goes (`Title.FreeScenery`, `0x80055698`), and the next builds and fades in over 100, after the sixth the first. Without the Pak the fifth is passed over. The place (`Title.FlycamPlace`, `0x800e0a54`) moves on only once one has ended, and nothing resets it, so going back to the title shows the place it last left.
- **The logo** (`IntroMenu.ShowPressStart`, `0x800432b4`): its shadow, the grey `0x0104` (213 × 180), at (40, 20), the logo `0x0103` over it at (54, 20), and PRESS START, the image `0x0102`, at (123, 205), 5 under the shadow, all white at the logo's alpha, which rises 20 a tick from 0. **PRESS START blinks** (`IntroMenu.Blink`, `0x80042e5c`): a count to 101, then back to 70, turning it on or off each time, so it's hidden for 101 ticks and then shown and hidden 31 each.
- **A, B or Start** bring the menu up (`IntroMenu.OnA` `0x800442b4`, `OnB` `0x800440d8`, `OnStart` `0x800441c8`, all `IntroMenu.ShowList` `0x80043598`), each with its sound: the logo fades out 20 a tick as the list fades in, and nothing takes a press until both have (`IntroMenu.Input`, `0x80043e44`). Up and down play the cursor's sound even here.
- **No idle timeout:** the title never goes back to the cards or to a demo, and it can't start the credits (`Credits.Source` 0 is only the unwritten default).

### The menu

The pause menu's System tab's pages (`SystemMenu` with `title`), in the title's colours on feathered black boxes (`SystemStyle.Title`), so a change to a page changes both, the user's choice: the list, the options, the load screen and the name entry are laid out where [the System tab](pause-menu.md#the-system-tab) has them, but for the list.

- **The list** (`IntroMenu.MakeList`, `0x80043ae8`): the title `0x00F2` at (130, 75), and New Game, Load Game and Options (common lines 159–161), then the port's Quit Game, centred on x 160 from y 115, 19 apart (the scroll menu's gap of 8 under each line), in off-white (225, 225, 225), the cursor's row pulsing between the scroll menu's grey (100, 100, 100, 100) and white. Load Game is always there, saves or none. **B does nothing** on it; Start chooses, as A does.
- **The boxes** (`IntroMenu_ShadowBG`, `IntroMenu.ShadowBox` `0x80042eb0`): the conversation box's band and gradients, black, each page's at its alpha as the pages crossfade (`IntroMenu.Draw`, `0x80042aa8`). The list's is (94, 67)–(226, 183) for the original's three rows, reaching a row (19) further down for the port's fourth; the name entry's (52, 43)–(268, 179) and the load screen's the save screen's group box. The original's options box runs from 16 left of the page and 40 above it to 192 right and 20 below its last row; the port's options pages differ, so each is boxed 12 beyond its rows either side (on Video and Enhancements to the widest of any of their values, so changing one doesn't move it), from 6 above the title to 8 under the last row. Their boxes differ in size, so each page is moved to centre its box on the screen, and its title and the main page's rows are centred on the box, the user's choice; the pause menu's stay where they are on the parchment, the title at the top.
- **The pages crossfade** 20 a tick, 13 ticks (`IntroMenu.Step`, `0x80042d0c`), where the System tab's do 60, and take no press until they've settled.
- **New Game** (`IntroMenu.ShowNameEntry`, `0x800437f8`) opens the name entry on Alaron's name ([renaming Alaron](pause-menu.md#renaming-alaron)). OK, or Start twice, confirms (`IntroMenu.Confirm`, `0x80043cfc`, state 6); B or the cancel key goes back to the list.
- **Load Game** (`IntroMenu.ShowLoad`, `0x800439a8`) opens the pause menu's load screen ([saving and loading](pause-menu.md#saving-and-loading)) in the title's colours. The original's is 16 further left (51, 24), without the System roller to clear; the port keeps the System tab's place. **A and Start make no sound** on it. B goes back to the list (`IntroMenu.OnLoadBack`, `0x80043984`).
- **Options** (`IntroMenu.ShowOptions`, `0x800436c8`; `OptionsPage.Create` with 0) is the port's options, without Rename Character, as the original's leaves out its GAME rows.
- **Quit Game**, the port's, quits at once: there's nothing to lose, so no box asks, and the title ends with no fade (`PauseExit.Quit`, then `Transition.Quit`).
- **Leaving:** a confirmed name, or a save chosen, fades the whole screen out by 0.05 a tick, 20 ticks (`Title.Action`, `0x800d958c`). Then `Title.Leave` (`0x80055718`) stops the title's music, and New Game loads the opening (`Cinematic.Load(0, 1, 1)` at `0x80055434`), a loaded game going straight to trek. In the port, New Game names Alaron, builds trek's world where a new game arrives and fires `game.started` ([events](events.md)) on the same session; a save chosen is loaded as the System tab's Load does.
- **The sounds** (`IntroMenu.OnUp` `0x80044020` on): the cursor `0x073B` for up and down, A `0x0740` for A and Start, B `0x0741`, and the System tab's for the stick's left and right on the options.

### Coming back to it

Alaron's death and the ending's credits restart the game at the title: the death scene's ending kind 2 makes `CutScene.Free` (`0x8005169c`) return mode 0, and the ending's credits' end (`Credits.Frame`, `0x8001f648`, with `Credits.Source` 2) does the same; `ScreenFade.Step` frees the game and `App.State` goes back to 0, so **the five cards play again** before `Game.Restart` builds the title, on the fly-through it last showed. In the port both are `Transition.GameOver` ([events](events.md)), and the runtime starts its scene again on the title with a new game ([runtime](runtime.md#transitions)), its cards skippable as at launch.

### In the port

- **The session** (`GameSession.ShowTitle`): trek's world is freed and its music stopped; the title's music plays as the zone engine's, and its scenery's chunks skip their music objects, as the original's do on `Zones.Mode` 2 (`MapSounds.SkipsMusic`). Each fly-through's scenery is a stage of trek's zone engine on the Overworld (`GameSession.Scenery`), its chunks following the camera, its sounds heard from it, and the sky entering its chunk. No time is played on the title.
- **The camera:** the runtime poses the fly-through's scene, hidden, at the title's time (`FlycamDriver`), its root at the origin chunk's corner, puts the view where its camera is, roll and all (`CameraRig.ShowFrom`), and tells the core (`TitleScreen.Look`). The view's field of view is trek's 45°, near 0.1 and the sky's far plane.
- **The fades:** the fly-through's darkens the world (the trek UI's black layer), under the logo and the menu; the leaving fade darkens everything.

## The credits

The credits widget (game mode 0x13, `Credits.Frame` at `0x8001f648`, `Credits.Start` at `0x8002c0b0`, `Credits.Step` at `0x8002c6d8`), which the System tab's Credits row plays ([pause menu](pause-menu.md#the-system-tab)), and the ending after the last cinematic ([cinematics](cinematics.md#what-starts-them)). `CreditsRoll` in the core, which the session steps and ends (`GameSession.Credits`), drawn by `CreditsLayer`, from the credits record (`aidyn:credits/0`), which carries the cards, the number shown one at a time and "The End".

- **The way in:** the row (id 9999 in `pauseMenuSelectCallBack`, `0x800403ac`) sets `Credits.Source` to 1 and the menu exits with code 5 (`PauseMenu.Exit`, `0x80048e64`): it rolls up as closing does, then trek's state 0x19 returns mode 0x13. There's no fade: the screen cuts to black, filled every frame (`Screen.FillRect`), and the trek is held as it stood, not freed.
- **The cards.** `Credits.Start` cuts the credits' string table (table 10) into 33 cards by a table of their first lines in the code (`Credits.CardStarts`, `0x800CF060`, ROM `0xCFC60`); a card's first line is its heading, in yellow (225, 225, 0), and the rest its names, in white (225, 225, 225). There's no marker in the text: THQ Credits and Studio X are cards of a heading alone. Each line is a text widget in the main font (`WidgetClipText`), 11 pixels a line, centred across at (320 − width) / 2; three names start with a newline, which leaves a blank line above them. The table's last line, "The End", is the ending's alone.
- **The first 12 cards** (H2O Credits to Lead Tester) show one at a time (`Credits.StepCards`, `0x8002c7b8`): each is moved up from the screen's foot by half its height and the screen's, which centres it, fades in by 4 × delta a frame, holds for 120 / delta frames and fades out by 4 × delta, and the next is set up on the frame after. They stop when the card ending at line 29 (a literal `0x1D`), Designers, comes up: it's the first to scroll.
- **The rest scroll** (`Credits.StepScroll`, `0x8002ca64`), every one starting with its top at the screen's foot. Every other frame, the first card still on screen moves up a pixel, and each after it too once the one before has its foot 32 pixels above the screen's, so the cards come 32 apart. A card that's gone off the top is passed. This counts frames, not ticks; the original runs it at 60 frames a second, and so does the port. The scroll ends when Special Thanks has gone off the top, on a black screen.
- **The end:** from the System tab, the credits end there (`Credits.StepEnd`, `0x8002cb50`). From the ending, "The End" shows in white at y 114, centred, fading in by 2 × delta a frame, holding 240 frames and fading out, and then the game goes back to the title, its intro cards first ([coming back to it](#coming-back-to-it)).
- **A, B and Start** end them at once, with no fade (`Credits.Skip`, `0x8002cd90`); the ending's can't be skipped.
- **The music:** `Credits.Start` plays 0x05D9 (the sounds record's `Credits`), looped, at the music volume as it is then, and sets the sound and music volumes to 0, so trek's music and the map's sounds go on silently under it (`ProcessAudioBubbles` keeps running; `GameAudio.Muted` in the port). As they end (`Credits.Destroy`, `0x8002c5a0`) 0x05D9 stops at once and the volumes are set back. The calendar and the weather stand still; the play time counts on.
- **The way out:** the System tab's credits return mode 9: trek fades in from black over 30 ticks (`0x800ce214`, 1/30), with input ignored, and then the menu opens on the System tab's list with the cursor on Load Game, rolling in as it always does. The original skips the fade-in the first time after the console starts (its flag `0x800db06c` is 0 only then); the Theatre's way back shares this. The port always fades in.
- **In the port** they're drawn on a UI screen of their own, as tall as the menu's lines, with the cards centred across and down it.

## The trek HUD

Trek's HUD (`draw_hud_elements`, `0x80020bd4`) is the sundial at the bottom left, the party's portraits at the bottom centre, and at the bottom right the minimap where the player's chunk has a piece, or else the compass under an outdoor sky (sky type 3), or nothing. `TrekHud` (`HBHT.Core.Ui`) is the state, with `Sundial`, `Compass`, `PartyPortraits` and `Minimap`; `TrekLoop` owns it and `HudView` draws it.

- **Drawn only in trek frames**, and not while a conversation or sign shows (`diaClass->menu`), the pause menu, a shop, training or a cut-scene. Popups draw over it. Every colour and alpha is the scene's brightness times its own, so it fades with the screen.
- **The images** are texture rectangles at quarter-pixel positions, texel × colour, blended (`borg8DlistInit` with flag 6, `Borg8_DrawSimple`). The rings and the frames have their feathering painted in.

### The sundial

`Sundial::Draw` (`0x800248a4`), centred on (49, 199):

- **The ring** `0x001B` (60 square) at (19, 169), white at alpha 80.
- **The moon** at (39, 189), in (160, 160, 220) at alpha 160, by its phase ([rendering](rendering.md#the-skys-objects)): new `0x01B8`, crescent `0x01B3` or `0x01B4` and quarter `0x01B6` or `0x01B7` in the first and second halves of the moon's four weeks (`(week & 3) >> 1`), full `0x01B5`. It shows at every hour.
- **The rays** `0x01BA` (50 square) at (24, 174), at alpha 80, in the hour's colour.
- **The sun** goes round the ring **twice a day**, 30° an hour clockwise from the top, so noon and midnight are both at the top: its centre is (49 + 25 sin a, 199 − 25 cos a), a in whole degrees, (int)(time of day × 720) mod 720. By day it's the glow `0x01BB`, by night the small dot `0x01B9`, each 20 square.
- **The hour's colour**, for the rays and the sun: from 7 to 21 peach (255, 190, 150), the glow at alpha 160 and no dot; from 23 to 5 blue (120, 100, 190), the dot at 160 and no glow. At dawn (5 to 7) and dusk (21 to 23) the colour blends linearly between them, and the glow and dot cross-fade at 80 an hour. The original dims day's and night's colour by the scene's brightness but not the blend's.
- **The moon and sun hide in caves** and interiors: under sky type 4 (terrains 6, 8, 9, 11 and 13–16, the port's terrains that don't draw the sky). The original decides only on crossing from a chunk under one sky to the other (`TeleportPlayer` at `0x8000bfd0`, through `Sundial::ToggleSun` and `ToggleMoon`, whose names are swapped), and trek starting shows them wherever it is (`Sundial::Init`), so after loading in a cave they show until the player leaves and comes back. **The port fixes it**, deciding by the sky when trek starts too, the user's choice. The Detect Moon Phase and Detect Sun Phase spells write to show them too (`0x800703c8`), but they're cast only in a battle, where trek's sundial is gone, so they show nothing ([magic](magic.md#what-each-spell-does)). The ring and rays always show.

### The compass

`Compass::Draw` (`0x800240b8`), centred on (271, 199):

- **The ring** is the sundial's, at (241, 169), white at alpha 80.
- **The letters** are 16-square images, N `0x0018`, S `0x0019`, W `0x001A` and E `0x0017`, drawn in that order in (160, 160, 220), each centred 25 out from the centre. With (a, b) the camera's look in X and Z, normalised (`rotationXZ`): N at (−25a, 25b) and alpha 160 − 95b, S at (25a, −25b) and 160 + 95b, W at (25b, 25a) and 160 − 95a, E at (−25b, −25a) and 160 + 95a. So the way the camera looks is at the top, at alpha 255, and behind it at the bottom, at 65. North is −Z and east +X.
- **It shows** only under an outdoor sky with no minimap showing.

### The party's portraits

`Portraits::Draw` (`0x8002569c`):

- **The frames**, `0x00DF` (the conversation box's) at 0.8, so 41.6 square and overlapping by 3.6, at x 84, 122, 160 and 198, all four even for an empty slot, in white at alpha 128. **The portraits** at 0.75, 30 square, 6 inside, at x 90, 128, 166 and 204, at alpha 128 too, in party order. A dead member looks the same; nothing shows hit points or who's chosen. The original never sets the portraits' blue, which carries the frames' over and is dimmed by the brightness again; at full brightness that's the same.
- **They slide** 4 pixels a frame, once a rendered frame whatever its ticks: up to 184 while they're up, down to 240, off the screen, while they're down, 14 frames either way.
- **B raises or lowers them** (`Actor::Move` at `0x80015110`), but not through a fade. **Pushing the stick past 0.05 lowers them** (`0x8001536c`), unless R holds the camera. **Standing still doesn't raise them**: only B and trek starting do (`InitGameTrek`, `0x80020f40`, which sets their flag, `0x800d813c`, and grows the minimap with it), at a new game, on loading, and after a cut-scene, a battle, a backdrop or the help menu, but not after the pause menu, a conversation or a sign. Those three are the flag's only writers. A backdrop and the help menu leave trek as a cut-scene does (their script's start sets trek's state to 0xb, `0x80051fd4`, which frees trek, and `InitGameTrek` builds it again as the script ends), so after reading a book or a plaque the portraits come up and the minimap grows, and walking off lowers them again at once; a sign shows in the box over trek (`0x80052014`) and ends without leaving it (`0x80052088`). The port starts the HUD as trek is built again (`TrekLoop.Rebuild`, `TrekHud.Start`).

### The minimap

The minimap (`MiniMap`, `minimap.cpp`) is pieces of a drawn plan, one per chunk or part of one, in eight map sets (`LoadData` `0x80049a08`, `Update` `0x8004a5a8`, `Render` `0x8004ad14`, `UpdateSection` `0x8004af0c`, `LoadSections` `0x8004b404`, `toggle_show_minimap` `0x8004b6f0`, `ShowingSpecialSection` `0x8004b96c` and `BarrowsFlags` `0x8004bfac`). The pieces and the rules are the minimap records ([hardcoded content](../hardcoded-content.md)).

- **Which map sets:** Gwernia castle (4), Ugarit 2 (14), Erromon (7), the Ehud underground (12), the Jundar interiors' mage school (11), the misc interiors' Roog entrance (6, only K03), the Barrows (9) and the Roog cave (10), loaded with the map set (`BorgMaps::LoadMap`).
- **The player's piece** is the first piece for the chunk he's in, but where a rule says otherwise. The rules: Gwernia's courtyard (I07) by floor, its ground by which side (y under 4: z above 26 or not; under 8; above); the castle walls (A01) by quarter (x under 35, z under 25); the tower (O09) and the mage school's stairs (A01) by floor; the Ehud underground's C11 by quarter (48) and I09 by x under 16; the Ehud maze room (O05) and the Roog entrance (K03) by the first of their room flags set (4222–4232, 4233–4237), or none; and in the Barrows, nine room flags (4286–4294) that show a room's other state, some another chunk's piece, and E01's strip under x 1.3 showing D01's. Positions are the player's in the chunk. The courtyard's piece `0x29` is never chosen, though it shows dim, seen from the start. A chunk with no piece shows no minimap, and the compass or nothing takes its place.
- **The box** is `0x00F5`, a square with a feathered rim, in black, stretched around the plan with a border of 8 times the scale. **Small**, with the portraits down: the plan from (237, 163) to (297, 223), the box 76 square at (229, 155), the player's piece centred, (267 − x − w/2, 193 − y − h/2) for a piece at (x, y) of width w and height h, halved by shifts. **Large**, with them up: from (177, 52) to (297, 172), the box 152 square at (161, 36), the whole plan from the corner. Over the pieces, the vignette `0x00F3` in black, stretched to the plan. The pieces are cut to the plan's area.
- **Growing and shrinking** go with the portraits (`toggle_show_minimap`, which does nothing if it's that size already), called only by B, the stick and trek starting (`Actor::Move`, `InitGameTrek`): the box's edges, the scale and the plan's place each move a 14th of the way they started from a frame, so 14 frames, linearly.
- **The group:** the pieces that show are the player's group's seen ones: a floor or a building. Another group's piece takes over at once; the old group fades out, 10 an alpha a frame, and the plan snaps to the new piece. A piece of the same group slides into place over 14 frames.
- **The colours:** the player's piece starts at grey 155 and alpha 0, rises 10 an alpha a frame to 255, its grey shimmering between 165 and 175 until its alpha is past 145, then 155. The others settle at grey 96 and alpha 128, 10 a frame. The original draws the group's pieces newest first, so the first piece of the table is on top.
- **Seeing** a piece is when it's first chosen. The courtyard's five and the tower's upper floor in Gwernia castle, and the mage school's stairs' upper floors, are seen from the start. The original saves one bit a piece, 188 in all, in the table's order (`MiniMap_Save`, `0x80049760`); the port saves each minimap's seen pieces ([saves](saves.md)).
- **No sounds.**

### In the port

- **On other screens,** each part keeps its place from the bottom and from its side: the sundial from the left, the compass or minimap from the right, and the portraits centred.
- **Frames:** the portraits' slide and the minimap's choice and animation step once every two ticks, the original's frame at an idealized 30 fps ([ADR 0017](../adr/0017-the-core-steps-every-tick.md)).
- **The fade:** the runtime's fade is a black overlay on trek's whole UI screen, so the HUD is drawn at full brightness under it, where the original dims each colour and alpha.
- **Not ported yet:** the editor showing the HUD.

## The battle screen

What a battle draws over its arena (`combatgui.cpp`, `0x80085A80`–`0x80087C60`, and the radar, `0x8008DD30`–`0x8008E3C0`), matched to the user's capture `roms/combat.png` (Brenna's turn against four rats with six in reserve) and checkpoint 02's RAM. `CombatGui` (`HBHT.Core.Ui`) is the model, which the battle holds (`Battle.Gui`) and steps each tick; `CombatRadar` and `CombatLogBox` are its radar and log box; `CombatGuiView` (`HBHT.Game`) draws it on trek's UI screen. Every part is drawn in every state of a battle, the original's in this order (`CombatGui.Draw`, `0x80085F64`; `DrawAll`, `0x800863E4`): the log box, the radar, the frames, the portraits, the HP bars.

### The portraits

`CombatGui.Create` (`0x80085B80`) makes an entry for each party member, two placeholders and each of the enemy list, reserves too, and a frame (a "bar", `CombatGui.Bars`) for each member, two spares under them and each of the list (`CreateBars`, `0x800864F4`). Their order is fixed.

- **The frames** are opaque images drawn under the portraits and tinted, so only their rims show: large `0x01C6` (28 × 26) with a 23-square portrait, small `0x01C7` (14 × 13) with an 11 × 10 one, the portraits scaled from 40. The party's run down x 270 and the enemies' x 21, from y 18, 28 apart; the party's portrait is 4 in (its HP bar's strip on the left), the enemies' 2 (the strip on the right). The first four of the list are large; the rest are small, 12 apart from y 130 (overlapping a pixel), four at x 21 and then at 35. The two spares under the party (y 18 + 28 × size) are hidden, for whoever's in neither list (a summons). The Shadow is in its battles' enemy lists, so it takes an enemy's frame. Empty frames draw nothing.
- **Who goes where** (`AssignPortrait`, `0x80085FC8`, as `Combat.CreateBody` makes each body): the first waiting entry of the combatant's entity takes its slot and the first free frame of its side, so the party in slot order top to bottom and the enemies on the field in the large frames. The first tick gives the reserves the small frames in the list's order (`0x80086C84`). A reserve that joins (`Combat.SpawnReserves`, [combat](combat-actions.md#an-attack)) swaps frames with the first dead or fled enemy, which goes out of the fight into the small frame. Anyone in neither list takes a placeholder and the first spare.
- **They slide** (`PlaceInBar`, `0x800869F4`): every portrait starts at (160, 120), 40 square, and goes to its frame over 60 ticks of the frame's delta (`0x800D43E4`), so a second whatever the frame rate: x in a straight line, y easing out (`MenuTween.EaseOut`), the width and height easing in and out (`MenuTween.Ease`). At the start that's under the fade in.
- **The dead and fled** (`MarkDead` `0x80086208` as the body is freed, `MarkFled` `0x80086230` as a flight ends, `MarkReserveFled` `0x80086338`): the skull `0x016D` fades in over the portrait, 0 to 255 over 60 ticks, sized to it, and the portrait stays in its frame. A fled one's frame is orange (200, 164, 0, 255). They lose their HP bar and tints.
- **The HP bars** (`DrawHpBars`, `0x80087168`), over everything, at the frame at once and from the live hit points: 3 wide and the portrait's height, 4 left of the party's portrait (x 270) and 24 right of an enemy's (47), black, then the fill from the bottom, (int)(height × hp ÷ max) and never under a pixel. Its colour has no steps (`HpColour`, `0x80085A80`): u = min(255, hp × 255 ÷ max), (255 − u, u, 0).
- **The tints** are the frames' colours (`CombatGui.TintAll`, `0x8008713C`), worked out every frame, each rule over the one before: black at alpha 200; orange for a fled one; then for each in play, orange while the AI has it fleeing (`EnemyAI.IsFleeing`), dim green (0, 200, 0, 150) as set A's candidate (what the current combatant can attack), **dim red (200, 0, 0, 150) as set B's candidate**, bright green (0, 255, 0, 255) as set A's selected while an attack or a flask is aimed (states 9 and 18), and bright red (255, 0, 0, 255) as set B's selected while a spell or its area is (10 and 11) (`TintByState`, `0x80086EB4`). They don't pulse, and side doesn't matter.
- **Why red shows when nothing's being cast:** set B holds what the current combatant's *selected* spell could be cast at, and a caster keeps a selected spell (its entity's `+0x3D` and `+0x3E`) from turn to turn. So on Alaron's, Rheda's or Sholeh's turn, the dim red is on everyone their spell reaches, the party too for a healing spell, from the turn's first frame and in every state. Brenna and the rats have no spell, so their turns, the capture's among them, show none.
- **C-left does nothing.** It toggles a flag (`CombatGui.Shown`, `0x800E3140`, in `Combat.ControlsByState` at `0x80085114`) that nothing reads, in 1.0 or the debug build, so nothing hides the screen. The port has no flag.

### The log box

A `TextBand` (`CombatGui.LogBoxCreate`, `0x80087A78`), the cinematics' caption box's widget ([cinematics](cinematics.md#the-caption-box)), not the conversation box's, that never drops away. `CombatLogBox` lays it out.

- **The original's** rectangle is (0, 180) to (246, 240), with no growing: the band from 180 down and the gradient 11 tall above it, both right across the frame; the line in the main font in (225, 225, 225, 200) at (76, 190), wrapping at 241 and cut at 230, which keeps it off the radar; the conversation box's border `0x00DF` at (20, 174) and the current combatant's portrait at (26, 180), level with the band's top. An enemy's turn shows the enemy's portrait. Before the first turn's line it shows only the band.
- **The port floats it as the conversation box**, the user's choice, so the two read as one panel: laid out in the original's frame at the bottom centre of the UI screen, the band from 16 to 304 and 180 to 228, feathered on every side with the conversation box's gradients (10 above, 12 below, 16 beside), and the border and portrait at (20, 180) and (26, 186), level with the box's top as the conversation box has them, 6 lower than the original's. The line stays at the original's (76, 190), lowered as the box rarely holds more than one.
- **The lines** are the battle's (`CombatLog`, `Battle.Lines`, the struct's `+0x4FC4`–`+0x52C4`): shown, queued and a third. `Show` replaces the line at once (`Combat.ShowLog`, `0x8005E604`); `Queue` only keeps the next (`QueueLog`, `0x8005E658`), and `Flush` shows it at a state's change (`FlushQueuedLog`, `0x8005E6F4`: an attack's result as its animation ends, a cast's end, a potion), or the third in its place if Banish queued one (`QueueLog2`). So "Brenna attacks Giant Rat" shows at once, and "Giant Rat killed" queued behind it comes up as the blow lands. **There's no timer**: a line holds until the next. `CombatLog` is the box's one writer, and cues each change to the battle's host ([combat](combat.md#what-a-battle-tells-its-host)).
- **The hold** (`+0x543C`, `Combat.HoldForMessage`, `0x80022AEC`): only a reserve fleeing as it arrives sets it, as the next turn begins ([combat](combat-ai.md)). For 60 ticks the box shows "Enemy reinforcements flee" and the battle's states and input wait; then the shown line comes back.
- **Locked** as the party falls (`TurnOrder.Defeat`, `0x8006A780`): its line, and in a non-lethal battle Alaron's portrait, and nothing changes the box after (`CombatLog.Lock`).

### The radar

`CombatRadar.Draw` (`0x8008DF04`):

- **The ring** is the sundial's, `0x001B`, at (245, 170), white at alpha 164 (the HUD's are 80), under the dots.
- **The dots** are 2 × 2, alpha 164, one for each body in use, by slot: the camera's owner yellow (150, 150, 0) at (274, 199), the party green (0, 150, 0) and the rest red (150, 0, 0), under the frames.
- **It's centred on the camera's owner** (`0x800D8448`): the current combatant, or while an attack is aimed the target cursor's body (`Battle.CameraOwner`). A body gone from the field has no dot. **It turns with the camera**: with (a, b) the camera's look in X and Z (`rotationXZ`), a body (dx, dz) from the owner goes (dx × −b + dz × a) × 0.5 right and (dx × a + dz × b) × 0.5 up, truncated, so the way the camera looks is up. Half a pixel a unit. A body more than 20 pixels out is held at 20, its colour halved.
- **While a spell, its area or a flask is aimed** (states 10, 11 and 18), the dots of set B's selected throb, each channel that isn't 0 plus the pulse, and the rest are halved. The pulse (`CombatRadar.Step`, `0x8008DE44`) goes from 0 to −150 and back, 4 a frame, 76 frames a cycle. A flask aims with set A, which the radar doesn't read, so in state 18 every dot is halved.
- **Checkpoint 02** (Rheda's turn) pins it: every dot where the original's arithmetic puts it (`BattleCheckpointTests`).

### On other screens

The user chose where the screen sits on a screen of another shape (`CombatGui.Draw`), and it follows the Menu Size setting, as the HUD does:

- **The enemies' column** keeps its place from the screen's top left corner, and **the party's** from the top right.
- **The log box** floats at the bottom centre.
- **The radar** sits in the screen's bottom right corner, the user's choice, its place in the frame kept from that corner as the compass's is, so on a wide screen it's clear of the log box; or, as the original has it, in the log box at its place in the frame (`RadarAnchor`). On a 4:3 screen the two are the same. F9 swaps them, and `--radar box` starts in the box.

### In the port

- **Ticks:** the portraits' tweens step a tick at a time (the original's delta), and the radar's pulse once a frame at 30 fps.
- **The fade** darkens the whole UI screen, as for the HUD; the original fades each widget's colour but not the HP bars' black.
- **The target sets** are the battle's ([combat](combat-actions.md#an-attack)), which the tints and the radar read through `Battle.Marks`, with the AI's fleeing flag (`Combatant.Fleeing`).
- **The aim's marker** (`Combat.DrawTargetCounts`, `0x80021448`) isn't the GUI's and draws no numbers: it's a cylinder in the arena ([combat](combat-actions.md#spells-potions-and-skills-in-a-turn)), the runtime's `AimMarkerView`.

### The held menus

C-down's spells and skills and C-up's potions and item spells (`CombatMenu`, states 22 and 23), open while their button is held and chosen from as it's let go; [combat](combat-actions.md#spells-potions-and-skills-in-a-turn) has what they list and do. `Ui.CombatMenu` is the model the battle holds (`Battle.Menu`), and `CombatGuiView.DrawMenu` draws it over the battle screen at the original frame's centre. Matched to the user's captures of Alaron's turn in the rats' battle, `roms/combat-cdown.png` (Perform, Air Shield, Spirit Shield, Wind and three `vs.` spells, with `vs. Star` below the list) and `roms/combat-cup.png` (four potions and Whitefire): with `--ui-screenshot` of the same turn from a save of the Combat 01 checkpoint, every row's text, every icon and the down arrow line up at the same pixel.

- **The list** is a `ChoicePopup` (`ChoicePopup.CreateList`, `0x80045AF4`, with 120 and 95; 55 without the Expansion Pak, and the port has the Pak's): a `WidgetPulseMenu` 120 wide at x 99, cut to x 99–221 and its top to its bottom, inclusive (`Widget.SetClipRect`, `0x80004C38`). `ChoicePopup.Layout` (`0x80046488`) makes it as tall as its rows up to 95 and puts its top at 120 less half that, so 73 for a full list.
- **The panel** is the conversation box's feathered black one (`ChoicePopup.CreatePanel`, `0x80045F24`: `0x01C4` drawn black, edges `0x01C0`–`0x01C3`, corners `0x01BC`–`0x01BF`), each piece an inclusive rectangle (`Widget.CreateImageBoxItem`, `0x800B310C`): the centre 100 to 220 across, the list's height down, a pixel right of the list's left edge; the top and bottom edges a pixel taller than their images, the sides a pixel wider and a pixel further out. No title, no padding.
- **A row** (`CombatMenu.MakeEntry`, `0x80088714`) is its icon at the list's left edge and its text in the main font after it, at the menu's icon width + 5 (10, the lute's, for C-down; 16, the flask's, for C-up, whatever the row's own icon is: so x 114 and 120) and half that width less half the text's 11 down (0 and 3). A row is as tall as its icon or its text, 11 for C-down's and 16 for C-up's, and **3 more under it** (`Widget.SetSpacing`, `0x800B2098`, which both builders call on the list before laying it out; `WidgetPulseMenu.Step`, `0x80027C98`, adds it after every row, the last too), so C-down's rows are 14 apart and C-up's 19. Five of C-up's fill the 95 exactly. A spell with no school (the `vs.` spells) has no icon and its text keeps its place. The icons are drawn as they are; the text is the row's colour: skills blue (0, 128, 225, 225), spells red (225, 80, 80, 225), potions green (0, 200, 0, 225), item spells yellow (200, 200, 0, 225).
- **The cursor** has no box: its row's colour goes from (100, 100, 100, 100) toward its own and back, 20 ticks each way (`WidgetPulseMenu.Draw`, `0x8002809C`; `Color.Mix`, `0x80027F1C`, truncating each channel), its icon unchanged. It wraps; the stick's or D-pad's up and down move it, and a held stick repeats as the menus' do ([input](#input)).
- **Scrolling**: the list follows the cursor 10 pixels a tick, until the cursor's row, its gap left out, is inside it.
- **The arrows** `0x38D0` and `0x38CF` (5 × 8, white) are placed as the menu is built, 8 left of the list (x 91), the up arrow its height and 2 above the list's top and the down arrow 2 below its bottom (y 170 for a full list). `ScrollArrows` (`Create` `0x800B1640`, `Step` `0x800B1908`) fades each from 0 as the menu opens, 20 a tick to 255 and back: the up arrow while the first row is above the list's top, the down arrow while the last row's foot is below its bottom, so a list that just fits shows none.
- **Nothing to list** shows the Party tab's message instead: "No spells or skills available" or "No potions or item spells available".
- **Sense Aura's popup** is that message box too, its lines white and centred, as wide as its longest up to seven pixels a character (`CombatGuiView.DrawAura`).

## A faithful mode, later

The floating panels and the resizable screen are enhancements the user chose over the original's look, leaving a faithful mode for a setting later (ADR 0018, [roadmap](../roadmap.md) Phase 6). It would fix `UiScreen` at 320 × 240, pillarboxed at 4:3 with the 3D view, and draw [the overscan bars](#the-screen) over both last. The conversation box and the battle's log box would take the original's bands right across the frame ([above](#the-conversation-box), [the log box](#the-log-box)): a `FeatheredPanel` with no bottom or side feathering, which `UiImages.DrawPanel` already skips. The rest of the UI is already at its original place. The Expansion Pak's high resolution would draw the 320-wide canvas into a 512-wide one, bilinear, before the VideoOut filter, with bars of 20 at the sides; the VI's filters and a TV's look would be further `UiFilter`s.
