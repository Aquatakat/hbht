# UI: text, the conversation box, the pause menu and the HUD (roadmap 1F, M2)

How the port lays out and draws the original's UI. The fonts and string tables themselves are in [text](../formats/text.md).

## The screen

The original draws its UI on a 320 × 240 screen (`SCREEN_WIDTH`, `SCREEN_HEIGHT`); the Expansion Pak's high-resolution mode is 512 wide, and `borg8DlistInit` scales images to it. Every UI image is a texture rectangle with bilinear filtering and a combiner of texel × primitive colour (`N64BorgImageDraw`), so text is its sheet's texels times the text's colour, alpha too. At 1:1 the filter samples each texel exactly: text and portraits land on the framebuffer pixel for pixel, and only scaled images, such as a box's gradient or the party's portraits at 0.75, are filtered.

**The soft look is the video output.** The emulator's captures of the original are 640 × 240, and their columns alternate between the framebuffer's pixels and the average of the two either side (the compass's letters read 153, 109, 65 across). Each line's 320 pixels are spread over 640 by linear interpolation, and the lines aren't touched. On a real console the VI does that resampling and adds its own anti-aliasing and divot filters, and then a composite or S-Video signal and a CRT soften it further; the captures show only the first step.

**The original covers the screen's edges** with black bars for overscan (`Graphics::EndList`): in 1.0 and 1.1, 16 pixels at the left and right and 12 at the top and bottom, filled rectangles drawn after everything else. The EU version draws 18 and 12 with its image code. So the UI's own boxes run under the bars and are cut off by them.

`UiScreen` (in `Aidyn.Game`, which the editor shares) draws the UI the same way: views draw in a `SubViewport`, in its pixels, with linear filtering as the console has it, and the screen is scaled up afterwards to fill the window, by one of three filters (`UiFilter`). The screen is `Lines` pixels tall, 240 by default as the original's, and as wide as the window's shape, but never narrower than 4:3. Views lay themselves out by its size rather than assuming 320 × 240. A taller screen draws the UI smaller, with room for more: at 480 lines, text is half the size. Text itself is always drawn as authored, pixel for pixel, and wraps at the same widths. The filters:

- **VideoOut**, the default: as the captures show it, each line doubled in width with the averages between, then scaled up sharp.
- **Pixels:** sharp.
- **Smooth:** bilinear both ways.

The canvas blends in on-screen values, as Godot's 2D does without HDR, so a glyph's alpha and colour multiply as the N64's do. It's premultiplied, since alpha blending into a transparent target leaves colour times alpha, and the upscale filters average it that way. A later pass could add the VI's other filters or a CRT's.

## Laying text out

`TextLayout` (`Aidyn.Core.Ui`) is the font engine's layout, over a font record (`Font::PrintMain`, `GetHeightScaled`, `GetWidthScaled`):

- A character draws its glyph at the pen, as wide as its measured width and a whole cell tall, and moves the pen by that width. A character with no glyph is skipped and takes no room.
- **Text wraps only at spaces.** After a space, if the next word would end past the right edge, the line breaks and the space is dropped. A space at the start of a line takes no room, and a word longer than the line runs past the edge. `\n` breaks the line.
- Lines are the cell's height plus 2 apart: 11 pixels in both fonts.
- Text is drawn only inside its widget's bounds. A glyph across an edge is cut, and one outside is dropped.
- Characters from `0x7F` are the controller buttons, drawn in their own colour with the text's alpha.

## The conversation box

`DialogueBox` (`Aidyn.Core.Ui`) places what a conversation shows, as `DiaMenuSub::InitDefault` and `DialougeClass::Setup` do, and `ConversationBoxView` draws it in the main font. It's for conversations and the other modes without a backdrop; backdrops and the help menu draw on a full-screen image (`BigBGDialoug`), which `BackdropPage` lays out ([below](#books-and-the-help-menu)).

The box is laid out in the original's 320 × 240 screen, placed at the bottom centre of the UI screen, so on a 4:3 screen of 240 lines it's where the original's is, and elsewhere it keeps its size. The positions below are in that frame.

- **The original's band** is translucent black: its image, `0x1C4`, is a single white texel of alpha 136, drawn black. It runs right across the screen from the box's top to 180 plus the portrait border's height, 232, so the overscan bars cut it at 16, 304 and 228. Above it a 10-pixel gradient fades upward, image `0x1C3` squeezed from 16 pixels.
- **The port floats it** instead, since a wide screen has no bars to cut it off. The band runs from 16 to 304 and down to 228, where the bars would stop it, and it's feathered on every side where the bars were, from the pieces the original's own popups are made of (`WidgetChoiceDia::SetImages`, see `FeatheredPanel`): the 10-pixel gradient above, `0x1C2` below, `0x1C0` and `0x1C1` beside it, each fading outward from alpha 136, and the rounded corners `0x1BC`–`0x1BF`. On a 4:3 screen it looks as the original's does inside its bars. The decomp names three of the four gradients wrongly; the machine code puts `0x1C3` above a popup, `0x1C2` below, `0x1C0` left and `0x1C1` right, and the dialogue box's gradient is `0x1C3`.
- **The portrait's border** (`0xDF`, 52 × 52) is at x 20, level with the box's top, and the portrait (40 × 40) sits 6 pixels inside it. A speaker with no portrait gets `0x0125` (`BORG8_PortraitNPCMale1`).
- **The line** is in (200, 200, 200), from x 78 (the border's width + 26) to 294, and ends 2 pixels above what's under it: the band's bottom, or the choices. The box's top is 4 pixels above the line, but never below 180, so the box is at least the border's height: a short line sits at y 184, and the box grows upward from five lines.
- **Choices** are in tan (200, 180, 100), from x 88, 4 pixels apart, and end at 228. The cursor's choice pulses toward (50, 45, 25, 63) and back over 24 frames each way, whole steps per channel (`WidgetScrollMenu::Tick`).
- **A line before its own speaker's choices shows alone.** When a menu's first choice has the line's speaker, the line shows first, and A brings up the choices alone under Alaron's portrait. The box shrinks to the choices, but the portrait and its border stay where the line put them, since `Setup` doesn't move them on that path.

Not ported yet: the widgets' fade in and out (`fadeFloatMirror`) and scrolling a menu taller than its bounds.

## The pause menu

The Party tab is ported, with what A does in it, and the [System](#the-system-tab) and [World](#the-world-tab) tabs. What the decomp, the machine code, the imported images and the user's captures (Oct 1, 2026, from the "Before final encounter" checkpoint; see the [reference rig](../reference-rig.md#emulator-captures)) show (`BigAssMenu.cpp`, `DollMenu*.cpp`). Positions are in the 320 × 240 frame.

- **The tabs are one long scroll.** System, Party and World are each a widget tree in its own 320 × 240 frame, 320 pixels apart, and changing tab slides them all 10 pixels a frame. Over them is a 3D scroll, `aidyn:actor/0x0E6B` (scene `0x0E63`), seen from above, which slides with them: System shows its wooden roller at the left, World at the right, and Party, in the middle, neither. It unrolls as the menu opens and rolls up as it closes, the tabs showing only between its rollers: see [opening and closing](#opening-and-closing). Past the torn top and bottom edges the darkened world shows through.
- **Each tab has a full-screen image** over the scroll (`pause_menu_borg8`): `0x00D8`, `0x00D9` and `0x00DA`, all 320 × 240. `0x00D9` is opaque inside its torn edges.
- **The Party tab's layout is painted into its image.** `0x00D9` is one parchment with the silhouette and its slot frames, the portrait frames, the labels HitPoints, Level and Gold, the HP bar's frame, the knotwork divider down the middle and "Details" with the C-down button. None of those labels are in the string tables. The widgets only fill it in.
- **The header** (`DollMenuPortraits`, `WidgetHealthGold`): the member's portrait at (22, 26), 40 square, and the others' at (67, 39), (96, 39) and (125, 39), 24 square, in party order from the member shown. The name at (69, 28) in the slim font. Under the large portrait, 4 below it, the aspect's icon and 2 after it the school's. In the main font, hit points at (223, 41) and their maximum at (255, 41), the level at (223, 55) and gold at (223, 69). The bar, `0x00E0`, is drawn 80 × 10 at (220, 27) and cut to hit points ÷ maximum × 80, truncated (a float division in the machine code).
- **The silhouette** (`DollEquipmentMenu`): 15 slots, 16 square, each the worn item's icon or the slot's own image of what it takes (`gItemSlotBorg8Indecies`). A gear slot shows the first, second or third piece of its kind in the gear's order. The decomp reads cloaks for the helmet's slot; the machine code reads helmets.
- **The list** (`DollMenuLists`) is one of four, in the order the stick steps through: inventory, spells, skills, stats (training drops stats; barter keeps only the inventory). It's at (160, 101), bounded from x 160 to 300 and y 101 to 205, in the slim font, rows in (130, 80, 80). Its title image is centred at x 230, 20 above it; the decomp's `- 70` is `+ 70`. The arrows that show hidden rows are 15 in from each side and 13 above, in (225, 225, 225). The cursor's row pulses from (68, 42, 34) toward (151, 141, 191) and back over 20 steps, as a scroll menu's do (`WidgetFastScrollMenu.Step`, `0x80028b00`), but two steps a frame: each list's step steps its scroll menu twice, once at the end of the lists' shared layout (`PartyMenu.ListBaseStep`, `0x80030888`, at `0x800309cc`) and again itself (`0x80035770` for the inventory). So it shows only even steps, peaking at 18, and takes 19 frames a cycle, 3.2 a second, twice the other menus' pulses, which the user timed at about 3.4 (Oct 16, 2026). Barter's and training's lists are the same widgets. It's the same doubling that plays the tab's cursor sound twice. Changing member keeps each list's cursor: stats clamp it to the last row, skills and spells go back to the top if it's past the end.
  - **Inventory**, 13 pixels a row: the wearer's portrait and the item's icon at 12 square, then the count at 26 and the name at 42, or 30 without a count; the decomp's 40 is 42 in the machine code. Worn items first, each member's weapon, armor, shield and gear, the member shown first and then round the party, then what's carried. Each part is sorted by kind and then by name in byte order. The kinds' order is potions, weapons, wands, scrolls, amulets, rings, helmets, cloaks, gloves, boots, belts, armor, shields, key items, misc; the decomp's copy leaves scrolls out.
  - **Stats**, 11 a row: the six attributes' base values at 79, and modified less base, when it isn't 0, at 100, as "(+n)" in (0, 128, 0) or "(n)" in (128, 0, 0). Then 4 below the rows, XP Remaining (experience to spend), Total XP and Next Level (what the next level needs, or MAX), the values lined up 4 after the first label.
  - **Skills**, 13 a row: the skills above 0 with their icons, then the weapon skills and the shield, which have none; the level at 79 and the difference at 95.
  - **Spells**, 13 a row: the aspect's icon (a spell's special, Solar or Lunar), the school's at 12 and the spell's symbol at 24, the rank at 36 and the name at 50. Unschooled spells have no school icon.
- **C-down's details** (`WidgetItemDetail`), shown while it's held, on the 160 × 160 parchment `0x00E2` at (0, 58) with its shadow `0x00E4` at (5, 60), in the slim font from x 23 to 149 and from y 70:
  - **An item:** its icon and name level with the icon's foot, then label and value lines: a weapon's skill, aspect, Min. Strength, Base Hit and Damage; armor's aspect, Protection, a shield's Defense, Dexterity and Stealth (the decomp labels Stealth with the armor's name). Then what it adds: a stat (Stamina as Spell Battery, what it holds now), a skill, Magic: and Spell: with the spell's name indented 16, Charges: on a wand, and Resists: with the element and the share it takes off. Armor ends with Total Protection and Total Defense, now and with it on, the second green or red, ending at y 213. Worn gear shows Min. Intelligence and, if it has one, Min. Strength; the original prints the Strength it needs under Intelligence and the other way round, which the port puts right. Potions, scrolls and other items show their description, and a scroll its spell's Min. Wizard Rank at y 187; key items and items without a description, such as the maps, show nothing. Descriptions are on the item records.
  - **A stat or skill:** its description. **A spell:** its symbol and name, its description, and at y 187 the Wizard rank it needs and the reagent it uses, with the reagent's icon at 9 square.
  - The user's capture of Mechanic's "Create Armor" listing the hides is A's crafting popup on the same parchment: see [the actions](#the-party-tabs-actions).
- **Scaled images sample as the RDP does:** its first pixel on the first texel and a step of the scale per pixel, where Godot's linear filter starts half a step in. `UiPainter` offsets the source rectangle to match, which brought the 12- and 24-square portraits to within the captures' noise.
- **A red arrow** under the knotwork at (126, 207) points both ways in trek (`BORG8_SliderArrowLR`) and only left in combat, in (130, 80, 80).
- **Later:** clicking. Hovering a row moves the cursor, but what a click lands on in the editor isn't clear yet, and the user has left it for later (Oct 1, 2026).
- **Changing member and list** animate: [below](#changing-member-and-list).
- **Not ported yet:** the details fading in and out 60 a frame, the slot's and bar's crossfades when an item is equipped or a potion drunk (the same as a member change's), and the sounds.
- **The World tab:** [below](#the-world-tab).

Measured against the captures in the console's own pixels (every eighth column and fourth line of ares' 2560 × 960), the classic layout's text, icons and portraits match to within the noise the console's 16-bit framebuffer and dithering leave, about 6 per pixel.

### Changing member and list

From 1.0's machine code (Oct 15, 2026). The menu's widgets animate with tweens (`MenuTween.CreateShort` at `0x800cc880`, `CreateByte` `0x800ccb88`): a value moved from where it is to an end over so many frames, a step a frame, on a curve that eases in and out, 0.5 sin(πt − π/2) + 0.5 (`MenuTween.Ease`, `0x800e6590`), truncated to a whole number each step. A tween takes its frames and finishes on the next, so the widget is busy a frame longer. `MenuTween` in the core has the curve, and `PartyMenu.Motion.cs` the state.

- **C-left shows the next member and C-right the one before** (`PartyMenu.CLeft` `0x8003e1c8`, `CRight` `0x8003e1f4`, slots 0xBC and 0xC4 of the menu's handlers, which the C-buttons' bits 0x2 and 0x1 call). Until Oct 15, 2026 the port had them the other way round; the user confirmed this way in the emulator (Oct 16, 2026).
- **The portraits move** (`PartyMenu.PortraitsChange`, `0x8003b3cc`): each member's portrait moves and resizes from its old place to its new one over 15 frames, the x, y, width and height each tweened. With C-left, the next member's grows into the large frame, the one shown shrinks into the last small one, and the others move one frame left. The white frames are painted into `0x00D9` and stay, so the portraits pass over them. They're drawn in the party's order, one widget per member.
- **The rest crossfades over the same 15 frames,** the old fading out as the new fades in: the name; the aspect's and school's icons, where they change; the hit points, their maximum and the level, always, even where they're the same (`PartyMenu.HealthGoldChange`, `0x8003be5c`); and the silhouette's slots. The bar's width tweens to the new member's. The gold and the list on the right change at once.
- **The silhouette** (`PartyMenu.EquipmentChange`, `0x8003e374`): the original crossfades each slot whose item differs, so empty to empty is still. The port crossfades a slot only where one member wears something in it and the other doesn't, and changes from one worn item to another at once, as the user asked (Oct 15, 2026).
- **The stick's left shows the next list and its right the one before** (`PartyMenu.ListsLeft` `0x800319f0`, `ListsRight` `0x80031a10`), wrapping from stats to the inventory; the port had these the other way round too. The list, its title and its arrows slide together, and the old list and the new move the same way at once, 140 pixels over 20 frames (`PartyMenu.ChangeList`, `0x80031620`): left brings the next list from x 20 to 160 and pushes the old from 160 to 300; right brings the one before from 300 and pushes the old to 20. The rows are cut to the list's bounds, x 160 to 300 and y 101 to 205, and the title to x 170 to 300 (`PartyMenu.ListsCreate`, `0x80030c50`); the port cuts the title at 160 with the rows. Details' button is painted into the tab and stays.
- **The timing matches.** The user found these move in lockstep with the emulator's (Oct 16, 2026), at the port's 60 frames a second.
- **A change waits for the last** (`PartyMenu.Changing`, `0x800304b4`): no member change while the header, the silhouette or the lists are still moving, and no list change while the lists are. Training's member change animates the same way; its lists don't slide yet.

### The model and the layouts

`PartyMenu` (`Aidyn.Core.Ui`) is the tab's state: the member shown, the list showing, each list's rows (`PartyMenuRows`) and cursor (`MenuList`), the silhouette's slots, and C-down's details for the cursor's row (`MenuDetails`). `MenuImages` and `InventoryOrder` hold the chrome's image IDs and the kinds' order, which the tests check against the ROM's tables. `PartyTabView` (`Aidyn.Game`) draws it with the widget kit, `UiPainter`: text in either font, images whole, scaled, cut out or feathered, the parchment popup, and lists with a cursor and arrows. It draws `0x00D9` whole at the screen's centre, filled in at the original's coordinates, at every screen size ([ADR 0014](../adr/0014-menus-keep-the-originals-layouts.md)). A wide layout, the original's sheet grown to fill the screen, was tried (Oct 1–3, 2026) and dropped: growing bespoke art leaves seams all over, and new layouts want new art, which is for mods after 1.0.

### Opening and closing

From 1.0's machine code (`PauseMenu.DarkenStep` at `0x8004847c`, `DrawBackdrop` `0x800484cc`, the menu's `OpenStep` `0x8002f1d0`, `CloseStep` `0x8002f4d4`, `SlideOut` `0x8002e9d0` and `Update` `0x8002ee38`) and the user's captures (`roms/pause-ref.png` and `pause-darkened.png`, Oct 12, 2026). `PauseScroll` in the core has the rules, and `PauseMenu` and `PauseScrollView` in the runtime draw them.

- **Everything here steps once a frame.** The menu's frame (`PauseMenu.Frame`) counts its delta, but the widgets, the scroll and the fades take one step a frame whatever it is (`WidgetManager.Update`, `0x800ad10c`). So their times are in frames, and the menu's frame rate sets how long they take. The code doesn't give that rate. The port runs the menu at 60 frames a second (`Cadence.MenuFrameTicks`): at 30, the tabs' slide took 64 ticks, which the user found slow beside the emulator's (Oct 12, 2026), and 60 is the only faster whole number. The Party tab's 15- and 20-frame tweens, run at 60, move in lockstep with the emulator's, as the user found (Oct 16, 2026), which settles it. The cursors' pulses step at the same 60 (`MenuPulse`), once a frame (`WidgetManager.Update` takes one pass whatever its count): the user timed the System tab's and the help menu's at about 1.6 cycles a second, 38 frames a cycle, where the port's had half (Oct 16, 2026). The Party tab's lists step theirs twice a frame ([above](#the-pause-menu)).
- **The world freezes and darkens first.** Any way into the menu (Start, a shop or trainer's script, a container) copies the frame on screen, HUD and all, into a 320 × 240 image (`GetSnapShot`, `0x800208b4`), which the menu draws behind itself multiplied by a grey (`PauseMenu.BackdropTint`, `0x800d9564`): 255, then 10 less a frame to 128 (the last step 7), 13 frames, half as bright, as the captures measure (0.50 to 0.52). The menu is built on the 14th. Until then only the darkening world is drawn. The copy isn't softened: the plain half of `pause-ref.png` matches `pause-darkened.png` within 2.05 a channel on average, and no blur does better: one of up to a game pixel across ties (2.05 to 2.12), and any blur down is clearly worse (2.57 to 2.60). What looks soft on the console is the halved contrast and its 16-bit colour's grain, a little more at half brightness, which the port, drawing in full colour, doesn't have. Once the menu is open, the original draws black behind it and only the copy's top and bottom 40 lines, which the scroll leaves showing; the port draws the whole copy, which a screen wider than the frame shows beside the scroll.
- **In the port the world isn't copied: it's drawn on, still.** The menu stops trek, and with it the scenery's actors, which animate with the frame, and the free camera; the world and the HUD go on being drawn as they stand, and the menu darkens them with a black layer at one less the tint, which is the same as multiplying them by it. So the world behind the menu is redrawn as the window is resized, at the window's new shape rather than a picture stretched, and the HUD is laid out again as the Menu size option changes, so the player sees how it will look before closing the menu. The user asked for all this (Oct 16, 2026), as the HUD is always in the darkened world in the emulator. The world settles the last part of a tick as the menu opens, as the project interpolates between ticks: less than a frame's movement at 60. A copy of the frame missed the HUD whenever the frame on screen had none: the HUD was hidden at the start of each tick the menu or a page showed, so it was missing for a tick after the menu closed, from the help menu's page, which opens as the menu closes, and from a book's or sign's page when a tick ran before the next frame was drawn, which at 120 frames a second is about every other time; and a shop or trainer opens as its conversation ends, when the HUD, which isn't drawn during a conversation, hadn't been drawn again. The port still copies the frame on screen as the menu opens, for a save's screenshot ([below](#saving-and-loading)).
- **The scroll.** The actor's states, by request, are idle (0, 11 frames, the rollers at ±35.3, off the frame), opening on System or World (22, 37 frames, the rollers parting from ±2.6), opening on Party (10, 53 frames: the scroll comes up from below rolled, then parts from the middle to about ±13.5, and then idles), closing from Party (19, 53) and from System or World (11, 39); 20 and 21 aren't the menu's. The opening is the tab's, not the window's: barter, training and the loot window open on Party, as the full menu does on that tab. The original's actors take a request for one step and then go back to 0, so each plays once. The menu sets the scene's x by the tab, 27 for System, 0 for Party and −26 for World (`0x800de9c8`), and slides it with the tabs, 1/32 of the way a frame. Its camera is 27 above the scene, looking straight down with −Z up the screen, 45° over the 240 lines, near 1 and far 256. It's drawn over the tabs. Its scene's two lights light it as directional lights, the warm one from the left and the dim blue one from the right, with no ambient; so lit, the port's System roller matches the capture within 4 a channel on average.
- **The tabs show only where the scroll has unrolled.** Each tab's image and widgets are cut to columns of the frame (`PauseMenu.ClipLeft` and `ClipRight`, `0x800d9568`), in x only, that follow the scroll's frame f in a straight line:

  | Tab | Opening | Closing |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | System | from the right: the left edge 320 until frame 8, then 0 by 36 | from the left: the left edge 0 at frame 1, 320 by 31 |
  | Party | from the middle: 160 to 160 until frame 36, then 0 to 320 by 55 | to the middle: 0 to 320 at frame 1, 160 to 160 by 14 |
  | World | from the left: the right edge −5 until frame 6, then 320 by 35 | from the right: the right edge 320 at frame 2, 0 by 32 |

  Each opens when its animation ends: Party at frame 53, its clip 17 to 303, its tab showing from frame 37; System and World at 37. Only then does the menu take input. The original's clips stop short of the frame's edges, behind the TV's overscan, which the captures show black over the outer 16 columns: Party's ends at 17 and 303, and the rollers then drop away to idle, off the frame. The port shows those columns, so a side within 17 columns of the frame's edge counts as at it (`PauseScroll.Overscan`), as the user asked (Oct 12, 2026). The port also cuts the tabs at the rollers, along their middles where they're drawn (`PauseScrollView.Arrange`), so the parchment never shows past one, as the overscan hides on the console and as a scroll would be; a side at the frame's edge reaches the roller on that side, so a wider screen shows the tabs beside the frame as far as the rollers. The user asked for this (Oct 12, 2026).
- **On screens wider and taller than the frame,** what the original's scroll does beyond it shows, and the port changes it, as the user asked (Oct 13, 2026), in `PauseScroll` and `PauseScrollView`:
  - **The animations were made to run on from one another.** The Party opening's last 9 frames are the System and World opening's first 9, exactly, and the System and World closing's frames 31 to 39 are the Party closing's 1 to 9; that closing is their opening backwards, two frames longer. So the Party tab's scroll runs on into the System and World opening at its frame 10 (`ActorStateMachine.Join`), its rollers going on out to the ends of the System and World tabs, which show as they pass, rather than jumping there as the original's do once off the frame. The menu takes input from frame 53, as the original's does. Closing from Party, the System and World closing brings the rollers back in to the frame's edges, from where the opening had got to if it's still running on, and then the Party closing runs from its first frame.
  - **Fades.** The Party tab's scroll comes up from below the frame, which a taller screen shows, so it fades in over its opening's first 12 frames, and out over its closing's last 12 as it drops away. The System and World tabs and their scroll fade out together from their closing's frame 29, as the roller reaches the frame's edge, gone by its end, rather than the rolled-up scroll hanging beside the frame as the world brightened.
  - **Each roller is drawn as it is when its own tab is open.** One camera over the scene's middle shows a roller further out than that from the side, wider and reaching in over its tab: at the Party tab, the World tab's roller covered its calendar's last column. So past that, each roller has a camera of its own that follows it, and its picture moves out with the tabs, a tab's 320 pixels a tab. The scroll's x moves 27 between the System and Party tabs and 26 between the Party and World tabs, which at the rollers' depth is about 319 and 307 pixels, so the one camera also let the World tab's roller drift 13 pixels in over its tab at Party; now each roller stays where it is on its tab as they slide. Within the frame it's drawn as before.
  - **Barter and training** can't change tab, so their scroll runs on only to frame 11 of the System and World opening and holds there, the rollers just past what the Party tab draws, with its parchment's edges still under them; it closes as the Party tab's does. Where the Party opening ends, the rollers covered the large portrait and the start of the loot's "Found" line, as the user saw (Oct 13, 2026); at frame 10 the right one still covers the edge of Details' button, and from 12 the world shows between the parchment and both rollers, as the Party tab's image is only the frame's 320 wide.
  - **Drawn as it is.** The project interpolates nodes between physics ticks, and the scroll, posed a frame at a time, drew a tick behind its pose, so its first frame showed where it had been reset to, rolled out, before it jumped to where it starts. Its viewports don't interpolate.
- **Closing,** the scroll rolls up at once (`SlideOut` steps it), and when its animation ends it stops and the port hides it, and the world brightens 10 a frame back to 255, 13 frames, before trek goes on. The loot window and shops (Game.Mode 7) snap back to full brightness instead. Camping waits for the menu to close; loading a save closes it at once.
- **The post-battle window** (pause state 5) opens and closes without the scroll, its widgets fading in and out 60 a frame (`OpenFade`, `CloseFade`). That comes with combat.

**In the runtime** (`PauseMenu`), Start opens the tab over the world, on a `UiScreen` 240 lines tall by default, and the runtime's F7 changes it. C-left and C-right are Q and E or the shoulders (C-left showing the next member), the stick is the movement keys, arrows, left stick or D-pad, C-down is Tab or the top face button, held, A is Space or the bottom face button, and Start or B closes a popup, or the menu if none is open. The rules:

- **The slots.** The original's saves are notes on the Controller Pak, up to 16, in the pak's order. The port's are the files in the saves folder, newest first.
- **"Create New Save"** (553), in dark brown where the name goes, offers only Save and Exit. The original puts it last; the port puts it first, so a new save is a press away whatever's saved. It writes at once, to `Save n.json`. With nothing to load, the load screen says "There are no saved games." where the original talks about the Controller Pak, and offers only Exit.
- **Saving over a game asks first:** "Are you sure you would like to save over that game?" (410), with Overwrite and Cancel. The decomp reads the delete question here, but the machine code reads 410. Saving says "Game successfully saved." (422) with Continue (176), which leads back to the System tab's list, showing the slot just written. The original's "Saving. Do not turn the power OFF…" step isn't needed.
- **Deleting asks first:** "Are you sure you would like to delete that game?" (372), with Delete and Cancel. Deleting takes the screenshot with the save, and starts the list again from the first save.
- **Loading** happens at once, with no question. The menu closes and the game starts again from the save, as the original's teleports there. The runtime reloads its Godot scene with the save, keeping the plugins loaded.
- **The questions** are `WidgetChoiceDia` panels 150 wide in off-white, with no B, as the leave-items choice is.
- **The screenshot** is taken as the menu opens (`QuestData::TakeSnapshot`). The original crops the frame's centre half, (80, 60) to (240, 180), and samples it down to 64 × 32 at 16 bits. That's why its screenshots look stretched: the decomp's 60 is 64 in the machine code. The port takes the centre half of the window's view, cut to 4:3, at 208 × 156, twice what the screen shows. It goes beside the save as a PNG of the same name.
- **Not ported yet:** the slots sliding in as the stick changes them, and the screen's fade.

## The trek HUD

Trek's HUD (`draw_hud_elements`, `0x80020bd4` in 1.0) is the sundial at the bottom left, the party's portraits at the bottom centre, and at the bottom right the minimap where the player's chunk has a piece, or else the compass under an outdoor sky (sky type 3), or nothing. The compass and the sundial are separate widgets. From 1.0's machine code and the user's captures (Oct 4, 2026, `roms/hud-*.png`: outside Gwernia castle with the portraits up and down, and inside it with the minimap large and small), which the port's positions match to the pixel. `TrekHud` (`Aidyn.Core.Ui`) is the state, with `Sundial`, `Compass`, `PartyPortraits` and `Minimap`; `TrekLoop` owns it and `HudView` draws it.

- **Drawn only in trek frames**, and not while a conversation or sign shows (`diaClass->menu`): not under the pause menu, a shop, training or a cut-scene. Popups draw over it. Every colour and alpha is the scene's brightness times its own, so it fades with the screen.
- **The images** are texture rectangles at quarter-pixel positions, texel × colour, blended (`borg8DlistInit` with flag 6, `Borg8_DrawSimple`). None uses the feathered popups' pieces: the rings and the frames have their feathering painted in.

### The sundial

`Sundial::Draw` (`0x800248a4`), centred on (49, 199):

- **The ring** `0x001B` (60 square) at (19, 169), white at alpha 80: a dark disc with a thin white rim.
- **The moon** at (39, 189), in (160, 160, 220) at alpha 160, by its phase ([rendering](rendering.md#the-skys-objects)): new `0x01B8`, crescent `0x01B3` or `0x01B4` and quarter `0x01B6` or `0x01B7` in the first and second halves of the moon's four weeks (`(week & 3) >> 1`), full `0x01B5`. It shows at every hour.
- **The rays** `0x01BA` (50 square) at (24, 174), at alpha 80, in the hour's colour.
- **The sun** goes round the ring **twice a day**, 30° an hour clockwise from the top, so noon and midnight are both at the top: its centre is (49 + 25 sin a, 199 − 25 cos a), a in whole degrees, (int)(time of day × 720) mod 720. By day it's the glow `0x01BB`, by night the small dot `0x01B9`, each 20 square. The capture's glow at about (72.5, 208.8) is a quarter to four in the afternoon, where one turn a day would put it at the lower left.

The original dims day's and night's colour by the scene's brightness but not the blend's.
- **The moon and sun hide in caves** and interiors such as Gwernia castle's: under a cave's sky (sky type 4: terrains 6, 8, 9, 11 and 13–16, the port's terrains that don't draw the sky). ### In the port

- **On other screens,** each part keeps its place from the bottom and from its side: the sundial from the left, the compass or minimap from the right, and the portraits stay centred. On a 4:3 screen of 240 lines that's where the original has them. The user agreed (Oct 4, 2026).
- **Frames:** the portraits' slide and the minimap's choice and animation step once every two ticks, the original's frame at an idealized 30 fps ([runtime](runtime.md)).
- **The fade:** the runtime's fade is a black overlay on trek's whole UI screen, so the HUD is drawn at full brightness under it, where the original dims each colour and alpha.
- **In the runtime** B is Backspace or the right face button. The pause menu, shops, training and cut-scenes hide the HUD.
- **Not ported yet:** the Detect Moon Phase and Detect Sun Phase spells, and the editor showing the HUD.

## A faithful mode, later

The floating panel and the resizable screen are enhancements. The user chose them over the original's look for now (Sep 30, 2026), and left a faithful mode for the fidelity profile at the very end ([roadmap](../roadmap.md), Phase 6's enhancements). What it would take:

- **The screen.** `UiScreen` fixed at 320 × 240 whatever the window's shape (`Lines` 240, width 320), shown pillarboxed at 4:3, with the VideoOut filter. The 3D view would need the same 4:3 frame, since the bars below cover it too.
- **The overscan bars.** Black rectangles over everything, drawn last, as `Graphics::EndList` does: 16 pixels at the left and right and 12 at the top and bottom in 1.0 and 1.1, 18 and 12 in the EU version. They'd belong over the whole frame, 3D and UI, not in a UI view, and they'd cut the conversation box off at 16, 304 and 228 as the original's is.
- **The conversation box.** A style for `DialogueBox`: a band right across the frame, from 0 to 320 and down to 180 plus the border's height (232), with only the 10-pixel gradient above it. That's a `FeatheredPanel` whose bottom and side feathering are 0, which `UiImages.DrawPanel` already skips, and the frame at (0, 0).
- **Other UI.** Everything laid out by the screen's size would be at its original place once the screen is 320 × 240, as the conversation box is. Nothing else changes.
- **High-resolution mode**, if the fidelity profile offers the Expansion Pak's option: a 512 × 240 screen, where the original lays UI out in 320 × 240 and `borg8DlistInit` stretches every image 1.6 times across, bilinear, so text is filtered horizontally. `UiScreen` would draw its 320-wide canvas into a 512-wide one with linear filtering before the VideoOut filter. Its overscan bars are 20 pixels at the sides.
- **The VI and the TV**, which apply to either mode: the VI's anti-aliasing and divot filters and a composite or CRT look are further `UiFilter`s.

## In the editor

A script's player shows the conversation box above the page's controls, a loot table's roll and a container on the map the loot window, a shop's page its window, a trainer's character page the training window, and the inventory record's page the Party tab, whose A and B buttons act on the editor's game, each over a plain backdrop, at 1×, 2× or 3×, on a 4:3 or 16:9 window, with a UI screen of 240, 360 or 480 lines and with any of the three filters (`UiScreenOptions`). When a line shows alone before its choices, Continue brings them up, as A does. Hovering a choice puts the cursor on it, as hovering a row of the Party tab's list does; the UI screen passes the mouse to its views in its own pixels (`ForwardsMouse`). `--zoom <1-3>`, `--aspect 4:3|16:9`, `--ui-lines <n>` and `--ui-filter pixels|videoout|smooth` set these for screenshots, `--press a,down,a` presses the Party tab's buttons, and `--ui-screenshot <png>` saves the UI screen in its own pixels, to compare with a capture.
