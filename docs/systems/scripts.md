# Scripts: the Borg13 VM (roadmap 2.4)

`HBHT.Core.Scripts`. The ROM's format is in [dialogue](../formats/dialogue.md); the plugin's, with every op, is [below](#the-format-adr-0034).

`ScriptRunner` runs one script (`aidyn:script/0x…`) against the flag network and the game state. It ports the walk over the nodes (`DialogNode.cpp`) and the callbacks that carry out the ops (`dialogue.cpp`, `dialogmode.cpp`), from the debug build's machine code. `GameState.RunScript` makes a runner against the game: its flags, its world stream of random numbers and the flag that rolls skill checks 0–21. Conversations, cut-scenes and the editor's script page all start theirs there; the editor passes a world of its own that fixes skill checks by hand.

## The format (ADR 0034)

A `script` record (`ScriptRecord`, `aidyn:script/0xHHHH`, one file each in the base plugin under `records/scripts/`) has its `number`, a `label` for tools (the number and the first speaker's name, or the mode), its `mode`, the cut-scene's `actors`, its `doneFlag`, `backdrop` and `battleFlag`, `rest` where it's true, and its `nodes`. A node (`ScriptNode`) has its `index`, `kind`, `speaker`, `text`, `setup` (a list of ops), one `condition`, one `effect`, its `links` and, under a Case, the range that `selects` it. The original's command numbers are only in the importer (`ScriptImporter`) and in the table below; [dialogue](../formats/dialogue.md) has the ROM's layout.

- **A script names marks, not map objects.** A mark is a reference point's `mark`, counted from the number the script was started with (`ScriptStart.Marks`): a script object's `marks`, or 0 for a talker. So the same script staged by two objects can name different points.
- **The speaker is the node's.** Who says a line, whose portrait shows and who talks for the press that shows it. A node with none shows the fallback portrait, as books, signs, rumours and the help menu do. `ScriptRunner.Speaker` is the speaker of the last line shown (a menu's last choice), and `ScriptEnding.Speaker` is a shop's keeper or a training's trainer by the op, or else that.
- **Ops name what they act on.** An actor op names its actor, and the shop, training, experience and member-stat ops their entity. Nothing reads a speaker but the box.
- **Setup ops run in their order** as the node is reached. 1.0 runs the ones that set something up for later (`camera.time`, `battle.*`, `script.resetChoices`) in a pass before the cameras and actors (`Dialoug_commands`), and the importer writes them first: 18 nodes had one after a camera or actor op.
- **The condition** is what an If or a Case evaluates; on a Line or a Menu it's never read (one base node has one, kept as the data has it). **The effect** runs as a line is left, or at once for a menu or case.
- **Where an op can run** is its type's (`ScriptOp.Use`: setup, condition, effect, trigger), and `PluginSet.Validate` refuses it anywhere else, as it does a camera move with both or neither of `mark` and `actor`, and a range on a node's condition. An op no build knows loads as a plain `ScriptOp`, which does nothing; validation reports it. `lua` is reserved in every place: it loads and does nothing until Phase 5.

### Speakers

The importer gives each node the speaker 1.0's walk leaves in force when it's reached (`ScriptImporter.Speakers`): its own SetSpeaker, else the speakers of the nodes that link to it, found to a fixed point. A menu sets each choice up in turn as it shows them (`ShowMenu`), so a choice without its own takes the choice before it, and the first choice the menu; that's what makes Alaron ask "What's Sheridan got to do with it?" (1091 node 65) and "Why do you guard the inside of this gate" (10922 node 10) where the menu's speaker is someone else. A SetSpeaker naming no record (0xCCCC, script 7103 node 0) sets no one. Only a node that shows a line, one with text or a random line, has its speaker written.

Of the 17,569 nodes with text, 3,043 have no speaker: the 3,041 whose script names none on any way to them (narration, rumours, books, signs, the help menu), 2413 node 7 ("Boy... BOY!", reached through flag checks before any speaker), and 1426 node 5, which nothing links to. Two lines are reached with different speakers in force:

- **Script 1446 node 6, "Farewell."** (Cadme's talk, `aidyn:script/0x03B9`) follows Alaron's "I have no time to tell it now." at node 5, and five of Cadme's closing lines ("Go gently." and others), after which 1.0 shows Cadme's portrait: she sends Alaron off and won't talk to him again (flag 113). It's split, the user's choice (importer 81, `ScriptImporter.SplitNodes`): node 5 goes to a copy added after the script's last node, Alaron's, and node 6 is Cadme's, so each way shows the speaker 1.0 shows. Written as Alaron's throughout (importers 69–80), it read wrong as she closed the talk, which the user found on Nov 6, 2026.
- **Script 1470 node 37, "Let's see what the old man has."** has a hand choice (`ScriptImporter.HandSpeakers`). It follows a check for Dougal after Rheda's or Brenna's line, or Alaron's, by who's in the party; the node aims the camera at Alaron (`CameraOnActor`), so it's his. The user played it on Nov 6, 2026, and found it right as Alaron's.

Elsewhere where the ways disagree, the importer takes the speaker of the lowest-numbered node that leads in with one. 1.0's scripts have no such line, and no op whose actor it would decide: the 138 textless cut-scene nodes reached with more than one speaker in force (1384's node 20 after the party's members are spawned, and the like) hold only camera moves onto marks, which name no actor. 1.0 reads the speaker's actor for those too (`DialoguePreCallback`), but only to crash without one; the move's place and aim are the mark's. So they need no rule. The user watched three cut-scenes on leaving Gwernia on Nov 6, 2026, Brenna's outside the castle, 1384 and the Chaos scene by the camp outside the town, and found them as expected.

What a speaker on the node can't follow: 1.0 shows a choice that has no SetSpeaker under whoever the choice before it set, which with that one taken and hidden is the menu's; the port shows the first showing's. And an effect run as a choice is left reads the speaker the last choice set up; the port's names the chosen choice's. No base script's choices differ that way.

## Its boundary

- **Rules** go to the flag network, and to `IScriptWorld` for the party, inventory, gold, skills, stats and experience ([characters](characters.md)). Where the debug build crashes on a failed call (taking an item the party doesn't have, adding to a full party, giving experience to a non-member), retail carries on, and so does the runner.
- **Presentation** goes out as cues on `IScriptHost` (ADR 0003): cameras, spawning, facing, walking and running, animations, held items, sounds and the fade. The runtime carries them out, and tests record them.
- **The player** answers the page: `Continue` moves on from a line, `Choose` takes a choice, and `Skip` ends the script, as the debug build's B does; 1.0 has no skip. While the scripted camera moves, the original drops input (see [cut-scenes](#cut-scenes)).
- **The player's name.** The runner replaces `{player}` (`ScriptRunner.PlayerToken`) in every line, choice and random line with the first party member's name as the page is made, so a rename ([ui](pause-menu.md#renaming-alaron)) shows on the next page. With no one in the party the text keeps `{player}`.
- **Waits** end with `Resume` from the runtime: an actor arriving, an animation ending or the camera stopping. `Step(ticks)` counts down `Wait`, whose operand is in ticks.
- **Map objects remember** a skill check's result and a random line's roll (the original's `VoxelChart`, the port's `MapObjectMemory`: [saves](saves.md#map-objects-memory)), through `IScriptWorld.TryRecall` and `Remember`. So talking to someone again can't reroll a check or a rumour. Troubadour checks aren't remembered.
- **Randomness** comes from an `IRandom` stream (ADR 0006). `Lcg` is the original's generator, and `Range` is `Random::Range`.
- **Gating again.** The original gates a map's objects again by the flags only when it reloads the map (`loadGameBorgScenes`): after the pause menu, a shop, training, a battle, a cut-scene or a teleport, but not after a plain conversation or sign, whose changes show when the chunk next loads ([wanderers](wanderers.md#talking)). The port regates as the pause menu closes (`TrekLoop.Regate`, which gates the chunks again through `ChunkStreamer.Regate` and tells the host) and rebuilds trek after a cut-scene, backdrop or help script (`Conversation.RebuildsTrek`).
- **Flags and the journal.** `flag.set` and `flag.clear` only write (`Dialogue.Effect`, `0x8004f7b8`, ops 0x20 and 0x21), and `check.flag` reads (`Dialogue.Condition`, `0x8004f680`, op 0x18), bringing the network up to date when its flag is derived. So in 1.0 a journal entry a script sets waits for something to read a derived flag; the port tells it as trek next has nothing up, the user's choice ([flags](flags.md#when-the-network-comes-up-to-date)).

## How it walks

A node is **reached** when the walk arrives at it, and its setup ops run in order. It's **left** when the player moves on from it, and leaving runs its effect. 1.0's condition and effect checks (`dialoug_func_b_check`, `dialoug_func_c_check`) stop at the first one, which is why a node has one of each; ops 55, 59 and 60 aren't effects in the jump table. Where the walk can go on without the player, through an If or a line with no text, it does so at once. The condition ops, and the effect ops that change the game's state (the flags, gold, items, members and experience, with their sounds), are `ScriptOps`, which the plugins' triggers run too ([events](events.md)); the runner gives it its kept skill checks and the map object that started it.

- **Lines** show their text. Leaving one goes to its link. With no link, it goes back to the last menu or case, which is reached again: its setup and effect run again, and it's shown with the choices not yet taken.
- **Ifs** take their first link when the condition is **exactly 1** (`bne v0, 1`, not nonzero), and their second otherwise. Every base-game condition in an If is 0 or 1, so this only matters for mods.
- **Cases** show their text and evaluate their condition. They count the links whose own range holds the value (each link's, not the case node's), taken or not. With two or more, the untaken matches are offered as choices. With fewer, the value is kept, and when the player moves on, the first untaken match is taken. The value is kept as 16 bits, and 65535 is the original's mark for none: a single-match case on exactly 65535 gold finds nothing and faults.
- **Menus** show their text and their untaken choices. Each choice is reached as it's added, so its setup ops run then, and a page's choices can have different speakers. Backdrops and the help menu offer taken choices again, and a backdrop's choice loop tests the next choice's slot after moving on, so it shows every choice.
- **Visited** is set when a node is left, not when it's reached. So a choice that ends the script is never marked, and it stays on offer.
- **Effects that end the script** (end, shop, battle, train, camp heal) end it at once. A cut-scene, backdrop or the help menu sets its done flag however it ends, including by `Skip` or a fault.

A page is the line and the choices under it, as the original's `diags` list holds them, with room for 9. The line loses its leading spaces, as `look_for_dialouge_v_Num` trims them, but choices keep theirs. When the first choice has the same speaker as the line, a conversation shows the line alone first and then the choices under Alaron's portrait (`DialougeClass::Setup`). What A does on a page is the core's `DialogueBox.Accept`, which every mode shares: it brings up the choices behind a line shown alone, else takes the cursor's choice or moves the script on.

## Ops that need more than a flag

- **`script.randomLine`** rolls 0–100, which the map object remembers, and picks a node of the other script by `roll / 100 / (1 / count)` in single precision, where the count is its nodes and its first line's extra weight: 1.0 counts a blank node some scripts end with and shows node 0 for it, which `firstLineWeight` 2 says. Twelve rumour scripts end in one, so they show their first rumour about a third of the time, twice as often as each of the others. The line shows under the speaker of the node that asked for it, and moving on leaves that node. The other script's text doesn't go through the player-name and escape processing the main script gets at load, but no rumour uses either.
- **`check.skill`** asks for the party's skill check once per script (`Party::SkillCheck`, a roll against the party's best: see [characters](characters.md#the-party)), keeps it, and remembers it on the map object. The base game only checks Diplomat, Loremaster and Troubadour. While the state flags record's `randomSkillChecks` flag (3717, a debug option) is set, it rolls 0–21 instead, after the lookup.
- **`actor.animate`** waits for the animation to end in a cut-scene, which only animates actors it has spawned ([actors](#actors)). Nine conversation nodes use it too, all lines, on handovers and dismissals (in 890, `0x037B`, `0x0387`, `0x041B` and `0x046C`), mostly request 19, a give-or-take gesture. **In 1.0 they never play.** The line shows as any line does, its speaker talking for the press that brought it up; the effect, run as the line is left, looks the actor up from the page it has just cleared (`FUN_800591e4`), finds no one, and waits for A with the same line showing again, so the line takes A twice: the lighthouse keeper's "Here... take the key" (Borg13 890, `aidyn:script/0x037A`, Overworld E15), then Alaron's "Thank you.". Some of the nine couldn't play anyway: the Male Commoner's actor (`aidyn:actor/0x27C4`, the keeper's and Batrin's) has no state for 19, and `0x037B`'s node 26 sets no speaker of its own.
  - **The port fixes it** in conversations, the user's choice ([ADR 0018](../adr/0018-the-port-fixes-bugs-and-sets-enhancements.md)): `ScriptRunner.Show` cues `AnimateCue(actor, request)` as an animated line shows, for the op's actor, the speaker the box shows (so `0x037B`'s 26, "Get out of my sight, worm!", is the Marquis's, from node 25), the line shows once, and the effect does nothing. `Conversation` asks the speaker's body for the request in place of the talk for that press ([wanderers](wanderers.md#talking)) if its actor has a branch for it in any state, and otherwise the speaker talks, as the keeper does; Alaron reaches out as he takes the key (his actor's state 33). Cut-scenes are as 1.0 has them.
- **`battle.start`** hands over the enemies from `battle.addEnemy` (a thirteenth faults), the arena (above 27 becomes 0), the collision and animation bytes, whether the party can flee (not in cut-scenes), and the record's `battleFlag`, as the ending's `BattleSetup` (`HBHT.Core.Combat`). In a conversation with someone who wanders, the original fights their party instead, under its flag C, in the arena their chunk's terrain chooses, with the bytes 0 and fleeing allowed (`BattleSetup.Against`, [combat](combat.md#in-and-out)), and a shop or training has them keep it rather than the op's keeper (`set_shopkeep`).
- **Where an ending leads** is decided in the core (`Transition.AfterScript`): the hours a camp heal or a camp scene passes, then the keeper's shop, their training, or the battle where the script ran. A keeper with no shop, or no keeper for training, is said instead, where the original would crash or open empty lists. Trek carries out a conversation's ending, the session a cut-scene's.
- **`party.campHeal`** heals the party and ends the script, and eight hours pass. A camp scene (`rest`) adds eight more, so a camp heal in one would pass sixteen.
- **`party.addMember` and `party.removeMember`** add and take away members, with their flags and kept sheets ([characters](characters.md#joining-and-leaving)). Retail ignores whether it worked: the scripts check the party's size first. `party.addMember` working in a conversation tells the runtime that the one talking leaves the map rather than going back to wandering ([wanderers](wanderers.md#joining)).

## Faults

Where the original crashes, or reads past its nodes, the runner ends the script as `script.end` would and puts the reason in `Ending.Fault` (the debug build sets `borg13End` after its crash message). The faults are: a menu or case with no text, leaving a menu or case, a line with no link and no menu to go back to, an If with no link to take, a thirteenth enemy, a random line from a script that isn't there, a move on that leaves nothing to show and nothing to wait for, and a walk through 10,000 nodes without showing anything.

## Cut-scenes

`CutScene`, `CutSceneActor` and `CutSceneStage` in `HBHT.Core.Scripts`, with `ScriptCameras` in `HBHT.Core.Cameras` and `ScreenFade` at the core's root. From 1.0's machine code.

### The world it plays in

- **Starting.** A script object (walked into or used) or a talker whose script is a cut-scene calls `dialoug_func`, and `funcs0[1]` (`0x80051e04`) starts trek fading out, at 1/30 a tick (`FUN_80024c54`). Trek runs on under the fade, clock and wanderers too, but the player's input is dead. Once the screen has been black three frames, trek frees its world (`clear_HUD_elements`): the chunks, the wanderers and the player's body, keeping where the player stood, his facing and the camera's place.
- **The stage** is the script object's staging chunk (its `stage`, a chunk ID): every one of the 271 cut-scene objects has one. A talker's cut-scene stages in **the chunk the player is in** (`monsterpary_func` passes `gameVars.mapShort1/2`), with marks from 0, and sets the party's flag B. `DialogueModeInitPrescripted` (`0x800515a8`) loads the 3×3 around it, gated by the flags as trek does, **without npcs' and encounters' bodies** (`look_for_monsterparties` returns in the dialogue state, `0x8000ef20`), and puts the camera at the stage's corner looking along X. Then the script's first nodes run, which spawn its actors and cut the camera (every cut-scene's first camera op is a cut), and the screen fades in over a second, 1/60 a tick.
- **Marks** are looked up in the staging chunk's map alone, by the reference point's `mark` (`FindReferncePoint`, `0x8000b6f0`), and nothing checks for a miss: the original reads address 0 and crashes. A chunk holds each staged scene's points in a block of its own, named for the scene (I08's `StageA_1156` block is 257–291, Edur's), and a script object's `marks` is its block's start less 1. Every point the 271 objects' scripts name is there.
- **Talkers' cut-scenes from marks 0.** Each of the 9 parties whose cut-scene names a mark their chunk lacks also has a script object for the same script, whose `marks` finds them all: Edur in I08 (`0x0391`), Keelin in Terminor (`0x0382`) and seven more; three of them (Dougal in Terminor C05, and the parties in Overworld F08 and L16) are silent, so only 6 talk. Talking to the body runs the script from 0, so it reads the chunk's first block, another scene's points, and crashes where that block has no such id. Edur's object is walked into, 14 across, so the player meets it first. Keelin's is used with A, 1.2 from her body, so which one A reaches depends on his facing ([what A reaches](map-objects.md#what-a-reaches)); from her body, her scene crashes after "Kisses." at her walk to point 34 whenever she isn't recruited (`TLB-FETCH` at `0x8004fbd4`, WalkTo's read of a missing point).
  - *In the port* each of the 6 that talk is an npc with a `stage`, that script object, which the importer finds: for an npc that isn't silent, a script object in the npc's chunk that plays the same script and whose `marks` find every mark the cut-scene names in its stage, where marks 0 in the npc's chunk don't (`MapImporter`, ADR 0035). Talking to the npc starts the cut-scene as the object would, from its marks on its stage (`TrekLoop.TalkerLaunch`), so Keelin's scene plays to its end from her body as from the object. That's the port's fix, chosen by the user (ADR 0018), and the user played Keelin's from her body on Nov 6, 2026, and found it played to its end as expected, without the wrong camera angles 1.0's start from her body gives. Sholeh appeared in the air and fell as it began: her mark (31, `8/C05/68`, at 1.95, the height of the marks above the stand-ins' 0.23) is inside the bookcase's collision, whose sides face out at x 9.61 and z 7.15 with no top and no floor within, so the spawn's sweep from 100 (`Body.SetDown`, 1.0's `CheckCollision` with no height) finds nothing on any of its tries and leaves her at 1.95; the push carries her 1.7 east out of the bookcase in the first ticks, and she lands on the floor about 20 ticks in. The spawn and the push follow 1.0's code and the mark is the ROM's, wherever the scene starts, so 1.0 should show the same; the user let it be. The other five weren't played from their bodies; they start the same way. The six: `0/C15/39` (0x0420), Edur `0/I08/19` (0x0391), `0/I08/27` (0x039A), `0/I19/4` (0x044E), `4/I01/5` (0x0377) and Keelin `8/C05/17` (0x0382), each `aidyn:mapobject/…`. Elsewhere a miss is noted in `CutScene.Problems` and the op skipped, so an actor stays put while the scene ends.
- **Each tick** (`Dialog_GameState` state 1): the actors within 30 of the camera's aim move and animate (`ProcessPlayers`, `Body.Near`), then the script's cameras; the sky, clouds and weather go on, but **the clock stops**, and map objects, trigger triangles and chunk streaming don't run (`handleZoneEngineFrame` with no player). The near and far planes are 0.1 and 125 rather than 1 and 180 (`RenderZones`, `0x8000d66c`), which brings the fog in to about 21 units ([rendering](rendering.md#the-n64-material)).
- **The box** is the conversation's (`DialougeClass`), and the player's A moves it on, but presses are dropped, not kept, while the screen fades, while the script waits (a walk, a timer, an animation or the camera: each of those also frees the box, so it disappears) and while a camera move runs (the box stays up). Each press the script takes asks the speaker's actor for request 4, talking (`Body.Talk`), unless the script played an animation (`funcs2[1]`, `0x80051ed4`). A conversation in trek does the same (`run_dialougemode_funcs2`), and both press through `SpeakerTalk`.
- **No skip.** 1.0 has no R + A + Z + B: `cutScene_control_func` (`0x80051378`) only tests B, which toggles a flag nothing reads.
- **The end** (`CutScene.CheckEnd`, `0x80051f4c`): the done flag is set, and the screen fades out over a second while actors and cameras carry on, then three frames at black. Every actor is freed (`DialougFreeActors`), the stage goes (`CutScene.Free`, `0x8005169c`), and then, in order:
  1. **The script's ending kind**, which `Script.Start` (`0x8004ff80`) looked up in the endings table (`0x800DFE1C`) into `0x800dfe4c`: three scripts end the game and two lead to the cinematics. Each takes over, so no trek is built. The port imports the table as the base plugin's triggers on `script.ended` ([events](events.md#the-base-plugins)).
  2. **The chain** (`0x80051810`): while trek starts again at a named place (`Zones.SavedPlace`, `0x800d9092`, not −1) and the script returns to trek, the first script object of the kept chunk's Borg9, in its order, that's tangible, holds the kept position within its radius and whose flag A holds (`MapObjects.Touches`, `0x800113bc`, whether it's active or not) starts at once, with `0x800dfe0c` set so trek queues no fade-out, and its cut-scene follows this one with one black frame between: no trek is built. Only the Goblin Ambush's end leaves a named place ([combat](combat.md#in-and-out)); going through a teleporter (`TeleportPlayer`, `0x8000bb54`), loading and a new game (`Title.Leave`) leave none. So Oriana's first cut-scene in the hut (#1181) leads straight into the next, in the blue area (#1183). The port keeps the place as `TrekLoop.AtPlace` and chains in `GameSession.EndCutScene`.
  3. **Trek** starts again where the player stood (`initGameTrek`): every map object gated again and every wanderer at its home node, the camera at its old place aimed at the player and swinging round quickly, and the screen fading in at 1/30 from black. Its first frame is drawn black, the fade-in raising it from the next (`ScreenFade.Step`, `0x8001f330`, runs before the mode), and that frame already walks into map objects: walking in has no fade term (`MapObjects.CanStart`, `0x80010d08`), so a tangible cut-scene where trek starts again cues on that first frame and fades out from black, and trek never shows. That's how the Goblin Ambush's battle, returning the party to Oriana's hut, goes into her first cut-scene. The port walks into objects through trek's fade-in but not through a teleport's or its fade-out (`TrekLoop.Tick`).
- **In the port** the session plays it (`GameSession.BeginCutScene`, `EndCutScene`) and the runtime draws it (`Game.CutScene.cs`); `--cutscene <id>` plays one at once. Trek frees its world, and the stage loads in a zone engine made from trek's (`ZoneEngine.Stage`): its own chunks, lights and rain about the cut-scene's camera, over trek's sounds and weather, with its own music. Trek's zone engine keeps its lights, dynamic ones included, for when trek resumes (`TrekLoop.Resume`).

### Actors

- **Spawning** (`actor.spawn`): a body of the actor's entity (its actor, radius, scale and height), set down at the reference point from on high (mode 0 of the original's `0x80011a64`, from `DialoguePreCallback` at `0x8004f4e4`; [movement](movement.md#setting-a-body-down)); the point's own height doesn't count. It faces the original's default, (−0.71, −0.71), and is kept 0.05 inside the staging chunk. The original's flags on it: `ACTOR_1000`, whose only reader is `SetFacing` (`0x80015910`), so **its first turn snaps**, however late it comes, and keeps any goal (`Body.SnapNextFace`); and `ACTOR_400`, which keeps it out of the push loop, so actors don't push each other. Spawning one again leaves the first body standing, as the original's pointer is overwritten. Alaron is in a cut-scene only if the script spawns him. The actor is the entity's own (`EntityDB::GetBorg7`): Phelan in the final scene (`aidyn:script/0x04FE`) is entity `0x0230`, whose only actor, `0x2918`, stands in its idle and lies only in the states the script asks for (20 and 21, then 9 to die).
- **A spawn over no ground** stays where the point is, height and all (`AllocPlayer` at the point's X, Y and Z, `0x8004f4c8`, then the set-down's tries to the side), and falls, as any body near the camera's aim does, until it's 30 from the aim and stops stepping. The port does the same. Back in Port Saiid from the Namer's Isle (`aidyn:script/0x0430`), Alaron is spawned at point 43, about 10 up over a hole in D15's collision, 33° off the camera's axis: outside a 4:3 picture, so the console never shows his fall, but inside a 16:9 one.
- **Turning** (`actor.face`) to a mark, 10° a frame where it stands after the first; nothing waits for it, but a turning actor counts as steered (below).
- **Walking and running** (`actor.walkTo`, `actor.runTo`): `SetAiDest` to the point's X and Z, within **1**. The script waits, and **a walk's wait ends when no actor has been steered for 30 ticks** and no camera move runs (`ProcessPlayers`, `0x800136c4`): there's no "arrived". An actor beyond 30 of the camera's aim isn't stepped, so it never walks and the wait ends 30 ticks on. An actor that comes no nearer for **300 ticks** is put at its goal and set down (`MoveTo`, `0x80015a7c`, `FUN_80019b08`; 30 in combat, never in trek): the 30 is the wait's rule, not this one. While the screen fades, a steered actor stands (unless it's dying, below), though its steering, and so its count, goes on (`Actor::Move`, `Body.Faded`). The steering is once a frame ([wanderers](wanderers.md)), so where an actor comes to rest depends on it. **Only the actor's root motion moves it** (`Bodies.Process`, `0x80012784`, at `0x80013318`–`0x80013400`: the root's step this tick times the body's scale along its heading); the steering only sets the stick, which sets the request and the heading (`Body.StepTowardGoal`, `0x800159c8`; `Body.ApplyInput`, `0x80014fec`), and nothing gives a body a speed of its own. So an actor with no walk stands where it is, turning to its goal, until the 300 ticks put it there: Pochanargat (`aidyn:actor/0x32B0`, whose requests 2 and 3 lead back to her idle, and none of whose animations drives the root) walking to point 3 after "It is time this miserable world was rid of you!" in her scene (`aidyn:script/0x04FD`) jumps there five seconds on. The port does the same, the user's choice: a mod could give her actor a walk, or change the cut-scene.
- **A walk waits behind a talk.** The count of no progress isn't reset as the actor gets nearer (`+0x25c` is zeroed only by `Body.WalkTo`, `0x80015724`), and it runs while an animation that takes no interrupt holds the actor. The press that brings up an actor's line asks it to talk, and some talks are long and uninterruptible: Shamsuk's (actor `0x294A`, request 4 to state 10, 360 ticks, whose only branch is at its end). In his tower (`aidyn:script/0x0494`) the press that shows "Finally! I was beginning to think…" (node 44) starts his talk, and moving on from it sends him walking to point 9 while it plays. Move on within about a second and a half and he stands through most of it, the 300 run out once he's under way, and `Body.GiveUp` puts him on point 9, from where his walk's step carries him a quarter past it, so the close-ups after (nodes 64, 72 and 116) have him at the picture's left edge. The console does the same.
- **Into another actor.** 1.0 refuses a walk to a point inside another actor (the Fire Lord's scene, `0x048F`): the walker stops at the other and turns, or goes off another way. What stops it isn't settled, as its push loop skips bodies flagged `ACTOR_400` (`ProcessPlayers`, `0x80013514` and `0x80013584`), which every cut-scene actor is. The port's actors walk where the script says, the user's choice.
- **Animations** (`actor.animate`): the actor is asked for the request (`ApplyStimulus`), which it keeps for the original's frame and the next, then stands (`CutScene.StandActors`, `0x80051e2c`, the debug build's `0x80059b50`, asks every actor to stand each frame unless the script animated it). A request that doesn't interrupt its state so never plays. **The wait is a timer**: after the tick it's seen, it lasts the length of the state the actor is then in (`FUN_80058ad4`, `aniLength`), counted from that tick. With no actor on the stage (its spawn missed), nothing waits. The box is gone through the wait, so A can't cut it short.
- **A death holds** (request 9). `ProcessPlayers` (`0x80013200`–`0x800132D4`) does it for every body, not only combat's: once the actor has entered a state for 9 (the body's `+0x18`) and 9 is no longer asked for, it sets `+0x77C`, and from then the actor plays to the end of the state it's in and stops there, its state machine no longer stepped, whatever it's asked for after. Nothing clears the flag but a body's making (`Player.Reset`, `0x80011C90`). No other request holds: the rest go back to idle by their states' branches. And the stick of a steered body asks it to stand only if it isn't dying (`Body.ApplyInput`, `0x80014FEC`, also while the screen fades), so an actor still turning on the spot keeps a death it's asked for. The port does both: `Body.Mortal` wraps each cut-scene actor's animation, and `Body.StandUnlessDying` is the stick's first word. Trek never asks a body for 9, and combat keeps its own ([combat](combat-actions.md#an-attack)). So the Fire Lord (`aidyn:script/0x048F`) lies where he fell, though his actor's death (state 6 of `aidyn:actor/0x2FBA`) goes back to idle for any request, and Niesen dies even when A on "I remembered..." asks it while he's still turning.
- **A death the actor's state takes no part in is lost.** Each press asks the new line's speaker to talk (above), and Niesen's talking (state 15 of `aidyn:actor/0x2BB3`, 241 ticks) has no branch for 9. So with Rheda in the party, A pressed on "Rheda, I see a place..." (node 230) within four seconds of it showing asks for his death while he still talks: it's never taken, neither the fall nor its cry plays, and the wait lasts the talk's 241 ticks before the fade. Waited out, he falls. 1.0 and the port alike. His cry, and the Fire Lord's, is the request 9 key of Alaron's profile (`aidyn:sequence/0x06EA` at the first frame), which every cut-scene actor keeps ([audio](audio.md#bodies-sounds)).
- **Held items** (`actor.hold`, `actor.putAway`): the item's model (`get_item_borg5`, imported as `Model` on weapons, shields and six items: see [db](../formats/db.md)) on the actor's scene's locator 0 (`AttachItemToPlayer`, `0x80014BA0`, slot 0), turned by the locator model's rotation and scaled with the body, drawn even while the body is hidden. An item without one, such as the two spells script `0x0434` shows, leaves what's held as it is. It's the slot combat puts a weapon in, drawn the same way ([combat](combat-actions.md#what-the-bodies-hold)).
- **The fade op** (`screen.dip`) dips: down over a second and back up over another by itself (fade mode 3), while the script carries on. No cut-scene uses it.
- **Command 53**, once near the start of every cut-scene, does nothing in 1.0: it only takes its node's effect slot. The import leaves it out.

### In the port

`CutScene` carries out each cue on its `CutSceneStage` as it comes. Two traps:

- **A wait waits on the stage,** not on the cue that started it: on the actors being steered or the cameras moving. So scripts could share a stage and run side by side.
- **The script's cameras keep their place by the game's ticker** (`GameState.Ticker`), as trek's camera objects do; a move is stepped every tick until it's freed.

The session's `Tick` hears the actors from the camera and steps the stage's sounds, as `CutScene.PlayFrame` (`0x80050f50`) runs `HandleZoneEngineFrame`; the plain `Tick` the tests use steps the cut-scene alone.
