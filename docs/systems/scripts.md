# Scripts: the Borg13 VM (roadmap 2.4)

`Aidyn.Core.Scripts`. The format and what the importer writes are in [dialogue](../formats/dialogue.md).

`ScriptRunner` runs one script (`aidyn:dialogue/0x…`) against the flag network and the game state. It ports the walk over the nodes (`DialogNode.cpp`) and the callbacks that carry out the ops (`dialogue.cpp`, `dialogmode.cpp`), from the debug build's machine code. `GameState.RunScript` makes a runner against the game: its flags, its world stream of random numbers and the flag that rolls skill checks 0–21. Conversations, cut-scenes and the editor's script page all start theirs there, the editor passing a world of its own that fixes skill checks by hand.

## Its boundary

- **Rules** go to the flag network, and to `IScriptWorld` for the party, inventory, gold, skills, stats and experience. The party ([characters](characters.md)) answers the skill and stat questions; its inventory comes with 2.3. Where the debug build crashes on a failed call (taking an item the party doesn't have, adding to a full party, giving experience to a non-member), retail carries on, and so does the runner.
- **Presentation** goes out as cues on `IScriptHost` (ADR 0003): cameras, spawning, facing, walking and running, animations, held items, sounds and the fade. The runtime carries them out, and tests record them.
- **The player** answers the page: `Continue` moves on from a line, `Choose` takes a choice, and `Skip` ends the script, as the debug build's B does; 1.0 has no skip. While the scripted camera moves, the original drops input (see [cut-scenes](#cut-scenes)).
- **The player's name** is filled in by the runner: every line it shows, as a line, a choice or a random line, has `{player}` (`ScriptRunner.PlayerToken`) replaced by the first party member's name (`IScriptWorld.PlayerName`) as it stands when the page is made, so a rename in the options menu ([ui](ui.md#renaming-alaron)) shows on the next page, in conversations, cut-scenes, backdrops and signs alike. The views draw the text as they get it. With no one in the party the text keeps `{player}`.
- **Waits** end with `Resume` from the runtime: an actor arriving, an animation ending or the camera stopping. `Step(ticks)` counts down `Wait`, whose operand is in ticks.
- **Map objects remember** a skill check's result and a random line's roll (the original's `VoxelChart`), through `IScriptWorld.TryRecall` and `Remember`. So talking to someone again can't reroll a check or a rumour. Troubadour checks aren't remembered. The chart itself, 19 memories that expire and are saved with the game, is `MapObjectMemory` (see [saves](saves.md#map-objects-memory)), and the game state answers for it.
- **Randomness** comes from an `IRandom` stream (ADR 0006). `Lcg` is the original's generator, and `Range` is `Random::Range`.
- **When a conversation ends**, the original reloads the map and gates its objects again by the flags (`loadGameBorgScenes`), so what the script changed shows at once. The port does that when the pause menu closes (`ChunkStreamer.Regate`) and rebuilds trek after a cut-scene, backdrop or help script (`Conversation.RebuildsTrek`), but a plain conversation or sign doesn't regate yet.

## How it walks

A node is **reached** when the walk arrives at it, and its setup ops run: the speaker from any op, then the settings (camera time, enemies, battle bytes, reset visited), then the cameras and actors. It's **left** when the player moves on from it, and leaving runs its first effect op. The condition and effect checks (`dialoug_func_b_check`, `dialoug_func_c_check`) stop at the first one, as the data expects; ops 55, 59 and 60 aren't effects in the jump table. Where the walk can go on without the player, through an If or a line with no text, it does so at once.

- **Lines** show their text. Leaving one goes to its link. With no link, it goes back to the last menu or case, which is reached again: its setup and effect run again, and it's shown with the choices not yet taken.
- **Ifs** take their first link when the condition is **exactly 1** (`bne v0, 1`, not nonzero), and their second otherwise. Every base-game condition in an If is 0 or 1, so this only matters for mods.
- **Cases** show their text and evaluate their condition. They count the links whose own range holds the value (each link's, not the case node's), taken or not. With two or more, the untaken matches are offered as choices. With fewer, the value is kept, and when the player moves on, the first untaken match is taken. The value is kept as 16 bits, and 65535 is the original's mark for none: a single-match case on exactly 65535 gold finds nothing and faults.
- **Menus** show their text and their untaken choices. Each choice is reached as it's added, so its setup ops run then, and a page's choices can have different speakers. Backdrops and the help menu offer taken choices again, and a backdrop's choice loop tests the next choice's slot after moving on, so it shows every choice.
- **Visited** is set when a node is left, not when it's reached. So a choice that ends the script is never marked, and it stays on offer.
- **Effects that end the script** (end, shop, battle, train, camp heal) end it at once. A cut-scene, backdrop or the help menu sets its done flag however it ends, including by `Skip` or a fault.

A page is the line and the choices under it, as the original's `diags` list holds them, with room for 9. The line loses its leading spaces, as `look_for_dialouge_v_Num` trims them, but choices keep theirs. When the first choice has the same speaker as the line, a conversation shows the line alone first and then the choices under Alaron's portrait (`DialougeClass::Setup`); that's for the runtime's UI. What A does on a page is `DialogueBox.Accept` in the core: it brings up the choices behind a line that showed alone, else takes the cursor's choice (`PicksChoice`: the page has choices and the script isn't waiting for A) or moves the script on, and says which it did (`DialogueAccept`). Each mode passes how its press moves the script, so conversations and cut-scenes ask the speaker to talk; backdrops and the help menu, which have no line to show alone, take `DialogueBox.Advance` with the choice they name.

## Ops that need more than a flag

- **`RandomLine`** rolls 0–100, which the map object remembers, and picks a node of the other script by `roll / 100 / (1 / count)` in single precision, where the count includes its blank nodes. A blank node shows node 0's text. Twelve rumour scripts end in a blank node, so they show their first rumour about a third of the time, twice as often as each of the others. The line shows under the node that asked for it, and moving on leaves that node. The other script's text doesn't go through the player-name and escape processing the main script gets at load, but no rumour uses either.
- **`CheckSkill`** asks for the party's skill check once per script (`Party::SkillCheck`, a roll against the party's best: see [characters](characters.md#the-party)), keeps it, and remembers it on the map object. The base game only checks Diplomat, Loremaster and Troubadour. While the state flags record's `randomSkillChecks` flag (3717, a debug option) is set, it rolls 0–21 instead, after the lookup.
- **`PlayAnimation`** waits for the animation to end. In a conversation, the original looks the actor up from the page it has just cleared (`FUN_800591e4`), so no one animates, and the script waits for the player to press A with the previous line still showing. The runner reproduces this: an `AnimateCue` with no actor, and a `Player` wait. Nine conversation nodes do it, such as the lighthouse keeper handing over his key (Borg13 890 node 13). Cut-scenes only animate actors they've spawned.
- **`Battle`** hands over the enemies from `AddEnemy` (a thirteenth faults), the arena (above 27 becomes 0), the collision and animation bytes, whether the party can flee (not in cut-scenes), and the record's `battleFlag`. In a conversation with someone who wanders, the original fights their party instead, and a shop or training uses them as the keeper.
- **`CampHeal`** heals the party and ends the script, and eight hours pass. A camp scene (`rest`) adds eight more, so a camp heal in one would pass sixteen.
- **`AddMember` and `RemoveMember`** add and take away members, with their flags and kept sheets ([characters](characters.md#joining-and-leaving)). Retail ignores whether AddMember worked: the scripts check the party's size first. AddMember working in a conversation tells the runtime that the one talking leaves the map rather than going back to wandering ([wanderers](wanderers.md#joining)).

## Faults

Where the original crashes, or reads past its nodes, the runner ends the script as `EndDialogue` would and puts the reason in `Ending.Fault` (the debug build sets `borg13End` after its crash message). The faults are: a menu or case with no text, leaving a menu or case, a line with no link and no menu to go back to, an If with no link to take, a thirteenth enemy, command 49, a random line from a script that isn't there, and a walk through 10,000 nodes without showing anything.

Two scripts have problems in the data:

- **Node 24 of script 1040** (the harbour reunion, id 2192) is Dougal's line, and it lost its link to node 25 (its siblings have one, and an op 2 naming the next node). With Dougal in the party, the original reads a node past the end of the script, and the rest of the scene doesn't play. The runner ends the scene there, setting its done flag. What the console does is open ([to check](#to-check-in-the-emulator)).
- **Trahern's conversation** (936) goes round nodes 64–69 forever once flags 67 and 84 are both set. Nothing sets 84, so it can't happen.

## Cut-scenes

`CutScene`, `CutSceneActor` and `CutSceneStage` in `Aidyn.Core.Scripts`, with `ScriptCameras` in `Aidyn.Core.Cameras` and `ScreenFade` at the core's root. From 1.0's machine code.

### The world it plays in

- **Starting.** A dialogue object (walked into or used) or a talker whose script is a cut-scene calls `dialoug_func`, and `funcs0[1]` (`0x80051e04`) starts trek fading out, at 1/30 a tick (`FUN_80024c54`). Trek runs on under the fade, clock and wanderers too, but the player's input is dead. Once the screen has been black three frames, trek frees its world (`clear_HUD_elements`): the chunks, the wanderers and the player's body, keeping where the player stood, his facing and the camera's place.
- **The stage** is the dialogue object's staging chunk: every one of the 271 cut-scene objects has one. A talker's cut-scene stages in **the chunk the player is in** (`monsterpary_func` passes `gameVars.mapShort1/2`), with reference points from 0, and sets the party's flag B. `DialogueModeInitPrescripted` (`0x800515a8`) loads the 3×3 around it, gated by the flags as trek does, **without monster parties' bodies** (`look_for_monsterparties` returns in the dialogue state, `0x8000ef20`), and puts the camera at the stage's corner looking along X. Then the script's first nodes run, which spawn its actors and cut the camera (every cut-scene's first camera op is a cut), and the screen fades in over a second, 1/60 a tick.
- **Reference points** are looked up by id in the staging chunk's map alone (`FindReferncePoint`, `0x8000b6f0`), and nothing checks for a miss: the original reads address 0 and crashes. A chunk holds each staged scene's points in a block of its own, named for the scene (I08's `StageA_1156` block is 257–291, Edur's), and a dialogue object's base is its block's start less 1. Every point the 271 objects' scripts name is there.
- **Talkers' cut-scenes from base 0.** Each of the 9 talkers whose cut-scene names a point their chunk lacks also has a dialogue object for the same script, whose base finds them all: Edur in I08 (`0x0391`), Keelin in Terminor (`0x0382`) and seven more. Talking to the body runs the script from 0 instead, so it reads the chunk's first block, another scene's points, and crashes where that block has no such id. Edur's trigger is walked into, 14 across and around him, so the player meets the right one. Keelin's is used with A, radius 2, 1.2 from her body, so A weighs her body against it ([what A reaches](map-objects.md#what-a-reaches)): standing against her, facing her within about 30° reaches her body, and turned 45° or more to Alaron's right reaches her object. From her body, her scene plays on the wrong points and crashes at her walk to point 34 (nodes 37, 132 and 134) whenever she isn't recruited: `TLB-FETCH` at `0x8004fbd4`, WalkTo's read of a point `get_map_referencepoint` didn't find. The console crashes there, after "Kisses." From her object, at base 128, every point is there and the scene plays. The port notes a miss in `CutScene.Problems` and skips the op, so from her body she stays put while the scene ends.
- **Each tick** (`Dialog_GameState` state 1): the actors within 30 of the camera's aim move and animate (`Body.Near`) (`ProcessPlayers`), then the script's cameras; the sky, clouds and weather go on, but **the clock stops**, and map objects, trigger triangles and chunk streaming don't run (`handleZoneEngineFrame` with no player). The near and far planes are 0.1 and 125 rather than 1 and 180 (`RenderZones`, `0x8000d66c`).
- **The box** is the conversation's (`DialougeClass`), and the player's A moves it on, but presses are dropped, not kept, while the screen fades, while the script waits (a walk, a timer, an animation or the camera: each of those also frees the box, so it disappears) and while a camera move runs (the box stays up). Each press the script takes asks the speaker's actor for request 4, talking (`PlayerMotion.Talk`), unless the script played an animation (`funcs2[1]`, `0x80051ed4`). A conversation in trek does the same (`run_dialougemode_funcs2`), and both press through `SpeakerTalk`.
- **No skip.** 1.0 has no R + A + Z + B: `cutScene_control_func` (`0x80051378`) only tests B, which toggles a flag nothing reads.
- **The end** (`0x80051f4c`): the done flag is set, and the screen fades out over a second while actors and cameras carry on, then three frames at black. Every actor is freed (`DialougFreeActors`), the stage goes, and trek starts again where the player stood (`initGameTrek`): every map object gated again and every wanderer at its home node, the camera at its old place aimed at the player and swinging round quickly, and the screen fading in at 1/30. Three scripts end the game and two lead to the cinematics (`borg_short_list`, `0x800DFE1C`: `0x0512`–`0x0514` game over, `0x04F4` Roog's true name with flag 3442, `0x04FE` the ending and credits). The runtime doesn't play those after a cut-scene yet, though the cinematics and credits are ported (`CinematicPlayer`, `CreditsRoll`). A check after the end for a tangible dialogue object at the player's place, which would chain into another cut-scene, only runs where the saved place's `mapDatB` isn't −1, which ordinary play never leaves, so it isn't ported.
- **In the runtime** (`Game.CutScene.cs`), `--cutscene <id>` plays one at once, as the first dialogue object or talker that starts it would. Trek frees its world (`TrekLoop.BeginCutScene`), the particle pool is emptied, and the stage loads in a zone engine made from trek's (`ZoneEngine.Stage`): its own chunks, lights and rain about the cut-scene's camera, landing on what its centre chunk draws, over trek's sounds, audio keys and weather sounds, and asking for its own music. When the cut-scene is over the stage ends (`ZoneEngine.End`: the rain forgotten, the pool emptied, its world freed), and trek's world is loaded again (`TrekLoop.Resume`). Trek's zone engine keeps its own lights through it, dynamic ones included.

### Actors

- **Spawning** (`CreateActor`): a body of the speaker's entity (its actor, radius, scale and height), set down at the reference point from on high (`Body.SetDown(p, 0, 0, 0)`, `0x80011a64`, from `DialoguePreCallback` at `0x8004f4e4`; [movement](movement.md#setting-a-body-down)); the point's own height doesn't count. It faces the original's default, (−0.71, −0.71), and is kept 0.05 inside the staging chunk. The original's flags on it: `ACTOR_1000`, whose only reader is `SetFacing` (`0x80015910`), so **its first turn snaps**, however late it comes, and keeps any goal (`Body.SnapNextFace`); and `ACTOR_400`, which keeps it out of the push loop, so actors don't push each other. Spawning one again leaves the first body standing, as the original's pointer is overwritten. Alaron is in a cut-scene only if the script spawns him.
- **Turning** (`FaceRefPoint`) to a point, 10° a frame where it stands after the first; nothing waits for it, but a turning actor counts as steered (below).
- **Walking and running** (`WalkTo`, `RunTo`): `SetAiDest` to the point's X and Z, within **1**. The script waits, and **a walk's wait ends when no actor has been steered for 30 ticks** and no camera move runs (`ProcessPlayers`, `0x800136c4`): there's no "arrived". An actor beyond 30 of the camera's aim isn't stepped, so it never walks and the wait ends 30 ticks on. An actor that comes no nearer for **300 ticks** is put at its goal and set down (`MoveTo`, `0x80015a7c`, `FUN_80019b08`; 30 in combat, never in trek): the 30 is the wait's rule, not this one. While the screen fades, a steered actor stands, though its steering, and so its count, goes on (`Actor::Move`, `Body.Faded`).
- **Animations** (`PlayAnimation`): the speaker's actor is asked for the request (`ApplyStimulus`), which it keeps for the original's frame and the next, then stands (`FUN_80059b50` asks every actor to stand each frame unless the script animated it). A request that doesn't interrupt its state so never plays. **The wait is a timer**: after the tick it's seen, it lasts the length of the state the actor is then in (`FUN_80058ad4`, `aniLength`), counted from that tick. With no actor on the stage (its spawn missed), nothing waits.
- **Held items** (`ShowItem`, `HideItem`): the item's model (`get_item_borg5`, imported as `Model` on weapons, shields and six items: see [db](../formats/db.md)) on the speaker's scene's locator 0, with no offset, scaled with the body, drawn even while the body is hidden. An item without one, such as the two spells script `0x0434` shows, leaves what's held as it is. Combat puts a weapon on the same slot (`AttachItemToPlayer` slot 0, locator 0, or 1 for a bow), so how it sits in the hand is checked with combat's arenas (M3). The goblins of the ambush (`0x0365`) hold their short swords this way.
- **The fade op** (`FadeOut`) dips: down over a second and back up over another by itself (fade mode 3), while the script carries on. No cut-scene uses it.
- **Command 53**, once near the start of every cut-scene, does nothing: it only takes its node's effect slot.

### In the port

`CutScene` takes the script, its start and its `CutSceneStage` (the staging chunk's map set, chunk, map and the loaded collision), and carries out each cue on the stage as it comes. What a wait waits for is a state of the stage, the actors steered or the cameras moving, not of the cue that started it, so scripts could share a stage and run side by side. The runtime calls `Start`, `Press` with what A does, and `Tick` with the stage's zone engine and each actor's animations (`IActorMotion`: root motion and the state's length), and brings trek back once it's `Over`. That `Tick` hears the actors from the camera as they step (each keeps its `BodyAudio`, with Alaron's sounds), then steps the stage's sounds from the camera's aim and the weather, as `CutScene.PlayFrame` (`0x80050f50`) runs `HandleZoneEngineFrame`; the plain `Tick` the tests use steps the cut-scene alone. The script's cameras keep their place by the game's ticker (`GameState.Ticker`), as trek's camera objects do; a move is stepped every tick until it's freed, so a run that doesn't count the ticker plays the same. A real-data test plays every cut-scene a dialogue object or a talker starts to its end in a new game, taking the first choices; the only problems are the talkers' missing points.

## To check in the emulator

Two things the runner reads from the code that the user hasn't seen play yet (also in [user-checks](../user-checks.md)):

- **The lighthouse keeper handing over his key** (Borg13 890 node 13). The runner has "Here... take the key" need A twice, with no animation, since the original looks up who animates from the page it has just cleared (`PlayAnimation`, above). If the keeper does animate, the lookup is wrong, and so are the other eight nodes that do this.
- **Dougal at the harbour reunion** (script 1040). With Dougal in the party, the runner ends the scene at his line (node 24), where the original reads past the end of the script. What the console does there, whether it ends, hangs or plays something else, decides what the runner should do.
