# The runtime (M1, M2, M3)

`src/Aidyn.Game` is the Godot game. It references `Aidyn.Plugins` and `Aidyn.Core`, and not `Aidyn.Rom`, so the build enforces ADR 0001. Import first ([importer](importer.md)), build with `dotnet build`, then run it with `tools/godot.ps1 --path src/Aidyn.Game`. It reads the plugins from the data folder, so set `AIDYN_DATA` for both if the import went elsewhere. It refuses a base plugin that isn't there or was imported by another importer (`PluginSet.LoadInstalledChecked`, `BasePlugin.Diagnose`), as the CLI and the editor do, with the same words saying to import again.

The runtime keeps its loops thin (ADR 0012): the core steps the game and the runtime draws what it leaves and carries out what it asks. Each system's doc says how its part is drawn; this one says how they fit together, and holds the controls and the command line.

## How a tick runs

- **The session** is the core's `GameSession` (`Aidyn.Core`): the game's state, trek with its zone engine, and what runs over or in place of it. The core starts it (`GameSession.Start`) where a `GameStart` says, a save's place or a new game's arrival with the command line's overrides (`Game.StartingPlace`): it makes the player's body there, enters the chunk so its terrain's flags are written before the chunks' objects are gated by them, loads the 3×3, sets him down from on high unless a save put him where he stood, gives the follow camera the mode the chunk's terrain chooses, and begins trek; the runtime then builds its player and camera views from `TrekLoop.Player` and `Camera`, and the tests start their sessions the same way. `Game._PhysicsProcess` calls its `Tick` once per 60 Hz tick, which counts the tick in every mode and steps the mode that runs, then the pause menu's `Step`. What runs is `GameSession.Mode`, by precedence a cinematic, the credits, the pause menu, a backdrop's page, a map object's popup, a conversation, a cut-scene, a battle, else trek, and the runtime holds the world's views still by it rather than working it out. Every press is the session's too: the runtime translates Godot's event to a `MenuAction` (`MenuStick`) and `GameSession.Press` sends it to the mode that runs and says what it came to (`PressOutcome`), the held directions repeating in the session's `StickRepeat` ([ui](ui.md#the-pause-menu)). The tick returns what it came to (`SessionStep`): the runtime draws the cinematic or the credits, the cut-scene, the battle or trek, and begins or ends a cut-scene or a battle when the tick says trek has faded out or it's over (`GameSession.BeginCutScene`, `EndCutScene`, `BeginBattle`, `EndBattle`), around which it draws. The session decides what's open itself: the pause menu is its own (`Menu`, the core's `PauseMenu`), opened on the transition that opens it (Start's `OpenMenu`, a container's `OpenLoot`, a script's `OpenShop` or `OpenTraining`, a battle's `PauseBattle`, a win's `PostBattle`, `ReopenMenu`) on the way to the runtime, stepped each tick by the scroll actor's clock the runtime gives it (`ScrollClock`), and closed as its step returns how it ended (`MenuClosed` with the menu's `PauseExit`, after which the session gates the map again and the tick returns `SessionStep.MenuClosed`), while `PauseMenuView` only binds the tabs' views to each opening's models, lays them out and plays the cues the menu sends (`IPauseMenuHost`); and a backdrop's or the help menu's page is trek's `Backdrop`, a `BackdropPage` trek makes as its conversation shows one and whose presses it takes, stepped each tick and dropped once it has faded out, which `BackdropLayer` only draws. A cinematic and the credits are the session's too (`Cinematic`, `Credits`): it steps the core's `CinematicPlayer` and `CreditsRoll` each tick and ends them itself, sending `EndCinematic` or `EndCredits` before what follows, and `CinematicLayer` and `CreditsLayer` only draw what each tick left and play their music.
- **Transitions.** Every way a mode, the menu or a script ends is a `Transition` (`Aidyn.Core`), decided and carried out in the core and handed to the runtime through `ITransitionHost.Transition` in order: a shop or training to open, the loot window (`OpenLoot`) and the pause menu on Start (`OpenMenu`), a battle begun on its arena, its pause menu asked for, the battle over (`StartedBattle`, `PauseBattle`, `EndBattle`), a win's post-battle windows (`PostBattle`, which the pause menu opens over black, `PauseMenu.OpenPostBattle`) and the game over (`GameOver`, after its death scene, which loads the save the game last came from or went to, or starts a new game with none); a battle to start, `StartBattle` with its `BattleSetup`, the same from a wanderer's ambush, a monster party used, a camp ambush and a script, is cued by trek, `TrekLoop.CueBattle`, and reaches a host only where nothing plays it, as on the editor's script page), the camp, hours rested (already passed), something to say, a cut-scene's stage begun or ended and trek back, the credits or a cinematic to play and their ends (`EndCredits`, `EndCinematic`, sent before what follows them), and the menu to reopen after them once trek has faded in (trek's state 8). A script's end becomes them in `Transition.AfterScript`, which the editor's script page shows too.
- **Trek** is the core's `TrekLoop` (`Aidyn.Core.Trek`), which the session steps: the fades into a door or a cut-scene, the clock, the map objects the player touches, the camera, the player, the wanderers and a conversation, in the original's order. The runtime carries out what it asks through `ITrekHost`: conversations show in the box, and the loot window opens. Godot's physics interpolation smooths the display between ticks.
- **The zone engine** (`ZoneEngine`, `Aidyn.Core.Chunks`; the original's `InitZoneEngine` and `HandleZoneEngineFrame`) is the world a mode plays in, and what trek and cut-scenes share: the chunk streamer with its collision and drawn surfaces, the chunks' sounds and light map objects as each loads and goes, the music request, the weather's step, rain and sounds, and bodies' footsteps heard from its camera. The runtime makes trek's and hands it to `TrekLoop`, which loads its wanderers with each chunk (`ZoneEngine.Loaded`). A cut-scene's stage is another made from it (`ZoneEngine.Stage`), with its own chunks, lights and rain over the same sounds and weather, and `CutScene.Tick` steps it; a battle's arena is a stage the same way, on the Battle arenas, which `Battle.Tick` steps ([combat](combat.md#the-port)). It holds the sky (`ZoneEngine.Sky`, the core's `SkyDirector`), which trek follows from chunk to chunk, the session chooses again as each world is built and steps after trek, a cut-scene or a battle, and `SkyView` only pushes to the shaders, with the backdrop and the lens flare drawn from it ([rendering](rendering.md#the-skys-colours)). The runtime starts each frame of lights through it (`BeginLightFrame`, which takes the sun and moon from the sky) and draws what it holds.
- **Cadence** ([ADR 0017](../adr/0017-the-core-steps-every-tick.md)). Where the original acts once per frame, or loops toward a target fixed for the frame, the port matches what it does at an idealized 30 fps, a `delta` of 2 (`Aidyn.Core.Cadence`), the user's choice: the console runs at 15–25 fps. Texture scrolling and rain spawn at 20 fps ([rendering](rendering.md#scrolling-textures), [weather](weather.md#rain-and-snow)), and the pause menu at 60 ([ui](ui.md#opening-and-closing)). Code that acts once a frame counts its ticks with `FrameGate`.
- **The game** is the core's `GameState` ([saves](saves.md)): a new game's, or a save's with `--save <path>`. A new game starts as the new game record says (`aidyn:newgame/0`, [hardcoded content](../hardcoded-content.md)): the party it builds, and where the player arrives (`GameSession.Arrival`: Overworld I08 at (16, 62), 10 up). The player's body is the party leader's actor (`GameSession.PlayerActor`). Plugins without the record, imported before it was added, are refused with a word to import again (`GameSession.NewGame`), as an out-of-date import is. Each tick in trek passes the game's time, and every tick, in the menu too, `GameState.CountTick` (from `GameSession.Tick`) counts the time played (`World::AddPlayTime`) and the game's ticker, the original's frame counter that camera objects and cut-scenes' cameras keep their place by ([cameras](cameras.md#camera-objects-processvoxelcamera)).
- **Chunks.** The core's `ChunkStreamer` (`Aidyn.Core.Chunks`) loads the 3×3 chunks' collision (`CollisionWorld`; Godot physics isn't used) and map objects, and `ChunkStreamerView` draws their scenes, scene objects and actors ([rendering](rendering.md)). Entering a chunk writes its terrain into the flags ([flags](flags.md)).
- **Bodies.** The player is drawn by `Game`, and every other body by a `BodyView`: `WandererView` for trek's wanderers, `CutSceneActorView` for a cut-scene's actors (which adds the item in its hand) and `CombatantView` for a battle's combatants, the party's among them, faded over combat's distance of 128. A `BodyView` holds the body's actor at its scale, faded by its distance from the camera's aim, lit by the point lights that reach it and with its shadow ([movement](movement.md#shadows)); the arithmetic of those distances is `BodyShadow`'s, in the core. An actor's state machine, timing and root motion are the core's `ActorMotion` ([actors](../formats/actors.md)); the runtime's `ActorAnimator` poses its scene at the state and clock that says, at once as a body's ticks, and with the frame, between ticks, for map actors and the pause menu's scroll. Bodies' branches roll on the game's `RandomStreams.Animation` (`Models.BodyRolls`), and other actors' on streams of their own.
- **Gating.** A scene object is placed only if its flag gate holds when its chunk loads (`VoxelObjects_Activate`), not every frame. The original decides again when it reloads the map, after the pause menu, a shop, training, a battle, a cut-scene or a teleport (`loadGameBorgScenes`), but not after a conversation in trek. The runtime regates after the pause menu (`ChunkStreamerView.Regate`), building again each loaded chunk whose scene objects come or go. After a cut-scene, a backdrop or the help menu it reloads the map set's chunks and their wanderers where the player stands (`TrekLoop.Resume`), as the original's trek starts again ([wanderers](wanderers.md#joining)).
- **Modes over trek.** The pause menu stops trek and draws over the world ([ui](ui.md#the-pause-menu)); a map object's popup stops the clock and takes input but leaves the world running ([map-objects](map-objects.md#popups)); a cut-scene frees trek's world and plays on a stage of its own, which ends before trek's is loaded again ([scripts](scripts.md#cut-scenes)); so does a battle, on its arena, with its own camera, Start asking for its pause menu (the System and Party tabs, `PauseMenu.OpenCombat`), and trek is built again where the battle began, or having fled, at the farthest breadcrumb ([combat](combat.md#in-and-out)); a backdrop pauses the game under its page ([ui](ui.md#books-and-the-help-menu)); the Theater's cinematics play over everything ([cinematics](cinematics.md)). After a cut-scene, trek is built again where the player stood and fades in.
- **Settings** ([ADR 0013](../adr/0013-settings-in-the-options-menu.md), [ui](ui.md#options)): `Game.ApplySettings` carries out the options menu's graphics page at once, and they're kept in `settings.json` in the data folder.
- **Audio** ([audio](audio.md)): the core's `GameAudio` mixes the original's sequencer, which `AudioOutput` plays on its own thread through a Godot generator stream.
- **Saving.** F8 saves the game, with where the player stands in his chunk (`GameSession.NotePlace`), to `quick.json` in the saves folder. Loading from the System tab reloads the Godot scene from the save, keeping the plugins loaded; the command line's options but `--screenshot` don't apply again.

## Stand-ins

- Alaron's head follows his neck, where the game keeps it upright and turns it toward nearby objects.
- **The spawn point.** A rock stands in a hole in the terrain in front of it, with collision walls and no top. The original drops the player from a height of 10; the runtime puts him on the highest ground below the spawn instead.

## Controls

- **Moving.** WASD or the left stick. The stick walks under 80% tilt and runs above; on a keyboard, Shift walks. Ctrl or the left trigger sneaks (Z on the N64).
- **The camera.** The original orbits it with R and the stick, and Alaron can't move while it does. Here that's the gamepad's right shoulder with the left stick, the right mouse button dragged, the right stick, or Q and E. Only the shoulder holds him still; the others turn the camera while he walks (a port addition, [cameras](cameras.md)).
- **A** is Space or the bottom face button: it uses the map object the player reaches, and closes or chooses in a popup. **B** is Backspace or the right face button: it closes a popup, or with none raises or lowers the party's portraits. **Start** is Enter, Escape or Start.
- **In a battle:** the party member whose turn it is walks in its field as in trek, and Z or the left trigger with the stick still skips the turn (Z or L). A (Space) with the stick still aims an attack at what's in reach; then left and right (the stick, the D-pad, A and D or the arrows) step the target, a bow's or thrown weapon's by Q and E (C-left and C-right) with the stick moving a free cursor, A attacks it, and B (Backspace) or Z puts it away.
- **In the pause menu:** Z and X or the triggers change the tab (Z or L, and R); Q and E or the shoulders change the member (C-left and C-right, C-left showing the next); the movement keys, arrows, left stick or D-pad change the list and move the cursor; Tab or the top face button shows the details while held (C-down); R or the left face button is C-up in a shop. In the name entry the keyboard types. Hovering a row or the wheel moves the cursor.
- **F-keys.** F toggles a free camera (Space and C for up and down, Shift for faster). F3 toggles the overlay: in a battle, its arena, state, whose turn it is and which of the round's, the field's radius and the ring, the combatants with their cells and radii, and the camera's eye and aim; the chunk, the position in chunk coordinates, the surface underfoot, the speed, the animation request, the pace, the camera's mode and the object holding it, the music and sounds playing, the particles in use, the rain's drops and splashes, and what a cut-scene waits for. F5 toggles the pace, F6 the sky's placement and F7 the UI screen's height. In a battle, F9 moves the battle screen's radar between the log box and the screen's corner, and F10 routs the enemies and F11 fells the party, so that the next turn's change (Z) wins or loses it (`Battle.Rout`, `Collapse`).
- **Debug:** holding either thumbstick down (L3 or R3) raises the tick rate from 60 to 180 a second, as far as the machine keeps up (`Game.SetTickRate`). The pause menu stays at 60.

## The command line

After `--`: `tools/godot.ps1 --path src/Aidyn.Game -- --chunk I08 --at 16,62 --yaw 0 --pitch -15 --distance 5 --hold move_forward,4 --screenshot <absolute path>.png` starts somewhere, holds an input, saves the window and quits. The run gives up after a minute. `LaunchOptions` reads the options (on top of `CommandLine`, the parser and screenshot run the editor shares), `Game.Start` builds the game in named steps from them, and `Game.Debug.cs` carries out the ones that drive it (`RunLaunchActions`: `--menu`, `--fly`, `--hold`, `--press`, `--cutscene`, `--cinematic`, `--screenshot`) and draws the F3 overlay.

- **Where:** `--set n`, `--chunk`, `--at x,z` (by default where a new game starts), `--yaw`, `--pitch`, `--distance`; `--fly x,y,z` starts with the free camera, which scene objects' distances are then measured from; `--camera outdoor|interior` starts in either mode (by default the start chooses as a door into its chunk would). Gwernia's open ground is around (30–80, 30–80) in M07, for example `--chunk M07 --at 40,55`; elsewhere in M07 there's no ground, and he falls and is put back again and again.
- **The game:** `--save <path>` starts from a save, where it stands unless `--chunk` or `--at` say otherwise. `--flags 5,50` sets plain flags before anything loads. A plain launch is the title's New Game: the opening cinematic, then script 866 on its stage, then trek in I08 (`GameSession.StartNewGame`, [cinematics](cinematics.md#what-starts-them)). Any option that says where to start or drives the run (`--save`, `--set`, `--chunk`, `--at`, `--flags`, `--fly`, `--hold`, `--press`, `--menu`, `--cutscene`, `--cinematic`, `--battle`, `--screenshot`) skips the opening and starts in trek, where the opening's cut-scene still starts from its object unless `--flags 5,11` is added; `--opening on` or `off` says which outright. `--time <hours>` sets the time of day.
- **Input:** `--hold` holds actions for so many seconds, several joined with `+`, as in `move_forward+walk,4`. `--press pause,menu_member_left,move_right` presses actions in turn, a fifth of a second apart, as input events, which `--hold` doesn't make; the screenshot waits for them. `wait:2` among them waits two seconds more, and `move_forward:1.3` holds the action that long (pressed and let go as events, so combat's held menus hear both), and with a + after it (`menu_details:1.6+`) the next presses come while it's held, so a battle can be scripted for the movie writer: `--battle aidyn:voxel/0x1097/2,3,ambush --press wait:3,menu_tab_previous,wait:1,move_forward:1.3,wait:0.5,menu_accept,wait:1.5,menu_accept`, from the Combat 01 save, skips Rheda's turn, walks Brenna to a rat and attacks it. `--press wait:4,menu_details:1.5,wait:1.5,menu_accept,wait:6` from the same save holds C-down on Rheda's turn, lets go on Endurance and casts it at her and Alaron; `--press wait:4,menu_details:1.4+,menu_down,menu_down,wait:1.5,menu_accept,wait:12` casts Weakness at a rat.
- **Menus and scenes:** `--menu 1` opens the pause menu, `--menu system`, `party` or `world` opens it on that tab, and `--menu loot` opens an empty loot window. `--cutscene aidyn:dialogue/0x0365` plays a cut-scene at once (the goblin ambush). `--battle aidyn:voxel/0x0F6B/3[,<arena>][,ambush]` starts a battle with that monster party where the player stands, in the arena given or the one the terrain chooses (two rats; `--battle aidyn:voxel/0x0F6B/3,1` in arena 1; `--battle aidyn:voxel/0x1097/2,3,ambush` with a save made from the Combat 01 checkpoint is the user's ten rats). In a battle, `--radar box` puts the battle screen's radar in the log box rather than the screen's corner (F9 swaps it). A battle's end can be scripted the same way: `--press wait:3,battle_rout,menu_tab_previous,wait:4` from the Combat 01 save comes to the post-battle window, then `menu_back` to the loot. The launch options run once a run: a load or a game over that starts the scene again doesn't play them again. `--cinematic <n>` plays a cinematic, 0 to 3, without the menu after, and what follows it in the story (the opening's script, Roog's True Name, the ending's credits and the game's end); `--cinematic 0,5,300` starts at shot 5's time 300, and `--cinematic 0,5,300,hold` stands still there.
- **Weather:** `--weather rain|snow|fog|clear[,intensity[,fog]]` sets it at once, already set in and with the rain sky up (rain and snow default to 0.5 with fog 0.2 and a storm as hard as the rain; fog to 0.5). Rain and snow take a few seconds to fall into view from a standing start, so hold something harmless, such as `--hold walk,8`, before the screenshot.
- **Settings for the run:** `--ui-lines <n>`, `--sky original|projected`, `--pace`, `--draw-distance <scale>`, `--detail nearest`, `--precipitation <scale>` (up to 8) `--lights off` (bodies and actors lit by the sky alone, as a baseline: [rendering](rendering.md#point-lights)) `--spell-auras each` (the options' Spell auras for the run) and `--spell-fx off` (no spells' visuals, sky dimming or body tints, as a baseline: [combat](combat.md#what-spells-look-like)).
- **Screenshots.** The path must be absolute, or Godot saves it relative to the project. `--ui-screenshot <png>` saves trek's UI screen with it, in its own pixels and with its alpha, for measuring the HUD and the battle screen against the user's captures. `--screenshot <png> --frames <n>` saves the next n frames as they're rendered, in real time, as `<png stem>-000.png` on, kept in memory until the last: for what only shows above 60 fps, between ticks. To see something tick by tick, Godot's movie writer saves every frame at a fixed 60 a second: `tools/godot.ps1 --path src/Aidyn.Game --write-movie <absolute dir>/f.png --quit-after <frames> -- --menu party`. It records at the project's window size, 1280 × 960; `--resolution` doesn't change it, and `--ui-lines 960` puts a window pixel to each of the UI screen's.
- **GUI runs need a timeout:** launch with a process you can kill, not blocking on it.
