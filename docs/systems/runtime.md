# The runtime (M1, M2)

`src/Aidyn.Game` is the Godot game. It references `Aidyn.Plugins` and `Aidyn.Core`, and not `Aidyn.Rom`, so the build enforces ADR 0001. Import first (see [importer](importer.md)), then run it with `tools/godot.ps1 --path src/Aidyn.Game` (build with `dotnet build` first). It reads the plugins from the data folder, so set `AIDYN_DATA` for both if the import went elsewhere.

- **What it does so far.** It streams the 3×3 chunks around the player: each chunk's scene and see-through scene, the models its scene objects place (Expansion Pak set; each level of detail faded in and out by the player's distance, as the original does), actors running their Borg7 state machines (the core's `Aidyn.Core.Actors.ActorStateMachine`, played on the scene by `ActorAnimator`, which the editor shares), and the collision. Alaron starts where a new game does, on the ground. The core's ports of the original's player movement and camera ([movement](movement.md), [cameras](cameras.md)) move him and follow him.
- **The tick.** Trek is the core's `TrekLoop` (`Aidyn.Core.Trek`), stepped once per 60 Hz tick in `_PhysicsProcess`: the fades into a door or a cut-scene, the clock, the map objects the player touches, the camera, the player, the wanderers and a conversation, in the original's order. The runtime draws what it leaves, and carries out what it asks (`ITrekHost`): the sky follows the player's chunk, conversations show in the box, and the loot window opens. The core's `ChunkStreamer` (`Aidyn.Core.Chunks`) loads the 3×3's collision and map objects, and `ChunkStreamerView` draws them. Godot's physics interpolation smooths the display. Where the original acts once per frame, or loops toward a target fixed for the frame, the port matches what it does at an idealized 30 fps, a `delta` of 2 (`Aidyn.Core.Cadence`). The user chose this. The console runs at 15–25 fps, and the camera feels snappier on an overclocked emulator.
- **Bodies' shadows** are the original's, from the core's `BodyShadow`, drawn as a decal that bodies' visual layer (2) is left out of: see [movement](movement.md#shadows).
- **The game** is the core's `GameState` ([saves](saves.md)): a new game's, or a save's with `--save <path>`, which starts where the save stands, with its party, flags, clock and what map objects remember. Each tick in trek passes the game's time, and every tick, in the menu too, counts the time played, as the original's frame loop does (`World::AddPlayTime`). F8 saves the game, with where the player stands, to `quick.json` in the saves folder.
- **Flags.** It runs the game's flags ([flags](flags.md)). Each tick writes the clock into them, and entering a chunk writes its terrain. A scene object is placed only if its flag gate holds when its chunk loads, as the original decides it then (`VoxelObjects_Activate`) and not every frame. The original decides again when it reloads the map, after the pause menu, a shop, training, a battle, a cut-scene or a teleport (`loadGameBorgScenes`), but not after a conversation in trek, which leaves the map loaded (checked in 1.0's code); the runtime does after the pause menu (`ChunkStreamerView.Regate`), building again each loaded chunk whose scene objects come or go. After a cut-scene, a backdrop or the help menu it loads the map set's chunks and their wanderers again where the player stands (`TrekLoop.Resume`), as the original's trek starts again after one, so the wanderers start at their home nodes and a recruit whose party is gated off is gone ([wanderers](wanderers.md#joining)). So at a new game Oriana's path lights (scene `0x1910`) are hidden, and they come with flag 50, which her conversation sets.
- **The HUD** ([ui](ui.md#the-trek-hud)): the sundial, the party's portraits, and the compass or the minimap, on trek's UI screen under its popups, each kept to its corner. B raises or lowers the portraits, the minimap growing and shrinking with them, and moving lowers them. A conversation, the pause menu and cut-scenes hide it. Which minimap pieces have been seen is saved.
- **The pause menu.** Start opens it over the world, and stops trek: the player, the camera, the clock and the scenery's animations. The world and the HUD freeze and darken, drawn on under a black layer rather than copied, so resizing the window or changing the Menu size redraws them, and the 3D scroll unrolls with the tabs between its rollers, and rolls up again as it closes ([ui](ui.md#opening-and-closing)). It's the System, Party and World tabs side by side, drawn by `SystemTabView`, `PartyTabView` and `WorldTabView` on a `UiScreen` ([ui](ui.md#the-pause-menu)), 240 lines tall by default; the Menu size setting or F7 changes it to 360 and 480, which draws the menu smaller, with more of the tabs either side. Closing it gates the map objects again. The System tab saves and loads ([ui](ui.md#saving-and-loading)): saving notes where the player stands, as F8 does, and writes the world as the menu opened over it beside the save; loading reloads the Godot scene from the save, keeping the plugins loaded, and the command line's options but `--screenshot` don't apply again.
- **Settings** ([ADR 0013](../adr/0013-settings-in-the-options-menu.md)). The options menu's graphics page changes `Settings`, which `Game.ApplySettings` carries out at once: the window's mode, v-sync, MSAA, the 3D view's resolution (Godot's 3D scaling, bilinear, by the window's height), the draw distance, the sky's placement, the pace, and both UI screens' height and filter. They're kept in `settings.json` in the data folder whenever one changes. F5, F6 and F7 change them too. `--ui-lines`, `--sky`, `--pace`, `--draw-distance` and `--detail` override them for the run.
- **Audio** ([audio](audio.md)). The core's `GameAudio` mixes the original's sequencer, which `AudioOutput` plays on a thread of its own through a Godot generator stream. Trek asks for each chunk's music, plays the loaded chunks' sound objects and bodies' footsteps and voices, and the pause menu, map objects and scripts play one-shots. The volumes are the options menu's. F3's overlay shows the music and how many sounds play.
- **Collision** is the collision assets in the core's `CollisionWorld`, which the chunk streamer fills and empties. Godot physics isn't used. Toggles change when a trigger opens the way, until their chunk loads again.
- **Map objects** ([map-objects](map-objects.md)). Each loaded chunk's objects are activated by the flags as it loads (`LoadedMap`), and only the chunk the player is in counts. A uses what he reaches; walking into tangible objects and onto trigger triangles works each tick; and the popups they show, on a UI screen of their own over the world, take the player's input until closed and stop the clock, but leave the world running, as the original's do ([map-objects](map-objects.md#popups)). A door fades out, black for 3 frames, and in, loading another map set if it leads to one, and sets him down at its arrival point facing its way. The camera goes with him, in the interiors' mode or the outdoor one by where he arrives, and camera objects take it over ([cameras](cameras.md)); the overlay (F3) shows the mode and the camera object holding it. `--camera outdoor|interior` starts in either; by default the start chooses as a door into its chunk would. Crossing into another chunk ages what map objects remember, as the original's own teleport between chunks does. A container's items open the loot window, the pause menu's barter state ([ui](ui.md#the-loot-window)). A sign shows in the conversation box, and a backdrop its page over the world, which stops under it ([ui](ui.md#books-and-the-help-menu)). Each loaded chunk's monster parties stand in the world as bodies that make for their home node, push the player and each other, and fade with distance from the camera's aim ([wanderers](wanderers.md)); the overlay counts them.
- **Cut-scenes** ([scripts](scripts.md#cut-scenes)). A dialogue object or a talker whose script is a cut-scene fades trek out, frees its chunks, wanderers and the player's body (`TrekLoop.BeginCutScene`), and loads the script's stage, its staging chunk's 3×3 without wanderers, in a chunk streamer of its own. The core's `CutScene` plays there: its actors drawn by `CutSceneActorView`, with what they hold on their scene's locator 0; its camera at a far plane of 125; its lines in the conversation box, which A moves on when the cut-scene takes presses. The clock stops. When it's over, trek is built again where the player stood, the camera where it was, aimed at him, and the screen fades in. `--cutscene aidyn:dialogue/0x0365` plays one at once (the goblin ambush), as what starts it would; F3's overlay says what it waits for.
- **Drawing.** Scenes use the N64 material, and the sky of the player's chunk sets the scenery's tint and fog by the time of day, which starts at a new game's noon and runs at the original's pace. The follow camera stops at the original's far plane, 180. Billboards turn to the camera each frame, and water and fire scroll once a frame, at 20 fps, by the scenes the original would draw. See [rendering](rendering.md).
- **The weather** ([weather](weather.md#in-the-port)): its rules run and write the rain flags, its fog moves the scenery's fog (but not in cut-scenes) and the sky's, its wind blows the clouds, rain and snow bring the rain sky, lightning flashes with its thunder, and rain plays its loop, in trek and in cut-scenes. The rain and snow themselves aren't drawn yet.
- **Stand-ins** still to replace: Alaron's head follows his neck, where the game keeps it upright and turns it toward nearby objects.
- **Controls.**
  - WASD or the left stick moves. The stick walks under 80% tilt and runs above. On a keyboard, Shift walks. Ctrl or the left trigger sneaks (Z on the N64).
  - The original orbits the camera with R and the stick, and he can't move while it does. Here that's the gamepad's right shoulder with the left stick, dragging with the right mouse button, the right stick, or Q and E. Only the shoulder holds him still; the right stick, Q, E and the mouse turn the camera while he keeps walking (a port addition, [cameras](cameras.md)).
  - F toggles a free camera (Space and C for up and down, Shift for faster). F5 toggles the pace.
  - Debug: holding either thumbstick down (L3 or R3) raises the tick rate from 60 to 180 a second, as far as the machine keeps up, so the whole game runs 3× faster (`Game.SetTickRate`). The pause menu stays at 60.
  - Space or the bottom face button is A: it uses the map object the player reaches, and closes or chooses in a popup, whose cursor the movement keys move; Backspace or B closes it. With no popup, Backspace or B raises or lowers the party's portraits.
  - Enter, Escape or Start opens and closes the pause menu. In it, Z and X or the triggers change the tab (Z or L, and R), Q and E or the shoulders change the member (C-left and C-right), the movement keys, arrows, left stick or D-pad change the list and move the cursor, Tab or the top face button shows the details while held (C-down), Space or the bottom face button is A, and Backspace or B closes a popup, goes back from a screen, or closes the menu. In the name entry the keyboard types. Skill checks and potions in the menu draw from the world's random stream. Hovering a row or the wheel moves the cursor.
  - F3 toggles the overlay. It shows the chunk, the position in chunk coordinates, the surface underfoot, his speed over the last second, the animation request and the pace, and the music and sounds playing.
- **For scripts:** `-- --chunk I08 --at 16,62 --yaw 0 --pitch -15 --distance 5 --hold move_forward,4 --screenshot <absolute path>.png` starts somewhere, holds an input, saves the window and quits.
  - `--hold` takes several actions joined with `+`, as in `move_forward+walk,4`.
  - `--save <path>` starts from a save, where it stands unless `--chunk` or `--at` say otherwise. `--menu 1` opens the pause menu, `--menu system`, `party` or `world` opens it on that tab and `--menu loot` opens an empty loot window, and `--ui-lines <n>` sets the UI screen's height. `--press pause,menu_member_left,move_right` presses actions in turn, a fifth of a second apart, as input events, which `--hold` doesn't make; the screenshot waits for them.
  - The screenshot path must be absolute, or Godot saves it relative to the project.
  - To see something frame by frame, such as the pause menu opening, Godot's movie writer saves every frame at a fixed 60 a second, a physics tick each: `tools/godot.ps1 --path src/Aidyn.Game --write-movie <absolute dir>/f.png --quit-after <frames> -- --menu party`. It records at the project's window size, 1280 × 960; `--resolution` doesn't change it, and `--ui-lines 960` puts a pixel of the window to each of the UI screen's, which shows the most beside the frame.
  - `--fly x,y,z` starts with the free camera, which scene objects' distances are then measured from. `--time <hours>` sets the time of day, `--weather rain|snow|fog|clear[,intensity[,fog]]` sets the weather at once, already set in and with the rain sky up (rain and snow default to 0.5 with fog 0.2, and a storm as hard as the rain, fog to 0.5); a new game starts in the opening cut-scene, whose fog ignores the weather, so add `--flags 5,11` to see trek, and `--flags 5,50` sets plain flags before anything loads. The run gives up after a minute.
  - `--draw-distance <scale>` draws scene objects that many times as far, and `--detail nearest` only their nearest level of detail (see [rendering](rendering.md#draw-distance)).
  - Gwernia's open ground is around (30–80, 30–80) in M07, for example `--chunk M07 --at 40,55`. Elsewhere in M07 there's no ground, so he falls and is put back again and again.
- **The spawn point** is tight. A rock stands in a hole in the terrain in front of it, with collision walls and no top, and a birch's collision box is beside it. The original drops the player from a height of 10; the runtime puts him on the highest ground below the spawn instead.
