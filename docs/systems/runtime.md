# The runtime (M1, M2)

`src/Aidyn.Game` is the Godot game. It references `Aidyn.Plugins` and `Aidyn.Core`, and not `Aidyn.Rom`, so the build enforces ADR 0001. Import first ([importer](importer.md)), build with `dotnet build`, then run it with `tools/godot.ps1 --path src/Aidyn.Game`. It reads the plugins from the data folder, so set `AIDYN_DATA` for both if the import went elsewhere.

The runtime keeps its loops thin (ADR 0012): the core steps the game and the runtime draws what it leaves and carries out what it asks. Each system's doc says how its part is drawn; this one says how they fit together, and holds the controls and the command line.

## How a tick runs

- **Trek** is the core's `TrekLoop` (`Aidyn.Core.Trek`), stepped once per 60 Hz tick in `_PhysicsProcess`: the fades into a door or a cut-scene, the clock, the map objects the player touches, the camera, the player, the wanderers and a conversation, in the original's order. The runtime carries out what it asks through `ITrekHost`: the sky follows the player's chunk, conversations show in the box, and the loot window, shops and training open. Godot's physics interpolation smooths the display between ticks.
- **The zone engine** (`ZoneEngine`, `Aidyn.Core.Chunks`; the original's `InitZoneEngine` and `HandleZoneEngineFrame`) is the world a mode plays in, and what trek and cut-scenes share: the chunk streamer with its collision and drawn surfaces, the chunks' sounds and light map objects as each loads and goes, the music request, the weather's step, rain and sounds, and bodies' footsteps heard from its camera. The runtime makes trek's and hands it to `TrekLoop`, which loads its wanderers with each chunk (`ZoneEngine.Loaded`). A cut-scene's stage is another made from it (`ZoneEngine.Stage`), with its own chunks, lights and rain over the same sounds and weather, and `CutScene.Tick` steps it; combat's arena, with M3, is a stage the same way. The runtime starts each frame of lights through it (`BeginLightFrame`) and draws what it holds.
- **Cadence** ([ADR 0017](../adr/0017-the-core-steps-every-tick.md)). Where the original acts once per frame, or loops toward a target fixed for the frame, the port matches what it does at an idealized 30 fps, a `delta` of 2 (`Aidyn.Core.Cadence`), the user's choice: the console runs at 15–25 fps. Texture scrolling and rain spawn at 20 fps ([rendering](rendering.md#scrolling-textures), [weather](weather.md#rain-and-snow)), and the pause menu at 60 ([ui](ui.md#opening-and-closing)).
- **The game** is the core's `GameState` ([saves](saves.md)): a new game's, or a save's with `--save <path>`. Each tick in trek passes the game's time, and every tick, in the menu too, `GameState.CountTick` counts the time played (`World::AddPlayTime`) and the game's ticker, the original's frame counter that camera objects and cut-scenes' cameras keep their place by ([cameras](cameras.md#camera-objects-processvoxelcamera)).
- **Chunks.** The core's `ChunkStreamer` (`Aidyn.Core.Chunks`) loads the 3×3 chunks' collision (`CollisionWorld`; Godot physics isn't used) and map objects, and `ChunkStreamerView` draws their scenes, scene objects and actors ([rendering](rendering.md)). Entering a chunk writes its terrain into the flags ([flags](flags.md)).
- **Gating.** A scene object is placed only if its flag gate holds when its chunk loads (`VoxelObjects_Activate`), not every frame. The original decides again when it reloads the map, after the pause menu, a shop, training, a battle, a cut-scene or a teleport (`loadGameBorgScenes`), but not after a conversation in trek. The runtime regates after the pause menu (`ChunkStreamerView.Regate`), building again each loaded chunk whose scene objects come or go. After a cut-scene, a backdrop or the help menu it reloads the map set's chunks and their wanderers where the player stands (`TrekLoop.Resume`), as the original's trek starts again ([wanderers](wanderers.md#joining)).
- **Modes over trek.** The pause menu stops trek and draws over the world ([ui](ui.md#the-pause-menu)); a map object's popup stops the clock and takes input but leaves the world running ([map-objects](map-objects.md#popups)); a cut-scene frees trek's world and plays on a stage of its own, which ends before trek's is loaded again ([scripts](scripts.md#cut-scenes)); a backdrop pauses the game under its page ([ui](ui.md#books-and-the-help-menu)); the Theater's cinematics play over everything ([cinematics](cinematics.md)). After a cut-scene, trek is built again where the player stood and fades in.
- **Settings** ([ADR 0013](../adr/0013-settings-in-the-options-menu.md), [ui](ui.md#options)): `Game.ApplySettings` carries out the options menu's graphics page at once, and they're kept in `settings.json` in the data folder.
- **Audio** ([audio](audio.md)): the core's `GameAudio` mixes the original's sequencer, which `AudioOutput` plays on its own thread through a Godot generator stream.
- **Saving.** F8 saves the game, with where the player stands, to `quick.json` in the saves folder. Loading from the System tab reloads the Godot scene from the save, keeping the plugins loaded; the command line's options but `--screenshot` don't apply again.

## Stand-ins

- Alaron's head follows his neck, where the game keeps it upright and turns it toward nearby objects.
- **The spawn point.** A rock stands in a hole in the terrain in front of it, with collision walls and no top. The original drops the player from a height of 10; the runtime puts him on the highest ground below the spawn instead.

## Controls

- **Moving.** WASD or the left stick. The stick walks under 80% tilt and runs above; on a keyboard, Shift walks. Ctrl or the left trigger sneaks (Z on the N64).
- **The camera.** The original orbits it with R and the stick, and Alaron can't move while it does. Here that's the gamepad's right shoulder with the left stick, the right mouse button dragged, the right stick, or Q and E. Only the shoulder holds him still; the others turn the camera while he walks (a port addition, [cameras](cameras.md)).
- **A** is Space or the bottom face button: it uses the map object the player reaches, and closes or chooses in a popup. **B** is Backspace or the right face button: it closes a popup, or with none raises or lowers the party's portraits. **Start** is Enter, Escape or Start.
- **In the pause menu:** Z and X or the triggers change the tab (Z or L, and R); Q and E or the shoulders change the member (C-left and C-right, C-left showing the next); the movement keys, arrows, left stick or D-pad change the list and move the cursor; Tab or the top face button shows the details while held (C-down); R or the left face button is C-up in a shop. In the name entry the keyboard types. Hovering a row or the wheel moves the cursor.
- **F-keys.** F toggles a free camera (Space and C for up and down, Shift for faster). F3 toggles the overlay: the chunk, the position in chunk coordinates, the surface underfoot, the speed, the animation request, the pace, the camera's mode and the object holding it, the music and sounds playing, the particles in use, the rain's drops and splashes, and what a cut-scene waits for. F5 toggles the pace, F6 the sky's placement and F7 the UI screen's height.
- **Debug:** holding either thumbstick down (L3 or R3) raises the tick rate from 60 to 180 a second, as far as the machine keeps up (`Game.SetTickRate`). The pause menu stays at 60.

## The command line

After `--`: `tools/godot.ps1 --path src/Aidyn.Game -- --chunk I08 --at 16,62 --yaw 0 --pitch -15 --distance 5 --hold move_forward,4 --screenshot <absolute path>.png` starts somewhere, holds an input, saves the window and quits. The run gives up after a minute. `LaunchOptions` reads the options (on top of `CommandLine`, the parser and screenshot run the editor shares), `Game.Start` builds the game in named steps from them, and `Game.Debug.cs` carries out the ones that drive it (`RunLaunchActions`: `--menu`, `--fly`, `--hold`, `--press`, `--cutscene`, `--cinematic`, `--screenshot`) and draws the F3 overlay.

- **Where:** `--chunk`, `--at x,z`, `--yaw`, `--pitch`, `--distance`; `--fly x,y,z` starts with the free camera, which scene objects' distances are then measured from; `--camera outdoor|interior` starts in either mode (by default the start chooses as a door into its chunk would). Gwernia's open ground is around (30–80, 30–80) in M07, for example `--chunk M07 --at 40,55`; elsewhere in M07 there's no ground, and he falls and is put back again and again.
- **The game:** `--save <path>` starts from a save, where it stands unless `--chunk` or `--at` say otherwise. `--flags 5,50` sets plain flags before anything loads. A new game starts in the opening cut-scene, so add `--flags 5,11` to see trek. `--time <hours>` sets the time of day.
- **Input:** `--hold` holds actions for so many seconds, several joined with `+`, as in `move_forward+walk,4`. `--press pause,menu_member_left,move_right` presses actions in turn, a fifth of a second apart, as input events, which `--hold` doesn't make; the screenshot waits for them.
- **Menus and scenes:** `--menu 1` opens the pause menu, `--menu system`, `party` or `world` opens it on that tab, and `--menu loot` opens an empty loot window. `--cutscene aidyn:dialogue/0x0365` plays a cut-scene at once (the goblin ambush). `--cinematic <n>` plays a cinematic, 0 to 3, without the menu after; `--cinematic 0,5,300` starts at shot 5's time 300, and `--cinematic 0,5,300,hold` stands still there.
- **Weather:** `--weather rain|snow|fog|clear[,intensity[,fog]]` sets it at once, already set in and with the rain sky up (rain and snow default to 0.5 with fog 0.2 and a storm as hard as the rain; fog to 0.5). Rain and snow take a few seconds to fall into view from a standing start, so hold something harmless, such as `--hold walk,8`, before the screenshot.
- **Settings for the run:** `--ui-lines <n>`, `--sky original|projected`, `--pace`, `--draw-distance <scale>`, `--detail nearest`, `--precipitation <scale>` (up to 8) and `--lights off` (bodies and actors lit by the sky alone, as a baseline: [rendering](rendering.md#point-lights)).
- **Screenshots.** The path must be absolute, or Godot saves it relative to the project. `--screenshot <png> --frames <n>` saves the next n frames as they're rendered, in real time, as `<png stem>-000.png` on, kept in memory until the last: for what only shows above 60 fps, between ticks. To see something tick by tick, Godot's movie writer saves every frame at a fixed 60 a second: `tools/godot.ps1 --path src/Aidyn.Game --write-movie <absolute dir>/f.png --quit-after <frames> -- --menu party`. It records at the project's window size, 1280 × 960; `--resolution` doesn't change it, and `--ui-lines 960` puts a window pixel to each of the UI screen's.
- **GUI runs need a timeout:** launch with a process you can kill, not blocking on it.
