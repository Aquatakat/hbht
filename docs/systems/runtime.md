# The runtime (M1)

`src/Aidyn.Game` is the Godot game. It references `Aidyn.Plugins` and `Aidyn.Core`, and not `Aidyn.Rom`, so the build enforces ADR 0001. Import first (see [importer](importer.md)), then run it with `tools/godot.ps1 --path src/Aidyn.Game` (build with `dotnet build` first). It reads the plugins from the data folder, so set `AIDYN_DATA` for both if the import went elsewhere.

- **What it does so far.** It streams the 3×3 chunks around the player: each chunk's scene and see-through scene, the models its scene objects place (Expansion Pak set; each level of detail faded in and out by the player's distance, as the original does), actors running their Borg7 state machines (the core's `Aidyn.Core.Actors.ActorStateMachine`, played on the scene by `ActorAnimator`, which the editor shares), and the collision. Alaron starts where a new game does, on the ground. The core's ports of the original's player movement and camera ([movement](movement.md), [cameras](cameras.md)) move him and follow him.
- **The tick.** The player and camera are stepped once per 60 Hz tick in `_PhysicsProcess`, and Godot's physics interpolation smooths the display. Where the original acts once per frame, or loops toward a target fixed for the frame, the port matches what it does at an idealized 30 fps, a `delta` of 2 (`Aidyn.Core.Cadence`). The user chose this. The console runs at 15–25 fps, and the camera feels snappier on an overclocked emulator.
- **Alaron's shadow** is the original's (`Aidyn.Core.Movement.BodyShadow`, from `Actor::CalculateShadow`, `renderPlayerShadows` and `PlaneObj_Render`, checked against the ROM).
  - Each tick, after he and the camera move, it sweeps a sphere of radius 0.5 from his centre down 3, skipping see-through triangles. It lies where that hits, directly below him, turned to the triangle's normal.
  - **Unlike the original, it skips walls.** The original takes the first triangle its collision grid lists that the sweep touches. Against a leaning wall that's often the wall, and the shadow stands up on it. The user has seen this in the original, but rarely. Our grid's order differs from the original's, and so do the spots where it happens. The port takes the nearest triangle no steeper than 60°, and treats walls as no ground. With Alaron settled by the physics at every half unit of I08, the original's rule tilts the shadow past 45° at 263 of 23,039 spots and the port's at 12. At 28, nothing under 60° is in reach, so it keeps its last placement.
  - It's a square whose corners are twice his radius from its middle, of the shadow texture (`aidyn:texture/0x0003`, `BORG1_PlayerShadow`) in black, with the texture's intensity as alpha, times 80 of 255. It shrinks and fades as he rises. With no ground in reach, it keeps its last height and follows him in X and Z.
  - Shadows are drawn within 20 of the camera's aim in trek and 128 in combat. Over the last 2 units, the fade starts from 255 rather than 128, so a shadow darkens before it goes. Other bodies' shadows show only within 10 of the aim, and come with the bodies.
  - The original draws a flat square without a depth test, before the bodies and the centre chunk's objects, so it never sinks into the ground. The runtime projects the same square as a decal, which bodies' visual layer (2) is left out of. It looks the same, but can't sink where the visible ground and the collision differ. The decal's box reaches 0.75 above and below the ground and fades toward its ends, so where the ground bends there's no hard edge. A wall or tree right beside him still takes a little, as the original's square would.
  - Its alpha is corrected for blending in linear light (see [rendering](rendering.md)).
- **Flags.** It runs the core's flag network from a new game ([flags](flags.md)). Each tick writes the clock into its flags, and entering a chunk writes its terrain. A scene object is placed only if its flag gate holds when its chunk loads, as the original decides it then (`VoxelObjects_Activate`) and not every frame. The original also decides again when it reloads the map after a conversation, the pause menu or a teleport, which the runtime can't do yet. So at a new game Oriana's path lights (scene `0x1910`) are hidden, and they come with flag 50, which her conversation sets.
- **Collision** is the collision assets in the core's `CollisionWorld`, which the chunk streamer fills and empties. Godot physics isn't used. Toggles stay as stored until the flag engine can change them.
- **Drawing.** Scenes use the N64 material, and the sky of the player's chunk sets the scenery's tint and fog by the time of day, which starts at a new game's noon and runs at the original's pace. The follow camera stops at the original's far plane, 180. Billboards turn to the camera each frame, and water and fire scroll once a frame, at 20 fps, by the scenes the original would draw. See [rendering](rendering.md).
- **Stand-ins** still to replace: the flat sky colour, and the fixed light on bodies. Nothing sets flags in play yet, and the weather isn't ported, so the rain flags stay clear. Alaron's head follows his neck, but the game keeps it upright and turns it toward nearby objects.
- **Controls.**
  - WASD or the left stick moves. The stick walks under 80% tilt and runs above. On a keyboard, Shift walks. Ctrl or the left trigger sneaks (Z on the N64).
  - The original orbits the camera with R and the stick, and he can't move while it does. Here that's the gamepad's right shoulder with the left stick, dragging with the right mouse button, the right stick, or Q and E.
  - F toggles a free camera (Space and C for up and down, Shift for faster). F5 toggles the pace.
  - F3 toggles the overlay. It shows the chunk, the position in chunk coordinates, the surface underfoot, his speed over the last second, the animation request and the pace.
- **For scripts:** `-- --chunk I08 --at 16,62 --yaw 0 --pitch -15 --distance 5 --hold move_forward,4 --screenshot <absolute path>.png` starts somewhere, holds an input, saves the window and quits.
  - `--hold` takes several actions joined with `+`, as in `move_forward+walk,4`.
  - The screenshot path must be absolute, or Godot saves it relative to the project.
  - `--fly x,y,z` starts with the free camera, which scene objects' distances are then measured from. `--time <hours>` sets the time of day, and `--flags 5,50` sets plain flags before anything loads. The run gives up after a minute.
  - `--draw-distance <scale>` draws scene objects that many times as far, and `--detail nearest` only their nearest level of detail (see [rendering](rendering.md#draw-distance)).
  - Gwernia's open ground is around (30–80, 30–80) in M07, for example `--chunk M07 --at 40,55`. Elsewhere in M07 there's no ground, so he falls and is put back again and again.
- **The spawn point** is tight. A rock stands in a hole in the terrain in front of it, with collision walls and no top, and a birch's collision box is beside it. The original drops the player from a height of 10; the runtime puts him on the highest ground below the spawn instead.
