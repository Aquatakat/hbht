# The runtime (M1–M4)

`src/Aidyn.Game` is the Godot game. It references `Aidyn.Plugins`, `Aidyn.Core` and `Aidyn.Views`, and not `Aidyn.Rom`, so the build enforces ADR 0001. It draws with what it shares with the editor in `Aidyn.Views` (ADR 0022); the rest of its views, and `Game`, which `Main.tscn` attaches by path, are its own. Import first ([importer](importer.md)), build with `dotnet build`, then run it with `tools/godot.ps1 --path src/Aidyn.Game`. It reads the plugins from the data folder, so set `AIDYN_DATA` for both if the import went elsewhere. It refuses a base plugin that's missing or was imported by another importer (`BasePlugin.Diagnose`), with the words the CLI and the editor use.

The runtime keeps its loops thin (ADR 0012): the core's `GameSession` plays the game, and the runtime draws what each tick leaves and carries out what it asks. Each system's doc says how its part is drawn; this one says how they fit together, and holds the controls and the command line.

## How a tick runs

`GameSession.Start` builds the game where a `GameStart` says (a save's place, or a new game's arrival with the command line's overrides, `Game.StartingPlace`), and the runtime builds its player and camera views from `TrekLoop.Player` and `Camera`; the tests start their sessions the same way. Then `Game._PhysicsProcess` calls `GameSession.Tick` once per 60 Hz tick, which in order:

1. hands on the settings if they changed (`GameSession.Apply`), before anything steps ([ADR 0023](../adr/0023-the-core-is-handed-its-settings-each-tick.md));
2. counts the tick, in every mode: the time played and the game's ticker (`GameState.CountTick`, [saves](saves.md), [cameras](cameras.md#camera-objects-processvoxelcamera));
3. repeats the held directions (`StepStick`, [ui](ui.md#opening-and-closing));
4. steps the pause menu by the scroll actor's clock (`ScrollClock`); if it has closed, the session carries out its exit, gates the map again, and the tick returns `SessionStep.MenuClosed` without stepping a mode ([ui](ui.md#opening-and-closing));
5. steps the mode that runs (`GameSession.Mode`, by precedence a cinematic, the credits, the pause menu, a backdrop's page, a map object's popup, a conversation, a cut-scene, a battle, else trek): trek's `TrekLoop` ([map objects](map-objects.md), [wanderers](wanderers.md)), a cut-scene's `CutScene.Tick` ([scripts](scripts.md#cut-scenes)), a battle's `Battle.Tick` ([combat](combat.md#the-port)), a cinematic or the credits ([cinematics](cinematics.md), [ui](ui.md#the-credits));
6. fades a backdrop's page (`Trek.StepBackdrop`, [ui](ui.md#books-and-the-help-menu)) and twinkles the stars (`Sky.StepTwinkle`, [rendering](rendering.md#the-skys-objects)).

The tick returns a `SessionStep`, and the runtime draws the mode it names, holding the world's views still by `GameSession.Mode` rather than working it out. When the step says trek has faded out or a cut-scene or battle is over, the runtime calls `BeginCutScene`, `EndCutScene`, `BeginBattle` or `EndBattle`, and draws around them. Everything else the core asks comes through its hosts, which `Game` is:

- `ITransitionHost.Transition`: every ending of a mode, the menu or a script, in order, decided in the core (`Transition`; a script's in `Transition.AfterScript`, [scripts](scripts.md)).
- `ITrekHost`: conversations shown in the box, the loot window, chunks gated again (`Regated`).
- `ICombatHost`: a battle's one-offs as cues ([combat](combat.md#what-a-battle-tells-its-host)).
- `IPauseMenuHost`: the menu's cues. `PauseMenuView` only binds the tabs' views to each opening's models and lays them out ([ui](ui.md#the-pause-menu)).

A press is the session's too: the runtime turns Godot's event into a `MenuAction` (`MenuStick`), and `GameSession.Press` sends it to the mode that runs and says what it came to (`PressOutcome`, [ui](ui.md#opening-and-closing)). `Game._Process` draws between ticks, and Godot's physics interpolation smooths bodies.

## What it draws

- **The world** a mode plays in is a `ZoneEngine` (the original's `InitZoneEngine` and `HandleZoneEngineFrame`): the chunks with their collision, sounds and lights, the music, the weather and the sky. Trek has one, a cut-scene's stage is another made from it (`ZoneEngine.Stage`, [scripts](scripts.md#the-world-it-plays-in)), and a battle's arena is a stage on the Battle arenas. `ChunkStreamerView` draws its chunks' scenes, scene objects and actors ([rendering](rendering.md)); `SkyView` pushes its sky to the shaders, with `SkyBackdropView` and `LensFlareView` ([rendering](rendering.md#the-skys-colours)); each frame's lights start with `ZoneEngine.BeginLightFrame` ([rendering](rendering.md#point-lights)).
- **Bodies.** The player is `Game`'s (`Player`); every other body is a `BodyView`: `WandererView`, `CutSceneActorView` (with the item in its hand) and `CombatantView` ([combat](combat.md#the-port)). A `BodyView` holds the actor at its scale, faded and lit, with its shadow (`ShadowDecal`, [movement](movement.md#shadows)); the core's `ActorMotion` times it and `ActorAnimator` poses it ([actors](../formats/actors.md)).
- **Screens.** `HudView` and the battle screen's views, `PauseMenuView`, `TrekPopupView`, `BackdropLayer`, `CinematicLayer` and `CreditsLayer` draw what the core's models hold ([ui](ui.md), [cinematics](cinematics.md)).
- **Audio.** `AudioOutput` plays the core's `GameAudio` on its own thread through a Godot generator stream ([audio](audio.md)).
- **Settings** ([ADR 0013](../adr/0013-settings-in-the-options-menu.md), [ui](ui.md#options)) are kept in `settings.json`, with the command line's lying over them (`LaunchSettings`). `Game.ApplySettings` makes the session's `SessionSettings` again as they change, and carries out what's only drawn (the window, v-sync, anti-aliasing, the 3D resolution, draw distance, model detail, the UI's lines and filter).
- **Saving.** F8 saves to `quick.json` in the saves folder, with where the player stands (`GameSession.NotePlace`). Loading from the System tab reloads the Godot scene from the save, keeping the plugins loaded ([saves](saves.md), [ui](ui.md#saving-and-loading)).

## Traps

- **Cadence** ([ADR 0017](../adr/0017-the-core-steps-every-tick.md)): where the original acts once a frame, the port matches an idealized 30 fps, a `delta` of 2 (`Cadence`), the user's choice; the console runs at 15–25 fps. Code that acts once a frame counts its ticks with `FrameGate`.
- **Nothing in the core reads `Settings`** but the options menu, which changes them. The rules play by the `SessionSettings` each tick hands in; headless tests hand `SessionSettings.Original`.
- **Trek's chunks stream only while its world is built** (`GameSession.StreamsTrek`). `Game._Process` moves the 3×3 with the player or the free camera, under the pause menu too, but not under a stage, an arena or the post-battle windows, where loading trek's chunks would play their sounds.
- **A loaded chunk is known by its `ChunkKey`**: map set, row and column. Collision, drawn surfaces, map objects (`LoadedMap.Key`), trigger triangles, an arena's grid and a stage's rain all name it, so two map sets' I08 are never taken for each other.
- **Gating.** A scene object is placed only if its flag gate holds as its chunk loads (`VoxelObjects_Activate`), not every frame. The original decides again whenever it reloads the map (`loadGameBorgScenes`): after the pause menu, a shop, training, a battle, a cut-scene or a teleport, but not after a conversation in trek. The port gates again as the pause menu closes (`TrekLoop.Regate`, rebuilding each chunk whose objects came or went through `ITrekHost.Regated`), and after a cut-scene, a backdrop or the help menu reloads the chunks and their wanderers where the player stands (`TrekLoop.Resume`, [wanderers](wanderers.md#joining)).

## Stand-ins

- Alaron's head follows his neck, where the game keeps it upright and turns it toward nearby objects.
- **The spawn point.** A rock stands in a hole in the terrain in front of it, with collision walls and no top. The original drops the player from a height of 10; the runtime puts him on the highest ground below the spawn instead.

## Controls

- **Moving.** WASD or the left stick. The stick walks under 80% tilt and runs above; on a keyboard, Shift walks. Ctrl or the left trigger sneaks (Z on the N64).
- **The camera.** The original orbits it with R and the stick, and Alaron can't move while it does. Here that's the gamepad's right shoulder with the left stick, the right mouse button dragged, the right stick, or Q and E. Only the shoulder holds him still; the others turn the camera while he walks (a port addition, [cameras](cameras.md)).
- **A** is Space or the bottom face button: it uses the map object the player reaches, and closes or chooses in a popup. **B** is Backspace or the right face button: it closes a popup, or with none raises or lowers the party's portraits. **Start** is Enter, Escape or Start.
- **In a battle:** the party member whose turn it is walks in its field as in trek, and Z or the left trigger with the stick still skips the turn (Z or L). A (Space) with the stick still aims an attack at what's in reach; then left and right (the stick, the D-pad, A and D or the arrows) step the target, a bow's or thrown weapon's by Q and E (C-left and C-right) with the stick moving a free cursor, A attacks it, and B (Backspace) or Z puts it away.
- **In the pause menu:** Z and X or the triggers change the tab (Z or L, and R); Q and E or the shoulders change the member (C-left and C-right, C-left showing the next); the movement keys, arrows, left stick or D-pad change the list and move the cursor; Tab or the top face button shows the details while held (C-down); R or the left face button is C-up in a shop. In the name entry the keyboard types. Hovering a row or the wheel moves the cursor.
- **F-keys.** F toggles a free camera (Space and C for up and down, Shift for faster). F3 toggles the debug overlay (`Game.Debug.cs`): in trek the chunk, position, surface, speed, request, pace, camera, sounds, particles, rain and what a cut-scene waits for; in a battle its state, turns, field and combatants. F5 toggles the pace, F6 the sky's placement and F7 the UI screen's height. In a battle, F9 moves the radar between the log box and the screen's corner, and F10 routs the enemies and F11 fells the party, so that the next turn's change (Z) wins or loses it (`Battle.Rout`, `Collapse`).
- **Debug:** holding either thumbstick down (L3 or R3) raises the tick rate from 60 to 180 a second, as far as the machine keeps up (`Game.SetTickRate`). The pause menu stays at 60.

## The command line

After `--`: `tools/godot.ps1 --path src/Aidyn.Game -- --chunk I08 --at 16,62 --yaw 0 --pitch -15 --distance 5 --hold move_forward,4 --screenshot <absolute path>.png` starts somewhere, holds an input, saves the window and quits. The run gives up after a minute. `LaunchOptions` reads the options (on top of `CommandLine`, the parser and screenshot the editor shares), `Game.Start` builds the game in named steps from them, and `Game.Debug.cs` carries out the ones that drive it (`RunLaunchActions`). The launch options run once a run: a load or a game over that starts the scene again doesn't play them again.

- **Where:** `--set n`, `--chunk`, `--at x,z` (by default where a new game starts), `--yaw`, `--pitch`, `--distance`; `--fly x,y,z` starts with the free camera, which scene objects' distances are then measured from; `--camera outdoor|interior` starts in either mode (by default the start chooses as a door into its chunk would). Gwernia's open ground is around (30–80, 30–80) in M07, for example `--chunk M07 --at 40,55`; elsewhere in M07 there's no ground, and he falls and is put back again and again.
- **The game:** `--save <path>` starts from a save, where it stands unless `--chunk` or `--at` say otherwise. `--flags 5,50` sets plain flags before anything loads. A plain launch is the title's New Game: the opening cinematic, then script 866 on its stage, then trek in I08 (`GameSession.StartNewGame`, [cinematics](cinematics.md#what-starts-them)). So is a save with no place (`GameState.StartsAsNewGame`, [saves](saves.md#the-format)). Any option that says where to start or drives the run (`--save`, `--set`, `--chunk`, `--at`, `--flags`, `--fly`, `--hold`, `--press`, `--menu`, `--cutscene`, `--cinematic`, `--battle`, `--screenshot`) skips the opening and starts in trek, where the opening's cut-scene still starts from its object unless `--flags 5,11` is added; `--opening on` or `off` says which outright. `--time <hours>` sets the time of day.
- **Input:** `--hold` holds actions for so many seconds, several joined with `+`, as in `move_forward+walk,4`. `--press pause,menu_member_left,move_right` presses actions in turn, a fifth of a second apart, as input events, which `--hold` doesn't make; the screenshot waits for them. Among them, `wait:2` waits two seconds more; `move_forward:1.3` holds the action that long, pressed and let go as events, so combat's held menus hear both; and a `+` after it (`menu_details:1.6+`) sends the next presses while it's held. `battle_rout` routs a battle's enemies, as F10 does.
- **Menus and scenes:** `--menu 1` opens the pause menu, `--menu system`, `party` or `world` opens it on that tab, and `--menu loot` opens an empty loot window. `--cutscene aidyn:dialogue/0x0365` plays a cut-scene at once (the goblin ambush). `--battle aidyn:voxel/0x0F6B/3[,<arena>][,ambush]` starts a battle with that monster party where the player stands, in the arena given or the one the terrain chooses. `--radar box` puts the battle screen's radar in the log box (F9 swaps it). `--cinematic <n>` plays a cinematic, 0 to 3, without the menu after, and what follows it in the story (the opening's script, Roog's True Name, the ending's credits and the game's end); `--cinematic 0,5,300` starts at shot 5's time 300, and `--cinematic 0,5,300,hold` stands still there.
- **Weather:** `--weather rain|snow|fog|clear[,intensity[,fog]]` sets it at once, already set in and with the rain sky up (rain and snow default to 0.5 with fog 0.2 and a storm as hard as the rain; fog to 0.5). Rain and snow take a few seconds to fall into view from a standing start, so hold something harmless, such as `--hold walk,8`, before the screenshot.
- **Settings for the run:** `--ui-lines <n>`, `--sky original|projected`, `--pace`, `--draw-distance <scale>`, `--detail nearest`, `--precipitation <scale>` (up to 8) and `--spell-auras each` lie over the player's settings for the run (`LaunchSettings`), and are never written to `settings.json`. Each holds until the player changes that setting, in the options menu or with F5, F6 or F7; `--pace` gives way to the Pace setting. `--lights off` lights bodies and actors by the sky alone ([rendering](rendering.md#point-lights)), and `--spell-fx off` drops spells' visuals, sky dimming and body tints ([combat](combat.md#what-spells-look-like)), as baselines.
- **Screenshots.** The path must be absolute, or Godot saves it relative to the project. `--ui-screenshot <png>` saves trek's UI screen with it, in its own pixels and with its alpha, for measuring the HUD and the battle screen against the user's captures. `--screenshot <png> --frames <n>` saves the next n frames as they're rendered, in real time, as `<png stem>-000.png` on, kept in memory until the last: for what only shows above 60 fps, between ticks. To see something tick by tick, Godot's movie writer saves every frame at a fixed 60 a second: `tools/godot.ps1 --path src/Aidyn.Game --write-movie <absolute dir>/f.png --quit-after <frames> -- --menu party`. It records at the project's window size, 1280 × 960; Godot's `--resolution` doesn't change it, and `--ui-lines 960` puts a window pixel to each of the UI screen's.
- **GUI runs need a timeout:** launch with a process you can kill, not blocking on it.
