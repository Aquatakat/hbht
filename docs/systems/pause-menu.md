# The pause menu: its tabs and windows (roadmap 1F, M2, M3)

The System, Party and World tabs (`BigAssMenu.cpp`, `DollMenu*.cpp`), the Party tab's windows (the loot window, the shop, training and the post-battle windows), and the System tab's options, controls, name entry and save screens. The screen, laying text out, input and the rest of the UI are in [ui](ui.md); the menus' sounds in [audio](audio.md#sound-effects). Positions are in the original's 320 × 240 frame, and addresses are 1.0's.

- **The tabs are one long scroll.** Each tab is a widget tree in its own 320 × 240 frame, 320 pixels apart, and changing tab slides them all. Over them is a 3D scroll, `aidyn:actor/0x0E6B` (scene `0x0E63`), seen from above, which slides with them: System shows its wooden roller at the left, World at the right, Party neither. It unrolls as the menu opens and rolls up as it closes ([opening and closing](#opening-and-closing)).
- **Each tab has a full-screen image** over the scroll (`pause_menu_borg8`): `0x00D8`, `0x00D9` and `0x00DA`, all 320 × 240.
- **The menus keep the original's layouts at every screen size** ([ADR 0014](../adr/0014-menus-keep-the-originals-layouts.md)): each tab's image is drawn whole at the screen's centre and filled in at the original's coordinates.
- **Not ported yet:** a mouse click choosing what it's over, and the mouse on the World tab. On the Party windows and the System tab, hovering a row and the wheel move the cursor, in the runtime and the editor.

## Opening and closing

`PauseMenu` and `PauseScroll` in the core have the rules, and `PauseMenuView` and `PauseScrollView` in the runtime draw them (the original's `PauseMenu.DarkenStep` at `0x8004847c`, `DrawBackdrop` `0x800484cc`, the menu's `OpenStep` `0x8002f1d0`, `CloseStep` `0x8002f4d4`, `SlideOut` `0x8002e9d0` and `Update` `0x8002ee38`).

- **Everything in the menu steps once a frame.** The menu's frame (`PauseMenu.Frame`) counts its delta, but the widgets, the scroll and the fades take one step a frame whatever it is (`WidgetManager.Update`, `0x800ad10c`), so their times are in frames. The port runs the menu at 60 frames a second (`Cadence.MenuFrameTicks`), which matches the original's timing in the emulator, tweens and pulses alike (`MenuPulse`).
- **The world freezes and darkens first.** Any way into the menu (Start, a shop or trainer's script, a container) copies the frame on screen into a 320 × 240 image (`GetSnapShot`, `0x800208b4`), drawn behind the menu multiplied by a grey (`PauseMenu.BackdropTint`, `0x800d9564`): 255, then 10 less a frame to 128 (the last step 7), 13 frames. The menu is built on the 14th. The copy isn't blurred. Once the menu is open, the original draws black behind it and only the copy's top and bottom 40 lines, which the scroll leaves showing.
- **In the port the world isn't copied but drawn on, still**, the user's choice: the menu stops trek, with the scenery's actors and the free camera, and the world and the HUD go on being drawn as they stand under a black layer at one less the tint. So resizing the window redraws the world at its new shape, and the HUD is laid out again as Menu Size changes. The HUD stays drawn under the menu and its pages rather than hidden tick by tick, which would lose it for a tick as they close. The port still copies the frame as the menu opens, for a save's screenshot ([below](#saving-and-loading)).
- **The scroll.** The actor's states, by request: idle (0, 11 frames, the rollers at ±35.3, off the frame), opening on System or World (22, 37 frames, the rollers parting from ±2.6), opening on Party (10, 53 frames: the scroll comes up from below rolled, then parts from the middle to about ±13.5, then idles), closing from Party (19, 53) and from System or World (11, 39); 20 and 21 aren't the menu's. The opening is the tab's: barter, training and the loot window open on Party. The original's actors take a request for one step and then go back to 0, so each plays once. The menu sets the scene's x by the tab, 27 for System, 0 for Party and −26 for World (`0x800de9c8`), and slides it with the tabs, 1/32 of the way a frame. Its camera is 27 above the scene, looking straight down with −Z up the screen, 45° over the 240 lines, near 1 and far 256. It's drawn over the tabs. The scene's two lights light it as directional lights, warm from the left and dim blue from the right, with no ambient.
- **The tabs show only where the scroll has unrolled.** Each tab's image and widgets are cut to columns of the frame (`PauseMenu.ClipLeft` and `ClipRight`, `0x800d9568`), in x only, that follow the scroll's frame f in a straight line:

  | Tab | Opening | Closing |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | System | from the right: the left edge 320 until frame 8, then 0 by 36 | from the left: the left edge 0 at frame 1, 320 by 31 |
  | Party | from the middle: 160 to 160 until frame 36, then 0 to 320 by 55 | to the middle: 0 to 320 at frame 1, 160 to 160 by 14 |
  | World | from the left: the right edge −5 until frame 6, then 320 by 35 | from the right: the right edge 320 at frame 2, 0 by 32 |

  Each opens when its animation ends: Party at frame 53, its clip 17 to 303, its tab showing from frame 37; System and World at 37. Only then does the menu take input. The original's clips stop short of the frame's edges, behind the overscan. The port shows those columns, so a side within 17 columns of the frame's edge counts as at it (`PauseScroll.Overscan`), and it also cuts the tabs at the rollers, along their middles (`PauseScrollView.Arrange`), so the parchment never shows past one; a side at the frame's edge reaches the roller on that side. The user chose both.
- **On screens wider and taller than the frame**, the port changes what the original's scroll does beyond it, the user's choice (`PauseScroll`, `PauseScrollView`):
  - **The animations run on from one another.** The Party opening's last 9 frames are the System and World opening's first 9, and the System and World closing's frames 31 to 39 are the Party closing's 1 to 9. So the Party tab's scroll runs on into the System and World opening at its frame 10 (`ActorStateMachine.Join`), its rollers going out to the ends of the System and World tabs rather than jumping there once off the frame. The menu still takes input from frame 53. Closing from Party, the System and World closing brings the rollers back in to the frame's edges, then the Party closing runs from its first frame.
  - **Fades.** The Party scroll fades in over its opening's first 12 frames, and out over its closing's last 12, as it comes up from below the frame. The System and World tabs and their scroll fade out together from their closing's frame 29, gone by its end.
  - **Each roller has its own camera** past the frame, following it, its picture moving out with the tabs a tab's 320 pixels a tab. One camera over the scene's middle won't do: the scene's x steps aren't quite 320 pixels at the rollers' depth, so a roller further out drifts and widens over its tab.
  - **Barter and training** can't change tab, so their scroll runs on only to frame 11 of the System and World opening and holds there, the rollers just past what the Party tab draws.
  - **A battle's pause** has no World tab, so its World end's roller holds where barter's does and its System end's runs on, the user's choice (`PauseScroll`'s `lastTab`). On a turn, with the System and Party tabs, the World end's roller holds just past the Party tab while the System end's goes out past the System tab; rolling up, the World end's waits there, turned back, until the System end's comes to it, and then both go on into the Party tab's closing. Off a turn, with the System tab alone, the scroll is barter's a tab over, centred on it, and unrolls and rolls up as barter's does, the user's choice (`PauseScroll.CentredOnSystem`). `PauseScrollView` draws each end's roller from a copy of the scroll of its own, so the two can be at different frames.
  - **The scroll's viewports don't interpolate** between physics ticks, or its first frame would show where it was reset to.
- **Closing,** the scroll rolls up at once (`SlideOut` steps it), and when its animation ends the world brightens 10 a frame back to 255, 13 frames, before trek goes on. The loot window and shops (Game.Mode 7) snap back to full brightness instead. Camping waits for the menu to close; loading a save closes it at once.
- **How it ends** is its exit code (`PauseMenu.Exit`, `0x80048e64`, a jump table at `0x800d1240`), which the trek pause menu leaves as it rolls up and which sets trek's next state as the slide ends (`PauseMenu.NextTrekState`, `0x800df031`, copied at `0x8004898c`): 0 for B or Start (trek runs on, state 3), 1 for Camp (state 0x11, the camp), 2 for the Theatre's cinematic (states 0x16 and 0x17), 3 for Help (state 0x12, script `0x054B`), 4 once a save has loaded (state 4, trek built again from it) and 5 for the credits (state 0x19). The loot window, shops and training close straight to trek (`PauseMenu.CloseToTrek`, `0x80048f40`). In the port the pages return the exit (`PauseExit`, a save's path among them), the core's `PauseMenu` returns it from its `Step` once the menu has closed, and the session carries it out ([runtime](runtime.md#how-a-tick-runs)).
- **The post-battle windows** (game mode 6) open with the Party scroll's opening over the black the battle left, and the loot window that follows closes with its closing roll; between them the post-battle window's lists fade out and the loot window's in, 60 a frame (`PauseMenu.CloseFade`, `OpenFade`), the scroll staying open ([below](#after-a-battle)).
- **Each opening starts the tab afresh** on the inventory's top row, but on the member last shown, so the presses to reach something are the same every time from the same member.
- **The member** every Party window opens on, the Party tab, barter, training and the post-battle windows alike, is one byte, `PauseMenu.Member` (`0x800DE9F0`), which `DollMenu::InitMenu` (`0x800300B0`) builds them on. Only three places in 1.0 store to it: C-left and C-right in any of them (`PartyMenu.ChangeMember`, `0x8003054C`, stores at `0x800305C8` and `0x800305F0`), a battle's pause from a turn, which sets the member whose turn it is (`Combat.StatePause`, store at `0x800226D0`; the pause from any other state, `0x80022930`, leaves it), and `InitMenu` itself, which steps back from an empty slot to the one before with a member and keeps that (`0x80030128`). So it outlasts trek, combat, the windows and a save loaded, and it isn't saved: it's 0 at power-on. The port keeps it in the core's `PauseMenu`, which the session holds. **Where the port differs:** loading a save builds a new session, and with it a new menu, so the member goes back to the leader and the remembered tab ([changing tab](#changing-tab)) to System, the user's choice. A loaded game should be as it was saved, not carry what was left by that save's future or another save's: a slot number from another game can open on someone else, and the leader is the same on every load. The tab after a load matches what the user saw in 1.0, System; whether a game over, the title and a load reset 1.0's is left unsettled, as it doesn't matter to the port.
- **Input** reaches the menu as a `MenuAction`, as every menu's does ([ui](ui.md#input)): `GameSession.Press` gives it to the menu, which gives it to the tab or window up.

## Changing tab

- **Z or L goes left, R right** (`bigAssMenu_LZFunc`, `RFunc`), System, Party, World, with no wrap. The tabs slide 10 pixels a frame over 320, 32 frames, in a straight line, and the scroll with them (`PauseMenu.Update`); presses wait while they slide.
- **The System tab can be left only from its list.** The port also keeps the Party tab while a popup or message is up.
- **The menu remembers the tab** in `gGlobals.pauseMenuSection`, which isn't saved, and [the member](#opening-and-closing) last shown. A new game's first opening is on System. Barter and training open on Party and can't change tab.
- **The sounds:** the cursor `0x73B`, A `0x740`, B `0x741`, a tab back `0x74D` and forward `0x74E` ([audio](audio.md#sound-effects)); the Party tab's lists play the cursor's twice. The scroll's unrolling plays `0x74D`, or `0x74E` on World, as the menu is built, and its rolling up the other. The core's menu cues them to its host in order (`IPauseMenuHost.Cue`, ADR 0019).
- **Beside the tab in use**, on a screen wider than the frame, the port shows the neighbouring tabs as one scroll, the user's suggestion: the three images join edge to edge into one 960-pixel scroll, laid 320 apart. Past the System tab's left and the World tab's right are the rollers, and beyond them the darkened world. Barter and training show the Party tab alone.
  - **The veil.** A neighbouring tab's widgets are faded into the parchment by a veil drawn over them at 60%, which clears as the tab slides into the middle. The veil is the tab's image with what's painted on it covered by patches of plain parchment from elsewhere in it, each blended in to meet the image and the patches before it, made once as the menu first opens (`UiImages.Plain`), so the painted layout fades with the widgets. The Party tab's patches (`PartyTabView.PlainPatches`) cover everything painted but the parchment; the World tab's are [below](#in-the-port). The System tab's image has nothing painted on it.
  - **The details' parchment** (`0x00E2`, with its shadow) starts at the frame's left edge, at (0, 58), where the console's overscan hid its edge; beside the System tab it showed a hard line. It fades in across the frame's first 17 columns, the overscan and the column the original's clip stops short of, the user's choice (`UiPainter.Parchment`). The loot window, training and the post-battle windows draw theirs the same, under the roller.

## The Party tab

`0x00D9` is one parchment with the silhouette and its slot frames, the portrait frames, the labels HitPoints, Level and Gold, the HP bar's frame, the knotwork divider down the middle and "Details" with the C-down button painted in; none of those labels are in the string tables. The widgets fill it in.

- **The header** (`DollMenuPortraits`, `WidgetHealthGold`): the member's portrait at (22, 26), 40 square, and the others' at (67, 39), (96, 39) and (125, 39), 24 square, in party order from the member shown. The name at (69, 28) in the slim font. Under the large portrait, 4 below it, the aspect's icon and 2 after it the school's. In the main font, hit points at (223, 41) and their maximum at (255, 41), the level at (223, 55) and gold at (223, 69). The bar, `0x00E0`, is drawn 80 × 10 at (220, 27) and cut to hit points ÷ maximum × 80, truncated.
- **The silhouette** (`DollEquipmentMenu`): 15 slots, 16 square, each the worn item's icon or the slot's own image of what it takes (`gItemSlotBorg8Indecies`). A gear slot shows the first, second or third piece of its kind in the gear's order. The helmet's slot shows helmets.
- **The list** (`DollMenuLists`) is one of four, in the order the stick steps through: inventory, spells, skills, stats (training drops stats; barter keeps only the inventory). It's at (160, 101), bounded to x 300 and y 205, in the slim font, rows in (130, 80, 80). Its title image is centred at x 230, 20 above it. The arrows that show hidden rows are 15 in from each side and 13 above, in (225, 225, 225). Changing member keeps each list's cursor: stats clamp it to the last row, skills and spells go back to the top if it's past the end.
  - **The cursor's row pulses** from (68, 42, 34) toward (151, 141, 191) and back over 20 steps (`WidgetFastScrollMenu.Step`, `0x80028b00`), but two steps a frame: each list's step steps its scroll menu once at the end of the lists' shared layout (`PartyMenu.ListBaseStep`, `0x80030888`, at `0x800309cc`) and again itself (`0x80035770` for the inventory). So it shows only even steps, peaking at 18, 19 frames a cycle, twice the other menus' pulses. The same doubling plays the cursor sound twice. Barter's and training's lists are the same widgets.
  - **Inventory**, 13 pixels a row: the wearer's portrait and the item's icon at 12 square, then the count at 26 and the name at 42, or 30 without a count. Worn items first, each member's weapon, armor, shield and gear, the member shown first and then round the party, then what's carried. Each part is sorted by kind and then by name in byte order. The kinds' order (`InventoryOrder`) is potions, weapons, wands, scrolls, amulets, rings, helmets, cloaks, gloves, boots, belts, armor, shields, key items, misc.
  - **Stats**, 11 a row: the six attributes' base values at 79, and modified less base, when it isn't 0, at 100, as "(+n)" in (0, 128, 0) or "(n)" in (128, 0, 0). Then 4 below the rows, XP Remaining (experience to spend), Total XP and Next Level (what the next level needs, or MAX), the values lined up 4 after the first label.
  - **Skills**, 13 a row: the skills above 0 with their icons, then the weapon skills and the shield, which have none; the level at 79 and the difference at 95.
  - **Spells**, 13 a row: the aspect's icon (a spell's special, Solar or Lunar), the school's at 12 and the spell's symbol at 24, the rank at 36 and the name at 50. Unschooled spells have no school icon.
- **C-down's details** (`WidgetItemDetail`), shown while it's held, on the 160 × 160 parchment `0x00E2` at (0, 58) with its shadow `0x00E4` at (5, 60), in the slim font from x 23 to 149 and from y 70:
  - **An item:** its icon and name level with the icon's foot, then label and value lines: a weapon's skill, aspect, Min. Strength, Base Hit and Damage; armor's aspect, Protection, a shield's Defense, Dexterity and Stealth. Then what it adds: a stat (Stamina as Spell Battery, what it holds now), a skill, Magic: and Spell: with the spell's name indented 16, Charges: on a wand, and Resists: with the element and the share it takes off. Armor ends with Total Protection and Total Defense, now and with it on, the second green or red, ending at y 213. Worn gear shows Min. Intelligence and, if it has one, Min. Strength; **the original prints each under the other's label, which the port puts right**. Potions, scrolls and other items show their description, and a scroll its spell's Min. Wizard Rank at y 187; key items and items without a description show nothing.
  - **A stat or skill:** its description. **A spell:** its symbol and name, its description, and at y 187 the Wizard rank it needs and the reagent it uses, with the reagent's icon at 9 square.
- **A red arrow** under the knotwork at (126, 207) points both ways in trek (`BORG8_SliderArrowLR`) and only left in combat, in (130, 80, 80).

### Changing member and list

The menu's widgets animate with tweens (`MenuTween.CreateShort` at `0x800cc880`, `CreateByte` `0x800ccb88`): a value moved from where it is to an end over so many frames, a step a frame, on a curve that eases in and out, 0.5 sin(πt − π/2) + 0.5 (`MenuTween.Ease`, `0x800e6590`), truncated to a whole number each step. A tween takes its frames and finishes on the next, so the widget is busy a frame longer. The port's tweens and fades are `MenuTween` and `MenuMotion.cs`.

- **C-left shows the next member and C-right the one before** (`PartyMenu.CLeft` `0x8003e1c8`, `CRight` `0x8003e1f4`, slots 0xBC and 0xC4 of the menu's handlers, which the C-buttons' bits 0x2 and 0x1 call).
- **The portraits move** (`PartyMenu.PortraitsChange`, `0x8003b3cc`): each member's portrait moves and resizes from its old place to its new one over 15 frames, x, y, width and height each tweened. With C-left, the next member's grows into the large frame, the one shown shrinks into the last small one, and the others move one frame left. The white frames are painted into `0x00D9` and stay. One widget per member, drawn in party order.
- **The rest crossfades over the same 15 frames:** the name; the aspect's and school's icons, where they change; the hit points, their maximum and the level, always (`PartyMenu.HealthGoldChange`, `0x8003be5c`); and the silhouette's slots. The bar's width tweens to the new member's. The gold and the list on the right change at once.
- **The silhouette** (`PartyMenu.EquipmentChange`, `0x8003e374`): the original crossfades each slot whose item differs. The port crossfades a slot only where one member wears something in it and the other doesn't, and changes one worn item to another at once, the user's choice. That choice is the member change's alone: for an item put on or taken off (below) the port crossfades every slot whose item differs, as the original does (`PartyMenu.SlotCrossfades`).
- **Changing the member shown runs the same crossfades.** `HealthGoldChange` doesn't count the numbers up or down: it builds the new hit points, maximum and level as new texts, fades them in from alpha 0 and the old ones out over 15 frames (`MenuTween.CreateByte`, freed at the end by `MenuTween.FadeAndFree`, `0x8003f1b0`), and tweens the bar's width (+0xA0) from where it is to hit points ÷ maximum × 80, only if they differ, over the same 15 (`MenuTween.CreateShort`). Before that it finishes any of its tweens still running, so a second change starts from what's shown. Its other callers are:
  - **A on the inventory** (`PartyMenu.InventoryA`, `0x80035fa0`): whatever isn't refused with a message ends at `0x80036694` with `EquipmentChange` and `HealthGoldChange` for the member shown. So a potion drunk, a scroll read (whatever it says), an item put on or taken off (even one the inventory has no room for, which stays on), and in a battle's pause a flask taken in hand, all crossfade the numbers and the changed slots; a potion that would do nothing, an equip refused and the Archmage Staff's message don't.
  - **A skill's result** (`MenuPopup.ShowResult`, `0x80055ed4`, for the Alchemist, the Mechanic and the Healer's three tasks): the numbers, for the member whose skill it is, who's the member shown, whatever the task did. Laying on hands costs the Healer Stamina, so their hit points drop as the result shows.
  - **A training purchase**, the skills', spells' and stats' (`0x80037ccc`, `0x800391e0`, `0x8003a90c`), at a trainer and after a battle, once the rank has gone up: the numbers, an Endurance point raising the maximum.

  The port keeps the numbers as they stood (`HeaderNumbers`) and the silhouette's worn icons (`PartyMenu.WornIcons`) when the change starts, as `PartyMenu.PreviousNumbers` and `PreviousSlots` with their eased shares `NumbersChange` and `SlotsChange`; `PartyMenu.ChangeNumbers` is the training windows' and the skills' way in. The portraits, name and icons (`Previous`, `MemberChange`) move only for a member change.
- **The stick's left shows the next list and its right the one before** (`PartyMenu.ListsLeft` `0x800319f0`, `ListsRight` `0x80031a10`), wrapping from stats to the inventory. The list, its title and its arrows slide together, the old and the new 140 pixels over 20 frames (`PartyMenu.ChangeList`, `0x80031620`): left brings the next list from x 20 to 160 and pushes the old from 160 to 300; right the other way. The rows are cut to the list's bounds, x 160 to 300 and y 101 to 205, and the title to x 170 to 300 (`PartyMenu.ListsCreate`, `0x80030c50`); the port cuts the title at 160 with the rows.
- **A change waits for the last** (`PartyMenu.Changing`, `0x800304b4`): no member change while the header, the silhouette or the lists are moving, and no list change while the lists are. So for 16 frames after a potion drunk or an item put on, C-left and C-right do nothing. Training's member change animates the same way.

### The model and the layouts

`PartyMenu` (`HBHT.Core.Ui`) is the tab's state: the member shown, the list showing with its rows (`PartyMenuRows`) and cursor, the silhouette's slots, and C-down's details (`MenuDetails`). `MenuImages` and `InventoryOrder` hold the chrome's image IDs and the kinds' order. `PartyTabView` (`HBHT.Views`) draws it with the widget kit, `UiPainter`.

**The Party tab's windows** are `IPartyWindow` (`HBHT.Core.Ui`), each a `WidgetDollMenu` in one of the original's pause states: the tab itself (`PartyMenu`), the loot window and shops (`BarterMenu`), the training window (`TrainingMenu`) and the post-battle window (`PostBattleMenu`, pause state 5, [below](#after-a-battle)). The pause menu sends every press to whichever window is up, and neither it nor the view knows which; the stick's left and right (`Sideways`) are the tab's next or previous list, the loot window's pile or party list, or training's skills or spells. The editor's panels press the same `IPartyWindow.Press`, so the two can't drift. `PartyTabView` draws the header for every window and the window's own parts on a layer over it (`DrawWindow`, with a case for each), whose alpha carries the post-battle windows' fades.

**Lists are laid out in the core.** `ListLayout` is a list's bounds in the 320 × 240 frame and its rows' pitch, which ADR 0014 fixes: the Party tab's 8 rows of 13 (the stats 9 of 11), barter's pile and training's 6 of 13, crafting's 11 or 10 slim-font lines or 4 members of 28. The menus build their lists with the layout's count, so a list scrolls with no view drawn, and the views draw and hit-test from the same layout rather than repeating the pitch and bounds.

### The Party tab's actions

What A does (`DollMenuLists::AFunc`), in `PartyMenu.Actions`, with the popups as `MenuMessage` and `CraftingMenu` and `PartyTabView` drawing them.

- **The inventory** (`WidgetInvShop::AFunc`). A on a worn item takes it off into the inventory, silently staying on if the inventory has no room. The Archmage Staff stays, and says it "contains powerful magic that only" its wielder "can control" (`XPowerfulMagic`). A on a carried item: a potion is drunk by the member shown ([items](items.md#potions)); a scroll is read, which says how it went whatever happens (`Party::UseScroll`); anything else is equipped on the member shown, putting back what it replaces ([items](items.md#equipment)). A refusal says why, from `equip_error_labels` (common strings 95–101): the aspect, Strength or Intelligence it needs, or else "You cannot equip that item!", which is also what too many rings, no free gear slot and no room for what it replaces say (`GetEquipError3` and the item kinds' functions return 1). The port's own refusal, replacing the staff, uses the staff's message. After equipping, the cursor stays on what's left of the stack, or on its row, which the next item moves up into, so it never jumps up to the worn items; after taking off, it stays on its row. Equipping the last item, with nothing to move up into its row, leaves the cursor on the item that was second-last, in 1.0 as in the port, which clamps it to the list's last row.
- **The skills** (`WidgetSkillInfo::AFunc`, trek only): A on the Alchemist, Mechanic or Healer opens their popup; on any other skill, a spell or a stat it does nothing. The Mechanic with no hide for a recipe their base level reaches says "You do not have the components to create any armor." instead (`makeArmorMenu`).
- **A popup takes every press** while it's open, as the original's widget handler gives them to the top widget: the stick moves its cursor, A chooses, B closes it, and the member and list don't change. `MenuOverlay` (`HBHT.Core.Ui`) is that rule once, for the popups every Party window, the World tab and the save screens keep: a message, the help, a question over choices (`MenuChoice`) and C-down's details. The newest, in the order message, help, choice, details, takes the press, and closing it hands the next to the one under it. The Party tab's crafting popup and the drop question (`BarterConfirm`) are the menus' own, beside it.
- **The popups' fades** (`PopupFade`). The skills' popups (the Mechanic's `0x80002a08` from `PartyMenu.MakeArmorMenu`, `0x80002890`; the Alchemist's `0x80002fc4`; the Healer's `MakeHealerMenu`, `0x800034a0`; all through `MenuPopup.Menu`, `0x80055c98`) and C-down's details (`PartyMenu.InventoryDetails` `0x80036710`, `SpellDetails` `0x80037994`, `SkillDetails` `0x80038ea4` and `StatDetails` `0x8003a7d0`, through `MenuPopup.Create`, `0x800337bc`) are one widget family, `itemdetail.cpp`'s, and fade alike:
  - **In.** Each opens at alpha 0 (`MenuPopup.Open`, `0x800336a0`), and each frame steps it before it's drawn: 60 more while it's under 195, else 255, where it's open (`MenuPopup.FadeIn`, `0x80032840`). So it's drawn at 60, 120, 180, 240 and 255.
  - **Out.** 60 less while it's 61 or more, else it's freed before it's drawn (`MenuPopup.FadeOut`, `0x800328ac`): 195, 135, 75 and 15. B on a popup starts it (`MenuPopup.OnB`, `0x80055e10`), and so does closing the message with a popup's result. The details start it once they're open and C-down isn't held (`PartyMenu.CloseDetails`, `0x80034c18`), tested only then, so a quick tap still fades them fully in and straight out.

- **The main page:** the Options title `0x00FD` centred at y 40. Under it Audio, Video, Enhancements and Controls, centred on x 160 as the System list is, then Rename Character in a game, set off by half a line (8). A opens a page; B on a page comes back to the row that led there.
- **Audio** lists Sound Volume and Music Volume at x 84, each with its bar under it, taking two lines; no label in the game has a colon, so these don't either.
- **The bars** are `0x00D7`, a braided rope 152 × 18. Each is drawn whole in (100, 100, 100) at a quarter of the widget's alpha, then again in the rows' colour, cut at 152 × the volume. The volumes are the original's floats from 0 to 1, which the stick steps by 0.01, so the port keeps them in hundredths. They start at 1.0 for sound and 0.65 for music (`app.cpp`). A volume steps every frame the stick is held, from the first, with no wait (not the menus' held repeat).
- **The cursor's row** pulses from (130, 80, 80) toward dark brown (103, 70, 60), its alpha falling by 12 a step, so it fades nearly out at the deepest. Each channel moves a whole 20th of its way a step, rounded toward zero, so the colour reaches only (110, 80, 60). It steps in the page's draw (`OptionsPage.Draw`, `0x800419c8`), once a frame, and turns at 20, 40 frames a cycle.
- **Video and Enhancements** list each setting at x 72 with its value at x 204, clear of the System roller's inner edge at about 61. Eight rows show at once, the last ending above the parchment's foot; a page with more scrolls as the Controls page does, keeping the cursor's row in the middle as far as it can, with the Controls page's arrows at x 304 saying there's more above or below (on the title's box, the box reaches them). The stick's left and right change the cursor's setting, wrapping round, and so does A. - **The Video page lists what the screen allows.** The runtime says which screen the window is on as the options open (`IPauseMenuHost.Screen`, a `ScreenInfo`), and the page's rows are made again after each change, so Window Size and Render Resolution follow the display mode and the window's size. Godot 4 can't change the screen's own mode, so there's no exclusive fullscreen and no choice of the screen's resolution or refresh rate; Window Size and Frame Rate Limit stand in for them, the user's choice.
- **Window Size is the window as it is**, the user's choice. Choosing a size always changes the window, restoring it first if it's maximized, and resizing, maximizing or restoring the window by hand changes the setting: `Game.WindowChanged` takes the window's size (`Settings.WindowWidth`, `WindowHeight`) or that it's maximized (`Settings.Maximized`, which keeps the size it's restored to), kept half a second after the last change of a drag, and tells an open Video page (`OptionsMenu.Refresh`), which lists a size dragged to among the others. Fullscreen and minimizing leave it be.
- **The sky's placement** is off the menu, the user's choice: the projected sky is always drawn, and F6 or `--sky original` draws the original's for comparing ([rendering](rendering.md#the-skys-objects)).
- **How a setting takes effect** is the runtime's ([runtime](runtime.md#what-it-draws), [ADR 0023](../adr/0023-the-core-is-handed-its-settings-each-tick.md)): the menu changes the player's `Settings`, which nothing else in the core reads. The command line's settings for a run lie over the player's and are never saved ([runtime](runtime.md#the-command-line)), so the page shows the player's own.
- **The original's resolution row** switches to the Expansion Pak's high resolution (512 × 240) and back; without the pak, it's greyed and the cursor skips it. "32 Bit Color" is in the table, but nothing reaches it.
- **The original keeps its volumes and resolution in every save** (`QuestData::Save`), so loading set them back. The port keeps the settings per player instead.

### Controls

The port's own page ([ADR 0026](../adr/0026-controls-are-bound-by-situation.md), [ADR 0027](../adr/0027-conversations-take-the-menus-controls.md)): every control can be rebound, on the keyboard with the mouse and on a controller apart. `OptionsMenu.Controls.cs` holds the page over `Settings.Controls` (`ControlBindings`), and `SystemTabView.Controls.cs` draws it; the runtime turns the bindings into Godot's input actions as the game starts and whenever they change (`InputBindings`, [runtime](runtime.md#controls)).

- **The controls** are what the player does in each situation, not the N64's buttons (`GameControls`), so C-down is both the menus' Details and combat's Spell Menu, which share a default. The defaults:

  | | Camera Hold | Right Click | RB | the N64's R: steers with what moves |

  **Cut-scenes and conversations take the menus' controls** ([ADR 0027](../adr/0027-conversations-take-the-menus-controls.md)), as do trek's popups, a backdrop's and the help menu's page, a cinematic and the credits: Move Cursor moves the choices, Accept continues, Back closes a popup, goes back on the help menu's page or skips a cinematic, and Close Menu skips a cut-scene, a cinematic or the credits. They're live in both situations (`Situation.Talking` is still what those modes are), so a rebound Accept is a conversation's too and the page can't say one thing while a conversation does another; the menus' other controls don't reach them. A battle's turn walks and turns the camera with trek's controls; Sneak still acts there but isn't checked against combat's buttons, since the left trigger is also Skip Turn. The debug keys ([runtime](runtime.md#controls)) aren't on the page and stay fixed.
- **The layout:** the Device row (Keyboard, Controller) at y 70, then the list from y 88, nine lines 12 apart, a situation's heading taking a line of its own, centred in a darker brown. Labels are at x 72 and values at x 188, clear of the longest label, Previous Member, to 304; a value longer than that is printed in the slim font, as a save's long name is. The list scrolls to keep the cursor's row in the middle, with the System list's arrow `0x3757` turned up or down at the right where there's more. Under the list, at y 202, the last change's message for three seconds, or on a binding what clears it ("R: Clear"). One device shows at a time: a column for each won't fit the 238 pixels between the roller and the frame's edge. Mouse Sensitivity (0.25x to 4x, 1x the runtime's drag) is on the keyboard's page, then Reset to Defaults, which resets both devices.
- **A button's row:** A shows "Press a Key... 5" (or "Press a Button...") in its value, the row steady in the headings' brown, counting the seconds down. The next key, mouse button or wheel turn (on the keyboard's page), or controller button, stick or trigger pushed past 0.6 (on the controller's), replaces the cell, and the prompt closes. Nothing else ends it but the five seconds, which change nothing, so Escape, Backspace, Start and B can be bound like any other; an input of the other device and the debug keys' F-keys are passed over. While it waits it takes every event, before the menus (`Game.BindPrompted`), and the System tab's mouse handling lets the wheel and buttons by. The menus' Appraise (R, X) clears the cell.
- **A direction's row** (Move, Camera, Move Cursor, Target) is a choice among its sources' combinations on the device: the stick's left and right or A step through None, each source, and each combination, "Both Sticks" for the two sticks. The mouse is only Camera's; Move Cursor never offers None.
- **Conflicts** are checked only within a situation, and the newest binding wins: whatever else in the same situation had the input loses it, a button its binding and a direction the source holding it, and the foot says "Taken From Portraits". Two directions may share a source, which is how Camera Hold works. A battle's aiming (`Situation.Aiming`) is apart from its turn, so RB is both Camera Hold on a turn and Ranged Right when aiming.
- **No lockout:** the essential controls, trek's Menu, the menus' Move Cursor, Accept and Back, and combat's Pause, always keep a binding on each device. Clearing one is refused ("Accept Needs a Binding"), and so is a rebind that would take one's last binding ("Accept Needs It"); a `settings.json` that leaves one with none gets its default back. Without these the player couldn't open the menu, move on its pages, or leave a conversation.
- **The file** keeps only the controls that differ from their defaults, each with all its bindings, under `controls`; unknown controls, bindings that aren't one of the forms and the debug keys are skipped as it loads. Each cell is replaced whole, so the page never adds a second key; the defaults' Enter and Escape come back with Reset.

### Renaming Alaron

Rename Character opens the name entry (`InputMenu`) over the options. It renames Alaron, and the save keeps his name (`SaveParty::SaveAlaron`, see [saves](saves.md)). Scripts' `{player}` and the cinematics' captions use it ([scripts](scripts.md#its-boundary)).

- **The layout:** the title `0x00F9` centred at y 50, and the name centred at y 85 in (130, 80, 80). Below them the grid at (60, 110), 13 keys a row, 15 apart: A–Z, 0–9, then the symbol keys, which the font draws as its own symbols (a skull, a heart, a diamond for the underscore), then rub out, cancel and OK. The cursor is `0x00F8`, a white square 15 px across, pulsing grey 5 a step between 95 and 230 from 150, 54 frames a cycle (`InputMenu.Step`, `0x8002d7e4`), with its key in black on it. The letter being chosen pulses the same way (`InputMenu.Draw`, `0x8002d464`); where the square's own tint comes from isn't traced, and the port gives it the same. The port marks the next letter's place with a short line.
- **The keys:** the stick moves over the grid, wrapping round its rows, and writes the key into the slot being chosen. A types it and moves on, but not past the tenth (`IMNameLength`). Start goes to OK, and a second Start confirms. B cancels. The first letter and any letter after a space are capitals, the rest small. The cursor starts on OK, so Start, Start keeps the name.
- **The letter in the slot is only shown until A types it**, the user's choice: moving onto rub out, cancel or OK puts back what the slot held, so an empty slot shows its line again and a full name's tenth letter comes back, and Start's jump to OK, rubbing out and confirming all leave it out.
- **On a PC** the keyboard types into the name: a printable key types its character, Backspace rubs out the last letter, Enter confirms and Escape cancels, before any of them reach the menu's actions.
- **Not ported:** the cheat codes, which the original checks the name against here (`Cheats::Check`; see [hardcoded content](../hardcoded-content.md)).

### Saving and loading

One saved game at a time, which the stick's left and right step through. The positions are absolute in the frame:

- **The title** at (132, 24): Load Game `0x00F1` or Save Game `0x00FF`. On the title screen's box it's centred over the box instead, at y 24.
- **The screenshot**, stretched to 104 × 78 at (73, 52).
- **The party's portraits**, 40 square at x 73, 128, 183 and 238, y 133.
- **The arrow** at (183, 101): right only on the first save, both ways between, left only on the last, and none with one save.
- **The name** at (183, 111) and "Hours: %u:%u:%u" (common 481) at (183, 122). The name is Alaron's, or a save's label, such as the checkpoint it was made from, in the slim font if it's too long for the main one. The hours are the time played: ticks ÷ 216000, then the minutes and seconds. The original prints them unpadded, as in 18:4:28; the port pads them, 18:04:28, the user's choice.
- **The actions** at (237, 180), 11 apart: Load or Save (423, 424), Delete (425) and Exit (426), with the list's colours but over 15 steps, turning at 14, 28 frames a cycle (`SaveMenu.Actions`, `0x8007e658`).

The rules:

- **The slots.** The original's saves are notes on the Controller Pak, up to 16, in the pak's order. The port's are the files in the saves folder, newest first.
- **"Create New Save"** (553), in dark brown where the name goes, offers only Save and Exit. The original puts it last; the port first. It writes at once, to `Save n.json`. With nothing to load, the load screen says "There are no saved games." on one line centred under the title, where the original talks about the Controller Pak, and offers only Exit.
- **Saving over a game asks first:** "Are you sure you would like to save over that game?" (410), with Overwrite and Cancel. Saving says "Game successfully saved." (422) with Continue (176), which leads back to the System tab's list, showing the slot just written. The original's "Saving. Do not turn the power OFF…" step isn't needed.
- **Deleting asks first:** "Are you sure you would like to delete that game?" (372), with Delete and Cancel. Deleting takes the screenshot with the save, and starts the list again from the first save.
- **Loading** happens at once, with no question. The menu closes and the game starts again from the save ([runtime](runtime.md#transitions)). A save of a new game, with no place (`hbht save new`, the editor's new game), starts as the title's New Game does, with the opening cinematic ([saves](saves.md#the-format)).
- **The questions** are `WidgetChoiceDia` panels 150 wide in off-white, with no B.
- **The screenshot** is taken as the menu opens (`QuestData::TakeSnapshot`). The original crops the frame's centre half, (80, 60) to (240, 180), and samples it down to 64 × 32 at 16 bits, which is why its screenshots look stretched. The port takes the centre half of the window's view, cut to 4:3, at 208 × 156, and saves it beside the save as a PNG of the same name.
- **The slide.** The pages are a widget group (`./menus/widgetgroup.cpp`, `WidgetGroup.Create` at `0x8002b86c`), a box 219 × 192 from the title's corner, (67, 24) to (286, 216), that cuts its pages to it. Each save's page holds its arrow, screenshot, portraits, name and hours (`SaveMenu.AddSlot`, `0x8007e504`); the title and the actions are outside it and stay put, and the actions change at once. Right (`SaveMenu.Right`, `0x8007d498`, then `WidgetGroup.Next`, `0x8002bc68`) slides the page shown out to the left by the box's width as the next comes in from the right, and left the other way (`SaveMenu.Left`, `WidgetGroup.Previous`, `0x8002bd94`). Both are tweens of the page's x over 60 frames on the ease-out curve, sin(π t / 2) (`MenuTween.EaseOut`), truncated to whole pixels. While a page slides, every press is dropped (`SaveMenu.Input`, `0x8007d508`), and the page that slid out goes once the tweens end (`WidgetGroup.Step`, `0x8002ba00`). The stick's left and right play 0x073D whether or not there's a save that way.
- **The port slides in 30 frames** (`SaveMenu.SlideFrames`), half the original's, the user's choice. The presses dropped while they slide make no sound. The sliding pages are drawn with the rest of the tab in the same frame, cut to the box (`SystemTabView.SlidingPages`); in a control of their own, shown a tick late, they flicker.

## The World tab

The pause menu's last tab (`WidgetCalendar`, built by `PauseWidget::BuildCalendarMenu` at `0x8002eca8`, set up in `calendar_func` at `0x8004069c`), with the world map (`WidgetMenuWorldMap`, `ShowMap` at `0x80049080`) and the journal (`Journal`, `InitMenu` at `0x80052228`). `WorldMenu` (`HBHT.Core.Ui`) is the tab's state and `JournalMenu` the journal's, drawn by `WorldTabView`. The map's pieces, the towns and the ring are the world map record (`aidyn:worldmap/0`, [hardcoded content](../hardcoded-content.md)).

- **Painted into `0x00DA`**, with no widget: the sea and coast with no land, the compass rose with its N, the calendar's 28 days in their grid with the moons' discs, and the red arrow at the bottom left, pointing only left. The moons are art: the same discs stand in every half season, and no code computes a phase here.
- **The season's name** at (160, 40), in the main font in (130, 80, 80), wrapping between x 160 and 260, so "THE EARTH" over "SEASON". It's one of twelve, common lines 147–158: two a season, by whether 28 days of the season have gone (`0x8004078c`).
- **Today** is `0x00DC`, a white 16-pixel square drawn 17 square and untinted, at x 160 + 14 × column − 1 or − 2 and y 65 + 14 × row. The day of the half season, d from 1 to 28, is at column (d + 2) mod 7 and row (d + 2) div 7. The 1 or 2 is from a table at `0x800d0b50` by row × 7 + column: 1 for 4, 8, 9, 11–18, 21, 23, 26, 28 and 29, 2 for the rest.
- **The world map** (at (0, 0)) is its pieces: `WorldMapPieces` (`0x800df040`), eighteen of flag, image, x and y, each drawn with its top-left at (x, y), unscaled, in the table's order, in (130, 80, 80), while its flag is set. **The fog is the pieces' absence.** The pieces fade in, their alpha rising 5 a tick to 250 and then 255, each time the tab is slid to (`bigAssMenu_RFunc` resets it), and only while it isn't sliding. Some key items set one ([hardcoded content](../hardcoded-content.md)), and so does entering a chunk of the region: below.
- **The regions are found by entering them** (`ZoneEngine.EnterRegion`, `0x8004960c`, at the end of `LoadGameBorgScenes`, `0x8000b5f4`): as trek's world is built (`InitZoneEngine`), on every crossing into another chunk and every teleport (`ConfirmPlayerWithinZone` queues one, `TeleportPlayer`), after the pause menu (`PauseMenu.Leave`) and as a cut-scene's stage loads (`CutScene.LoadStage`). The centre chunk's row and column are looked up in a table of row, column and flag (`CodeTable.RegionFlags`, `0x800d126c`, 162 rows, flags 788–805), and the first match's flag is set (through `FlagNetwork.Set`) when three things hold: the zone engine was started for trek (`Zones.Mode`, `0x800d90a6`, 0: not a battle's arena, 1, nor the title's scenery, 2, which the opening's stage still has), no minimap is loaded (the minimap's first word, `0x800d9598`, which `Zones.SetMapSet` loads and frees with the map set), and the centre chunk's sky is drawn (`Sky.BackgroundType`, `0x800d94d8`, 3). The prologue's forest and Gwernia, H08 to N08, are all region 1 (788). The table names no map set, so another set's chunk at a listed row and column counts too; that and its two rows for chunks the Overworld lacks are in [hardcoded content](../hardcoded-content.md). Nothing clears a region's flag.
- **In the port** each chunk carries its region (`ChunkEntry.Region`), written from the table for every map set's chunk at a row and column it names, so a mod moves or adds a region in data; the rule stays in `ZoneEngine.EnterRegion`, which `TrekLoop.Enter` calls as the player comes into each chunk and `GameSession.StartCutScene` as a stage loads after trek. A new game builds trek's world before the opening ([events](events.md#where-the-port-differs)), so it finds region 1 then rather than after script 866; nothing reads it in between.
- **The towns' names** are six images with their own dot, drawn untinted whatever's found, from a table at `0x800d0b20`: Erromon `0x37BC` at (27, 60), Gwernia `0x37BD` (116, 41), Port Saiid `0x37BE` (23, 99), Talewok `0x37BF` (64, 86), Terminor `0x37C0` (41, 128) and Ugarit `0x37C1` (125, 178).
- **The party's place** is `0x00F6`, a 9-pixel ring, its top-left at x 35 + trunc((row − 1) × 6.15) and y 29 + trunc((column − 4) × 6.68), from the chunk's row (A is 1) and column, worked out once as the menu opens (`0x80049234`). Its colour pulses from black toward (165, 158, 255) a tenth a frame, by whole steps, so it peaks at (160, 150, 250), 20 frames a cycle (`0x80049428`). It shows only outdoors (sky type 3) with the minimap off. For a column below 4, the original's u16 wraps and puts it far off; no overworld chunk the party reaches has one.
- **Camp and Journal**, the tab's only cursor: Camp `0x00DD` at (215, 155) and Journal `0x00E6` at (192, 174), in (130, 80, 80), the cursor's pulsing from (68, 42, 34) toward (151, 141, 191) and back a step a frame, turning at 20, 40 frames a cycle (`WorldMenu.TabStep`, `0x80040dc4`). The cursor starts on Camp. Up and down move it, playing `0x73B` even when it doesn't move; left and right, and the C-buttons, do nothing.

### The journal

- **The book** is `0x0182`, 320 × 240 and opaque, over the tab, which keeps drawing beneath. It fades in 60 a frame (60, 120, 180, 240, 255) and out the same way. **A, B or Start** close it, back to the tab.
- **The entries** are a list from (64, 30), bounded to x 245 and y 220, in the main font wrapping at 181, 11 a line, 4 between entries. A blank line comes first and last. An entry's text is in (98, 70, 60); the newest's in (130, 80, 140). With none, "No entries have been made in the journal." (common 496) in the same brown (`0x80052310`).
- **Which, and in what order.** The original lists every entry whose flag is set, in its table's order, reading each entry's own flag by the number its text begins with (`Journal.AddEntry`, `0x8005256c`). The port lists them in the order they were added ([flags](flags.md), [saves](saves.md#the-format)), the user's choice, and then any whose flag is set but which the network hasn't added yet, as after a battle or a conversation, in the table's order (`WorldMenu`). The flags are plain but entry 32's, the lighthouse's, which is derived 5017, so reading it brings the network up to date as the journal opens, adding those entries and raising the notice, which comes once the menu has closed ([flags](flags.md#when-the-network-comes-up-to-date)).
- **The newest** is the entry whose flag last turned on (`newestJournal`, set by `set_journalentry_flag`). Opening the journal shows it in purple and forgets it (`Journal::Load`, `0x8005263c`, which takes the newest before it lists, and forgets it as that entry is listed). An entry the opening itself adds isn't purple then, but is the newest the next time. Loading turns every entry on again ([saves](saves.md#loading-as-the-original-does)), yet the original shows none purple after a load; how it forgets isn't known. The port leaves none the newest once a save has loaded (`FlagNetwork.Restore`).
- **It opens at the bottom** when the list is taller than 190, and otherwise at the top. In chronological order, the newest is at the bottom anyway.
- **Scrolling is by entry** (`WSL_DownFunc`, `WSL_UpFunc`): down brings the next entry whose foot is below 220 up until its foot meets 220, and up the first whose top is above 30 down until it meets 30, never past either end. The list slides 5 pixels a frame, and a press waits for it. Held, a direction keeps scrolling with no wait (`Journal::Control` falls back to the held bits).
- **The edges fade** into the page: strips of the book redrawn over the text from x 62 to 245, at 0.75, 0.5, 0.25 and 0.125 of its alpha from y 28 to 40, and the same from 220 up to 210.
- **The arrows**, `0x00D6` (8 × 36) at (247, 30) and `0x00D5` at (247, 184), in (130, 80, 80), each fading 30 a frame in while there's more that way: above 30 for the first item, below 220 for the last item (by the count). They draw only at the book's full alpha.
- **A new entry's notice**: when flag 3617 is set in trek, with no fade or popup up, the game clears it and says "A new journal entry has been added.", a literal in the code, in trek's message box (`NewJournalEntryPopup`, `0x80020eb0`; in the port `TrekLoop` clears it and fires `journal.added`, on which the base plugin's trigger says it, [events](events.md#the-base-plugins)). 3617 is a plain flag that the network sets as it comes up to date, which reading it doesn't do ([map objects](map-objects.md#map-objects-write-flags)), so a trigger's entry is told the tick after, and one a script or a battle sets when something next reads a derived flag. The port brings the network up to date before it looks, so every entry is told on trek's first tick with nothing up, the user's choice ([flags](flags.md#when-the-network-comes-up-to-date)). The original never says it after loading, though loading sets 3617; the port clears it once a save has loaded ([saves](saves.md)).

### In the port

- **The journal is drawn over the whole menu** (`JournalView`), centred on the screen, and the tabs and the scroll fade out under it as it fades in, leaving the darkened world beside it, the user's choice: on a wider or taller screen the scroll's rollers would show over the book's edges. The help menu and the books in the world show the same way ([ui](ui.md#books-and-the-help-menu)).
- **The ring** shows only in the Overworld, which the original's formula was made for; the original shows it in any outdoor map set without a minimap. "Outdoors" is the chunk's terrain drawing the sky.
- **Beside the tab in use**, on a screen wider than the frame, the tab is veiled as the others are ([changing tab](#changing-tab)), the user's choice. The party's ring isn't drawn and Camp and Journal don't pulse, though the cursor is kept. The veil (`WorldTabView.PlainPatches`) covers the calendar's days and moons, the painted map, leaving the torn edge's darkened rim, the compass rose's left and the arrow under the map. The map's pieces fade out at the rate they fade in once the tab has been left and the slide has finished, so they don't vanish as it's slid to again.
