# Wanderers: monster parties' bodies (M2 session 5)

`Aidyn.Core.Trek` (`Wanderers`, `Wanderer`) and `Aidyn.Core.Movement` (`Body`, `Crowd`). Ported from `wander.cpp`, `PlayerMove.cpp` (`Actor::SetAiDest`, `SetFacing`, `MoveTo`, `Move`) and `PlayerData.cpp` (`ProcessPlayers`, `PlayerShadowAlpha`, `renderPlayers`). What a monster party holds is in [maps](../formats/maps.md#map-objects-in-the-base-plugin-06).

A monster party is an NPC to talk to, if it has a dialogue, or a group of enemies the player sees as its leader. Either way it's one body in the world, which wanders between wander nodes. What it does on its own (5b) is below, and talking to it (5c) will be.

## Bodies as their chunk loads (5a)

- **When.** As a chunk loads, each of its monster parties that's active by its flags ([map-objects](map-objects.md#a-chunks-objects-at-run-time)) and not remembered as beaten gets a body (`look_for_monsterparties`, `AllocWanderer`); as it goes, they go (`FreeWandererFromZone`). The original skips this in dialogue mode and on the title screen.
- **No limit.** The original has room for 39 at once in trek (`WanderHead`) and crashes past it, at least in the debug build. At a new game the 3×3 around Overworld H13 holds 44 parties that would get one, the only block over 39 and the most anywhere; the user thinks the original leaves some out rather than crashing, but can't reach H13 to see. The port gives every party a body, as the user chose (Oct 2, 2026): mods will add their own, and there's no reason in play to leave any out. Across the Overworld's 172 chunks there are 441.
- **Towns behind walls.** Talewok's outskirts (I12) and the town (I13) are divided by a wall with a gate between them, a teleporter within the Overworld, and so are Port Saiid (C15) and C14; Gwernia and Erromon have no such wall. Both chunks are in the same 3×3 either way, so the wall doesn't keep their bodies from loading together. If it was for the original's sake, it was more likely to spare drawing a busy town from outside it.
- **Its look** is the party's entity's body ([actors](../formats/actors.md)): its actor, its collision radius, its scale and its height. Every entity has one, and 32 of the 37 dialogue entities; 28 parties are dialogue entities. The body is drawn at its scale, its animations carry it that much farther, and bodies push each other with spheres that much bigger; its sphere against the world keeps the radius as it is.
- **Where.** At the party's position, set down from the party's height as a teleport sets the player down (`Actor::CheckCollision`). All but one stand within 0.06 of the party's height; a bat in I08 is half a unit lower.
- **Its home node** must be one that wanderers walk to; every party's is. It takes the node's values (`WanderGetNextNode`), and if it's further from the node than the node's radius it sets off for it (`FUN_80012d44`), **walking if the party has flag 0x2 and running otherwise**. The decomp reads that flag as the node's; 1.0's code reads `voxelObjs[VoxelIndex] + 0x58`, the party's flags. 990 of the 1,079 parties walk. The user confirmed the 89 that run are the game's: a man in Port Saiid and a few monsters run about (Oct 2, 2026).
- **Kept in its chunk.** A body other than the player's is kept 0.05 inside its chunk's edges, since the original keeps it in its chunk's coordinates.

## Bodies moving (5a)

A `Body` is the original's `playerData` for a body other than Alaron's, stepped each tick as `ProcessPlayers` steps it:

- **Steering** (`MoveTo`). A body sent somewhere (`SetAiDest`) points its stick at the goal, at half tilt to walk or 0.95 to run, and the stick turns it 20° a frame as the player's does. The original routes the stick through the camera's turn and back, which cancels out. The stick follows the goal only while the body comes nearer than it's been, by 0.01; otherwise it keeps its last way, so a body that starts facing away walks a short curve. Within the goal's radius it stops. In trek a body that can't get nearer keeps trying; in cut-scenes the original gives up after 30 frames and puts it there.
- **Turning** (`SetFacing`) turns it 10° a frame where it stands (`MoveTo`'s `0x3E32B7FE`), half what it turns on the move. A body with neither a goal nor a turn lets go of its stick, and keeps whatever animation it was last asked for.
- **Then** the world's collision, the push down on plain collision, and its animation's root motion, as the player's ([movement](movement.md)). Bodies don't set off trigger triangles (`CSPHERE_NoTriggers`).
- **Only near the camera.** In trek, bodies more than 30 from the camera's aim don't move or animate at all (`PlayerHandler.float_0x68`; 128 in combat).

## Bodies pushing each other (5a)

After each body moves, the player first, it's pushed out of the others in its chunk within 3 in height (`ProcessPlayers`, `FUN_800b003c`; checked in 1.0's code). The push is across the ground only, straight away from the other until they touch, with each sphere at its scale, and the body's velocity bounces and dampens as off a wall. The other doesn't move.

- **The first push starts the turn over** from the second body, not the first, so the player, who is first, isn't weighed again. The body that pushed is weighed again, and touching counts, so whether the body stays or goes back depends on rounding.
- **A second push puts the body back** where it began the tick.
- **The nearest body** to each, across the ground, is kept for A (`playerData.index`, `unk108`). On a miss the original measures it with the other's radius at this body's scale, and so does the port.

## Drawing them (5a)

In the runtime, `WandererView` draws each body's actor at its feet, facing its heading. Bodies show within 20 of the camera's aim and fade over the last 2 units (`PlayerShadowAlpha`). The original also skips those more than 5 from the aim that are outside its view, which only saves drawing. Their shadows show within 10 of the aim, measured from the body's position in its own chunk's coordinates against the aim in the player's chunk's, so a body in a neighbouring chunk seldom has one ([movement](movement.md)).

## Wandering, noticing and chasing (5b)

`Wanderer.Tick`, run over them all by `Wanderers.Tick`, is `WanderTick` (`0x8000f6a4` in 1.0). Its flow was read from the machine code on Oct 2, 2026, by a Sonnet subagent with `tools/n64dis.py`, and the constants and the points marked below spot-checked; the decomp's `goto`s untangle to the same flow, with the differences noted. `FUN_80012c58` is `0x8000f4c8` in 1.0, `FUN_80012d44` `0x8000f5b4`, `WanderGetNextNode` `0x8000f3e0`.

- **When.** Each frame in trek, unless the screen is fading, the game is paused, any widget is up (a popup, a conversation, the menu: `WHANDLE->GetTail()`), or the game state isn't trek's. The runtime calls it each tick after the player moves, unless a popup is up or a teleport is fading, and the menu stops trek altogether. It doesn't care how far a wanderer is from the camera, though one beyond 30 doesn't move (above).
- **The timer** counts down by the frame's ticks every frame, whatever the wanderer is doing; the port counts 1 a tick. It's a u16 the code compares as signed (`lh`, `bgtz`), so it moves on at 0 or below and goes on counting down.
- **Perception** is the party's entity's (`EntityDB::GetPerception`, 10 or 0), and 10 for a dialogue entity. Some talkers' entities are plain ones with 0; talkers never use it. It's measured from a point 0.3 × perception ahead of the body to the player, across all three axes: *perceived*. The plain distance is used for talkers and the ambush.
- **The ranges** are set once, in doubles, when perceived ≤ 2 × perception and they aren't set: p = perception × (1 − `fogTime` × 0.75); walking p, running 1.5p, the most 2p, sneaking 0.6p × s, anything else 0.55p × s, where s = `sneak_value(0.8)` = 1 − min(the best Stealth, modified / 10, 1) × 0.8, or 0 with no party (`NoticeRanges`). The weather isn't ported, so fog is 0 for now. The party's Stealth counts as it was when they were set: they stay until they're cleared, below.
- **Giving up and ending a chase** come first. Let *both out* be the body and the player both at or beyond the party's radius (the leash) from the party's position, across the ground. If the wanderer is chasing and either perceived ≥ the most or both out, or if the ranges are set and perceived ≥ 2 × perception (chasing or not, talkers too):
  - both out, it stops where it stands (`ResetMoveQueue`), its timer becomes 300 and its node flag 4 is set (`0x8000fa38`–`fa54`, checked), so when the timer runs out it goes back to its goal rather than choosing another;
  - otherwise, if chasing, it goes back to its goal at once (`FUN_80012d44`);
  - either way the ranges are cleared, and the chase ends if perceived ≥ the most or the body is at least the leash + 2 from home. So a body just past its leash after a player still in range stops, and the chase sets it off again the same tick, until it's 2 past.
  - The code measures the player's distance from the party's position in the party's chunk's coordinates against the player's in his own (it converts the position, then uses the unconverted one), so for a wanderer in another chunk that was wrong. The port measures in the world's coordinates.
- **Noticing.** A hunter (`moveType` 1: a party without a dialogue) notices the player when its body is within the leash of home across the ground, the wanderer's flag 1 isn't set (`Calmed`: set by `setFlag1`, cleared by `FUN_80012d44`), and perceived ≤ the range for the player's request: 3 running, 2 walking, 25 sneaking, and the standing range for anything else. Each comparison includes its bound. The code also notes for the battle what the player was doing (`EncounterDat.aniByte` 3, 2, 1 or 0), for 2.5.
  - In the original, one hunter noticing makes every wanderer after it in the list chase that frame: the flag is cleared once before the loop (`0x8000f71c`, checked; set at `0x8000fb20`–`fb74`). So while one enemy sees Alaron, every enemy listed after it anywhere in the 3×3 runs at him, whatever its leash, and can ambush him, and talkers after it stop moving on. The port has each notice for itself, as the user chose (Oct 2, 2026). A faithful mode for the fidelity profile would keep the one flag.
- **Talkers** (`moveType` 0) within 3 of the player (strict, across all three axes) are asked for request 0 and turn to face him (`SetFacing`, which forgets their goal), every frame. While turning they don't move on, so a talker stays facing him while he's near; when he leaves it waits out its timer.
- **Moving on.** Not noticed and not chasing: when the body isn't moving, turning or held (`playerData` flags & 0x70) and the timer is 0 or below, it goes back to its goal with flag 4 (clearing it), or else chooses the next node (`FUN_80012c58`), and sets off (`FUN_80012d44`). Talkers wander so too.
  - **The next node:** a roll of `GetFloat0ToX(1)` under the current node's chance takes its second next node, otherwise the first; there's no check on either. `WanderGetNextNode` copies the new node's values in, its wait among them, so **the wait starts when the node is chosen**, and a long walk uses it up. If the new node is one to walk to, its scatter follows: an angle `GetFloat0ToX(2π)` (`0x40c90fdc`, checked) and a length `GetFloat0ToX(scatter)`, in that order, the goal moved by (sin, cos) × length in X and Z. The first node, as the body appears, has none. Some nodes name themselves as both next nodes (I08's 0x0FB6 node 211), so the wanderer picks a new spot in the same node each time.
  - **Setting off** (`FUN_80012d44`) clears flag 1; walks or runs to the goal if it's beyond the node's radius, by the party's flag 0x2 (above); and at a node to turn to, turns to face the node: it's given the way from the node to the body (`Vec2Sub`, checked), which `SetFacing` takes as the original's reversed facing. 5a's port turned it away from the node.
- **Chasing.** Noticed or still chasing, it's chasing, and a hunter runs at the player, within 2 (`SetAiDest` with walk 0, `0x8000fc0c`, checked: the flag it sets is `ACTOR_WALKONLY`), again every frame.
  - **The ambush:** within 7 of him (strict, `0x8000fc28`, checked), with `enemyHostileFlag` (1, and nothing in 1.0 clears it) and no dialogue: if |Δy| < 3 (the decomp's test is mangled) and a sphere of radius 0.5 swept from the body's centre + 1 to the player's + 1 hits nothing in the wanderer's chunk (`CheckCollision`), the battle starts as an ambush (`collisionByte` 2) and it stops hunting (`moveType` 0). The port sweeps the whole loaded world. Battles wait for combat (2.5): the runtime logs the ambush, and the body stops and from then on acts as a talker.
- **Flee** (`moveType` 2, running directly away to within 1) is in the code, but nothing sets it.
- **Random numbers** come from the wandering stream. `GetFloat0ToX` (`0x80093428`) is 0 without a draw for 0, and otherwise `GetFloat0To1` (`0x800933d4`) × X in single precision: the new seed as an unsigned number, rounded to a float, × 2⁻³² (`0x2f800000`), which is X itself for the top 128 values. The decomp converts the seed as signed.
