# Combat parity (2.9)

Roadmap 2.9's differential tests: the port's [combat](combat.md) checked against 1.0's own execution, in two halves. **This is a record of what was checked and what it found, not a set of running tests.** The vectors, the replay and the checkpoint tests that read 1.0's RAM were retired on 2026-11-13: the combat checkpoints, the parity logs and the saves written for them were lost to a cleanup, and the port now differs from 1.0 on purpose. The findings stand in the topic docs, and the differences the port keeps are the lists below and each system's "where the port differs". A rerun would need new captures and new tests; the tooling is the [reference rig](../reference-rig.md#combat-parity-logs-toolsparityps1)'s. The port's own battle features are tested against `TestBattles` (story save A's party against ten rats in arena 3, built with no 1.0 RAM).

- **The flow**: battles the user played in 1.0 from saves written for them, logged by `tools/parity.ps1` and replayed into the port. Setups 1–14 were played; no more were planned, the user judging the port at combat parity (Nov 12).
- **The formulas**: 1.0's own functions run in the interpreter on a combat checkpoint's (the party against ten rats) or a log's RAM with the inputs varied, against the port's with the same draws ([below](#the-formula-vectors)). Every family was done.

What the logs settled about 1.0 itself is written in the topic docs: [combat](combat.md), [combat-actions](combat-actions.md), [combat-ai](combat-ai.md) and [magic](magic.md).

## The replay

The replay built each logged battle (a `battle-NN` folder of `out/parity`) from the RAM at `Combat.Setup`. Before each of the port's combat stream's draws, the stream is set to where the original's was before its draw of the same rank (`Lcg.BeforeDraw`). Draws whose callers are cosmetic (`CosmeticCode`: the weather, particles, audio keys, the sky and the spells' visuals) are skipped, since the port draws them on its cosmetic stream or not at all; the ring's retries, which the port draws on its cosmetic stream, are fed from the original's ring draws. The log unwinds a draw only three callers deep, so a deep ring retry's callers are all `CombatMarkers.RingPoint`, with nothing of the turn it's for.

It checked:

- **The setup**: its draws, one for one, and no `Battle.Problems`.
- **The first turn**: whose it is, the turn order, and each combatant's cell (to 0.0001), direction, footprint and radius.
- **The first ring**: as many ring draws, and every teardrop within 0.01, turned by the rings 1.0 laid before it in the session (`RingsBefore`), as 1.0's statics drift 0.006° a ring.
- **Each action**: the RAM at each turn's first `Combatant.ExecuteAction` laid over the battle, and the port's `Battle.ExecuteAction` run with the original's attacker, target and kind (the spell, potion and targets as chosen).
- **Each enemy's turn**: the port's AI playing from the RAM at its `TurnOrder.BeginTurn`, the bodies moved by their animations' roots, against the original's `Combatant.ExecuteAction`: whether it acted, whom it went for, and where it stood, to 0.5.

After an action or an enemy's turn, the next turn is checked as it begins, at the first cue after the port's `Advance` has chosen it, against the RAM dumped there: the draws' count, whose turn it is, every combatant's stats, skills, protection, weapon, death and flight, and each living enemy's AI record (kind, target, morale and flags). Bodies' places and the grid are shown, not checked. A won battle is checked at `Combat.Exit`, with the rewards' draws on the port's loot stream.

**The overlay** laid a dump's RAM over the port's battle. It had to lay everything the next turn reads, and each of these was missing once, so each is something 1.0 keeps and a reimplementation can miss:

- each combatant's effects, slot for slot with their timers, laid on without starting them, so what starting does is laid apart: Mirror's counter (`+0x28`), the sheet's protection, the battle's held part of the day (Light or Darkness's), Acid's corrosion as the protection it takes, and Wraith Touch's drained amount, which 1.0 keeps in the record's power byte (`+0x01`);
- the weapon the RAM wields, and the skills' bytes as they are (not through `CharacterSkills.Train`, which caps a base at the trainer's 10: Lugash's Wizard is 15);
- each worn item's battery (its `StatMod` at `+0xC`), which a cast draws before Stamina;
- the round's turn order, and reserves that have joined at the RAM's cells;
- the cells as 1.0's records last read them, put back after the target sets are rebuilt, since the height term of an attack's chance reads the cells (`ArenaGrid.HeightDifference`).

**The player's A in a walk** (`PressedA`): A skips an AI's walk, putting the body at its leg's end ([combat-ai](combat-ai.md#a-turn)). The logger records the press (`Combat.InputMoving` at its call of `Body.GiveUp`) and lists what it watched at `Combat.Setup`, and the replay pressed A as many ticks into the walk. For logs made before it watched (setup 2's first session and earlier), it inferred it: a walk the port's outlasts at six ticks a frame, with the original's body at the port's leg's end after it.

**Known differences** were two lists keyed by battle and dump, whose turns were logged rather than failed:

- **The cadence**: enemy turns that differ by 1.0's frame lengths alone. The logger slows 1.0 to frames of 3–6 ticks, where the port steps 2, so a body that sets off facing away curves wider and may give up, and a walk carries further past its arrival before 1.0 decides ([combat-ai](combat-ai.md#where-the-port-differs)). The replay didn't play 1.0's frame lengths, the user's choice, as it's the logger that slowed them. A turn goes on the list only once its difference is shown to be the frame lengths'.
- **The fixes**: actions and turns where the port fixes one of 1.0's bugs or differs by the user's choice.

## The known differences

**The cadence:**

- Setup 2's 0018 and second session's 0016, setup 4's 0006 and setup 8's 0037: the first corner lies astern, and 1.0's body, turning 20° a frame at the logger's long frames, walks a wide curve away and gives up near its start; the port's turns round and walks on. At 4–6-tick frames the port's gives up too; at the second session's 0016 it curves the other way. Which way a body whose first corner is dead astern first curves is left unsettled, the user's choice: 1.0's own `Facing.Turn` turns as the port's does, so the difference lies elsewhere in the walk's first frames, and in play a body turning left or right from a corner straight behind it looks the same.
- Setup 2's 0096, setup 6's 0040, setup 7's 0024, setup 8's 0018, setup 11's 0009 and setup 13's 0062: 1.0 decides two frames after a walk arrives, and the logger's frames carry its body on into (or out of) reach before it does; the port decides 2–3 ticks after, as 1.0 does at 30 fps.
- The second trial's slot 5, a reserve rat on its first turn, whose turn has no action, so the replay shows it rather than listing it: its first corner lay a quarter-turn off, and 1.0's body, idle as its turn began (state 0, clock 3, no fidget in a rat's actor), came no nearer than its start (`+0x258` 5.657) within the 30 ticks the stuck test counts over the whole point at the logger's 3-tick frames, drifting 0.26 back along its way in, and gave up by its spawn at (17, 43); the port's turns round and runs its leg toward (23, 49), 4.6 on. It's taken as the cadence's, the user's choice, without playing it at 1.0's frame lengths.
- Setup 11's 0068 isn't the cadence: 1.0's Hobgoblin was mid-fidget (its actor's state 12, clock 27 of 101), which takes no run request, so its walk gave up on its cell. The replay starts every body idle; seeded in that state, the port's does as 1.0's. Laying each actor's state and clock from the RAM would take it off the list, which the user judged not worth the scaffolding: the port's actors fidget and refuse a run mid-fidget as 1.0's do, so only the replay's starting state differs.

**Places left unchecked**, by the user's choice:

- The attacker's body after a blow stands up to 0.5 from the original's (a backstab), with its footprint a cell over. An attack's animation carries its body by its root, sometimes far (a Giant Bat hit from behind can be thrown out of reach of whoever hit it), and the port's does as 1.0's in play.

**The port's fixes:**

- Setup 3's four Fireballs, cast with a flask in hand, do their damage.
- Setup 5b's 0064, setup 8's 0016 and setup 10's 0008: 1.0's body stuck on another by the push's rounding and gave up; the port's slides on (`Crowd.Rounding`). With the slide off, each ends within 0.4 of 1.0's.
- Setup 11's 32 Hobgoblin turns: a 2-cell caster's own footprint no longer blocks its cast.

The fixes the logs found besides, which the replay couldn't list because it read 1.0's RAM: a talk's collision byte by the cone (`TrekLoop.TalkByte`) and the animation byte as what the player was doing when last noticed (`Wanderers.NoticedAs`), both from setup 9; a 2-cell caster's footprint, setup 11; a reserve in an ambush whose own point is taken coming in on its own side's points rather than the party's, setup 1 (at the end of slot 5's turn, slot 6 at `good0d` (13, 26) for 1.0's `bad0a` (24, 39), and slot 7 at `bad0a` for `bad0b` (19, 23)); the body set-down's draws after the turn order, setup 13; corrosion off the protection byte, which wrapped to 254, setup 3; and the casts' line naming the caster, setups 7 and 7b.

Two traps for a family that calls these functions:

- **Pointers as arguments must be sign-extended.** `FlaskExplosion` compares each record with the target's by the whole register (`0x8006546C`, `0x80065494`, `0x80065590`), and `lw` sign-extends the pointers it loads. A zero-extended target pointer is taken for someone else and clamped as one.
- **The blast's visual reaches the hardware.** `CombatStatIndicator.StartPotion` (`0x8008AA28`), which `AttachSpellVisual` calls, is stubbed. `ShowDamage`, `CheckDeath` and the log run as they are.

**Morale and the AI**: `Morale` (144), `MayCheckMorale` (22), `CheckFlee` (35) and `ReserveFleesOnArrival` (33) as functions; a turn (83) as `TurnOrder.BeginTurn` begins it, the check to flee as it asks, the body read, state 8 and the target sets, then `EnemyAI.Step`; and `FleeAdvance` (11) as state 6 calls it. On checkpoint 05 every term of [combat-ai](combat-ai.md#morale)'s table is varied with its edges (exactly half the enemies and the party able, the leader's morale from 7 to 12 and past, a record's morale from −128 to 127, each part of the day for either aspect, eight effects harmful and not), with the leader gone or unable, the morale spells at three levels, a weakness's flags, an engaged enemy and an arena without flee points, and the 2d6 both sides of the line. The turns are rats made styles 0–4 (a bow in hand for 2–4), near and far, with the truncated distances tied so that the sort's draw picks the target; a flight with its turns, engaged and with no flee points; and `FleeAdvance` by its turns left and the distance from its turn's start, at a unit exactly. 47 are on parity logs: setup 13's Gorgons after Solar Wrath, fleeing at once in arena 12, and their leader's turns; setup 4's Bandit Woodsmen and setup 5's Goblin Poisoners (style 3) at night and by day, a Poisoner's flight among them; and setup 11's Hobgoblin with a spell for its own side selected and its target spent.

### The tooling

A family was one Python module (`tools/aivectors/families/<name>.py`: `cases()` listing the vectors, `run(vector)` running one) and one C# test class, with `formulas.py` writing the table between them; both the tables and the tests are retired. A rerun would need new tests that build the port's battle from the same state, poke it as the vector does and compare names and values. What a family has to know about the interpreter:

- **Draws:** every draw on the main stream goes through `Rng.Next` or `Rng.Float0To1`, which `draws.py` hooks (`Float0To1` steps the stream itself). A draw's function is named by unwinding the generator's frames by their prologues, as the parity logger does.
- **Stubs:** none were needed for melee; the log lines and `sprintf` run in the interpreter. `Entity.SenseAura` opens a popup that halts the interpreter in `__osEnqueueAndYield`, so a casting family stubs it (`st.cpu.hooks[address] = lambda cpu: 0`). `PrepareCast` sets the acted flag; each vector is a fresh copy of the RAM, so nothing carries from one to the next.
- **States:** a parity log's `0002.rdram.gz` is its `Combat.StartFirstTurn`, where every slot is the setup's; a later dump works too, with the reserves that joined brought in. A function that reads the cells reads them as the records last read the bodies (`ArenaGrid.HeightDifference`).
- **Cost:** a vector runs in a few milliseconds in the interpreter.

### The known differences the formulas found

All are the user's choices, written where the system is ([magic](magic.md#where-the-port-differs), [combat](combat.md#in-and-out), [combat-actions](combat-actions.md#where-the-port-differs)):

- **The Stamina spell** raises Stamina; 1.0's lowers it by 2L as it lands and 2L more as it ends.
- **The enemies may hold the whole party**; 1.0 limits an enemy's hold by the enemies held (`CheckImmobilisingLimit`, whatever the caster's side).
- **A charm at a target its kind doesn't allow** (Control Elem at a non-elemental, the Marquis, the Zombies) charms; 1.0 adds a harmless record. Targeting rules the target out first, so play never reaches it.
- **Twelve sure ones under a most past 12**: 1.0 rolls the extras and writes a thirteenth entry over the bonus loot; the port stops at 12.
- **A flask's target** shows and says both of the blast's and the blow's shares; the last flask of a stack bursts; an attack's line of sight runs head to head; Wind holds the weather clear.
- Not run: the five casters' cases that wear a weapon as armor or a shield to give an action slot a spell, which the port's equipment won't wear and no enemy in the data has.
