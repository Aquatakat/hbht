# Maps: chunk lists and Borg9 (roadmaps 1C and 1E)

A map set is a grid of chunks, and each chunk is a Borg9 (collision, its grid and the map objects) plus up to two Borg5 scenes. `MapChunks` reads the chunk lists, `Borg9Map` the collision and grid, `Borg9Voxels` the map objects (`VoxelObject.cs`) and `CollisionGltf` writes the collision as glTF, all in `HBHT.Rom.Maps`. The base plugin has a `chunk` record per cell, which holds all of it ([below](#chunks-in-the-base-plugin-adr-0035)).

## Map objects

The original calls them voxel objects (`RenderVoxelScenes` in all three ROMs' strings; `GetVoxelObjectFromLink`, `ProcessVoxelObjects` and "Invalid Voxel Object for trap" in the debug ROM's). The port calls them map objects, keyed by their chunk (`aidyn:mapobject/8/C05/17`, ADR 0035), and says "voxel" only to cite the original.

- **Layout.** A map object is 0x6C bytes: a 0x28-byte header (position, radius, a run-time time stamp, flags, type, two links, flags A, B and C, and a run-time pointer), then 0x44 bytes that depend on the type. The Borg9 header's count at 0x28 and pointer at 0x44 find them.
- **The types.** Scene objects place 1 or 2 levels of detail of a Borg5, a Borg7 actor, or a container's model followed by its opened and destroyed models. They scale, then rotate, then move, and rotate by `guRotateRPYF` with roll = `rot.x`, pitch = `rot.y` and heading = `rot.z`, in radians: `RenderVoxelScenes` passes `rot.y, rot.x, rot.z` to `Scene::MatrixARotate`, which swaps its first two back. That isn't the Borg5 transforms' order ([scenes](scenes.md), matrix conventions): the chests' baked collision boxes, the 11 knocked over among them, line up with their models only in this one. Lights hold two colours and a way of moving between them (static, alternate, sine, random), and light the player and the maps' actors within their radius ([rendering](../systems/rendering.md#point-lights)). Sounds play a Borg12 with a volume and a random wait. Wander nodes form chains, each naming the next two. Cameras move the camera or its aim to a reference point (or above the player) by cutting, panning or easing in three ways, or set how far behind and how high the outdoor camera follows ([cameras](../systems/cameras.md#camera-objects-processvoxelcamera)). Code objects and save points hold nothing.
- **The three flag slots.** A flag check is the flag's value XOR an invert bit, where flag 0 is always false and flag 1 always true, so an inverted 0 means "always".
  - **C** (inverted by bit 0x80) says whether a scene, light, sound, teleporter or trigger object (the port's tripwire) is there at all.
  - **A** (inverted by 0x100) says whether a teleporter, trigger object or dialogue works, and whether a monster party is there.
  - **B** is written when the object is used: set, or cleared if bit 0x200 is on. A monster party without a dialogue (the port's encounter) is gone while its B is set, and winning its fight sets it. With a dialogue (the port's npc), talking to it sets B. A dialogue object's B always equals its script's done flag, and the game doesn't read it.
- **Type-specific flags:** containers have an "opened" and an "exploded" flag; teleporters have lock, unlock, trap, disarm and secret-door flags.
- **Dead writes:** the game only writes plain flags from 2 up. Two SetFlag trigger objects name derived flag 5495 and flag 0, and six monster parties name derived flags as their B.

## Collision, the grid and placement (1C)

- **Layout.** A 0x50-byte header, then six parts, each 8-byte aligned: the cells' triangle indices (u16), their object indices (u16), the vertices (3 floats), the triangles, the map objects and the cells (0x14 bytes each). The header holds the grid's origin (0x00, 3 floats) and cell size (0x0C, 2 floats), the u16 counts of the triangle indices (0x14) and object indices (0x18), the columns and rows (0x1C, 0x1E), the vertex count (0x20, u32), the triangle, object and cell counts (0x24, 0x28, 0x2A), and the offsets from the start of the data of the triangle indices (0x2C), object indices (0x34), vertices (0x38), triangles (0x3C), objects (0x44) and cells (0x48). The offsets at 0x30 and 0x40 are fixed up by the game and never used. 24–48 bytes follow the cells, all zeros except in one file.
- **Neighbour bytes.** Byte 0x1B is a bit for each neighbour the zone engine draws whatever the camera's view while the player is in the chunk, imported as the chunk's `drawnNeighbours` ([rendering](../systems/rendering.md#scrolling-textures)). Byte 0x1A is a bit for each neighbour the zone engine doesn't load while the player is in the chunk: as `LoadGameBorgScenes` (`0x8000b05c`) builds the 3×3, the centre first, it reads the centre's byte (`0x8000b374`) and frees each neighbour whose bit is set (`Zones.FreeCell`) before the rest are built, so that chunk has no scenery, collision, map objects or wanderers ([wanderers](../systems/wanderers.md#bodies-as-their-chunk-loads)). The bits are in the order of `Zones.UnloadedNeighbours` (`0x800cd59c`), as row and column within the 3×3 from its north-west corner: (0, 0), (1, 0), (2, 0), (0, 1), (2, 1), (0, 2), (1, 2), (2, 2), so 0x07 is the west column. 63 of the Overworld's chunks set it, and no other set's (H13 0x07, H12 0xE1, H14 0xA7, G12 0xAF among them): chunks hidden from the centre, behind the tree walls round Gwernia and the wall between the northern forest and Talewok, or already drawn out past their own chunk's edge: where a chunk's scenes copy a neighbour, it's the one the byte leaves out (H08's copy of I09, E14's of D13, B14's of A15, C15's of B14, C14 and D14). The port doesn't read it and loads the whole 3×3, the user's choice, and the importer takes those copies out of the scenes ([rendering](../systems/rendering.md#draw-distance)).
- **Triangles** are 0x20 bytes: three vertices as byte offsets into the vertex list, a unit normal, a surface (u32 at 0x18), flags (u16 at 0x1C) and a ground type (u16 at 0x1E). They wind counter-clockwise about their normals, apart from a few with no area and some slivers.
- **Surfaces** are the 26 physics properties the game names in `String/Common`: Default, Collision Player And Camera, Collision Player, then footstep materials such as Grass, Stone and Water, and "Trans" versions of each. The data uses 19 of them. Most walls and floors are *Player And Camera*. *Player* is for invisible walls: all of them, and only them, have flag 0x200, which line-of-sight tests skip, so the camera passes through. Whatever touches those two surfaces keeps its speed and slides along; every other surface stops it (`EnvPropBools`). Water has a speed property that halves speed and nearly cancels gravity, but no triangle has flag 0x2, which applies it.
- **Flags** say which edges and corners collide, besides the face: 0x4 is edge A→B, 0x10 is B→C and 0x8 is C→A, and 0x20, 0x40 and 0x80 the corners A, B and C. Each edge and corner has its bit on the first triangle in the file that has it, and on no other, so the game tests each once: they don't mark which are exposed, and a flat quad's diagonal has its bit like a wall's end. A few break the rule: 441 shared edges are flagged on both triangles and 15 on neither, and 13 corners nowhere ([movement](../systems/movement.md#narrow-gaps)). No triangle is passable (0x1) as stored.
- **Ground types.** The top four bits give a triangle's role, and the game rewrites the rest when it loads the chunk:
  - Most are ordinary.
  - **Triggers**: the low bits index a map object, which runs when the player walks on the triangle. They name cameras and dialogues. These are trigger triangles, the collision's role; the trigger *object* is the port's tripwire.
  - **Toggles**, with an id one more than the low bits. A `ChangeCollision` trigger object (a tripwire that changes collision) that names the id makes them passable, so the way opens. Every toggle id has its tripwire in the same Borg9, and every such tripwire has its triangles.
  - **Expansion Pak only**. Without the pak, the game makes them passable.
- **The grid** starts at the collision's minimum corner, with cells about 5 units square. Each cell lists the triangles near it, and the objects the player could use from it or that it draws: scenes, containers, lights, teleporters, cameras, dialogues, triggers and the three save points. Sounds, wander nodes, monster parties and reference points are handled per chunk. Pressing A weighs only the objects the cell under the player lists (`obj_ref_func`), so the lists are imported as each chunk's `grid`. Every cell an object's reach covers lists it. `getZonePositionShorts` finds the cell by truncating toward zero, so up to a cell before the grid's start counts as its first column or row, and a point off the grid either way counts as cell (0, 0). So beyond its collision's extent, a chunk's objects are out of A's reach unless the first cell lists them. Traps in the data: one dialogue, `aidyn:mapobject/6/C05/0`, is off its map's 3 × 3 grid altogether, so A never reaches it; 26 triangles, in 8 Borg9s, are in no cell, so nothing collides with them; and Chaos Isle's A09 (Borg9 5019) has a box of 8 triangles about 297,000 units away, which stretches its grid to cells 12,383 units wide, so every collision test there checks every triangle.
- **Map chunk lists** are 17 zero-terminated lists of 12-byte entries (row, column, scene, alternate scene, Borg9, terrain), back to back in the code segment, at `0xDBD90` in 1.0, `0xDBDD0` in 1.1 and `0xEA850` in the debug ROM, identical in all three. Some Borg9s aren't in any list, and 9 are in more than one entry ([below](#chunks-in-the-base-plugin-adr-0035)). The Battlefields' list is also the arenas' numbering: arena n is its nth entry (`Zones.ArenaChunks`, `0x800DCA74`).
- **Chunks in a set.** A chunk's own coordinates start at (row × cell size, 0, column × cell size): rows run along X. The cell size is a literal in `BorgMaps::LoadMap`: 100, except 500 for the misc interiors, 30 for Erromon, 200 for Terminor, 25 for the Barrows, 1000 for Ehud's tower and 150 for the battlefields. Neighbouring chunks' collision overlaps by 10–30 units.
- **Towns.** No scene is in the chunks of two map sets, so no town is modelled twice. Gwernia (M06, M07), Jundar (H13, I13), Erromon (B08–C09), Port Saiid (B15–D15) and the Goblin Camp (A04–C07) are out of doors in the Overworld, and their own map sets hold only their interiors and caves, each reached by a teleporter from those Overworld chunks. Terminor (G01–G05), Ugarit (rows M and N) and Gwernia Castle's courtyards are out of doors in their own sets, which the Overworld enters by one or two teleporters (I19; S27 and T27; M06). Terrain 19 marks a town's streets: the Overworld's B09, C08, C09, H13, I13, M06 and M07, and Terminor's G01–G05.
- **A chunk's second scene** is drawn after everything else (`renderTransZones_`), so it holds see-through surfaces such as water. It isn't an alternative to the first.
- **The lit lighthouse.** As `MakeGameZoneNames` (`0x8000aa94`) fills each cell of the 3×3 from its map set's list, `CheckToggleZoneScene` (`0x8000aa14`) tests the cell's first scene, and nothing else of it, for Overworld A15's (`0x0EA9`) and Lighthouse A05's (`0x11F9`). While 5017 holds it puts the lit one in its place, `0x35BA` and `0x35AF`, which are in no chunk list, and leaves the second scene and the map as they are. 5017 is derived from plain 286 alone, which the scroll's cut-scene sets (`aidyn:script/0x04F1`, node 10); reading it brings the network up to date ([flags](../systems/flags.md#when-the-network-comes-up-to-date)). `LoadGameBorgScenes` keeps a loaded cell only while its scene and map both match the new one (`Zones.KeepCell`, `0x8000a94c`), so once 5017 changes, the next load of the 3×3 drops the cell and loads it whole, objects and wanderers too: a crossing, a teleport, the pause menu closing, a cut-scene's stage, or trek built again, as it is after the scroll's cut-scene, whose stage loaded dark. Nothing swaps while the player stands still.
  - *What's lit.* The lit A15 is the dark one's mesh, vertex for vertex, but for one quad high on the tower (a masked, textured quad, Borg1 4578, becomes a small grey untextured one) and the transforms of some nodes. The lit A05 bakes its lantern room's vertex colours several times brighter, moves 52 of its 242 vertices, and is the one chunk scene with lights of its own, a directional (0.6, 0.775, 1) and a white ambient, which change nothing seen in 1.0 ([rendering](../systems/rendering.md#a-scenes-own-lights)); it carries eight still Borg6s and is the scene of an actor (`0x35B8`) that nothing places. The lantern coming alight and turning is neither: it's actor `0x0DE0`, scene object 5 of A05's map, which Animate trigger 1 plays once 277 is set.
  - *In the port.* The importer reads the swaps from the code (`ToggledScenes`, the instructions from the first test on, which all three ROMs share) and gives each chunk whose first scene is a dark one a swap (`ChunkRecord.Swap`): the flag, the lit scene and the chunk's own second scene. It welds the lit scene's seams to the chunks round it as the dark one's are. `ChunkStreamer` loads a chunk with its swap's scenes while the flag holds (`ChunkRecord.Drawn`), and loads a chunk again whole on `Update` or `Regate` when the flag says other scenes than it was loaded with, as 1.0 does. Draw Distance's ring draws the same, reading the flag as it's stored so that drawing changes nothing. The editor draws a chunk's own scenes.
- **Collision glTF.** The importer writes each Borg9's collision as `assets/aidyn/collision/0xHHHH.glb` in the base plugin (`CollisionGltf`), which every chunk copied from it names as its `collision`. Each vertex carries its triangle's surface, role, flags and target as the attributes `_SURFACE`, `_ROLE` (a `CollisionRole`'s number), `_FLAGS` and `_TARGET` (`CollisionMesh`'s constants, which the writer and the reader share): the target is the object a trigger triangle runs, by its index in the chunk, or a toggle's id, and 0 otherwise.

## Chunks in the base plugin (ADR 0035)

`MapImporter` writes a `chunk` record per cell (`ChunkRecord`), a file each at `records/chunks/<set>/<name>.json`, which the plugin set reads only once it's asked for (ADR 0036): the map set's entry for the cell (`WorldImporter.BuildCells`: scenes, terrain, music, region, swap, fill, Draw Distance's Extended and what it shows) with its Borg9's objects, grid and drawn neighbours. A map set's record lists no chunks; the plugin set finds a cell's chunk by its ID (`ChunkIndex`), and the minimap is on the map set (`minimap`).

- **The name** is the original's: `"New Grid: %d-%c%02d"` in the debug build prints the map set, the row's letter (`row − 1 + 'A'`) and the column, so `aidyn:chunk/8/C05` is Terminor's row 3, column 5. The 24 sea cells the importer makes north of the Overworld's row A, rows 0 and −1 (columns 13–17 and 19–25), have no letter: a cell outside rows A–Z is named `<row>_<column>`, `aidyn:chunk/0/0_13` and `aidyn:chunk/0/-1_13` (`ChunkRecord.NameOf`). The original has no such cells.
- **680 chunks**: the original's 543 entries and the 137 cells of sea the importer makes for fills ([rendering](../systems/rendering.md#draw-distance)).
- **The 9 shared Borg9s are copied** into each cell that uses them, so each object has one ID. Each copy's `borg` names the Borg9 it came from (provenance, which nothing reads), and its `collision` the one asset of it:

| Borg9 | Cells |
| --- | --- |
| `0x3559` (sea, no objects) | Overworld L23, S17, S18, T17 and the 137 sea cells (141) |
| `0x0E87` | Battlefields A01, A03 |
| `0x0E88` | Battlefields C01, C03, G01, G03, K11 |
| `0x0E89` | Battlefields E01, E03 |
| `0x0E8A` | Battlefields I01, I03, I05, I07 |
| `0x3629` | Battlefields K01, K03 |
| `0x363B` | Battlefields A11, A13 |
| `0x3675` | Battlefields E11, M17 |
| `0x3672` | Battlefields M15, M21 |

- **References to a cell are chunk IDs**: a teleporter's `destination`, a script object's `stage`, the new game's chunk, a non-lethal battle's `end.place`, the title's fly-throughs and a terrain's arenas. The arena numbers the original keeps (a terrain's ranges, a script's SetBattlefield) are resolved into the Battlefields' chunks by their place in its list (`Arenas.Chunks`), so a terrain's `arenas` are groups of chunks and `battle.arena` names one ([combat](../systems/combat.md)). A fly-through's `origin` needn't be a chunk: the first's, Overworld K06, isn't one.

- **Gates that always hold aren't written.** A slot whose flag is 0 inverted always holds, and the importer leaves it out, so a missing `visible` or `enabled` means always (14,082 and 1,751 objects). A gate on flag 0 not inverted, which never holds, is written: 6,666 reference points, 6 scenes and 2 teleporters have one, and 8 dialogues, 2 teleporters and 4 monster parties an `enabled` that never holds.
- **The Expansion Pak.** Header bit 0x800 marks an object only there with the Pak (1,082) and 0x1000 one only there without it (4: a sound in `0x0E96` and three scenes in `0x11AF`, which nothing links to). The port always plays as with the Pak, so the importer leaves those four out, with them their grid cells' listings, and writes no `pak`.
- **Trigger objects are tripwires** (`TripwireObject`), so that trigger records (2.10) have the word to themselves: `does` is a `setFlag` (`flag`), an `animate` (`request`, asked of the linked actor) or a `changeCollision` (`toggle`, `keepFlags`, `addFlags`). The original's kinds 0, 3 and 5 do nothing and no object has one; the importer refuses one (258 set a flag, 35 animate, 9 change collision).
- **Camera objects' three floats** (+0x08 to +0x10) are the pitch, the distance and the ticks a move takes, written `pitch`, `distance` and `ticks`.
- **Scene objects' models.** The first `lods` of a scene object's models are its levels of detail; a container's model has one level and then its opened and destroyed models. The importer writes the levels as `models` and the other two as `opened` and `destroyed`: the 370 objects containers link to are exactly those with one level and three models. Scene flag 0x8 is set on no object and read nowhere in 1.0 (every read of the flags, `RenderVoxelScenes` `0x8000C67C`, `MapObjects.SetActorLights` `0x80013AC4`, `Zones.FreeCell` `0x8000A798` and the queueing at `0x8000C524`, tests 1, 2, 4, 0x10, 0x20 or 0x40), so it isn't imported.

- **Which flag slots each type reads** comes from the "is it there" functions (`gVoxelFuncs2`) and the use functions (`gVoxelUseFuncs`). Lights, sounds, wander nodes, reference points, cameras, scenes, teleporters and tripwires are there while C holds. Monster parties are there while A holds, and without a dialogue, while B is clear. Dialogues and save points are always there. Chests, sacks, crates and barrels are there until opened or destroyed, and herb, spice, gem and custom containers are gated by A and C. A record leaves out the slots its type doesn't read, and flag B where it's 0.
- **Header bits.** 0x8000 (active) is only ever set at run time, from the gates. 0x2000 (tangible) matters for teleporters, tripwires and dialogues, which go off when walked into; a tangible teleporter skips its secret, trap and lock. 0x400 marks what the player can reach to use. 0x4000 is set on most scenes, containers, teleporters, tripwires and dialogues, and on no other type; nothing is known to read it, and it isn't imported. A light's 0x1 (24 in EhudUnder and one in JundarInteriors A01) makes it shine from the camera's eye (`WorldLights.AddNear`, `0x8004d384`), imported as `facesCamera`.
- **Links.** Both link slots always hold the same index, and the game only reads the first. To find a container's model or the actor a tripwire animates, `GetVoxelObjectFromLink` follows links from object to object until it reaches the type it wants, up to 51 steps. Every container reaches its scene, and every tripwire that animates an actor. Code objects all link to object 0.
- **Containers.** The trap's level is at +0x0E and the word before it is its state (0 while armed, 0xFF once disarmed or set off) (`TrapMenu::GetTrapLV` returns +0x0C, the state, and the menu reads the level from the word after it). The state is 0 in all the data. The lock's level is the u16 at +0x14. A loot table is found by `GetIDIndex`, which for a potion's ID returns its low byte, so the two containers in Borg9 0x10F5 whose loot names a potion roll the loot table at that position, and the import names that table. A monster party's loot can be a single item. **A container's reach** is the float at +0x04 (`chestSize`), not the header's radius; reaches run from 1 to 5, most often 2. The word at +0x00 is a copy of the radius. `container_obj_check` uses the reach, plus the player's scaled radius, and nothing reads the radius for containers (the start chest opens from about 2 away, facing any way). Imported as `ContainerObject.Reach`.
- **Teleporters.** The trap's level is at +0x0A (the word before it, at +0x08, is its state), and a reference point for the camera at +0x0E. Any secret flag but 0 and 1 makes a door secret, whatever its level, which is 0 in all the data: using it while the flag is clear shows "secret door" and sets it. The eleven in EhudUnder C11, E03, E11 and I09 are the only ones, and the only setters of eight flags (see [hardcoded-content](../hardcoded-content.md)). The arrival and camera points are found by ID in the destination chunk, 0x7FF8 meaning the teleporter's own position, and the import names them by map-object ID in that chunk. An arrival's chunk is the destination, so `destination` is written only where there's no arrival: 893 teleporters have an arrival, and only patioL2 (below) a destination alone. Five teleporters into Jundar's interiors A01 name camera points it doesn't have, which the original ignores.
- **A cut door.** The patioL2 door in Jundar's interiors K07 (`aidyn:mapobject/11/K07/0`, beside the reference point MainDoorsPatio) leads to A01 (`aidyn:chunk/11/A01`) at point 0x800E, which A01 doesn't have; the original would read it through a null pointer. K07 is cut content, holding only the door and the reference point, and nothing leads into it, so the runtime need only not crash on a missing arrival point.
- **Monster parties** are the port's `npc` (with a dialogue) and `encounter` (without) objects ([wanderers](../systems/wanderers.md#npcs-and-encounters)). +0x28 and +0x2A are the battle's least and most enemies, and +0x2E the home wander node, which must be a moving one. +0x24 is filled in at run time. Flag 0x4 on the party makes talking to it do nothing though it has a dialogue, so it never sets its B or starts its script. The 15 silent parties are the people in staged scenes: a dialogue object, mostly a tangible one, starts 13 of their scripts, and the party is the body standing there, which A would otherwise start again; nothing starts the other two. Flag 0x2 makes the party walk to its wander nodes rather than run. With a dialogue, flag C is the battle's encounter flag if the dialogue fights. Every party has an enemy list, most talkers' naming only themselves: of the 421 npcs, 35 have a script that starts a battle, which fights the npc's party, and the importer keeps the lists of those and of the 31 whose list names someone else, and leaves out the other 355. How a party wanders, chases and starts a fight is [wanderers](../systems/wanderers.md#wandering-noticing-and-chasing)'.
- **Reference points** hold a facing vector at +0x14, which a teleporter arriving there turns the player to.
- **Opened and destroyed models.** A herb, spice, gem or custom container hides its linked scene when it isn't there; an opened or destroyed chest, sack, crate or barrel swaps its linked scene's nearest model for its `opened` or `destroyed` (the original's second or third, `exploding_container_check`). The core's `MapObjectPresence` applies these gates ([map objects](../systems/map-objects.md#a-chunks-objects-at-run-time)).
- **Dialogue objects** (the plugin's `script` objects, ADR 0034) hold a script, imported as `plays`, a staging chunk, which is none when its row is 0, and a base the script's reference point numbers count from, a multiple of 128, imported as `marks`; a reference point's number is its `mark`. Using one doesn't write its B.
