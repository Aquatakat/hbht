# Actors: Borg7 (roadmap 1B)

An actor is a Borg7: a scene with a state machine of animations. `Borg7Actor` (`HBHT.Rom.Graphics`) reads it.

- **Borg7 (203 actors)** is a 0xC-byte header (the root transform, the state count and the initial state), then 0xC-byte states (a Borg6, a u16 of flags, and the count and offset of the branches), then 4-byte branches (request, chance, flags, target state). Some files are padded to 8 bytes with leftover text. Every actor's root transform is 2 and every actor starts in state 0. All of an actor's animations animate one scene.
- **Requests.** Each frame, the game asks each actor for an animation by number (`Borg7_SetAnimation`). The requests' names include 0 idle, 2 walk, 3 run, 7 hit, 9 dying and 25 sneak. The branches use 0–22, 25 and 255, which means any request. Dying's states go back to idle like any other's; it's the body that holds a death's last frame ([scripts](../systems/scripts.md#actors)). A state's flag 0x1 marks the initial state, in all but two actors (7613 and 7620). The game doesn't read it, and uses bit 0x2 of the same field at run time.
- **The requests as records** (`aidyn:request/0`–`25`, ADR 0033), named by what 1.0's engine asks each for. A number has no meaning across kinds of actor beyond a body's: a door's or a lever's actor (a map object's) takes 1 as already used and 2 as use, and the pause menu's scroll 0, 10, 11, 19 and 22 as its rest, opening and closing. The weapon records name their attack's request; actors' branches (none for any), emitters, sound keys, the tripwire's `animate` and the body's `strikes` name requests; scripts' PlayAnimation still takes the number. Branches per request over the 203 actors: 0 126, 1 18, 2 186, 3 174, 4 71, 5 106, 6 31, 7 67, 8 66, 9 69, 10 20, 11 8, 12 45, 13 33, 14 7, 15 14, 16 11, 17 4, 18 3, 19 25, 20 5, 21 4, 22 4, 25 175; none has 23 or 24.

  Walk, run and sneak are held, asked for each frame the stick is; `Combat.AfterFrame` (`0x80023360`) zeroes every other request a battle asks for after a frame but 0–3, 20–22 and 25. Whether an animation loops or interrupts is its actor's states' and branches', so the request records don't say.
- **Branching** (`Borg7_TickAnimation`). When the scene clock reaches the animation's length, the game rolls 0–99. It walks the branches in order, and for each whose request is the current one or any, it subtracts the chance. It takes the first branch at which the roll drops below 1, so a chance of 10 after the roll is 11 in 100. With no branch taken, the animation restarts. When the request changes, branches with flag 0x1 that match it are rolled for at once, and the animation doesn't advance that tick. A state takes only a request it has a branch for, by the request's number exactly (`ActorStateMachine.SetRequest`, `0x80096530`; 255 doesn't count); for any other the actor's request is its default, 0, which `ActorStateMachine.Tick` (`0x80096570`) puts back after every tick. So a request still asked for in the state it led to counts as none, a change, and the tick after the state is entered doesn't advance. The port's `ActorStateMachine.Taken` is the request a state takes.
- **The rolls** are `Range(0, 99)` (`Rng.RangeOn`, `0x80093384`, the game's generator on a stream it's given) on one of two streams of the actor animations' own, each shared by every actor: bodies' (`ActorStateMachine.Tick`, from `ProcessPlayers` once a body a frame) roll on the word at `0x800e69c0`, and map actors' and the pause menu's scroll's (`ActorStateMachine.Advance`, `0x800967a4`, which loops a frame's ticks) on `0x800e69c4`. Both are in `.bss`, start at 0 and are never seeded or saved, so a session's first roll is (100 × `0x3C6EF35F`) >> 32 = 23. The rolls are cosmetic; nothing in the rules reads them. Where the port's streams differ, `ActorStateMachine`'s remarks say why.
- **Brenna's actor (10723)** is a typical one. Idle (state 0) repeats 90% of the time and fidgets 10%. Walk or sneak starts a walk (1), which alternates two steps (2 and 3) and ends in a stop (4 or 5) when walking isn't requested. Run goes to state 6. Requests 5, 6, 12, 13 and 15 interrupt from 18 of her 25 states; the rest, including hit (7) and dying (9), interrupt only from idle and a few others.
- **Root motion.** The game draws the actor with transform 2's position zeroed (`Borg7_Render`), and moves the actor by how far that position moved each tick. It skips the tick a branch is taken or an animation restarts, and the two ticks after; and a body moves only while its request, or the request its state was entered under, isn't idle ([movement](../systems/movement.md)), so a stop's root returning to rest moves nothing. The walk steps are cut from one continuous walk, and skipping those ticks hides the jump back between steps. Brenna's walk comes to about 1.9 scene units a second.
- **In the port** the core's `ActorStateMachine` and `ActorMotion` (`HBHT.Core.Actors`) play an actor headlessly, timed and moved by what `ActorClips` (`HBHT.Plugins.Assets`) reads from its scene's glTF: each animation's `length` extra and the root's per-tick samples, so a mod that replaces the scene's glTF replaces its timing and motion with it. An animation the scene lacks lasts a tick, and one that doesn't drive the root moves nothing. The views' `ActorAnimator` poses the scene ([animation](animation.md)).
- **Alaron's head** is its own scene, drawn at his body's locator 3 ([code tables](code-tables.md#the-world)). The original loads it for any body that takes Alaron's actor (`Bodies.LoadActor`, `0x80014878`, the scene at `Bodies.HeadScene`, `0x800DA4A0`) and keeps it upright. It turns it toward what the player is near: `ProcessVoxelObjects` (`0x800838DC`), for trek's player body alone, keeps the nearest usable map object (header flag 0x400) within its radius as the head's target (body `+0x10C` its distance, 100 for none, `+0x110` its place), and `Bodies.Render` (`0x800142E0`) turns the head toward it, up to 80°, while it's under 100 away. The port's (`Models`) follows his neck, a stand-in.
- **Entities' actors** (`EntityModels`, from `gEntityExtras` and `dailougEnt_info_array`). Each entry is keyed by the record's position in the code's entity or dialogue entity list, plus 1. Both lists are in the same order as the DB tables. Keying by the record's ID instead would give Alaron Brenna's actor. Every entity and 32 of the 37 dialogue entities have an actor, a portrait, a height and a scale. Entities also have a collision radius and the share of an attack's animation at which its blow lands, which combat reads ([combat](../systems/combat-actions.md#an-attack)). The import gives each entity its `body`: actor, radius, scale and height. The radius of those with no sheet is 0.55, which the code gives them all, and the five without an entry get the bat (`0x2D4A`) at scale and height 1, as the code does (`EntityDB::GetBorg7`). The other actors belong to scenery, spell effects, the pause menu and the like.

The editor shows an actor's states and branches and drives them ([editor](../systems/editor.md)).
